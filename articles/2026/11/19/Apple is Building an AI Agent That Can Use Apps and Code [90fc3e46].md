# Apple is Building an AI Agent That Can Use Apps and Code

MacRumors · Juli Clover · 2026-11-19

Apple is working on a proactive AI agent like Meta's Muse or OpenAI's Dots, according to job listings spotted by Bloomberg.
The listing describes an AI agent that can code, use apps, and browse the web.
Imagine an AI agent that can use a computer the way a capable person does. It browses the web, works across apps, runs commands, writes and debugs code, and calls services, and it chooses the right tool for each step. It does all this privately and with the polish people expect from Apple. Our team is building the foundation for that future, and we're looking for a senior engineering leader to make it real.
Apple's listing notes that safety, privacy, and security need to be "central to the design" of its agentic AI. Apple is seeking someone who can work across the Siri, UI frameworks, and Apple Foundation Models teams, joining a group already working on "general-purpose computer-use agents."
People already do their most valuable work by moving fluidly between tools: a browser, a document, a terminal, an editor, a service. We're building AI agents that can do the same. We're a team of engineers, AI applied researchers, and systems specialists working on general-purpose computer-use agents. These agents plan and carry out multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and APIs, and they can coordinate specialized sub-agents along the way.
Bloomberg previously said Apple was aiming to make Siri "more like an agent" as soon as 2027. With almost every major AI company adopting proactive AI, it makes sense that Apple is rushing to create a competitive product.
Meta came out with its Muse agent in September, and the app quickly shot up the App Store charts. OpenAI followed with Dots, Anthropic launched Claude Duet, and Google turned its Gemini Spark agent into "Tasks" and added new features. All of the proactive agents offer to do tasks for users without being asked, which isn't something Siri AI is capable of.
New Apple CEO John Ternus has reportedly been pushing software engineering chief Craig Federighi to move faster on AI.
