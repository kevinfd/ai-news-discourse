# The Best Companies for Future Leaders 2027

TIME · Charlotte Hu · 2026-11-19

The career paths of future leaders may look markedly different than they did a decade ago, as AI becomes increasingly commonplace and new startups disrupt legacy corporations as early career hotspots.
TIME and Statista partnered on the fourth edition of the Best Companies for Future Leaders to highlight the career stepping stones that shaped over 5,900 influential U.S.-based leaders from business, government, academia and society, with emerging younger leaders added to the research list each year.
Analysts at Statista researched each leader’s documented education and career positions up to and including their first top-level position. For the 2027 edition, Statista also partnered with MixRank, a provider of data on companies and professional career histories, to support its research.
Of the 175 companies on the list this year, 20 companies are new compared to last year, including tech companies like Uber and AMD. McKinsey & Company and IBM stay at no. 1 and 2, as Google parent company Alphabet rises to no. 3 from no. 9 last year, making progress each year of the list. Alphabet is one of the most common career stops among TIME honorees. They are especially notable in shaping the buildout of different applications of AI, including Moonshot AI co-founder Yang Zhilin, Ricursive Intelligence co-founders Azalia Mirhoseini and Anna Goldie, Kera Health Platforms founder Moustapha Cissé, and OpenAI’s lead of Health AI Karan Singhal.
Similar to the 2026 rankings, healthcare is again the most represented industry, with 21 organizations included. Banking and Financial Services is the second-largest industry with 19 companies, with technology following closely behind with 14 IT Software & Services companies and 9 from Semiconductors, Electronics & Electrical Engineering.
Three of the “big four” consulting firms hold their positions in the top ten, showing that these companies are still important career launchpads for leaders, especially as early-career stopovers, as they provide hands-on experiences related to how businesses operate across a wide range of industries. Some also provide skills to adapt to the changing business and technology landscape as well. For example, PwC (no. 9) launched a critical thinking and judgment certification for more than 85,000 of its employees this November as a way to strengthen human capabilities as AI transforms jobs. The certificate is designed to “help professionals navigate complex decisions, establish context, challenge information and assumptions, and communicate recommendations with clarity and accountability.”
Notably, companies in Media & Advertising, rank higher in this year’s list compared to years before, a reflection of individuals featured in TIME’s editorial lists and their prominence and influence in the U.S. culture. Disney rises to no. 6 this year, up from no. 23 last year. Disney’s new CEO, Josh D’Amaro was included on the list of TIME’s 100 Most Influential People of 2026. The newly launched TIME100 Sports also added notable personalities from ESPN, such as Elle Duncan and Bill Simmons, to the consideration list. SkyDance, formed as a merger between Warner Bros. and Paramount, is ranked no. 12, higher than either of the individual companies were ranked last year. The merger is set to make SkyDance one of the biggest players in entertainment in the coming years, putting channels including HBO, Showtime, CBS, MTV, Comedy Central, Cartoon Network, Nickelodeon, TNT, TBS and CNN under one umbrella.
