# No Bailout for the AI Bubble, Anthropic IPO Protesters Say

Tech Policy Press · cdrnsf · 2026-11-19

No Bailout for the AI Bubble, Anthropic IPO Protesters Say
Justin Hendrix / Nov 19, 2026
On Thursday, Anthropic executives were at Nasdaq in New York City’s Times Square for the opening bell. The company’s initial public offering (IPO) saw it price shares at $83, and initial demand appears strong before the stock starts trading at midday. But while CEO Dario Amodei and his team of executives — all of whom are set to “reap fortunes” today — were all smiles as they applauded the listing, outside Nasdaq a group of demonstrators had rather different expressions.
Affiliated with groups including Stop Funding Billionaires, Climate Defiance, Sunrise NYC, Act Up NYC, NYC-DSA, JVP-NYC, Climate Defenders, Planet Over Profit and New York Communities for Change, the demonstrators held signs that read “Stop Tech Oligarchs” and “Freeze AI” as the stock ticker raced by on screens behind them. Representatives of the group used a small, hot-pink megaphone to address the media and onlookers. Between speeches, they chanted slogans such as “Hey, hey. Ho-ho. This IPO has got to go!” and “Anthropic, get off it, put people over profit!”
Their concerns ranged from the potential economic impact if the AI bubble bursts to AI’s effects on the environment, healthcare, surveillance, and jobs.
“We're here because Anthropic poses a threat to us, not Terminator-Skynet-style, even though that might be true, but pension fund-style, because AI is a bubble, and it's a worse bubble than 2008 with the real estate crisis,” said James Inniss, an organizers with New York Communities for Change. “And if our pension funds are the ones that are going to be stripped in five, 10 years when this bubble bursts, we are the ones that have to be out here to fight and tell everybody that that is not acceptable, that is not okay, and we are not standing for that,” he said.
Bryce Rogers, an organizer with a group called Resisting Automated Disempowerment (RAD), said he was there to protest the fact that the IPO was moving ahead despite a lack of information about the risks the company’s technology poses. He said he’d read the auditing firm METR’s recent report on Anthropic, but was left with more questions than answers.
“All these years that I've been storyboarding the upcoming chapters of my life, these oligarchs have been at the negotiation table with the publishing company,” said Rogers. “It's just been moving so much faster than I foresaw and faster than our government has been able to maturely react.” He praised New York’s Responsible AI Safety and Education (RAISE) Act, and called for more organizing to demand greater regulation and transparency. “We can reclaim authorship of our future,” he said.
ACT UP New York’s Brandon Cuicchi said he was concerned about AI’s impact on healthcare.
“What we are seeing around the country already in healthcare is horrifying,” said Cuicchi. “We have hospitals that are cutting staff and trying to replace it with AI. We have medical offices that are trying to pre-screen people. Instead of having a live professional, they will try to screen you with AI. We have AI deciding medical outcomes and treatments in this country. Shame. Shame.”
Kenny Moll, an activist affiliated with a campaign called QuitGPT and the Sunrise Movement, said he was concerned about the environmental impact of AI, but also about the relationship between AI firms, surveillance, and the government.
“These companies are working hand in hand with fascist governments. We cannot allow this,” he said.
Aber Kawas, a Palestinian-American democratic socialist recently elected as a New York State Senator, said AI companies are among the biggest enemies of the progressive movement.
“People across this nation are trying to turn the tide to create a country to have their state, their city invest in communities during an affordability crisis, and not in war and destruction,” said Kawas. “And the biggest enemies to that are AI companies and corporate interests, which drive our government to invest in things like AI companies which are destroying our climate, destroying people's lives,” she said.
There were arrests. One demonstrator in a Greenpeace T-shirt was led away by police after seating themselves with a sign at the entrance to Nasdaq. In a statement following the demonstration, organizers attributed a quote to one of the demonstrators that was arrested. “Our demand is clear: freeze reckless AI development and don’t bail out Big Tech when their bubble inevitably pops,” they said.
