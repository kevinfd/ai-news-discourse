# James McAvoy’s random encounter triggers a delusional fantasy in Parasocial Activity

The Guardian · Mark Fisher · 2026-11-19

Knowing what to believe is getting harder. Technology has blurred the line between truth and lies. Certain facts, though, we can demonstrate. We know, for example, that James McAvoy appeared in Cyrano de Bergerac at the Theatre Royal in Glasgow in the mask-wearing spring of 2022. We can also take playwright Briony Martha at her word when she says she bumped into the actor in a nearby coffee shop.
It is even true that McAvoy was in the audience of Martha’s Parasocial Activity towards the end of this summer’s run on the Edinburgh fringe: a performance that can only have had a heightened air of unreality.
Because Martha’s play, which she performs with wide-eyed energy, imagines what might have happened after that chance encounter. In a pink jumpsuit and Hello Kitty T-shirt, she plays Jenny, physically a woman, emotionally a child, who meets the celebrity in the Sauchiehall Street branch of Costa Coffee and convinces herself there is romantic significance in his gift of two tickets to see him on stage.
In another era, Jenny’s fantasy would have remained just that: an innocuous dream. But in the age of generative AI, she has the digital tools to sustain that fantasy. In Megan Brewer’s high-precision production, the delusion is as convincing to the audience as it is to her. Sure, if McAvoy has gone to the trouble of following her on Instagram and posing for a picture at the stage door, why would he not also text her an affirming message, sweetly reflecting back her desires?
The more she engages, the more he tells her what she wants to hear. We all love a rags-to-riches love story, so we want to hear it too. Except when the interaction goes beyond innocent flirting and into AI psychosis, we find ourselves, like Jenny, adrift on the wrong side of the truth/lies divide.
- At Òran Mór, Glasgow, until 21 November. At Southwark Playhouse, London, 13-30 January.
