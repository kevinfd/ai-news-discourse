# Academic integrity matters, but students shouldn’t be penalised for using AI | Letter

The Guardian · Guardian Staff · 2026-11-19

Is this really the best we can do: draconically penalise the young for making use of the latest cognitive resources and tools available to them (Ofqual urges ‘life-altering’ penalties for students who use tech to cheat, 18 November)? Our generation has allowed the introduction of “life-altering” new technologies into every walk of life without much democratic debate, public scrutiny or governmental oversight. And now our education leaders are threatening to jeopardise young people’s career prospects for deploying these technologies within an education system that can’t cope and struggles to adapt?
We all know that AI is here to stay. And importantly, it is so much more than just a cheap cheating device. Within an incredibly short space of time, it has transformed how we work, communicate, solve problems, research and learn. Surely the pedagogical response cannot simply be to devise ever more elaborate ways of preventing students from using it?
Of course, academic integrity matters. There is an important distinction between using AI as a cognitive resource and passing off the result as entirely one’s own work. But placing the ethical burden of navigating this technological tsunami on young people while the adult world struggles to catch up is an extraordinary dereliction of pedagogical responsibility and care. Our job is to provide future-proof guidance and teach the young how to engage with and live and work alongside technologies that our generation continues to welcome so naively and unquestioningly into the world.
 
Berthold Schoene 
  
Professor emeritus, Manchester Metropolitan University
