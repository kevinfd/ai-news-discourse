# Anthropic workers can swap their shares for these 3 homes. Getting to the office is another question.

Business Insider · Katherine Li, Katherine Li (katherineli@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-19

1210 Chickory Lane in Los Angeles is one of the priciest publicly listed properties accepting Anthropic shares as payment. Courtesy of Carolwood Estates Anthropic is eyeing a $1.4 trillion valuation IPO on Thursday. Anthropic employees could use their IPO shares to buy multimillion-dollar homes without cashing out. Listings range from a $6 million Brooklyn townhouse to a nearly $30 million Los Angeles mansion. With Anthropic's long-awaited IPO set to kick off Thursday, luxury home sellers across the country are dangling an unusual offer to its employees: Trade your Anthropic equity for a multimillion-dollar home, and — depending on the address — a radically different commute. With luxury homes proving harder to move in a challenging real estate market , sellers are getting creative about finding buyers, and the impending IPO has put a pool of prospective homeowners on their radar. At its targeted $1.4 trillion valuation, Anthropic would be one of the world's most valuable companies despite having only 5,300 employees. For workers with meaningful equity, the IPO could turn their paper fortunes into something they could touch and live in. Here are three properties Anthropic employees could buy directly with their shares — unless they want to stake their entire fortune on AI — and the commutes that come with them. Los Angeles: The priciest property yet Courtesy of Carolwood Estates The 1210 Chickory Lane mansion in the Brentwood neighborhood of Los Angeles, listed for $29,995,000, may be the most valuable property yet to accept equity from an AI company as payment. The newly built five-bedroom estate sits at the end of a quiet path, with floor-to-ceiling glass, an infinity pool, and a rooftop bar offering 270-degree views from downtown Los Angeles to the Pacific. Designed by IR Architects and Colossal Properties, it also includes a home theater, wine room, gym, sauna, chef's kitchen, four-car garage, and an elevator. The closest Anthropic office to the property, however, is a five-hour drive away in San Francisco. Ideal for those who interpret RTO as a test of personal endurance. The highlight is the view Courtesy of Carolwood Estates Phillip Braunstein, the owner of the property and a developer, told Business Insider that he offered to trade the house for equity in Anthropic because the local market for luxury homes was soft, especially after a proposed billionaire tax sparked talk of moving among those who could afford such a home. "I believe the business behind the demand for intelligence may hit road bumps, but overall it's going to be up and to the right," Braunstein said. "So I think that even if there was a correction and the shares became worth less than what I had set the dollar amount of the house for, over time they would be worth multiples of that amount in the long enough timeframe," Braunstein added. The house has 110 feet of glass Courtesy of Carolwood Estates Philip said he uses AI extensively in his own life, from experimenting with stock-trading tools to automating routine tasks. He said an AI agent now handles his bill payments, including his mortgage and utility bills. "I would say the views are probably number one," Braunstein said of his favorite features of the house. "There's also 110 feet of glass that's motorized. Even though it's very modern, it's also very warm-feeling. You feel tucked in when you go down into the house." New York City: A luxury apartment in Tribeca Francisco Rosario | DD-Reps Sebastian Sagar, a finance professional now based in Florida, previously told Business Insider that he listed his Tribeca apartment at 415 Washington Street for $6.25 million and would prefer payment in Anthropic shares. The apartment features a patio overlooking the neighborhood, a kitchen with dark marble countertops, and oversize windows. Sagar said he bought the property for about $7 million in late 2024 and first listed it in June 2025. He has since cut the asking price and switched brokers. The idea of swapping the apartment for Anthropic stock came after Sagar learned that the AI company was opening an office at 330 Hudson Street, roughly a 10-minute walk away. Close enough that a delayed elevator is the only transit disruption worth discussing. A Brooklyn four-bedroom with a fireplace The Brooklyn home, listed for $5.99 million, has four bedrooms, three full bathrooms, two powder rooms, and a finished basement with 12-foot ceilings. Barak Blackburn Team A Williamsburg seller is asking $5.99 million for their Brooklyn home and told Business Insider they will accept bitcoin or vested Anthropic shares as payment. The four-bedroom home sits in a private mews beside Domino Park. It has three full baths, two powder rooms, a finished basement, and a garage with EV charging. Features include a landscaped garden, a rooftop deck with Manhattan skyline views, white oak floors, and a full-floor living room with a gas fireplace. The seller, who has lived in the neighborhood for a decade and uses Anthropic's products daily, said in a statement to Business Insider in June that news of the company's confidential IPO filing prompted them to add shares as an option.
