# What does the Anthropic IPO mean for your 401(k)?

Fast Company · Adele Peters · 2026-11-19

You may have no interest in investing in AI companies, and you may even suspect that the AI boom is a bubble about to pop. But you could already have a stake in one of the industry’s biggest players anyway: Anthropic, which went public today, may be in your retirement account or about to show up there.
Some mutual funds, including American Funds Growth Fund of America (AGTHX) and Fidelity Select Technology (FSPTX), hold substantial equity stakes in Anthropic. And some index funds will also soon acquire shares, after recently changing their rules to make it easier for massive companies to join shortly after going public. Previously, companies often had to wait months or a year to be included in major indexes, in part because of concerns about stock volatility.
Earlier this year, ahead of the SpaceX IPO, Nasdaq shortened its waiting period from three months to 15 trading days for qualified mega-IPOs. Nasdaq argued that companies as large as SpaceX and Anthropic shouldn’t have to wait so long.
FTSE Russell has similarly shortened its waiting period to just five trading days, though inclusion depends on how many shares are available for public trading. Morningstar already had a short five-day waiting period, but relaxed its rules about available shares. The S&P 500, meanwhile, still requires companies to wait 12 months and turn a profit before joining—two hurdles Anthropic has yet to clear.
When Anthropic is included in a retirement portfolio, it won’t have a large weight initially, Rodney Comegys, chief investment officer at Vanguard Capital Management and head of global equity at Vanguard, tells Fast Company. That’s because a relatively small portion of the company’s shares will be available to public investors to begin with. Vanguard has estimated that Anthropic will represent 0.05%–0.15% of its total market index fund. If you have $500,000 in that fund, that might mean $250 to $750 worth of indirect exposure to Anthropic.
How to find Anthropic in your 401(k)
To know if your 401(k) or an index fund you own will include Anthropic, you’ll have to dig into the details. “Most people don’t look at their index funds, or anything that their 401(k) is invested in,” says John Polonis, a former Wall Street lawyer who now writes about finance. Even if you’ve chosen not to invest in the Nasdaq-100, for example, the stock could still be hiding somewhere else. Other funds, including those that invest in the largest publicly traded companies, could also hold Anthropic shares. “There may be ways that you are getting exposure,” Polonis says, “and if you don’t want any exposure at all, you really have to do a lot of diligence to kind of screen that.”
To check your own portfolio, log into your 401(k) account. If you have actively managed mutual funds, look up each fund’s portfolio holdings and search for Anthropic. If you have index funds, check to see which indexes they track. The indexes that will soon acquire Anthropic stock include Russell 1000, Russell 3000, Nasdaq-100, Morningstar U.S., FTSE Global All Cap, and MSCI USA.
