# Anthropic accused of self-serving hypocrisy on eve of $1.5-trillion IPO

The Hindu · Vasudevan Mukunth · 2026-11-19

A group of science and industry leaders, including Nobel laureates and members of xAI and Hugging Face, has accused U.S. company Anthropic of contradicting its own warnings about the dangers of increasingly autonomous artificial intelligence (AI) by racing to develop the very same capabilities, in a short letter titled “The MisAnthropic Statement” published online.
The group has also charged that Anthropic has been weakening its own safety commitments as well as influencing government regulations to its advantage.
The statement appeared online on November 18, only a day ahead of Anthropic’s shares beginning to trade on the Nasdaq stock exchange under the symbol ‘ANT’. The company has priced its initial public offering (IPO) (IPO) at $83, more than its previously indicated $70-78, with a valuation of around $1.5 trillion. The offering itself is of 980 million shares worth around $81.3 billion.
The MisAnthropic statement has been signed by Harvard University psychologist Steven Pinker, economics Nobel laureate Oliver Hart, Turing Award winner Judea Pearl, mathematicians Peter Scholze, James Maynard, and David Mumford, and legal scholars Lawrence Lessig and Laurence Tribe. Technology industry signatories include Hugging Face cofounder Thomas Wolf, xAI cofounder Igor Babuschkin, and AI researcher Gary Marcus. Physicist Max Tegmark, Center for AI safety executive director Dan Hendrycks, and AI acceleration proponent Guillaume Verdon have signed as well.
This list of names is notable because these individuals have been known to have divergent views on the dangers of AI use and the extent to which its development should be restricted. While some, including Mr. Tegmark and technology ethicist Tristan Harris, have said sufficiently powerful AI models could threaten humankind’s survival, Mr. Pinker and others have opposed restrictions they have said would impede technological progress that is necessary.
