# Anthropic to begin public trading at $1.5tn valuation after largest ever IPO

The Guardian · Johana Bhuiyan · 2026-11-19

Anthropic debuts on the US stock market on Thursday after raising the largest amount of investment in history for an initial public offering.
Public trading will begin at $83 per share, higher than the expected range set last week, making the company’s fully diluted market cap $1.5tn. Anthropic announced on Wednesday that it had sold 980m shares to underwriters, an upsized offering that raised $81.3bn, surpassing the previous record set by SpaceX’s initial $75bn fundraise in June.
The artificial intelligence startup, which makes the Claude chatbot, is expected to become one of the most valuable publicly traded businesses in the world, though it was founded in just 2021.
CEO Dario Amodei is expected to ring the bell to open trading on the Nasdaq exchange. The company’s listing will serve as a bellwether for the multitrillion-dollar artificial intelligence boom as the first frontier lab to be publicly traded. Wall Street investors and the entire global tech industry are watching closely how it fares on the public market.
The company has seen explosive growth in revenue every year of its existence – sometimes more than 1,000% – according to its investor prospectus, filed at the end of October. Anthropic brought in $16.2bn in the first six months of 2026, up from $4.6bn in revenue for all of 2025.
“You’ve seen an astronomical growth rate in terms of the top line,” said Evan Schlossman, a principal at Neostellar, a publicly traded venture capital firm. “The question investors are going to be looking at leading up to the IPO, and once it’s public, is what happens to that growth rate.”
Anthropic has also lost an enormous amount of money. In the first half of 2026, the company reported losses of $80bn, though that was largely due to a one-off charge. Losses grew to $104bn in the first nine months of 2026. The firm expects its costs to balloon as it works to secure more compute, which refers to the processing and computational power provided by arrays of semiconductor chips often located in data centers. It is the resource that powers generative artificial intelligence and Anthropic’s single largest expense, about $18.8bn in the first nine months of 2026.
“The development of frontier AI models requires substantial investments of compute resources, capital, and human capital to conduct large-scale training runs that may span weeks or months and cost many millions of dollars,” the company’s executives wrote in the prospectus.
Anthropic has disclosed in great detail all the ways its AI models may pose a danger to humanity – including being used to develop bioweapons or deepen repression around the world. Amodei has called for a slowdown on the development of the most advanced AI models after employees expressed concerns that the companies were building them irresponsibility.
The company is expected to enjoy a premium on its stock price because it beat competitors like OpenAI to launching on the stock market. However, the IPO comes amid uncertainty as the industry braces for any additional impact or fallout from the recent Nvidia scandal that led to its CEO’s ouster.
There is “huge volatility in the market”, and investors are going to be looking at how sustainable the company’s AI growth is, said Schlossman. Investors are also keen to see how important Anthropic will be in the larger AI ecosystem. The company has focused primarily on enterprise customers rather than individuals consumers. Anthropic’s Claude code product has proven popular with software engineers.
“Where we’ve seen Anthropic really do well is on the enterprise side, specifically coding,” Schlossman said.
