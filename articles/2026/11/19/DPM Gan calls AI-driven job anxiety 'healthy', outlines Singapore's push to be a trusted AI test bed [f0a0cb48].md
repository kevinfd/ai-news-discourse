# DPM Gan calls AI-driven job anxiety 'healthy', outlines Singapore's push to be a trusted AI test bed

AsiaOne · 2026-11-19

Singapore is positioning itself as a trusted global test bed for artificial intelligence while ushering in a landmark regional digital pact — the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) — said Deputy Prime Minister and Minister for Energy, Trade and Industry Gan Kim Yong on Wednesday (Nov 18).
Speaking at Singapore FinTech Festival 2026, he also urged fresh graduates to view AI-driven job anxiety as a "healthy" motivator to keep upgrading their skills.
During a fireside chat with Caesar Sengupta, co-founder and chief executive officer of Studio C6, Gan shared how he personally relies on AI to summarise research, trade data and background briefings ahead of negotiations and meetings.
However, he stressed that "human intelligence" must always oversee the technology, noting that users cannot take AI-generated answers at face value without applying their own judgment.
Building trust, talent and test beds
Noting that major labs such as Google, OpenAI and Anthropic have established local offices here, Sengupta pointed to Singapore's Economic Strategy Review, which identifies deploying AI as the nation's key competitive edge.
Gan emphasised that strengthening trust among the government, industry players and users is the starting point for scaling agentic AI, building on Singapore's reputation as a trusted financial hub.

To overcome talent constraints in a small city-state, Singapore is keeping its doors open to global practitioners while developing Kampong AI, a collaborative community designed to bring AI talents together.
The government has also launched three AI missions - strengthening financial crime prevention, improving access to financing, and enabling safe agentic transactions - for the financial sector to attract hyperscalers, developers and experts to co-create solutions locally.
Real-world deployment is also taking shape in Gan's own constituency, where the Punggol Digital District has been designated as an AI and robotics test bed. Residents there will increasingly see robots roaming the district as part of efforts to help the wider community adapt to living alongside AI-enabled technologies.
'Healthy' anxiety and lifelong learning
Drawing parallels to the early dot-com era and the rise of e-commerce, Gan predicted that companies will eventually shift from merely using AI for efficiency to building "AI-native businesses" from the ground up.
While echoing Prime Minister Lawrence Wong's stance that the government cannot protect every job from technological disruption but will protect every worker, Gan told university, polytechnic and Institute of Technical Education (ITE) graduates to embrace continuous upskilling rather than fear the shift.
"I will tell you that the anxiety is a healthy one. I think if you totally ignore the effect of AI and it’s just ‘business as usual’, I think we all will be in trouble. I think it's good to be anxious. It’s not wrong to have this anxiety," he said.
He even joked to a local polytechnic that it should abolish the concept of alumni because learning never stops, while assuring founders and investors that the government acts as a supportive partner rather than a problem.
Human judgment over AI agents
Mid-way through the session, Sengupta invited his startup's proprietary AI agent, C6, which functions as the company's "sixth team member" to question Gan on how AI agents can work alongside humans with natural oversight. Gan quipped that an AI moderator might replace Sengupta before arguing that face-to-face human interaction remains irreplaceable for reading subtle facial cues and underlying intent.
While AI can summarise hours of dialogue in seconds, Gan maintained that human judgment must guide how that information is used to achieve real-world goals. "I believe strongly in AI," he said, adding that he believes "even stronger in human intelligence."
