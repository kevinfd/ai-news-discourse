# Anthropic shares jump in Nasdaq trading debut after record IPO

CBS News · Mary Cunningham, Aimee Picchi · 2026-11-19

Anthropic shares gain 17% on first day of trading after record IPO
Anthropic's stock soared in early trading on Nasdaq Thursday before partly shedding those gains in the afternoon, as the artificial intelligence developer completed the largest initial public offering in history.
Trading under the ticker "ANT," Anthropic shares opened at $111.50, up 34% from the $83 offer price, and rose as high as $114.99, valuing the company at more than $2 trillion at the peak. The stock pared those gains over the course of the day, closing at $97.17, a rise of 17%.
"Both the jump from the offer price and the high volatility are not surprising, given the enormous potential of the company and the wildly different opinions about how profitable the company will become," Jay Ritter, an IPO expert and professor at the University of Florida's Warrington College of Business, said in an email.
Anthropic shares rose on a day when the rest of the stock market took a dive. Late in Thursday's session, the Nasdaq dropped 550 points, or 2.3%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average tumbled more than 1,100 points, or 2.2%, and the S&P 500 sank 2%.
"Seminal" moment
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei celebrated Anthropic's market debut at the Nasdaq MarketSite in New York City on Thursday.
"The goal is to make the whole industry better, regardless of whether Anthropic is winning or losing at any given time," Dario Amodei said Thursday in an event to mark the IPO.
"The advent of machine intelligence is one of the seminal moments in human history, and we have a rare opportunity to shape it for the better," he added.
San Francisco-based Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in its IPO, catapulting past SpaceX, which raised $75 billion when it went public in June. The IPO values the company behind the Claude chatbot at roughly $1.5 trillion, more than companies such as Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway, JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Walmart.
Anthropic's IPO represents an "A-plus success," Dan Ives, a tech analyst and partner of Yorkville Ives & Co., told CBS News. "I think it's a vote of confidence for the market relative to the tech trade," he added.
The public offering delivered a huge payday to the seven Anthropic co-founders, boosting their worth on paper to around $20 billion each. It will also turn many Anthropic employees into millionaires.
Anthropic said Wednesday that it would offer 980 million shares of Class A common stock priced at $83 per share, allowing it to raise a record-breaking amount. Anthropic originally priced its shares in a range of $70 to $78, but raised its per-share IPO price amid strong investor demand.
The IPO price of $83 a share is what Anthropic insiders and other early investors pay to receive an allocation of stock. According to investment bank Renaissance Capital, 7.4% of Anthropic shares are available for public trading (or up to 9.8% if Class A shares held by non-founder employees are included).
Even if Americans don't buy shares, Anthropic's IPO could still affect their 401(k), given how exposed the stock market is to AI stocks.
Anthropic has previously said it will raise an additional $26 billion from a slate of other companies, including chipmaker AMD, chip infrastructure provider Broadcom, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, bringing its fundraising total to around $107 billion.
Matthew Kennedy, a senior market strategist at Renaissance Capital, thinks Anthropic shares could reach $100 by the end of their first day of trading.
However, like many companies that go public in a blockbuster, and perhaps overhyped, share offering, Anthropic's stock could well waver in the months to come. In a Truist study of 32 of the biggest ever IPOs, more than half saw their shares slide by at least 50% at some point during their first year of trading.
Anthropic announced its plans to go public in October, inviting investors to be part of what it called one of the "seminal moments in human history." The company is the first frontier AI lab to go public.
Critical time for AI trade
Anthropic's IPO comes at a critical time for the AI industry, as investors are asking harder questions about the sector's growing losses, capital investments and returns, Sam Grelck, an equity strategy analyst at Truist Advisory Services, a unit of Truist Financial, told CBS News.
"I wouldn't view Anthropic's IPO as a verdict on AI, but the reception can give us another indication of investors' appetite for the AI theme after what's been a challenging few weeks," he said.
AI-related stocks have swung sharply in recent weeks as concerns mount about the mountainous debt companies are taking on to finance the data center buildout and a potential Democratic-led crackdown on the AI boom following the midterms. Jensen Huang's shock exit from Nvidia last week has also rattled investors.
At a time when investors are skittish, the robust demand for Anthropic shares suggests there is still faith that lofty investments in AI will pay off.
"Anthropic's IPO is a big near-term win for the tech sector and should help restore some investor confidence that the AI Revolution growth thesis is not waning," Ives said in a research note.
Financing its future
The IPO provides Anthropic with cash to finance its AI development plans. The company, which is yet to turn a profit, has said it wants to use its technology to "improve how humans live" by providing tailored financial and legal advice and speeding up scientific discovery.
"I believe sufficient and powerful AI, working in concert with humans, could compress a century of medical and scientific progress into a decade," Amodei said at Nasdaq in New York on Thursday.
While the IPO will boost its coffers, Anthropic could quickly burn through cash as it works to keep its AI models on the cutting edge and stay ahead of rivals like OpenAI.
As of Sept. 30, the company had $6.2 billion in debt, according to its regulatory filing. At the same time, it has committed to spending nearly $1 trillion on chips and AI infrastructure through 2045.
