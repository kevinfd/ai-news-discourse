# Apple wants its own AI agent for the Mac, and a late 2027 launch is already in view

Digital Trends · Anvinraj Valiyathara · 2026-11-19

Apple is working on its own AI assistant that can use a Mac the way a capable person would, according to a Bloomberg report. The report is based on a job listing Apple posted this week for a senior engineering manager to lead a proactive AI assistant. The posting asks you to imagine an agent that “can use a computer the way a capable person does.” According to the listing, that helper would browse the web and work across apps, then run commands or write and debug code.
What would this assistant do on your Mac?
The listing says Apple is building agents that “plan and carry out multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code, and APIs, and they can coordinate specialized sub-agents along the way.” The hire would work with teams across Siri, UI Frameworks, and Apple Foundation Models. This lines up with an earlier report that Apple wants Siri to act more like an autonomous helper, closer to Meta’s Muse and ChatGPT’s Dots.
Apple appears to have pulled the listing, since its careers site now says the job cannot be found. Before it came down, AI commentator Andrew Curran shared a screenshot of what appears to be the original posting on X. It matches the role number, the November 17 posting date, and the text quoted in the report.
Bloomberg says the work is still in the early stages, though Apple has eyed a launch as soon as the end of 2027. The listing also stresses safety, noting that agents that can run code and act on your behalf need “strong permission models, sandboxing, and transparency.”
Apple is also making it harder for other agents to control your Mac
At the same time, Apple is tightening how third-party agents get access to a Mac. Bloomberg reports that turning on ChatGPT’s ability to control a Mac in the macOS 27.3 beta now takes 14 distinct steps, up from four. Apple points to security as the reason, though Bloomberg says some AI companies and users see an attempt to slow competition. Those changes follow the company’s broader effort to limit how much access Mac apps can get in this same macOS 27.3 beta.
An assistant that can drive your Mac would go much further than today’s Siri, even after Apple opened Siri AI to more third-party actions. While Apple has not confirmed the project, the job listing is the clearest sign of where its AI plans are heading.
