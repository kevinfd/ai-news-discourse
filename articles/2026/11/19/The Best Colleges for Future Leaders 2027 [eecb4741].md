# The Best Colleges for Future Leaders 2027

TIME · Charlotte Hu · 2026-11-19

Women make up a majority of the college-educated labor force in the U.S., according to Pew Research Survey. This factor could partly explain why they are also a rising share of managers and professionals, although there is still room to grow in their representation in top executive roles—a working paper from a World Bank economist found that only 16% of CFOs and 7% of CEOs in large U.S. and European companies were women in 2023.
For the fourth time, TIME and Statista have traced the formative experiences and notable paths of the latest class of 5,900 American leaders across business, government, academia, science, healthcare, media, sports, culture and philanthropy. Their resumés reveal 125 colleges that most shaped their careers. This dataset is updated yearly to include younger leaders in emerging fields and industries, including new entrants from the TIME100 Most Influential People in Health, Philanthropy, Sports, and Creators.
This year, data partner Statista focused on growing both the longlist and the career data behind it, with particular attention to the representation of women leaders. “We added 999 new leaders to the longlist,” says Angela Peckham, an analyst at Statista. “Women now make up roughly 45% of the longlist.”
Even with these additions, the top of the college rankings stay relatively stable year over year, as Harvard and Stanford take no. 1 and 2 again this year, showing how concentrated their leadership pipelines are. These two institutions are also the most common alma maters among TIME honorees, including neurologist and best-selling author Freida McFadden, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar, and AI educator and creator Ethan Mollick.
Of the 125 institutions on the list, 10 are new this year. Although the majority of the top colleges ranked similarly compared to last year, University of Michigan moves into the top ten at no. 9, with alumni including pediatrician Mona Hanna and psychologist Jean Twenge.
With the heightened emphasis on the career paths of women leaders, Smith College and Spelman both rise in rankings compared to last year, and Wellesley College (no. 60) enters the list for the first time. Women-only institutions, some of which, including Wellesley and Smith, are over 150 years old, are still popular and serve as incubators for future leaders. According to the Boston Globe, Wellesley saw a spike in applications after Hillary Clinton, a famous alum, ran for president in 2016. In 2024, Wellesley launched the Hillary Rodham Clinton Center for Citizenship, Leadership, and Democracy to help nurture the next generation of civic leaders. A research project conducted in partnership with labor market analytics firm Lightcast in 2025 showed that Wellesley College graduates “are 17% better represented in executive, legislative, and other government roles than alumnae of other selective colleges and universities,” and they also “excel in fields where women are traditionally underrepresented.”
