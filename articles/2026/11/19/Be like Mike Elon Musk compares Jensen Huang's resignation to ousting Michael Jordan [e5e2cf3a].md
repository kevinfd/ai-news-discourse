# Be like Mike? Elon Musk compares Jensen Huang's resignation to ousting Michael Jordan

Business Insider · Pete Syme, Pete Syme (psyme@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-19

Jensen Huang is drawing comparisons to Michael Jordan. Tomohiro Ohsumi/Getty Images; Brian Drake/NBAE via Getty Images Elon Musk compared Jensen Huang's resignation to ousting NBA legend Michael Jordan. "There's no one that can really replace Jensen," Musk said. As well as winning huge acclaim, Huang and Jordan inspired admiration and sometimes fear among colleagues. Is Jensen Huang the GOAT of tech? Elon Musk compared the Nvidia founder's resignation to ousting the basketball legend Michael Jordan . "There's no one that can really replace Jensen. He does the roles of several people," Musk said Wednesday at The New York Times' DealBook Summit. The SpaceX CEO pointed to Huang's management of Nvidia's supply chain and his long-term relationships with critical suppliers, bolstered by eating street food together in South Korea and Taiwan. "His team loves him. Loves him. And many of them are there because of him and have had higher offers to go elsewhere, and have turned them down because of personal loyalty to Jensen," Musk said. "I think it's like kicking Michael Jordan off your team when you're on a winning streak, and it doesn't seem like a good thing for shareholders," he added. "You have to balance the good that someone does and brings, relative to the bad, you know?" Huang resigned from Nvidia earlier this month after the board opened an investigation into whether he was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort." He also apologized for being unfaithful to his wife. Huang's last dance It's not the first time the Huang-Jordan comparison has been made. Last week, Musk reposted a clip from "Air," a 2023 movie about the foundation of the Air Jordan brand, in which Nike executive Sonny Vacaro, played by Matt Damon, delivers a prophetic monologue. And one viral X post edited Huang into iconic Jordan moments like "The Shot," his buzzer-beater in the 1989 playoffs, and his emotional embrace of the Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy. But the comparison is more apt than simply winning huge acclaim in their respective fields. Jordan and Huang exhibited fierce devotion while inspiring both fear and admiration. Various media outlets have reported in the past month that Huang was known by his employees to be a demanding boss who was prone to yelling and berating people in meetings. "He put Nvidia first, above any person," an unnamed executive told The Wall Street Journal . Jordan, meanwhile, was notorious for berating his teammates and once punched Steve Kerr in the face during practice. "We were afraid of him," Jud Buechler, a former Bulls forward, said in the documentary miniseries "The Last Dance." It is also clear that Huang is hugely admired within and outside Nvidia. More than 100,000 people have signed a petition to reinstate Huang . "Over the past week, I've been overwhelmed by the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I've never met," Huang said Tuesday . He added that he would be "honored" to return, putting the ball back in the Nvidia board's court. However, the board is not considering bringing him back , Business Insider reported. After winning three NBA championships in a row, Jordan retired in 1993 — the year Nvidia was founded — saying he lost his motivation and had "nothing more to prove in basketball." He also said he wanted to spend more time with his family after his father was murdered a few months earlier. The announcement stunned the sports world. Less than two years later, Jordan returned to the Chicago Bulls with an iconic two-word statement that some Nvidians will also be hoping to hear: "I'm back."
