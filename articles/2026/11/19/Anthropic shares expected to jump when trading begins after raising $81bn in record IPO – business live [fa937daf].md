# Anthropic shares expected to jump when trading begins after raising $81bn in record IPO – business live

The Guardian · Graeme Wearden · 2026-11-19

Introduction: Anthropic's record IPO ahead of today's stock market float
Good morning, and welcome to our rolling coverage of the world’s biggest stock market listing, and other events across business, the financial markets and the world economy.
We’re going to see the biggest initial public offering in history today; Anthropic is floating on the US stock market, in what will be a major test for the market’s enthusiasm for the AI industry.
Despite fears that AI may be a bubble, primed to pop, Anthropic – the frontier AI firm behind the Claude chatbot – has seen a surge of demand from investors looking to buy a stake.
Last night, Anthropic announced it had raised a record-breaking sum from its IPO. In a sign of strong demand, the company increased the price at which its selling its shares to $83, up from a previous guide of $70-$78.
That will raise $81.3bn (£61.8bn), exceeding the $75bn raised by Elon Musk’s SpaceX in June.
If you include another $26bn raised through a private sale of shares to tech firms, Anthropic’s IPO has secured a record sum of $107.3bn, Sky News has calculated.
Anthropic’s CEO Dario Amodei and his fellow co-founders are expected to ring the Nasdaq opening bell at 9.30am New York time (2.30pm in the UK).
The company will then make its debut on the Nasdaq under the symbol “ANT”, although we may have to wait a little while before its shares start trading.
Anthropic joins the market at a time when the Nvidia crisis has rattled investors. Tech stocks have dropped in recent weeks, pulling the Nasdaq Composite down by over 10% from its record high at the start of October.
The IPO will value Anthropic at around $1.5tn (£1.1tn), placing it around 12th on the list of the world’s biggest companies.
That’s despite the company being loss-making; in 2025 it posted an operating loss of over $8bn, as expenses (nearly $13bn) outpaced revenues ($4.6bn).
Revenues have accelerated in 2026, though, but the company’s challenge is to a) maintain this, and b) turn it into profits.
Garry White, chief investment commentator at wealth manager Raymond James, explains:
 “Anthropic’s IPO should be a clear litmus test of current market sentiment towards the AI investment boom. If the shares enjoy a strong post-listing rally, it would signal that markets remain convinced a handful of AI leaders can eventually generate profits large enough to justify today’s vast spending on talent, infrastructure and computing power.
“Conversely, a weak reception or a sharp decline after flotation could suggest investors are becoming more selective, drawing a distinction between AI’s transformative potential and the commercial challenge of converting rapid revenue growth into sustainable earnings.
