# Two Criteria for Deciding Whether to 'Delegate, Collaborate, or Not Delegate' to Generative AI: From Consultant Practice

note · てらす企画｜新規事業ノート · 2026-11-19

Two Criteria for Deciding Whether to 'Delegate, Collaborate, or Not Delegate' to Generative AI: From Consultant Practice
When you start using generative AI for work, at first you want to ask it to do everything.
And then, at some point, you get a scare. Plausible-looking numbers might almost make it into a document without a clear source, or an explanation that seems logical might, upon closer reading, only present one side of the argument.
I worked as a mechanical design engineer at a manufacturing company for five years before becoming a consultant. Now, I use AI daily for market research, document creation, and business process improvement.
In this article, I will introduce how I categorize tasks into those I 'delegate' to AI, those I 'collaborate' on with AI, and those I 'do not delegate' to AI. By the end, you will be able to create your own decision-making table for your work.
Only two criteria for judgment
Whether or not to delegate to AI is determined by the following two factors:
- Can I notice mistakes myself?
- Who will be inconvenienced if it makes a mistake?
I delegate tasks that I can verify myself and where a mistake only results in minor rework. For tasks where I can verify the results but the output goes to a team or external parties, I work with AI and ensure I check and finalize everything myself. I do not delegate tasks that I cannot verify myself or tasks that would actually take longer if delegated to AI.
Furthermore, for any task, the prerequisite is whether the information is allowed to be entered into AI according to company policy. Tasks involving information that cannot be entered are outside the scope of this judgment from the start.
Delegate: Tasks that can be verified and only require minor rework
- Organizing meeting minutes: I have AI organize decisions and action items from meeting notes. Since I attended the meeting, I can immediately notice any mistakes.
- Excel formulas and macros (VBA): I have AI provide suggestions for fixing formulas or macros. By comparing the input and output, I can verify for myself whether they are correct. I consult AI on fixing reference ranges or output columns, but I always verify the consistency of the calculation results myself. I also check if they work correctly not just with normal inputs, but also with exceptional inputs and boundary values.
These two are the tasks that I feel have become the fastest since I started using AI.
Collaborate: Tasks where AI acts as a sounding board
Researching and organizing discussion points for documents are tasks I do in collaboration with AI.
For research, I used to do it myself, but now I delegate the actual act of searching to AI. However, I set the initial hypothesis myself. I think through 'What is the question to be solved?', 'What is the answer (hypothesis) at this point?', and 'What do I need to verify for that?' before I have it search.
This is because if you have AI generate a hypothesis from scratch, it tends to lack specificity or deviate from the actual feel of the field, ultimately resulting in something where 'it is unclear what the point is.' If you have it search without defining the question, you just get more information without getting closer to a conclusion (I will write about this method in detail in 'Market Research for New Businesses: Where to Start? How to Define Questions and Discussion Points to Solve').
For documents as well, rather than having it create the content immediately, I solidify the 'what I want the other party to decide' and 'what the discussion points are' while interacting with the AI.
However, the logic that AI creates can sometimes just 'look plausible'.
Previously, when I reviewed a comparison document with AI, the AI listed only the benefits and built a story that affirmed my own opinion. It was easy to read and persuasive. However, it was clearly too biased to comprehensively compare the options.
AI tends to return answers that align with your own thinking. I verified whether there were any gaps in the comparison criteria, whether it held up from the opposite perspective, and whether there was evidence, and then I rebuilt the structure myself.
Bouncing ideas off AI makes things faster, but you are the one who judges the validity of the logic.
Do Not Delegate: Tasks that are slower to delegate, or tasks that should not be input in the first place
What I do not delegate to AI is creating slides.
You might find this surprising. However, the slides AI creates are not yet at a quality level that I can present directly for work. Since it takes more time to fix them, I think through the structure with AI, but I create the slides themselves myself.
In addition, there are two things I 'do not delegate' that are common to any workplace.
- Do not input in the first place: Confidential information. The possibility of an accident occurring on the AI service side is not zero. I only use information permitted by company rules.
- Do not delegate final judgment: I do not use numbers or conclusions provided by AI in documents or for decision-making until I have verified the source and the timing myself.
Create a decision table for your own work
Finally, here is the procedure for categorizing your own work.
- List 10 tasks from your week (exclude tasks that involve information you cannot input into AI due to company rules).
- For each, mark 'Can I notice mistakes myself? (Yes/No)' and 'Who is inconvenienced if I make a mistake? (Myself/Team/External)'.
- If it is 'Yes' and 'Myself', delegate it; if it is 'Yes' and 'Team/External', collaborate; if it is 'No', do not delegate.
When you fill it out, it looks like the following, for example.
- Example: Organizing meeting minutes | Can I notice mistakes?: Yes / Who is inconvenienced if I make a mistake?: Myself / Judgment: Delegate
- Example: Organizing points for a proposal document | Can I notice mistakes?: Yes / Who is inconvenienced if I make a mistake?: External / Judgment: Collaborate
- Example: Numbers in a quote for a client | Can I notice mistakes?: No / Who is inconvenienced if I make a mistake?: External / Judgment: Do not delegate
