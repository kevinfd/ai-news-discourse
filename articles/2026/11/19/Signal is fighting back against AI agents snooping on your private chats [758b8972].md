# Signal is fighting back against AI agents snooping on your private chats

Digital Trends · Rachit Agarwal · 2026-11-19

Signal has just announced that it is going to restrict the ability to take screenshots in its privacy-centric messaging app on Android and iOS devices. In a blog post shared by Signal President Meredith Whittaker, the company notes that it is taking the step to prevent AI agents from reading what’s on your screen on a smartphone.
“Right now, these agentic AI integrations are changing this environment in a way that’s making it increasingly hostile to privacy. This is an existential threat to Signal, and our ability to responsibly uphold our mission,” Whittaker writes.
Signal took a similar step last year when Microsoft rolled out its controversial Recall system, which uses AI to continuously take screenshots and save them as a memory, so it can provide contextual answers when users need them. Recall proved to be one of the most controversial additions Microsoft has made to its computing OS. The company subsequently strengthened the guardrails around it, while also letting users disable it at will.
Explaining its move to enforce a similar measure on smartphones, Signal notes that smartphone operating systems are increasingly adopting AI agents such as Gemini Intelligence on Android and Apple’s Siri AI. As a result, the situation has reached a point where users face a trade-off between privacy and convenience. Signal also took a swipe at the companies behind the major operating systems, namely Google (Android), Apple (iOS and macOS), and Microsoft (Windows).
“In order to gain access to information about you, these OS AI agents ‘scan’ the information that’s on your screen. In doing so, your Signal data is captured, then made available to these agentic systems in order to allow them to answer your questions and do your bidding,” says the company.
Broadly, Signal thinks that these deep, system-level agentic AI capabilities, which are increasingly being baked into the very core of operating systems, are compromising its core promise of a secure messaging experience. That promise is exactly what sets it apart from rival platforms such as WhatsApp and Instagram DMs, among others.
On Android, Signal will automatically turn on its Screen Security feature when it detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen. On iOS 27, it will block screenshots within the app and add a new “Block Siri AI” setting. Of course, users will have a choice to keep these protections enabled or disable them, depending on how comfortable they are with sacrificing convenience in favor of more privacy, or vice versa. Despite this measure, Signal admits that blocking screenshots is not a complete solution, and that there are other ways AI agents can still access data linked to the app, such as content already stored on the device.
If you are a Signal user and want to enable this feature, follow the path below:
In Signal, tap your profile > Settings (iOS) > Privacy > Screen Security (Android) or Hide Screen in App Switcher (iOS).
On a Mac, Signal says it can’t reliably block screenshots, so it recommends adding Signal to the Excluded Apps list under System Settings > Siri.
