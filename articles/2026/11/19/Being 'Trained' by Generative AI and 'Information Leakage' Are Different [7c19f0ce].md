# Being 'Trained' by Generative AI and 'Information Leakage' Are Different

note · 経営ブログ 中小企業診断士 竹内幸次 · 2026-11-19

Being 'Trained' by Generative AI and 'Information Leakage' Are Different
I am Koji Takeuchi, a Small and Medium Enterprise Management Consultant. Today, I am consulting for a food retailer in Ome City, Tokyo, giving a lecture for small and medium enterprises at the National Federation of Wholesale Commercial Complex Cooperatives titled 'Business Efficiency for Association Secretariats: Document Creation, Meeting Support, and Planning Practice Using AI,' and consulting for a manufacturing company in Chiyoda Ward, Tokyo.
Today, in connection with my lecture, I will discuss how being 'trained' by generative AI and 'information leakage' are different.

While the use of generative AI is progressing,
I often hear people say, 'It is dangerous because the information I input will be learned by the AI.' However, learning by generative AI and information leakage are fundamentally different concepts. Correctly understanding this difference is necessary for small and medium-sized enterprises to utilize AI.
The 'learning' of generative AI refers to
using input text and the like to improve the performance of the AI model. On the other hand, 'information leakage' refers to information leaking to or becoming viewable by a third party who does not originally have access rights. Just because it has been learned does not mean that information leakage has immediately occurred.
For example, with ChatGPT,
conversations in personal plans may be used for model improvement, but this can be disabled through settings. Furthermore, in ChatGPT Business and Enterprise, input information is generally not used for model training.
However, not being learned and
the risk of information leakage being zero are also different. The possibility of information leakage remains due to unauthorized account use, incorrect sharing settings, and integration with external services.
What is important is
not to judge safety solely based on whether or not learning occurs. Small and medium-sized enterprises need to establish standards for inputting customer information and trade secrets, and confirm terms of service, retention periods, access rights, and external integration mechanisms.
Fearing generative AI and not using it
is also a management risk. Correct knowledge is what allows for both safe AI utilization and the creation of new added value.
To all small and medium enterprise managers,
do not confuse generative AI learning with information leakage, and let us actively promote AI utilization while managing risks correctly.
