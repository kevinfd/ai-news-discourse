# A PE firm scooped up an influencer marketing company in a bet on the industry's next growth area

Business Insider · Lucia Moses, Lucia Moses (lmoses@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-19

Emily Freeman and Forrest Brazeal started the creator marketing firm Freeman & Forrest. Freeman & Forrest A PE firm bought an influencer marketing company in a bet on the industry's next hot area. Falfurrias Capital acquired Freeman & Forrest, a B2B firm focused on tech companies. Freeman & Forrest will use the funding to expand to new industries, including the medical and defense sectors. Private equity is diving deeper into the creator economy. Falfurrias Capital Partners has made a majority investment in Freeman & Forrest, a firm that's helped Amazon, Google, and other tech companies use creators to reach technical customers, Business Insider exclusively learned. The deal price wasn't disclosed. Freeman & Forrest plans to use the capital to serve more Big Tech companies, as well as AI and cloud startups. It also plans to jump into other B2B categories such as the medical and defense sectors. Cofounder Emily Freeman will continue as CEO, while cofounder Forrest Brazeal will remain as president; both will be on the board. Also joining the board are ad industry vet and Falfurrias advisor Alex Jutkowitz, as executive chair, and Linda Boff, former global CMO of GE. Freeman and Brazeal founded the firm in 2024 after running influencer programs at companies like Microsoft and Google Cloud and finding that influencer marketing firms were designed for consumer brands, not B2B ones. Today, they say they work with one-third of the tech companies in the Fortune 100 across a network of more than 1,600 technical influencers. Its estimated annual revenue is about $50 million, per a person familiar with the company. Freeman said that while the 30-person firm has been doubling revenue every year, the capital infusion will help it expand faster by enabling it to hire more specialized account managers and developers to bolster its campaign-building platform. Private equity firms are playing a small but expanding role in funding creator economy companies as the sector matures, reflecting a growing recognition of the creator economy's potential. PE is directly or indirectly behind about 5% of all creator economy deals historically and about 8% of deals so far in 2026, according to estimates by Quartermast, an advisory firm. This month, H.I.G. Capital invested in Jake Rosen Entertainment, a talent firm, with plans for more deals. Earlier, Eldridge Industries invested in Fixated, a digital talent and content studio. B2B creator marketing is taking off US creator ad spend has grown four times as fast as media overall and is set to hit $44 billion this year, the Interactive Advertising Bureau estimated. What started as a medium focused on consumer brands is now accelerating in B2B. Forrester predicted that 75% of enterprise B2B companies would increase their influencer budgets in 2026 as trusted subject-matter experts play a bigger role in buying decisions. Freeman said B2B influencer marketing has nuances that set it apart from B2C. Personality is a big part of selling consumer products, and marketers often see an immediate impact on purchase decisions. B2B creator marketing puts more emphasis on expertise, and the sales cycle can be long. "Because the end user has a suspicion of anything that feels like a sales or marketing gimmick, you have to approach it as educational, making sure that the end user feels value from that content," Freeman said. The risk tolerance is also different. B2B brands tend to focus on making sure the creator follows FTC disclosure guidelines, doesn't use AI in a way that misrepresents the brand, and has the right persona. "We love influencers who are jalapeño-spicy, because it's interesting, but we don't want ghost-pepper-hot," Freeman said. "The definition of personality is a little different here." Private equity is warming to the creator economy Chris Erwin, founder of RockWater, an M&A advisor for the creator economy, said the AI boom has fueled B2B creator marketing, as firms increasingly use creator economy channels to reach decision-makers. Erwin said he's had a growing number of conversations with firms representing tech and knowledge creators who speak to enterprise buyers, small to medium-sized businesses, and more. Erwin said investors are drawn to B2B because agencies have specialized skills, marketers' budgets are large and often renewed, and credible technical creators are scarce. "The trade-off is a smaller market, and specialist agencies are often built around founders who are the brand," he said. "Buyers will pay for the expertise, but want to see the business scale beyond the founders." Freeman said B2B is just getting started in adopting creator marketing, but that she sees the opportunity to expand to any field or company, whether it's an AI startup that's trying to grow awareness, an enterprise that's rebranding, or a company trying to get its expensive technology into the hands of end-users. "We're seeing demand grow at the same time that you're seeing these influencers start to really recognize that their industry expertise is what makes them a great influencer," she said.
