# California subpoenas Nvidia as questions about CEO affair, AI chips mount

Los Angeles Times · Kevin Rector · 2026-11-19

California subpoenas Nvidia as questions about CEO affair, AI chips mount

- California Atty. Gen. Rob Bonta subpoenaed Nvidia for information on whether advanced chips or confidential data reached China and whether the company misled investors and the public.
- The inquiry comes amid upheaval at Nvidia, including CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation after an affair with an alleged Chinese spy.
California Atty. Gen. Rob Bonta on Thursday subpoenaed the AI chip manufacturer Nvidia — asking whether its advanced chips or other confidential information went to China and whether it had misled investors and the public on the issue.
Nvidia, which is based in Santa Clara, did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
The subpoena follows Nvidia co-founder and Chief Executive Jensen Huang’s resignation after it was revealed he was having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, and after Bonta’s office sued OpenAI — another AI company — over similar claims that it had misled the public about its advanced AI capabilities.
Bonta’s office said information gleaned from the subpoenas “will help the California Department of Justice evaluate whether Nvidia’s actions violated California law.”
Bonta said both Nvidia’s and Jensen’s conduct raises “bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors,” and his office intends to determine whether it did so.
“Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors,” Bonta said.
Anthropic is set to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday after pricing its IPO at $83 per share.
The shake-up at Nvidia has rattled markets. It has also raised questions about the company’s handling of its AI chips, whether it facilitated those chips being smuggled to China, and whether the U.S. Commerce Department was complicit or incompetent in failing to prevent such diversions despite its stated interest in preventing the powerful chips — crucial for supporting AI models — from getting into Chinese hands.
Democrats on the House Judiciary and Foreign Affairs committees in particular are investigating whether the administration’s response to Nvidia’s chip business was improperly influenced by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick — whose family has holdings in the company.
“Did Trump’s Commerce Secretary compromise our national security so that his family firm could profit from hundreds of millions of dollars invested in Nvidia?” Democrats on the House Judiciary Committee asked on social media Thursday.
The Democrats’ questions hold extra weight since their party won control of both chambers of Congress in this month’s midterm elections, which will give them greater investigative and subpoena powers come January.
In a Commerce statement shared by Lutnick on Monday, the department acknowledged an ongoing, years-long investigation into the Singaporean company Megaspeed and allegations it may have skirted export rules governing Nvidia’s powerful AI chips. It credited that investigation to its own “excellent work” under Lutnick.
The department noted that the investigation involved an interview of former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman with whom Nvidia’s Huang acknowledged having an affair and who U.S. intelligence officials suspect may be a Chinese spy.
The Commerce statement said it and the Justice Department “have an extremely strong track record of bringing semiconductor smugglers to justice” and “will bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred.”
Megaspeed has said that Alice Huang left the company in 2025 and that it is conducting its own internal investigation.
The explanations have reassured few, and criticism has not been limited to Democrats. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent also blamed the Commerce Department for failing to blacklist Megaspeed over possible export violations.
Nvidia’s stance came amid growing calls to hire back Huang, who himself said this week that he would be willing to return as CEO if the board invited him back.
Harvey C. Jones, Nvidia’s chairman, has said the company’s board unanimously accepted Jensen Huang’s resignation and does not intend to reinstate him — despite calls for exactly that from President Trump and other administration allies.
“While this was an unexpected transition, the board acted deliberately and in the best interest of the company’s employees, customers, partners, and shareholders,” Jones said. Colette Kress, Nvidia’s executive vice president and chief financial officer, is now serving as interim CEO.
On Tuesday, the company announced third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year prior. The company’s reporting has raised additional questions, as it stopped reporting Singapore revenue separately just as scrutiny of Megaspeed was arising.
In a post on X on Tuesday, Jensen Huang said he “did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA,” had “made serious mistakes” in his personal life and “created problems for” the company’s board.
But he also said he had received many messages from supporters encouraging him to return as CEO, which he would consider if the board wanted him to help the company as it “navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics.”
