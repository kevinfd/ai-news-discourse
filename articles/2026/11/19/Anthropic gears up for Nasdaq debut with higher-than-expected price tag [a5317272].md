# Anthropic gears up for Nasdaq debut with higher-than-expected price tag

Fox News · James Cirrone, Stephen Sorace · 2026-11-19

Anthropic gears up for Nasdaq debut with higher-than-expected price tag
Brushing aside fears about an AI bubble, Anthropic is launching the largest IPO in American history, raising roughly $81.3 billion by pricing nearly 1 billion shares at $83 apiece.
Anthropic, the maker of Claude, is set to make its public market debut Thursday following the 9:30 a.m. ET Nasdaq opening bell, capping months of speculation over when the frontier AI company would go public — or whether it would at all.
The broader tech selloff last week — fueled in part by the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang — had some on Wall Street thinking Anthropic might delay its IPO until markets stabilized.
And many critics believed Anthropic would decide against going public altogether, pointing to an amended IPO filing that showed a $1.3 billion operating loss through the first nine months of 2026 — including $567 million in the third quarter.
Yet Anthropic is pressing ahead, wagering that its rapid 2026 sales surge will continue for years to come — a bet that has to pay off if the company hopes to cover hundreds of billions of dollars in cloud computing bills and data center commitments.
Anthropic's initial public offering will bring 980 million shares of Class A common stock to the market at an IPO price of $83.
Anthropic locked in its $81.3 billion haul Wednesday night. Before an IPO begins trading publicly, Wall Street underwriters sell the initial block of shares directly to large institutional funds and select brokerage clients at the fixed offering price. Those early buyers will begin trading the stock with one another and every day investors on Thursday.
Retail investors will be able to buy shares under the ticker symbol "ANT" once live trading begins, which could be shortly after the opening bell or several hours later.
OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said Wednesday that the ChatGPT maker plans to go public at some point.
He made the remarks at The New York Times DealBook Summit, as rival AI company Anthropic prepares to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday.
"We plan to be a public company at some point," Altman said , adding that there are "many reasons why that's the right thing to do."
Altman cautioned that navigating the AI landscape is increasingly complex, pointing to recent months as a particularly challenging period for balancing safety requirements with the need to remain commercially viable and competitive.
