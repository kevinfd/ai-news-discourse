# AWS's Matt Wood has an AI agent help plan his outfit every day

Business Insider · Ana Altchek, Ana Altchek (aaltchek@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-19

Matt Wood said AI helps him choose his outfits. Ana Altchek Matt Wood, chief AI and tech officer at AWS, has agents working for him overnight. The agents scan his calendar and decide his dress code based on the meetings he has planned. Wood said he also has a "large crew" of agents help him build his ideas throughout the day. Matt Wood has a lot on his plate. Luckily, his outfit planning takes a lot less time these days. The chief AI and technology officer at AWS said that he has AI systems working on his behalf before he even wakes up, and one of his AI use cases has become "somewhat famous internally": letting an agent choose his attire every single day. The C-suite executive, who leads technology for the division of  Amazon  that provides cloud and AI services to millions of organizations, said AWS Quick, an agent used for hardcore work such as monitoring regulations and spotting supply chain inefficiencies, is connected to his calendar. Wood's unconventional usage has garnered attention from Amazonians, he said. Every morning around 4 a.m, the agent checks his schedule and identifies the most high-profile meeting that day. Based on that, it tells him the day's dress code. "I never want to be overdressed," Wood told Business Insider at Amazon's headquarters in Seattle. "I never want to be uncomfortable, and I definitely don't want to make a decision on what I'm wearing before I've got my coffee."

 The AI and technology chief added that he typically has about six cups of coffee by noon, and considers himself "useless" before the first three. Wood said the agent turns on a smart light bulb in his closet that corresponds with a category color, based on his most visible meeting that day, such as a keynote, an interview, or "hanging with the team." For example, it might flash red for a keynote day or blue for a builder day, Wood explained in a LinkedIn post. He added that the smart bulb then lights up in his closet, where all his clothes are arranged by color-coded tags. Wood then finds the clothes tagged with the matching color and gets dressed. For example, if he has a keynote, the bulb lights up red and Wood grabs one of the items with red tags in his closet, which could be a dark suit. "No thought necessary," Wood said. Wood uses AI in a handful of other ways throughout the day. By the time he has his first cup of coffee, he said he's scanning a report from his AI researcher, which compiles an overview of the news that broke overnight. It collects information from multiple sources, including X and Amazon communications. He also uses AI to help him prepare for meetings throughout the day and said he has a "large crew" of agents that help him build out whatever ideas he comes up with throughout the day. "Just this week, I've processed almost a billion tokens in AI usage, building things that I'm interested in or to help me with some of these other things," Wood said.
