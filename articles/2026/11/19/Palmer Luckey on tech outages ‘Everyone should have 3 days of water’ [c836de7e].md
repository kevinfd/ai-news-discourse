# Palmer Luckey on tech outages: ‘Everyone should have 3 days of water’

Business Insider · Truman Dickerson, Truman Dickerson (tdickerson@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-19

Palmer Luckey has some advice for people during widespread tech outages. Spencer Platt/Getty Images Palmer Luckey said if he were you, he'd batten down the hatches. He said so in response to questions about a series of recent tech outages that have roiled the US. Luckey said he thinks that updates to old technology are behind the breakdowns. Palmer Luckey has some advice during a global surge of tech outages: batten down the hatches. Speaking at The New York Times DealBook summit on Wednesday, the billionaire Anduril cofounder said your level of worry about systemic outages should depend on "how well prepared you are." "Everyone should have three days of water," he said. "Everyone should have three days of dry food." Luckey is no stranger to doomsday prepping. The tech bro behind some of the world's most advanced autonomous weapon systems is infamous for, among other things, owning missile bases where he stashes his video game collection . Luckey gave his advice in response to questions about a recent worldwide surge in outages affecting platforms ranging from Microsoft Outlook to FAA communication channels to classified NSA networks. US officials have repeatedly said the system interruptions aren't related and aren't the result of cyberattacks — assurances that haven't stopped widespread speculation from the general public. Sen. Mark Warner, the top Democrat on the Senate Intelligence committee, suggested on Tuesday that the outages were due to "legitimate efforts to secure our systems, not malicious activity." Vice President JD Vance, initially set to appear at DealBook for an interview with host Andrew Ross Sorkin, canceled his appearance in order to attend meetings at the White House about the outages, Sorkin said. At DealBook, Luckey speculated that the failures could be attributed to a rushed update process against bad actors using AI. "We're seeing the natural effects of a lot of these advanced AI tools allowing for the creation of various exploits," he said, adding that "old, outdated systems" are being updated to include countermeasures against AI hacking. In the meantime, he said, people ought to begin stockpiling the essentials lest the bad actors gain the upper hand. And for preppers, three days is a key number. "The United States is very resilient if you think on a timeline of what we can do to get everything running again in the course of three days," he said. Read the original article on Business Insider
