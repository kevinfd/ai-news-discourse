# Tesla's Cybercab gets more expensive than a Model 3 after just 4 years of driving

Business Insider · Ben Shimkus, Ben Shimkus (bshimkus@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-19

The Cybercab's $299-a-month subscription quickly makes it more expensive than its Tesla siblings. Tesla Tesla wants to deliver the first consumer Cybercabs in December. (RIP, MKBHD's hair?) The $29,990 car requires a $299-a-month subscription to drive. That subscription makes the Cybercab a confusing pricing fit in Tesla's lineup. Tesla's Cybercab is cheap. That is, until you ask it to move . The steering-wheel-free robotaxi starts at $29,990, making it Tesla's first vehicle with a headline price below $30,000 — but it won't drive without a $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription. At Tesla's current listed prices, that required fee makes the Cybercab more expensive than a base Model 3 with a $99-a-month FSD (Supervised) subscription in its 45th month of ownership. By its fifth year, the Cybercab would cost $47,631 — $3,160 more than the Model 3 and $160 more than a larger Model Y with Tesla's monthly FSD subscription. CEO Elon Musk agrees that the Cybercab has a pricing problem. On X, he responded to tech reviewer Marques Brownlee, known as MKBHD, writing that the Cybercab's $299 monthly fee was "too high" and should be closer to $115 a month. @MKBHD It will be available nationwide, as we receive regulatory approval. 

But the monthly fee is too high. Should match the cost of Tesla FSD and connectivity for other models, which is more like ~$115/month. — Elon Musk (@elonmusk) November 14, 2026 
 For years, Musk has promised a lower-cost Tesla that could open the door for more Americans to drive electric. Reuters reported in 2024 that Tesla scrapped a planned $25,000, human-driven vehicle. Instead, the company focused on autonomy. In the meantime, sub-$30,000 EV options remain scarce: Drivers can buy the $27,600 Chevy Bolt, the $29,990 Nissan Leaf, or wait for the coming $24,950 Slate truck . The average new EV transaction price was $54,813 in August, per Cox Automotive data. That's still 9.4% more than comparable gas vehicles. Seth Goldstein, an equity analyst at Morningstar, said the Cybercab's higher monthly fee makes sense if Tesla is selling drivers access to a more advanced autonomy system than FSD (Supervised). "The higher FSD price also makes sense as it will likely be the latest version, ahead of the consumer one, while also running the robotaxi operations," he told Business Insider. The fee also covers more than the self-driving software. Tesla says it includes roadside assistance and certain maintenance, such as tire replacements, windshield replacements, inspections, and wiper replacements. Ultimately, Tesla believes that owners will be able to send the car out for ride-hailing when it's not in personal use. If that bet pays off, the Cybercab could earn owners some money. Still, at $47,631 after five years of ownership, the Cybercab expects drivers to fork over a lot of money for a two-seat vehicle that's still geofenced to small areas in Texas. Plus, the car has been facing an NHTSA audit inquiry since September that could threaten its roadworthiness. Tesla invited select customers to purchase the vehicle for personal use, with deliveries expected in December. The EV maker included MKBHD in that list. That could matter a lot to the YouTuber. He bet that the company wouldn't deliver a self-driving consumer vehicle to US customers by the end of 2026 for under $30,000 — or he would shave his head. Will a strictly geofenced car count?
