# Who is Yash Patil? The 23-year-old Stanford dropout who sold his AI startup to Palo Alto Networks for $4.5 billion

Moneycontrol · Moneycontrol News · 2026-11-19

15
As part of the acquisition, Yash Patil will join Palo Alto Networks as co-chief AI officer, alongside the rest of the Applied Compute team.
  November 19, 2026 / 16:45 IST  
The 23-year-old Indian-American entrepreneur Yash Patil has had quite a month.
In early November, his one-year-old AI startup Applied Compute raised $350 million at a $3.25 billion valuation after hitting a $100 million annualised revenue run rate, with customers including Nvidia, DoorDash and Microsoft.
Just a fortnight later, cybersecurity giant Palo Alto Networks is acquiring the startup for $4.5 billion.
As part of the deal, Patil will join the $250-billion company as co-chief AI officer, alongside the rest of the Applied Compute team.
From Austin to OpenAI
Born to Indian immigrant parents, Patil grew up in Austin where he attended the Liberal Arts and Science Academy before studying computer science at Stanford University. His early technical work included modeling the Texas power grid at the University of Texas at Austin’s Webber Energy Group, simulating power generation and transmission.
Patil's journey into AI began with a chance encounter with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman at a dinner he hosted for Stanford engineering students. Following ChatGPT's launch in 2022, Patil emailed Altman expressing his interest in working on the chatbot. Altman encouraged him to apply to OpenAI's residency programme.
In 2023, at just 20, he took a leave of absence from Stanford University, where he was studying computer science, to join the ChatGPT maker as a member of its technical staff. At OpenAI, he worked on post-training infrastructure, reasoning models and the technology behind Codex, the company's AI coding assistant.
Patil met fellow Stanford alumni Rhythm Garg and Linden Li, with whom he would later co-found Applied Compute, while working at OpenAI.
What does Applied Compute do?
The idea for Applied Compute began taking shape in January 2025, when Patil and his future co-founders were on a skiing trip to Lake Tahoe. Chinese startup DeepSeek’s R1 model was able to demonstrate that powerful open-weight AI models could be developed without the resources of the biggest American AI labs.
The three researchers began exploring the opportunity to build infrastructure that would allow enterprises to customise and improve such models using their own data.
They left OpenAI in May 2025 and started working on Applied Compute in stealth mode. Patil became CEO, while Garg became CTO, and Li chief architect.
The initial idea of Applied Compute’s core offering was to build the infrastructure that allows businesses to turn existing AI models into specialised, enterprise-ready systems using their own proprietary data.
The billion-dollar sprint
Applied Compute quickly caught the attention of some of Silicon Valley's biggest venture capital firms.
In June 2025, Benchmark led a $20 million funding round that reportedly valued the startup at around $100 million. Just four months later, its valuation had soared to $500 million after another $60 million investment backed by Sequoia Capital, Lux Capital and Benchmark.
The San Francisco-headquartered startup emerged from stealth in October 2025, with Patil revealing that the company had reached $12.8 million in annualised revenue within roughly six months of incorporation.
By April 2026, the startup had become a unicorn, securing $80 million in fresh funding led by Kleiner Perkins at a $1.3 billion valuation.
Among its customer projects, Applied Compute has built an AI model for DoorDash that helps restaurants accurately generate menus when joining the food delivery platform. It is also working with Nvidia on post-training its open-weight Nemotron models to build complex AI agents.
Why Palo Alto Networks bought Applied Compute?
While announcing the deal on November 18, Palo Alto Networks said the acquisition would bring Applied Compute's AI platform and research team into its fold, as the cybersecurity giant looks to help enterprises securely adopt AI across both open-weight and proprietary frontier models.
Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora said Applied Compute had developed a strong post-training and inference platform, backed by an exceptional AI research team. The company plans to help enterprises securely train, customise and deploy open models across public cloud and sovereign environments.
“We saw that firsthand as a prospective customer, and our Unit 42 Frontier AI Assessment confirmed their strong security foundations,” Arora said.
