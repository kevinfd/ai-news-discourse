# Muse privacy risks have even Meta employees spooked

Digital Trends · Rachit Agarwal · 2026-11-19

If recent news about Meta’s Muse AI privacy risks has you spooked, you are not alone. The engineers testing it inside Meta ended up just as rattled. Muse, the agent with the smiling mascot called Jolly, has crossed 10 million downloads since launching in September, and Meta is betting billions in future AI revenue on it.
But the mascot’s cuteness cannot paint over the privacy risks it presents. A Reuters investigation dug through more than two dozen internal posts, and the pattern is strange. Staff described unprompted updates about a child’s care arrangements, purchases nobody made, and money sitting in accounts that were not theirs.
How is it getting into encrypted chats?
One employee opened Muse on her personal phone and found it reminding her to answer her sister on Signal, an encrypted app she had never connected to Muse. “This seems like an extreme privacy violation. I definitely did not grant it access to other apps,” she wrote internally.
Reuters passed the details to Signal and the AI Now Institute, and together they got Muse to do it again. Android was the easy route, since the agent can read your push notifications. The iPhone, interestingly, did not allow Muse to run rampant, so at least that’s something.
On a Mac, it went through screenshot and accessibility settings, the tools Apple built so people with disabilities can operate their machines. Apple has since locked down what Mac developers can do with those settings.
Whose conversation is this, anyway?
Other staff got answers to questions they had never asked. One was randomly offered help to “message the seller” about a coffee table, and when he pushed back, Muse confessed the context did not “seem to be yours.” A Meta Superintelligence team member admitted the hallucinations “bias towards the most uncomfortable topics, like finances/security/safety.”
Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts called these “isolated incidents” and said the agent only reaches other apps with permission. Signal president Meredith Whittaker was blunter, calling agents with that much access to people’s devices “technologically reckless.”
If Muse is on your phone, go audit its notification and accessibility access yourself. On Android, employees found the agent had notification access switched on from the start, and Muse could read the first lines of your messages through the notifications.
Meta’s terms already make you responsible for whatever your agent does next, so make sure to protect yourself by disallowing the permissions or completely uninstalling the app until these issues are resolved.
