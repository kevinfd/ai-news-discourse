# Data center electricity use could double by 2030, IEA says

CNBC · Natalie Feary · 2026-11-19

Global electricity use by data centers could double by 2030 and stretch power grids, potentially delaying new projects, the International Energy Agency warned.
Data centers could consume around 1,000 terawatt-hours of electricity a year by 2030, according to the IEA, which added that tight supply chains, local opposition and limited grid capacity could slow construction.
The warning is part of the IEA's Stated Policies Scenario, in which the agency forecasts are based on measures that have been put forward but have not yet been adopted.
Texas, Virginia, Finland and the U.K.
The IEA cites large data center project pipelines in Texas, Virginia, Finland and the U.K. In several regions, the combined capacity of data centers waiting to connect exceeds current peak demand.
Without grid upgrades, power networks could become more congested and less reliable, the IEA said.
New projects could face longer waits to connect, while generators could be forced to cut output when the grid cannot meet demand.
Peak electricity demand is projected to rise 40% to 50% by 2035, according to the IEA. Under the Stated Policies Scenario, grids should be expanded by 26 million kilometers of new lines by 2035, while about 16 million kilometers of existing lines need to be replaced or refurbished. Supplies of copper, cables and transformers are already tight, the IEA said.
Grid spending could rise 75%
Under the Current Policies Scenario, which is based on energy policies and regulations already in place, grid spending is projected to rise 75% over the next decade. The agency says power networks are "one of the biggest energy investment stories of the next decade."
The scenario projects that renewable power generation will grow 85% more in the next decade than in the previous one. Solar photovoltaics would become the world's largest source of electricity from the late 2030s
Electricity would account for nearly 30% of global final energy consumption by 2035, up from 23% in the IEA's main scenarios.
Energy security adds urgency
The IEA said the 2026 energy crisis could accelerate electrification as countries seek to reduce their reliance on imported oil and gas. Electrification can reduce exposure to imported fuels, but it also makes a reliable power grid more important.
Before the Iran war, an average of 20 million barrels of crude oil and oil products a day transited the Strait of Hormuz in 2025 — about 25% of global seaborne oil trade. The strait also carried nearly 20% of global LNG trade.
"The pressures of the current crisis must not obscure the choices that will shape energy security, sustainability and prosperity for the decades to come," IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol said.
