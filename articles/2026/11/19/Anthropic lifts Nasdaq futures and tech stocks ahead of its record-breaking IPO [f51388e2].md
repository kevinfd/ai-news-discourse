# Anthropic lifts Nasdaq futures and tech stocks ahead of its record-breaking IPO

CNBC · Leonie Kidd · 2026-11-19

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.
Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.
A historic IPO for markets and an exclusive for CNBC.
Andrew Ross Sorkin will speak to Anthropic co-Founders Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei at 10 a.m. ET, as the biggest public listing in history starts trading on the Nasdaq.
Happening now: Anthropic IPO day
Anthropic has priced its IPO above the expected range, at $83 per share. With around 980 million shares sold, it has raised $81.3 billion, surpassing SpaceX's record IPO back in June.
Anthropic's fully diluted market cap sits at around $1.5 trillion. The stock is expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq today under the ticker symbol "ANT." CNBC's Leslie Picker will report from the Nasdaq throughout the trading day.
- CNBC EXCLUSIVE: Andrew Ross Sorkin will speak exclusively to the co-founders, Dario and Daniela Amodei - don't miss that interview at 10 a.m. ET.
Nasdaq futures are higher in anticipation, as traders gear up for this historic moment. Shares in chipmakers and companies that are invested in Anthropic are also higher premarket.
- IPO and beyond: While many investors will want to buy during the IPO, retail investors will get access if they already hold certain mutual funds or ETFs.
- Patience required: It may take several days or weeks for certain index funds to include Anthropic, with the Nasdaq-100 and Russell 3000 set to take longer than for SpaceX.
- Super-charged wealth: CNBC's Robert Frank is taking a closer look at how the Anthropic IPO will create more than 400 new centimillionaires and thousands of millionaires in what could be the most rapid wealth-creation moment ever.
- In case you missed it: "Big Short" investor Steve Eisman says the "Anthropic IPO is not important anymore." Watch that interview here.
Big Movers: Tech trades
Shares in Nvidia are gaining in premarket trading, alongside other tech heavyweights Amazon, Alphabet and Broadcom.
- Headwinds: But Jim Cramer thinks Nvidia has a new problem - Anthropic's IPO. The highly anticipated offering could present another headwind for the group, by giving investors an attractive alternative to get exposure to the AI trade.
Palo Alto shares are jumping over 9% in premarket trading, after the group raised its annual revenue forecast. The move is also pulling cybersecurity shares higher too.
- CEO view: Nikesh Arora told "Mad Money" that companies need to train AI and security at the same time.
Trilateral: Trump, Xi and Putin meet
An unprecedented trilateral summit has taken place between U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.
In Shenzhen, China, each leader spoke about the importance of their "special responsibility" to ensure global peace. Here are some key quotes:
- President Trump: "No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities."
- President Xi: "World will be in chaos if major countries fight."
- President Putin: "Our three states have joint responsibility in maintaining peace and security."
GTA6: The wait is over
It's the most anticipated video game in a generation.
After 13 long years, gamers can finally play "Grand Theft Auto VI" as its global rollout gets underway.
It's not just players rejoicing; investors are also applauding the blockbuster release. Shares in the studio behind the game, Take-Two Interactive, traded higher on the revenue boost.
- Record launch: For more on how GTA6 could become the gaming industry's biggest ever launch, watch here.
- Industry impact: CNBC's Julia Boorstin reports on GTA6's wider impact on the industry, as game developers have moved their launches out of November to avoid conflicts, and games like EA's Battlefield and MSFT/Activision's Call of Duty, are expected to see the biggest dips in engagement.
And Finally...
Kash Patel skips House hearing after Mar-a-Lago gala with $50,000 access
After a string of outages disrupted airlines, banks and even the National Security Agency, U.S. House members gathered Wednesday to question the nation's top security officials. But FBI Director Kash Patel was nowhere to be seen.
The night before, Patel and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin had been advertised as "honored guests" at the Big Blue Bash, a lavish charitable fundraiser at President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago club, where attendees were offered access to a "private roundtable" and a stay at the venue for $50,000 a person.
