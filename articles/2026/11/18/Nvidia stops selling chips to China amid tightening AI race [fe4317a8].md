# Nvidia stops selling chips to China amid tightening AI race

Fox News · Stephen Sorace · 2026-11-18

Nvidia stops selling chips to China amid tightening AI race
Nvidia has halted shipments of powerful AI chips to China despite U.S. export approvals, raising new questions about the global artificial intelligence race.
Nvidia halts AI chip shipments to China despite Trump-backed export deal
Nvidia has stopped shipping powerful artificial intelligence chips to China, casting uncertainty over a Trump-backed export agreement as Washington and Beijing compete for AI dominance.
The U.S. chipmaker sold $2.4 billion worth of licensed Hopper-generation data center products to China last quarter but has no plans for additional shipments, interim CEO Colette Kress said during an earnings call Tuesday.
“We are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China,” Kress said, without explaining the reason for the halt or whether it would be permanent.
President Donald Trump eased restrictions on Nvidia’s H200 AI chips following a summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping last year, allowing exports to approved Chinese customers under strict licensing conditions.
But Beijing has also restricted imports of the advanced processors as it seeks to reduce reliance on American technology. Roughly 600,000 H200 chips were being held in Hong Kong earlier this month awaiting Chinese approval, Reuters previously reported.
Reuters contributed to this report.
AI model uncovers critical Java security flaw affecting widely used software
A cybersecurity startup used an advanced artificial intelligence model to uncover a critical security flaw in Java, a widely used software platform that supports systems at roughly 90% of Fortune 500 companies, according to The New York Times.
The startup, Calif, discovered the vulnerability using OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber model, revealing a weakness that could allow hackers to gain unauthorized access to sensitive computer systems.
The flaw, dubbed “Java4Shell,” affects technology used by banks, hospitals, government agencies and major corporations. Researchers demonstrated that it could be exploited to break into Keycloak, a widely used authentication system that protects organizational logins.
The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) urged organizations to “immediately patch” the vulnerability, while Oracle released a software fix Tuesday, the Times reported.
Calif CEO Thai Duong warned that unpatched systems could remain vulnerable for years, underscoring growing concerns about AI’s ability to uncover serious cybersecurity weaknesses faster than organizations can address them.
Sen. Mark Warner warns AI may be fueling widespread tech outages across US
Sen. Mark Warner, D-Va., warned Tuesday that artificial intelligence may be contributing to a wave of major technology outages across the country, while emphasizing there is no evidence of a coordinated cyberattack.
Warner, the top Democrat on the Senate Intelligence Committee, said AI is allowing cybersecurity teams to identify vulnerabilities at unprecedented speeds, prompting companies and government agencies to rush security updates that can strain critical systems.
“What appears to be happening instead is a consequence of how quickly artificial intelligence is changing the cybersecurity landscape,” Warner said in a statement.
The warning follows widespread disruptions affecting Microsoft, major banks, airlines and the National Security Agency in recent days.
Warner called the outages a “wake-up call” for stronger AI safeguards and cybersecurity protections, criticizing President Donald Trump’s administration over cuts to the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA).
Secretary of State Marco Rubio separately said Tuesday there was “zero evidence” linking the outages to hackers, foreign governments or other malicious actors.
