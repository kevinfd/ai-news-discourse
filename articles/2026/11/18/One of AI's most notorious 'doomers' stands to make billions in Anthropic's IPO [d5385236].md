# One of AI's most notorious 'doomers' stands to make billions in Anthropic's IPO

CNBC · Kate Rooney · 2026-11-18

An Estonian tech billionaire famous for his stance that AI could end humanity is expected to be one of the biggest winners in Anthropic's upcoming IPO.
Jaan Tallinn co-founded Skype and later sold it to eBay. He led Anthropic's first funding round, a $124 million Series A, five years ago — well before the company's Claude chatbot was on the market and took Wall Street and Silicon Valley by storm.
Anthropic is set to list on the Nasdaq Thursday under the ticker symbol ANT in what could be the largest IPO of all time, and the first public debut for an AI lab.
Tallinn's exact holdings haven't been disclosed. But given Anthropic's expected debut valuation of roughly $1.4 trillion, the return on his initial investment could be well into the tens of billions. His exact return would depend on the level of dilution in subsequent funding rounds and whether he sold any shares along the way.
Anthropic's earliest backers connected with the company through philanthropic circles worried about AI's existential risk. They're sometimes described as "doomers."
In 2020, Tallinn told CNBC that there's a 1-in-6 chance that humans won't survive this century, referencing a book from Oxford professor Toby Ord. Tallinn also described a runaway AI scenario where a model is left working overnight and "the world is no longer there in the morning."
While he continued to work in tech, Tallinn co-founded the Cambridge Centre for the Study of Existential Risk and the Future of Life Institute, known for one of the first public calls to pause AI. Despite high-profile signatories, including Elon Musk and Apple co-founder Steve Wozniak, the institute's "Pause Giant AI Experiments" letter in 2023 had little effect on the industry.
Tallinn's view on AI was influenced by researcher Eliezer Yudkowsky, who co-authored the 2025 book "If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies," about the catastrophic risk of AI without proper checks, according to a person familiar with the matter.
Tallinn, who has an estimated net worth of more than $10 billion, later crossed paths with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, who had defected from OpenAI with his eventual co-founders, citing views that Sam Altman's company wasn't taking AI safety seriously enough.
Amodei and Tallinn overlapped in the effective altruism community, according to people who know both of them. Effective altruism, sometimes known as EA, is a philosophy that aims to use data and evidence to maximize philanthropic impact. While there's no official membership, its Silicon Valley network ended up generating much of Anthropic's early venture capital money, according to multiple people.
Before AI was all the rage
Tallinn's early bet was that bankrolling a safety-conscious AI company was one of the best ways to keep a lid on the risk, these people said. Tallinn also invested in DeepMind, which was later acquired by Google.
One person, who asked not to be named, said that at the time of Tallinn's investment, there was no expectation that Anthropic "would be a profitable undertaking." Venture investors hadn't yet gone all-in on AI, and it was "before the era of putting AI in your pitch deck," said the person, who asked not to be named in order to speak candidly on the matter.
Tallinn declined to be interviewed for this story.
Anthropic's first round in 2021 also included Facebook co-founder Dustin Moskovitz, who founded Open Philanthropy — now known as Coefficient Giving — which is deeply rooted in effective altruism. The charity was co-founded by Holden Karnofsky, husband of Anthropic co-founder Daniela Amodei. Karnofsky also started GiveWell, also largely tied to effective altruism.
Former FTX CEO Sam Bankman-Fried, another known effective altruist, backed Anthropic in its early days alongside Tallinn. Bankman-Fried participated in the Series B in 2022 through his now-defunct hedge fund Alameda Research. The former CEO was convicted of fraud in 2023 and sentenced to 25 years in prison. His stake was sold in bankruptcy auction at a fraction of the company's current valuation.
Tallinn's early investment initially put Anthropic's price tag at $461 million, according to Pitchbook. It has ballooned in value since, and last raised money at a $965 billion valuation in private markets. The lab has raised money from tech giants Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Nvidia, and plans to sell $26 billion worth of shares to a group of tech companies at the IPO price.
Anthropic's early investors and some in the safety community aren't overwhelmingly cheering the IPO. Many worry behind the scenes that AI safety will take a back seat to Anthropic's commercial success and wider shareholder base, according to a person close to the group.
Many in the AI safety community "are opposed to the IPO," this person said.
Others point to Anthropic's unique corporate structure that gives the founders broad discretion and control of the company through a single super-voting share. The co-founders will be sole members of "Founder LLC," which will hold 50.1% of the voting power. But Anthropic is also built as a public benefit corporation, meaning it has a dual mandate beyond just a duty to shareholders. It has to serve the best interests of its "company purpose" to facilitate the "responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity."
