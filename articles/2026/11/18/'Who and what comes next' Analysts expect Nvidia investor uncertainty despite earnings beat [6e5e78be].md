# 'Who and what comes next': Analysts expect Nvidia investor uncertainty despite earnings beat

CNBC · Shikhar Gupta · 2026-11-18

Analysts expect uncertainty around Nvidia's future to linger despite another earnings beat, noting that much of the third quarter was already locked in before the turmoil that peaked in Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO.
Nvidia's third-quarter revenue came in at $115.84 billion, more than $6 billion above analysts' expectations of $109.10 billion, while fourth-quarter guidance of $130 billion topped the $125.1 billion consensus, according to LSEG.
Part of that increase reflects an extra week in the fiscal fourth quarter, which Melius Research estimated accounts for $9 billion to $10 billion of the sequential jump.
The company also lifted its fiscal 2028 growth forecast to 75% from 70%, even with China excluded from its data center compute revenue outlook.
"That's a team going full steam ahead, rather than just steadying the ship, and a clear signal that management believes next year can run just fine without Jensen Huang at the helm," said Josh Gilbert, eToro's lead analyst for Asia Pacific and the Middle East.
Still, even with the results that he called "nothing short of astonishing", Gilbert said "investors will still be left with the uncertainty of who and what comes next."
In his first statement since he resigned last week, Huang said before the results release that he was open to returning to the company. However, chair of Nvidia's board Harvey Jones said on the earnings call that the board does not intend to reinstate him as CEO.
The results should help ease some concerns created by Nvidia's recent leadership turmoil, said Morningstar senior equity analyst Brian Colello, who described the figures as "solid" and broadly in line with the firm's expectations.
Colello expressed continued confidence that "the AI infrastructure buildout is real," pointing to Nvidia's shipments holding steady in the third quarter.
Michael Wilson, chief investment officer and chief U.S. equity strategist at Morgan Stanley, told CNBC TV that the results were "somewhat expected," with no major drop-off in Nvidia's business anticipated in the near term and guidance remaining "pretty constructive."
Wilson said AI remained a "desirable investment," with no slowdown in spending seen or expected from hyperscalers and some neoclouds — newer cloud providers built around AI computing. However, he added that investment may not continue at the pace investors expected six months ago.
Nvidia is widely seen as the bellwether for the broader AI industry and spending.
Gilbert shared that view, saying the AI trade can now "let out a small sigh of relief" after holding its breath for weeks. Nvidia had been "firing on all cylinders" before Huang's departure, he said, and its underlying fundamentals remained intact.
He pointed to Nvidia's supply commitments, which rose to $390 billion from $279 billion in three months, as well as its Vera Rubin platform, which has already brought in $20 billion.
"It's the clearest sign yet that the AI boom still has plenty of fuel in the tank," Gilbert said.
Nvidia shares rose more than 5% in after-hours trading, though Gilbert said any rally is expected to meet resistance and "plenty of volatility" in the coming weeks, with the CEO search only just getting under way and investigations still playing out.
New U.S. export restrictions announced this week add another wrinkle. The Commerce Department will require licenses for shipments of advanced chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil, citing the risk that they could be diverted to China.
Gilbert said the direct impact on Nvidia's forecasts appears limited because its guidance already assumed no China data center revenue, while China accounted for only around 2% of sales this quarter. However, new licensing requirements for shipments to places such as Singapore and Malaysia could slow some neocloud build-outs, he said.
Melius Research analyst Ben Reitzes said fiscal 2028 is when sales to countries covered by the new restrictions would be expected to decline. He interpreted Nvidia's forecast as a sign that hyperscalers will take whatever supply becomes available elsewhere.
Nvidia said it is still studying the new Commerce Department rules but remains confident in its 75% growth target.
Colello pointed to longer-term risks around policy, international sales and hyperscalers developing their own chips, though he said Nvidia should remain an important player as the market expands.
He said investors will now be looking for the company to reaffirm its long-term outlook and show it can continue executing through the leadership transition.
"We're at a point of peak uncertainty because investors still don't know what Nvidia's long-term leadership will look like," Colello added.
"Jensen Huang's departure could create some near-term disruption, but a credible succession plan should go a long way toward steadying the ship. The next CEO will have big shoes to fill, but Nvidia's long-term opportunity does not hinge on one person."
