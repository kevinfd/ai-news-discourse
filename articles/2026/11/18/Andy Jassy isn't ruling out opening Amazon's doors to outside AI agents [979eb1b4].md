# Andy Jassy isn't ruling out opening Amazon's doors to outside AI agents

Business Insider · Ben Shimkus, Ben Shimkus (bshimkus@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-18

Amazon is open to outside shopping agents if there is a "fair exchange of value," CEO Andy Jassy said. Jae C. Hong/AP Amazon doesn't allow Meta's Muse to make purchases on its site. That doesn't necessarily need to be a permanent, CEO Andy Jassy said. AI agents have posed thorny new questions for IT and security departments in American retailers. Amazon shut the door on outside AI shopping agents making purchases — but CEO Andy Jassy said the company hasn't thrown away the key. "Well, I don't think that we've said we're not going to open it up," he said Tuesday at Semafor's Silicon Valley & The World conference. "It's just that it has to be a good customer experience, which it hasn't been to date, and it has to be a fair exchange of value." AI agents — like Meta's Muse, Instinct AI, and OpenAI's Dots — have created a new test for the IT and security departments inside American retailers. The agents are advertised as being capable of taking action on a customer's behalf, including making online purchases. That could cut retailers out of the browsing experience they have long controlled. It also opens up thorny questions about ad sales, account security, identity verification, and who is on the hook when an order goes wrong. In September, Amazon publicly booted Muse from making purchases on its platform, saying that the block "helps ensure a safe, secure, and reliable customer experience." Amazon has said it applies its policies evenly. Users have also reported that Instinct and Dots have struggled to purchase items on customers' behalf on Amazon. "We're talking to every one of those companies," Jassy said onstage Tuesday, before adding that every retailer should "have the right to decide how they're going to interact with agents." Amazon is also betting heavily on its own AI shopping tools, including Alexa for Shopping and Buy for Me — tools that are guided by Amazon's "About You" customer dossiers . "I happen to believe that the primary way that people are going to use agentic shopping is going to be in concert with the retailer that they like to use," he said. "You want to be able to tell the app, buy me the deodorant that I normally buy, and it has my past orders, and it has my shipping information, it has my payment information." Other companies have taken a different tack. Shopify has openly embraced outside AI shopping agents, while Home Depot's chief information officer recently told Business Insider that the home improvement store's online marketplace wants agents to identify themselves first.
