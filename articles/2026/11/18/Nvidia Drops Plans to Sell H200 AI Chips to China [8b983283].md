# Nvidia Drops Plans to Sell H200 AI Chips to China

PCMag · Jon Martindale · 2026-11-18

In the aftermath of Jensen Huang resigning as Nvidia CEO over an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, the company is taking steps to further distance itself from China.
In a Tuesday earnings call, acting CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia would no longer sell its H200 chips to China, TechCrunch reports. Meanwhile, the US government has placed new restrictions on remote access to advanced AI chips and servers.
Following the Biden administration's restrictions on the sale of cutting-edge chips to China, Nvidia built China-specific versions of its cards with reduced AI performance and memory. The Trump administration flip-flopped on these restrictions amid trade tensions, but ultimately allowed sales of last-gen H200 GPUs to China, as long as the US government took a 25% cut. Going forward, however, Nvidia will no longer sell H200s to China, Kress said.
The restrictions on AI chips for cloud providers, meanwhile, could cost Nvidia up to $62 billion in data center revenue next year, according to Jefferies data, TechCrunch notes. Kress argued that demand for Nvidia's upcoming Vera Rubin chips would cover any potential shortfalls.
Although that will take months or years to shake out, in the near term Nvidia is distancing itself from its former CEO, his dealings with potential Chinese agents, and his encouragement of direct sales to China. Despite Huang floating the idea of him returning to Nvidia, Board Chair Harvey Jones said, "The board does not intend to reinstate him."
This may be at least partly because of an SEC probe into Nvidia and Huang's actions and into whether they misled investors about AI demand. Huang and General Counsel Tim Teter will also appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and the House Intelligence Committee on Dec. 1 and 2, respectively, for transcribed interviews.
“The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and Nvidia’s role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal," the committee chairs said in a joint statement.
