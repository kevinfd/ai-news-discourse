# From reporting to reasoning: Getting enterprise data ready for AI agents

AWS Machine Learning Blog · Jane Ridge · 2026-11-18

Artificial Intelligence
From reporting to reasoning: Getting enterprise data ready for AI agents
AWS Quick User Group DC inaugural meetup recap
Many organizations are asking how to move beyond static reporting dashboards and begin using generative and agentic AI. But before an AI agent can answer questions or help teams act, it requires a trusted data foundation.
On Wednesday, October 7, 2026, data leaders, developers, and practitioners gathered at the AWS Skills Center in Arlington, Virginia, for the AWS Quick User Group DC meetup. The session brought together founders, software engineers, and enterprise leaders, among them a team from Financial Independence Group (FIG). Attendees explored practical strategies for modernizing legacy architectures, preparing structured data for agentic workflows, and automating routine tasks with no-code tools.
        User groups like this provide a forum for peer-to-peer learning and collaborative problem-solving. The event offered attendees an inside look at how organizations are putting AWS Quick to work to drive productivity gains.
What’s new with AWS Quick: Agents, skills, and connectors
For many in the room, this meetup was a first look at what agentic AI looks like in practice with AWS Quick.
The meetup kicked off by exploring the latest product capabilities across AWS Quick, focusing on its evolution into a unified AI assistant for work. Rather than functioning solely as a traditional business intelligence visualization tool, AWS Quick unifies analytics, natural-language interaction, and multi-step task orchestration.
- AWS Quick agents and skills: Turn natural-language questions into multi-step reusable actions that aid in decision making across enterprise data.
- Third-party connectors: AWS Quick includes built-in integrations that connect tools like Salesforce, Slack, Google Drive, and Microsoft 365, breaking down data silos.
- Unified workspace: A cohesive environment that bridges research, automated visual generation, and business artifact creation (presentations, documents, and spreadsheets) directly where users work.
Real-world innovation: Moving to AI analytics and agentic systems
The core of the day highlighted a real-world customer journey showing how one enterprise is rethinking its data architecture so AI agents can work from governed, trusted data.
Financial Independence Group: Innovating at the pace of technology
Vanessa Gonzalez, Vice President of AI at Financial Independence Group (FIG), shared how FIG keeps pushing past what’s possible today. They’re adopting new tools and platforms as technology changes, and building toward AI-powered analytics and agentic systems on top of their data with AWS Quick.
Technology is changing fast, and FIG wants to be at the front of that change. Building on its existing analytics, FIG moved to AWS Quick, a platform that brings AI into its dashboards, reporting, and analytics. AWS Quick is FIG’s “single front door” for enterprise analytics. It brings together structured data such as customer relationship management (CRM) systems, databases, and finance data. It also pulls in unstructured content such as internal knowledge bases, policy documents, and collateral, through a governed data layer. That gives FIG a base for AI-powered insights and agentic workflows built on its own data.
- The AI analytics loop: Moving from reporting on what already happened to a continuous cycle: Ask → Analyze → Automate → Act.
- Centralized discovery: An analytics portal organized by business area (sales, operations, marketing, commissions), with built-in AI chat assistants so teams can talk directly to their documents and datasets.
- Democratized insights: Self-service exploration tools for non-technical business users, plus a learning hub that builds prompt engineering and AI skills across teams while keeping proper governance in place.
Hands-on: Building apps and no-code flows
Attendees worked through two hands-on exercises:
- Build an AWS Quick app in 50 minutes: Every attendee in the room successfully designed and launched a custom application in AWS Quick. This showed how fast non-technical users and developers alike can deploy purpose-built decision tools without writing code.
- AWS Quick Flows workshop: A subset of six participants dove deeper into workflow automation. They built multi-step, event-triggered flows that handle repetitive data tasks, replacing manual reporting handoffs with automated, repeatable pipelines.
One of the key takeaways is how quickly teams can go from an idea to a working solution with AWS Quick. New ideas now take hours or even minutes to build, not weeks or months.
