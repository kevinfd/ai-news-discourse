# Nvidia's numbers and Anthropic anticipation drive Nasdaq futures up

CNBC · Leonie Kidd · 2026-11-18

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.
Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.
Investors will have to divide their attention between a series of huge market events today.
President Trump is in China for a crucially timed summit between America, China and Russia, with the future of AI chip exports a key issue.
Nvidia shares look set to lift the Nasdaq after the group posted a solid set of earnings amid its leadership shake-up.
And Anthropic is expected to price its IPO in another record moment for markets.
Happening now: Trump arrives in China
President Donald Trump has arrived in Shenzhen, China, for an unprecedented trilateral summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.
In a rare move, Xi met President Trump on the tarmac as Air Force One arrived, with CNBC's Sri Jegarajah reporting from Shenzhen that this is a symbolic act by the Chinese leader, sending a significant foreign policy message.
Ahead of the summit, set to take place tomorrow, all three leaders have laid out their priorities:
- President Donald Trump told reporters, "I don't think you want to insult China just before you get there" in relation to the tension over Nvidia and chip exports.
- Chinese President Xi Jinping opened the APEC meeting with a call to remove trade and investment barriers to "promote development that is balanced."
- Russian President Vladimir Putin has taken aim at Europe ahead of the trilateral meeting, saying, "The EU is now losing its industrial capacity and it's losing in the competition."
Big Movers: Nvidia the morning after
Nvidia shares are trading higher in premarket moves. Chipmaking peers, server stocks and neocloud firms are all trading higher alongside Nvidia.
The chipmaker appeared to brush aside its leadership turmoil to deliver another blockbuster quarter.
- The numbers: Third-quarter revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion. Earnings per share came in at $2.68, while non-GAAP gross margin also topped estimates at 74.9%.
- The guidance: Despite questions over whether Nvidia would scrap its guidance following Jensen Huang's departure, the chipmaker instead raised its outlook. It now expects revenue to grow 75% in fiscal 2028.
- The Analysis: There is cautious optimism following those results, with CNBC's Tobias Burns compiling some of the instant reactions from analysts. Find out more about how certain lines in the sand are cheering investors here.
In an exclusive interview with Jim Cramer, interim CEO Colette Kress focused on the path ahead, despite the speculation around Huang's hopes to return, saying "the most important thing we have seen is growth in the demand that's out there."
During the "Mad Money" interview Tuesday evening, Kress emphasized that major customers have continued expressing interest in the company's products. "I have seen already with many of these [customers] contacting me and saying, 'Great to see everything intact. Great to see that there's more supply. I need more demand.'"
- Cramer's exclusive: You can watch the full interview with Colette Kress here.
Scoop: Anthropic's pricing
Sources have told CNBC that Anthropic will likely price above its current IPO range of $70-78. That's according to multiple people speaking to CNBC's David Faber.
- Why it matters: The stock already has a 'buy' rating from one Wall Street research firm. New Street Research has issued a price target of $105, implying a 35-50% upside from the expected IPO range.
- History matters: CNBC's Kate Rooney has all the details you need to know ahead of the IPO amid Anthropic's breakneck rise.
Outage: Cyber concerns over disruptions
A series of technology disruptions has raised concerns over cybersecurity and the vulnerability of certain systems.
Over recent days, outages have impacted Microsoft, the FAA, JPMorgan Chase transactions and Apple.
Speaking to CNBC on Tuesday, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton dismissed suspicions that malicious activity, saying, "This is not cyberattack, and this is not a coordinated effort by a nation state... We've looked very closely."
- Exclusive: Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella will join "Mad Money" at 6 p.m. ET where he will address that major outage and what steps the company is taking to ensure secure service in the future.
And Finally...
Democrat Mary Peltola wins Alaska Senate election
Democratic candidate Mary Peltola won the Alaska Senate election, capping a midterm cycle that already was one of the best for Democrats in more than a half-century, the Associated Press reported Tuesday evening.
Peltola defeated incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan in a race that was surrounded by controversy over an independent candidate who shared his name.
