# The 5 Best AI Tools to Maximize Your Online Meetings

PCMag · Robert Anderson, Ruben Circelli · 2026-11-18

Table of Contents
Remote work is here to stay, which means video meetings are the norm for many companies. While a day full of video calls can be tedious, the good news is that AI tools can help you better prepare for them, engage your audience, and identify clear action items. We've been covering AI in all of its modern forms, from chatbots to note-taking apps. After scouring the internet, these are the best AI meeting tools we could find to help you get the absolute most out of meetings. Some can transcribe meetings in real time, others can help you create attractive presentations, and many seamlessly integrate with the most popular video conferencing services. Make sure to click through to our full reviews of the products below to see how their AI tools fit into the larger scope of their capabilities.

Zoom's AI alerts you if somebody says your name, answers questions about what's being discussed, and even constructs ongoing summaries of meetings. After a meeting, it can critique how you presented yourself and generate transcripts for you to read over. You don't have to use these features if you don't want to, but you do need at least a Pro subscription ($14.16 per user per month, billed annually) to get more than limited in-meeting access to AI functionality.
Anybody who often gets stuck in long meetings: Zoom's AI Companion shines for those times you're stuck in a long meeting. You can ask it questions and consult its ongoing summary if you lose track of the current conversation thread.
Professionals: Since Zoom locks most of its AI features behind a paid tier, only professionals who spend a significant amount of time in Zoom meetings can likely justify the cost. It's probably not worth paying for if you use Zoom for the occasional personal call.

Canva is all about simplifying and streamlining the graphic design process, and its suite of Canva Magic apps is a way to leverage AI to better accomplish this goal. You can use Magic Write to generate text for a presentation, while Magic Design can transform prompts into images. Alternatively, you can use Magic Animate to animate images or Magic Resize to edit them. Canva's free tier lets you use some AI features with limits, but for the full experience, you need to upgrade to Canva Pro, which costs $18 per month or $144 per year.
Aspiring graphic designers: Canva's accessible interface, coupled with its robust suite of AI features, makes it an exceptional platform for trying your hand at graphic design. It provides numerous assets and templates to support your creative work.
Presenters: If presenting slideshows is a key component of your job, Canva's AI tools can help you enhance your decks with easy-to-make graphics. Keeping your audience's attention is half the battle, anyway.

Miro specializes in data diagramming, flowchart creation, and whiteboard collaboration. The Miro AI can summarize what's on your board, generate action items, and then convert that information into images, sticky notes, or even entire presentations. You can also ask it to rewrite any text for clarity or tone. Miro's AI features use a credit system, meaning each action you run has a cost, and its free tier gives you 10 credits per month. More expensive tiers give you more credits.
Frequent collaborators: Miro is all about collaboration, making it a great tool if you and your team frequently build upon each other's ideas during and outside of meetings. Moreover, its robust integration support means you don't have to change up your established workflows.
Visual learners: If you struggle to articulate your ideas during meetings, Miro lets you back up your points with clear visuals that are easy to share.

Prezi helps you create visually intriguing meeting presentations using a free-form approach, allowing you to work through ideas on a canvas. But you don't need to start from scratch: Prezi AI can generate entire presentations from a prompt, which you can then customize. You can use Prezi AI for free on an AI credit basis, or sign up for Prezi's Plus plan ($19 per month, billed annually) to unlock unlimited usage. Through the Prezi desktop app, you can easily set up connections with Google Meet, Webex by Cisco, and Zoom Workplace for livestreaming and video conferencing.
Non-artists: If you need to give a presentation but don't have strong creative skills, you can lean on Prezi's AI tools to create something visually compelling. Making presentations with AI is incredibly easy.
People who dislike traditional slideshows: Prezi's non-linear slideshows offer a fresh approach to presentations, and its AI tools make creating them accessible. The platform gives you the freedom to present your thoughts in a way that makes sense to you.
Otter excels at automatic transcription of live audio: Its AI assistant takes notes and transcribes in real time with minimal errors. Like a chatbot, it can also answer questions about what's going on if you or anyone else loses track or joins late. Otter's free tier offers decent transcription limits and supports most of the AI features above. For additional transcription minutes and more advanced features, you need to subscribe to the Pro tier ($8.33 per user per month, billed annually). You can connect Otter with Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom Workplace.
Casual collaborators: If you need transcripts from only the occasional meeting, Otter's free tier is more than capable. You get all the service's core features and up to 300 minutes of transcription per month at no cost.
Record keepers: If you enjoy keeping records and taking notes, Otter's accessible, automatic transcription features free you from managing it all yourself. Both its mobile and web apps are enjoyable to use, so you won't dread revisiting your files.
You can use AI to boost your productivity outside of meetings, too. For example, you can find whatever you need online with AI search engines, leverage AI in your job search, or even study with AI, among many other things. Just don't make the mistake of spending more time trying to use AI efficiently than you would actually save by using it, a common trap.
