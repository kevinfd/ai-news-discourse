# Devin Bots are amazing. A new era of AI agents revealed through a thorough comparison with Grok Bot, Dots, and Muse

Devin Bots are amazing. A new era of AI agents revealed through a thorough comparison with Grok Bot, Dots, and Muse
On November 17, 2026, Cognition announced its new service, "Devin Bots."
In recent years, AI has evolved from chatbots that answer questions to AI agents that perform actual tasks.
While xAI's "Grok Bot," OpenAI's "Dots," and Meta's "Muse" have already appeared, Devin Bots is heading in a slightly different direction.
Its biggest feature is that the AI does not just execute tasks, but takes responsibility for the goal itself and manages multiple AIs.
In this article, we will compare these four services from the perspectives of functionality, autonomy, use cases, and pricing.
What are Devin Bots? AI leading a development team
Devin Bots is a new service from Cognition, the developer of the AI software engineer "Devin."
Conventional coding AIs have primarily operated by receiving individual instructions such as "implement this feature" or "fix this bug."
On the other hand, Devin Bots can be given continuous goals such as "keep the Android app's functionality in sync with the iOS version" or "continuously improve the website's display speed."
The Bot determines the necessary tasks itself, runs multiple Devins in parallel, and proceeds through implementation, testing, and review. It summarizes matters requiring human judgment or approval and reports them.
In other words, it is a structure of an AI manager that manages AI engineers.
1,134 PRs merged in 44 days
In a case study released by Cognition, a Devin Bot developed an Android app over 44 days and reported the following results:
- Merged pull requests (PRs): 1,134
- Kotlin code: 344,651 lines
- Screenshot tests: 2,841
- Completed tickets: 1,217
- Messages from humans: 183
It is said that there were no messages from humans for 22 of the 44 days.
However, these are figures released by Cognition itself and are not results verified by an independent third party. Quality cannot be judged solely by the number of PRs or lines of code.
Even so, it is worth noting as a concrete example of giving AI long-term development responsibility.
Grok Bot: An AI team that handles a wide range of tasks
xAI's Grok Bot is an AI agent that executes a wide range of tasks, not limited to development, but also including administrative work, marketing, and document creation.
It uses a dedicated cloud computer to perform tasks such as website operation, email management, and schedule coordination.
Grok Bot 1.0, announced on November 11, 2026, features enhanced team-shared bots and proactive task suggestions.
Multiple bots can work in coordination, and it also supports integration with Slack, Microsoft Teams, and video conferencing.
While Devin Bots specializes in software development, Grok Bot is designed to handle a broad range of general business tasks as one might say.
If you want to automate tasks beyond just development, Grok Bot is a strong candidate.
Dots: A resident AI that integrates with ChatGPT
OpenAI's Dots is an always-on AI agent based on ChatGPT.
It utilizes models like GPT-6 Astra and works continuously using a dedicated cloud computer and external applications.
Its feature is the ability to understand the user's work and preferences, providing support while maintaining long-term context.
It supports software development using Codex, document creation, data analysis, and information gathering.
The plugin mechanism that can connect to over 4,000 apps is also a strength.
On the other hand, team operation by multiple dedicated Dots is a future concept, and at this point, it does not provide the same development team management functions as Devin Bots as a standard feature.
It is suitable for those who use ChatGPT daily and want cross-functional support for their entire workflow.
Muse: Focusing on the automation of daily life
Meta's Muse is a personal AI agent for general consumers.
It can automate travel bookings, shopping, sending emails, and schedule management.
It integrates with Gmail, Google Calendar, Instagram, and others, and was launched in Japan on October 23, 2026.
It operates in an isolated virtual environment called a dedicated "Muse Secure VM," and requires user approval for critical operations such as payments or sending emails.
Another feature is that its basic functions can be used for free.
However, large-scale collaborative work involving software development or multiple specialized agents is not as central a use case as it is for Devin Bots.
When comparing the four services, what are the differences?
The main differences at this point can be summarized as follows.
Devin Bots
Specialized in software development. It manages multiple Devins to continuously carry out development, testing, and maintenance.
Grok Bot
Strong in general business tasks and team collaboration. It features robust coordination between multiple bots and operation of external services.
Dots
Centered on ChatGPT, it supports a wide range of tasks from development to document creation. It emphasizes long-term context understanding and external integration.
Muse
Focused on daily life and personal tasks. It is available for free and has high compatibility with shopping and reservations.
All of them share the ability to continue work in the cloud and execute tasks while seeking user approval.
Therefore, it is not just Devin Bots that move autonomously. The difference lies in what they are designed to manage autonomously.
Pricing and barriers to entry
Devin Bots is available on the MAX plan or higher for $200 per month. Since processes triggered by the bot also consume usage quotas, costs vary depending on the amount of operation.
Grok Bot is also included in the applicable paid plans, but usage quotas and team features differ by plan.
Dots is being rolled out gradually to ChatGPT Pro and applicable Business/Enterprise plans, and there are restrictions on usage conditions.
Muse offers a free tier, making it relatively easy to try out among the four services.
For long-term operation with multiple agents, it is also necessary to consider execution costs and the burden of approval tasks.
Conclusion: The true value of Devin Bots
The notable point about Devin Bots is not the performance of writing code itself.
It lies in the mechanism where AI continuously manages goal achievement, reducing the need for humans to assign tasks in detail.
With previous AI agents, humans had to think of the next task, check the results, and provide instructions again.
Devin Bots is attempting to take over even some of those management tasks.
However, you cannot completely delegate goal setting, quality standards, security, and cost management. As autonomy increases, the importance of human supervision also grows.
At this moment, there is no single AI agent that is perfect for everyone.
If you are delegating development projects, choose Devin Bots; for a wide range of business tasks, Grok Bot; for work centered on ChatGPT, Dots; and for daily life, Muse.
What will determine future competition is not just the intelligence of the AI model, but how consistently it can deliver reliable results with minimal instructions.
