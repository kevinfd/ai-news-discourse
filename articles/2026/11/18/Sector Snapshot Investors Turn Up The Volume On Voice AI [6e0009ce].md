# Sector Snapshot: Investors Turn Up The Volume On Voice AI

Crunchbase News · Mary Ann Azevedo, Judy Rider · 2026-11-18

Voice AI — technology that lets computers understand and generate human speech — is drawing billions of dollars from investors as startups find more ways to put it to work.
Companies developing or offering the technology have raised at least $9.5 billion in venture funding so far in 2026, according to Crunchbase data. The recipients range from companies building speech recognition and generation tools to those offering voice agents for customer service. Others use voice AI for creative work or accessibility, or build the infrastructure and chips that power the technology.
The broad trend: Investors are putting more money into fewer startups developing or offering voice AI technology and are backing everything from automated customer service to speech generation and content creation.
The funding surge also reflects the broader AI boom, with some of the largest rounds going to infrastructure and generative media companies whose businesses extend beyond voice. Multiple companies are raising rounds in quick succession.
The numbers: So far in 2026, startups developing or offering voice AI technology have raised $9.5 billion globally, Crunchbase data shows. As of Nov. 11, funding had already exceeded 2025 by 67%, with 154 reported deals versus 273 in 2025. Overall, deal volume is on track to fall for a second consecutive year — pointing to a market where investors are putting more money into fewer companies.
Unsurprisingly, given the AI boom, funding for the space has continued to climb in recent years compared to past levels, surging from $2.8 billion in 2021 to $5.7 billion in 2025.
Notable rounds
The largest rounds in Crunchbase’s voice AI micro-industry went well beyond speech synthesis, encompassing infrastructure, generative media and customer service agents.
The biggest individual financing went to Fireworks AI, which raised a $1.5 billion Series D in July at a $17.5 billion valuation. The San Mateo, California-based company is an AI infrastructure provider whose offerings include speech recognition. Atreides Management, Index Ventures and TCV co-led the round, with participation from investors including Nvidia and Lightspeed Venture Partners.
San Francisco-based Fal was the largest funding recipient across multiple rounds in 2026, raising $1.65 billion in total. That included a $1.3 billion Series F led by Bessemer Venture Partners, completed in October at a reported $16 billion valuation, doubling its valuation from its $350 million Sequoia Capital-led financing in April. The company provides infrastructure for running image, video and audio models.
Customer service AI also attracted substantial investment. San Francisco-based Sierra raised $950 million in May, led by Tiger Global and GV, at a reported $15.8 billion valuation. Its platform lets businesses deploy agents across voice, chat and other customer channels.
Meanwhile, Amsterdam-based Wonderful raised a $550 million Series C in September, led by Insight Partners, at a $5 billion valuation. That followed a $150 million Series B in March, bringing its total 2026 funding to $700 million as it expanded from conversational agents into broader enterprise automation.
Among companies focused on AI-generated speech, New York-based ElevenLabs announced a $500 million Series D in February, led by Sequoia Capital, at an $11 billion valuation. That was more than triple its valuation a year earlier. Additional institutional and strategic investors joined in later tranches, bringing the Series D total to $561 million. ElevenLabs allows creators, enterprises and others to use AI software to replicate voices in dozens of languages. The company is investing in conversational agents, expressive speech and dubbing.
Beijing-based ModelBest, which develops compact AI models that run on devices, also ranks among the largest recipients of funding this year, with a $591 million Series C in July.
Exits
Voice AI companies have also begun delivering exits. In January, Deepgram acquired OfOne, which automates restaurant and drive-thru orders.
In September, Inworld acquired Ultravox, a developer of conversational voice AI technology.
The sector also saw an IPO. Neosapience, the South Korean company behind synthetic voice platform Typecast, listed on the KOSDAQ on Sept. 21.
Looking ahead, additional acquisitions are in the pipeline. Automation Anywhere agreed to acquire Boost.ai, an enterprise voice and chat AI provider, in October. And Deepgram announced plans to acquire Daily, creator of the Pipecat voice-agent framework.
More recently, Five9 agreed to acquire Level AI for $325 million in cash, adding technology that analyzes customer conversations, assists contact-center workers and automates voice and digital interactions. The Level AI transaction is expected to close by year’s end.
Overall, the space is booming, and it doesn’t look like interest in voice AI will die anytime soon. Tom Hulme, managing partner and head of Europe at GV, told Crunchbase News in 2025 that one of the most exciting trends he believes is underway in the growth of voice AI is that “we’re returning to humanity’s most natural form of communication.”
After decades of adapting ourselves to technology, “technology is finally adapting to us,” he said.
