# Bengaluru leads India's deep-tech startup funding with $3.77 billion, twice Delhi-NCR's tally: Report

Moneycontrol · Moneycontrol News · 2026-11-18

15
Bengaluru accounts for 36.3 percent of India's native AI startup funding. Women-led AI and deep-tech ventures have raised $324 million, while 16 Bengaluru-based companies have gone public since November 2024, according to Bengaluru Innovation Report 2026.
  November 18, 2026 / 18:12 IST  
Bengaluru has emerged as India's leading deep-tech startup hub, attracting $3.77 billion in funding between 2010 and 2026 (year-to-date), more than double the $1.64 billion raised by startups in Delhi-NCR, according to the Bengaluru Innovation Report 2026, released at the Bengaluru Tech Summit on November 18.
The city has seen 1,556 deep-tech companies founded since 2010, of which 1,319 are active and funded. Delhi-NCR follows with 1,127 companies founded and 943 active, funded startups. Hyderabad ranks third with 518 companies founded, followed by Mumbai with 493 and Chennai with 419. In terms of funding, Chennai ranks third with $1.41 billion, followed by Mumbai at $1.33 billion and Hyderabad at $867 million.
Bengaluru accounts for 36.3% of India's native artificial intelligence (AI) startup funding. The city is home to 799 native AI startups, of which 764 are active and funded. These companies have collectively raised $2.28 billion.
Bengaluru is also home to 71% of India's AI and deep-tech unicorns created since 2023.
The report was released by Karnataka Minister for Electronics, IT and BT Priyank Kharge on November 18. Prepared by the Karnataka Digital Economy Mission (KDEM) in collaboration with 3one4 Capital, it draws on data from Tracxn.
Women-led startups attract $324 million
Nearly 30% of India's active, funded women-led startups in deep tech and native AI were founded in Bengaluru. The city has 103 such startups, compared with 58 in Delhi-NCR and 43 in Mumbai.
Women-led ventures in Bengaluru have raised $323.65 million, more than twice the $140.03 million secured by their counterparts in Delhi-NCR.
The report identifies access to patient capital, stronger industry-academia partnerships and advanced computing infrastructure as key requirements for helping these companies scale.
Bengaluru startups raise $85 billion
Bengaluru-based startups have raised approximately $85 billion since 2010, accounting for 47% of the venture capital deployed in Indian startups.
The city has 3,810 active, funded startups and 57 unicorns with a combined valuation of approximately $211 billion, accounting for 55.3% of India's total unicorn valuation.
Bengaluru also has 212 soonicorns, or startups likely to achieve unicorn status, accounting for 36% of the national total. Delhi-NCR has 134, while Mumbai has 121.
Sixteen Bengaluru-linked companies have listed since November 2024, with a combined market capitalisation of approximately $42 billion at listing. The city accounts for 33 of the 226 IPOs by funded companies in India during this period.
Startups in Bengaluru's ecosystem take an average of 8.2 years to achieve an exit, compared with the global average of 11.1 years.
Push to expand beyond Bengaluru
Under the ELEVATE grant-in-aid programme, Karnataka has supported 1,479 startups with Rs 393.58 crore since 2017, with 38.6% of beneficiaries located outside Bengaluru. The state has also established a Rs 75-crore Beyond Bengaluru Cluster Seed Fund.
Global Technology Centres are being developed in Mysuru, Hubballi and Belagavi. The Rs 1,000-crore Local Economy Accelerator Programme (LEAP) aims to generate five lakh jobs over five years.
Kharge said Karnataka's next challenge was to ensure that companies working in AI, quantum technology and semiconductors could scale in Kalaburagi, Hubballi and Mangaluru as successfully as they do in Bengaluru.
"Deep tech rewards patience, and Karnataka will stay the course," he said.
Siddharth Pai, founding partner of 3one4 Capital, said Karnataka had 2.7 million technology professionals, added 1.5-2 lakh professionals annually and accounted for 20% of the world's chip-design engineering workforce and 50% of India's AI and machine learning talent.
Sharath Bache Gowda, chairman of KEONICS, said Bengaluru's unicorn ecosystem had created a pipeline of companies that could become the next generation of unicorns.
Manjula N, secretary of the Department of Electronics, IT, BT and S&T, said the government's focus was on helping startups scale through government procurement, patient capital and the commercialisation of research.
