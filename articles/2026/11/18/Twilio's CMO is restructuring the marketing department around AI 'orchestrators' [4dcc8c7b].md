# Twilio's CMO is restructuring the marketing department around AI 'orchestrators'

Business Insider · Lara O'Reilly, Lara O'Reilly (loreilly@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-18

Twilio CMO Chris Koehler is restructuring the marketing department from a team built around narrow specialists to one led by AI 'orchestrators.' Twilio A version of this post appears in CMO Insider. Sign up for Business Insider's marketing newsletter . Chris Koehler, CMO of cloud communications platform Twilio , is redesigning his marketing team for the AI era. Koehler is shifting from a model built around a team of "95%" narrow specialists toward broader, more cross-functional marketers who oversee projects from start to finish, with AI taking on more tasks such as research, localization, and content creation. The goal: fewer handoffs and higher output, while keeping head count flat. "AI has simply been a game changer," Koehler told me. Koehler joins a growing group of CMOs restructuring their teams as AI takes on repetitive, data-driven tasks. Some CMOs are reshaping around "full-stack marketers" or "marketing engineers." For Twilio, it's "orchestrators." Koehler said it's enabled the company to stay nimble and condense time-intensive work, while demonstrating a return on investment, such as increasing the number of campaigns it produces and reducing the hours spent on projects. In the global campaigns team, for example, Twilio has created "pods" led by a single campaign strategist focused on a priority, such as delivering a campaign targeting a key customer persona. That strategist works with a small pod of cross-functional marketers to execute against the priority. Koehler estimated the pods will make up no more than 20% of the total marketing organization. "Our intent is for these pods to remain laser-focused on our important bets, while the team remains focused on other, critical 'business as usual' activities," Koehler said. "This enables us to experiment and adjust without risking what has been working for us." Increasing output, saving time Koehler said the campaign team's use of AI tools has condensed weeks of account research and personalized content creation into hours, and increased output to 15 account-based campaigns per quarter, up from three previously. Koehler said these campaigns performed "just as well as our usual campaigns." Elsewhere, the go-to-market communications team has been deploying AI agents to build campaigns in 10 minutes that used to take human-only teams two hours to prepare and send. Koehler said customers who engaged with its AI agent, ISA, were three times more likely to convert than those who did not, which helped contribute to the over-30% growth in self-serve revenue it reported in its third-quarter earnings. Koehler said the creative team has become more efficient by using built-in AI features in platforms such as Adobe, CapCut, and Canva. It's also testing newer AI startups, such as video platform Capsule , which can generate videos from a single text prompt. These experiments haven't always gone to plan. Koehler said when asked to localize a campaign for the French market, one AI tool attempted to put berets on all the photos. "There's still a human in the loop in all this stuff," Koehler said. "The reality is, it's getting better and better, and the more you reinforce it, train it, and put the effort in, you get better output." Koehler said he's currently figuring out how the marketing team's reorganization will affect job descriptions, job codes, and the management hierarchy. Asked whether it could lead to a reduction in roles, Koehler said the plan is to keep its overall head count "about the same" next year. Twilio's companywide workforce has also remained broadly flat. Its reported head count rose about 1.5% year over year through the quarter ended September 30, while revenue and gross profit each increased by 20%. Twilio joins a raft of companies reconfiguring their marketing departments as AI tools take on an increasing number of tasks. Wix CMO Omer Shai told CMO Insider earlier this year he's deploying AI and "super agents" and replacing specific roles with " full-stack marketers ." Klarna CMO David Sandström said he's looking to hire " marketing engineers " as his department goes all in on AI. Publicis Sapient CMO Teresa Barreira said earlier this year that 80% to 90% of the daily tasks her team performs can be accelerated or done entirely by AI, and created new job descriptions focused on the functions that matter. A recent survey of 194 business-to-business marketers by the B2B marketer network CMO Huddles and the research firm Benchmarkit found a gap between respondents who use AI for individual functions and those who integrate it into how their teams operate. While 98% of marketers surveyed said they used AI in at least one marketing function, only 3% said they had coordinated it across their go-to-market teams and processes. Drew Neisser, CEO of CMO Huddles, said it's important for marketers to demonstrate how AI can drive business growth, rather than simply pointing to productivity gains. Otherwise, he said, AI risks reinforcing the perception that marketing is a commodity that can be outsourced or cut as companies' AI costs rise. "The thing you can't lose sight of is, 'How is AI reinforcing differentiation?'" and, "'How is it increasing the exposure of the organization?'" Neisser said.
