# Former OpenAI researcher says he’ll donate his equity to AI safety: 'Everything else is a distraction'

Insider · Truman Dickerson · 2026-11-18

Jacob Coxon is forking over what could be a massive personal windfall in the name of AI safety.
The ex-OpenAI researcher, who also worked at Anthropic before he recently quit, said on Wednesday that he's committed to selling his equity in OpenAI and donating the proceeds to AI safety causes.
"We need to pause or drastically slow down the race to superintelligence," he said on X. "Everything else is a distraction."
In doing so, Coxon is forgoing what could be millions of dollars in profit that would become liquid when OpenAI goes public, an event that CEO Sam Altman has said is on the horizon.
Coxon made serious waves in September when he left Anthropic and issued a series of dire warnings that it and OpenAI were "gambling with our lives."
"The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade," he said in an X post on September 8 that has since racked up 175 million views. "This is not a marketing stunt."
Coxon worked at OpenAI from 2023 until 2026, before leaving for Anthropic. Since then, the 27-year-old from England has done the rounds with media to advocate for AI safeguards and, principally, a pause on recursive self-improvement, the process by which AI models train themselves.
In a November 9 talk at Stanford, Coxon called his stake in OpenAI "blood money" but balked at giving it up at the time.
"I like money, bro," he said. "My parents were like worried I was having a psychotic break. I think if I'd also given away all the money they'd be even more convinced of that."
