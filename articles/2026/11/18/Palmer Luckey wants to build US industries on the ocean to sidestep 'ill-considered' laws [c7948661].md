# Palmer Luckey wants to build US industries on the ocean to sidestep 'ill-considered' laws

Business Insider · Aditi Bharade,Matthew Loh, Aditi Bharade,Matthew Loh (abharade@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-18

Palmer Luckey floated his plan for building an industrial base off the mainland US. Kyle Grillot/Bloomberg via Getty Images Palmer Luckey wants to become a seasteader. He floated plans to build a seastead where industries could thrive, free from local or state laws. Luckey has pitched himself as a disruptor of systems that have caused the fall of American industrial might. Palmer Luckey wants to make American industry great again — by taking it out to sea. In a Tuesday X post, the Anduril cofounder shared his vision for a seastead called "SLAB" off the US coast, upon which critical industries could thrive, unencumbered by local laws. Luckey said his plan for the seastead was to make it cheaper than land in the US and free of the restrictions that "cripple critical American industries," such as oil refining, chemical engineering, and medical research. He did not specify what laws or regulations he was trying to sidestep. He added that a facility of this kind would "make America stronger," and act as a "relief valve for ill-considered or outdated rules and regulations."  Luckey attached what appeared to be an AI-generated image of "SLAB" to the X post, which depicted a massive concrete slab rising above the ocean's waves, with factories and corporate offices built atop it. He said he was only revealing the plan now because media outlets had gotten wind of the project and started calling it his "new floating AI data center project." "It is way cooler than that, way older than that, and I hate looking like a hype-chasing consensus loser, so now you have to read this tweet," Luckey wrote. He said that it would be funded by the money he made from selling Oculus — his virtual reality tech brand — to Facebook in 2014. Facebook bought Oculus for $2 billion. He said that some other unnamed "close friends" injected capital into the project at a $1 billion valuation. Luckey, the founder of defense tech startup Anduril, has frequently pitched himself as a disruptor of old systems that have caused American industry to wane, and often speaks of aiming to help the US strengthen its dominance in the military and tech sectors. Anduril specializes in software, drones, scalable weapons, and high-tech combat gear, clinching tens of billions of dollars in contracts across multiple US military branches and allies. It was valued at around $61 billion during a $5 billion funding round in May. A seastead business would add to Luckey's growing list of projects apart from Anduril. The billionaire has also founded a retro-gaming hardware firm, ModRetro, a digital bank called Erebor, and recently announced a working dog-breeding and cloning firm named Warhound . Luckey is not the first billionaire who has thought of regulation-free seasteads. In 2008, billionaire venture capitalist Peter Thiel invested $1.7 million in The Seasteading Institute , which aimed to host "startup communities that float on the ocean with any measure of political autonomy," according to the organization's website.
