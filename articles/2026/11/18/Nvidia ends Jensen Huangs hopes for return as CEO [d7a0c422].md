# Nvidia ends Jensen Huangs hopes for return as CEO

Mashable · Amanda Yeo · 2026-11-18

Nvidia ends Jensen Huang's hopes for return as CEO
Nvidia held its Q3 2027 earnings call on Tuesday, less than a week after former CEO Jensen Huang resigned amidst reports he had engaged in multiple extramarital affairs — including with an alleged Chinese spy. While Huang did not appear during the earnings call, he did publish a statement saying that he would happily return to his former role as Nvidia's CEO if asked.
The AI boom has sparked a significant spike in demand for computer components, leading to increasing profits for chipmakers such as Nvidia. Earlier this year, the company reported $81.6 billion in revenue during Q1 and $96.2 billion in Q2. This growth has continued in Q4, with Nvidia announcing its quarterly revenue had increased by 20 percent to $115.8 billion.
However, the elephant in the room was Huang, whose recent scandal and departure prompted a significant drop in Nvidia's stock. During Tuesday's earnings call, Nvidia chairman Harvey Jones stated that Huang had voluntarily resigned on Nov. 12 "after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms." Nvidia is currently searching for a permanent CEO, with CFO Colette Kress acting in the role in the interim.

"The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," said Jones. "While this was an unexpected transition, the board acted deliberately and in the best interest of the company's employees, customers, partners, and shareholders."
Jones then shut down any further discussion regarding Huang's situation, stating that while Nvidia's board would issue further communications "when appropriate," it would not address or answer any questions about it during the earnings call.
"The board is proactively dealing with the immediate consequences facing the company, as well as enhancing our oversight capacity going forward," said Jones.
Nvidia announced on Tuesday that it has established a committee to oversee its national security policies and export control compliance. The first of Huang's affair partners to be revealed was Alice Huang (no relation), a former Megaspeed executive accused of being a Chinese intelligence agent funnelling Nvidia chips to the country, which would be a violation of U.S. restrictions.
Interim CEO Kress also made efforts to reassure investors during Nvidia's earnings call, and stressed the value it places on its workers. Huang's second and third alleged affair partners were Nvidia employees, including its vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell.

"Our ambition will not change, our passion to innovate will not change, and our commitment to customers, partners, and shareholders will not change," said Kress.
"We will take care of our employees during this transition to make sure that they are safe and making sure the work can be done in the current environment. I think there's also a time to think about our leadership and how important our leadership is across this organization."
Huang says he would return as Nvidia CEO if asked
Hours prior to Nvidia's earnings call, Huang took to X to issue his first public statement since stepping down as CEO last Thursday. In it, he expressed his appreciation for his friends and colleagues, reiterated his love for the U.S., and admitted he has made "mistakes."
"I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life," said Huang. "I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret."
Huang further addressed calls for him to be reinstated as Nvidia's CEO, making it clear he would return if asked. The Financial Times previously reported that Huang had discussed reclaiming the role with people close to him, and was gathering other tech executives to lobby for his return.
"Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO," Huang wrote, noting that some had contacted him directly. "If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

It wouldn't be the first time a tech CEO reclaimed their role after being ousted. OpenAI's board fired CEO Sam Altman in 2023, accusing him of lying, retaliating against critics, and creating a "toxic atmosphere." Altman was swiftly reinstated after days of outcry from supporters, with almost the entire board ultimately leaving instead.
Even so, judging by the Nvidia board's statements on Tuesday, Huang's hopes for a similar comeback are likely to remain mere hopes for now.
