# Buy the AI boom or beware the hype? 5 investing pros weigh in on Anthropic’s IPO

Business Insider · Alex Nicoll, Alex Nicoll (anicoll@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-18

Anthropic co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei. Anthropic Anthropic's public debut will set a record for money raised in an initial public offering. The company could be ushering in a new technological era, or pop an AI bubble. We spoke to five investment strategists and investors about whether they'd invest. The next industrial revolution — or a potential existential threat to humanity —is coming to the public markets. In a year of blockbuster deals, Anthropic's initial public offering is poised to be the largest by money raised ever. Later on Wednesday, Anthropic will take orders for the IPO , opening ownership to institutional and some retail investors. Before the end of trading Thursday, anyone will have a shot to buy one of the biggest names in tech. The IPO comes at a turbulent time for AI: a data-center pushback helped fuel the Democrats' blue wave, the  Jensen Huang-Nvidia scandal  is raising national security concerns, and the market's AI sell-off continues. So is Anthropic a chance to get in on a historic technological transformation — or an expensive bet on an increasingly uncertain future? Amid this backdrop, we asked five investors whether they would buy shares in the IPO. They were split on the prospect, citing both how the company could be at the forefront of a true global transformation and the financial and existential risks the company holds. Phil Blancato, chief market strategist at Osaic Anthropic's workforce grew more than fivefold from the end of 2024 through September. Courtesy of Anthropic Would you buy? Yes. Anthropic's IPO is a must-buy event "if you believe AI will transform how we work and live," Blancato, who leads market strategy for Osaic, a wealth management network that oversees $810 billion in client assets, said. The firm points to a massive potential market: It estimates that the entire earnings of work by knowledge workers globally exceeded $40 trillion in 2025. Anthropic also cited IDC's estimate that AI will have a $22.5 trillion economic impact by 2031. While those figures don't directly translate to potential revenue, they illustrate the scale of opportunity and "extreme change" as AI takes on more work, he said. "Even if Anthropic captures only a small fraction of that value, there's still room for the business to grow well beyond where it is today," Blancato said. That doesn't mean there's no risk to a bet this big. Computing and infrastructure costs in the most recent quarter rose higher than revenue, and Anthropic does need to commit to data centers "years in advance," while customers can "cut back their usage much sooner," Blancato said. That, plus the possibility that customers may find low-cost alternatives in the future, is a real risk that could "limit future earnings," he said. Christian Giadolor, investment analyst at Chicory Wealth Mikala Compton/The Austin American-Statesman via Getty Images Would you buy? No. One of the first risks Anthropic disclosed in its S-1 filing is that it might not be able to secure sufficient computing power. The IPO and its $131 billion in cash could help it skip the queue, says Giadolor, who previously worked in macro and sustainable investing at Bridgewater Associates. But with compute costs "skyrocketing," he questioned whether the economics are "sustainable," especially as Americans increasingly push back against data centers . In the first half of the year, nearly 50% of Anthropic's revenue came from cloud service providers, putting them at significant risk of a slowdown in data-center construction or demand from the hyperscalers. This may be why the company is signaling that it could build its own data center infrastructure, Giadolor said, but thought doing so would expose it to the risks of asset-heavy ownership of physical infrastructure. Then there are the risks posed by potential future regulations. Anthropic has warned that "its models could pose an existential risk to humanity," Giadolor said. They could also carry a more boring risk: there's very little insurance available for AI models to protect them if they do something wrong. "If Rogue AI and this 'existential' threat emerges, it could place Anthropic in unprecedented legal jeopardy," Giadolor said. Dhruv Maniktala, chief investment officer at True North Advisors and Western Alternative Strategies Michael Nagle/Bloomberg via Getty Images Would you buy? Maybe. Maniktala's multi-family office, which manages $5.9 billion, previously acquired Anthropic shares at "much cheaper" valuations in the private market. The current valuations are too high for him to issue a blanket buy suggestion. It's not that Anthropic isn't a great firm in the midst of rolling out solutions for different verticals of the economy, he told Business Insider, but there's an "unreasonable amount of growth built in." In order to fund its projected data center buildout, the company will need $3.5 trillion in annual revenue, Maniktala estimated, the equivalent of spending $1,750 a month on AI per employee in the US, even including hairdressers and fast-food workers. But whether the valuation is $500 billion or $2 trillion matters less than the fact that it's "impossible to predict" what happens next, Maniktala said, given the scale of possibilities presented by Anthropic's IPO. If you have the money sitting around to make a small allocation to the company, it could make sense to buy and take a chance on the "big unknown," he said. Stephanie Link, chief investment strategist at Hightower Advisors Stephanie Link, Hightower chief market strategist Hightower Advisors Would you buy? Yes Link, who leads investment strategy for $350 billion wealth management firm Hightower, would invest in the company but keep the allocation small given the expected volatility, similar to the advice she gave to potential SpaceX investors earlier this year. Instead of the $2 trillion valuation that was floated earlier this year, $1.13 to $1.3 trillion offers a much more palatable entry point for investors, she said. As of Tuesday evening, there were reports that the company may be pricing closer to $1.5 or $1.6 trillion. She also likes that the IPO is six times oversubscribed. Add to that the company's "amazing" revenue growth — revenue grew 1,206% year-over-year, and third-quarter revenue was up 47% sequentially — and it makes sense to buy. That's not to say there aren't concerns Link is watching for. "I'm mildly concerned about the top five customers accounting for 21% of revenue, but I expect that to decline in the coming years as Anthropic signs up more customers," she said. Kristin Hull, founder and chief investment officer at Nia Impact Capital Anti-data-center protestors Anna Moneymaker/Getty Images Would you buy? No Nia Impact Capital's Kristin Hull is "sitting on the sidelines" for this IPO. There are financial reasons to be wary, according to Hull. These include the company's debt load, its complicated relationships with partners who are also competitors, its long-term lease commitments, an untested business model, and uncertainty about future AI margins as usage increases. "On the business model alone, and given the growing range of competitors, I would wait to see a clearer path toward both independence and profitability," Hull said. But the long-time impact investor said that there are also many "negative externalities" beyond the pure finances that also aren't being priced in. There's a pushback brewing : whether it's "future intellectual property litigation risk," the concern over mass surveillance and privacy currently manifesting against Flock cameras, or over their massive environmental costs. Earlier this month, more than 50 localities voted to limit or ban data center construction through ballot measures, according to Ballotpedia. All of this could create major financial challenges for Anthropic. "The recent November election also brought up important questions about who bears the environmental costs: Anthropic and its investors, or the communities hosting the infrastructure?" Hull said.
