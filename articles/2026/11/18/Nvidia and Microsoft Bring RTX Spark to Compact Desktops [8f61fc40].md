# Nvidia and Microsoft Bring RTX Spark to Compact Desktops

PCMag · Brian Westover · 2026-11-18

The Nvidia-Microsoft team-up that produced the RTX Spark line of laptops just a few short weeks ago has expanded to include a new class of small-form-factor Windows PCs built for local AI agents, content creation, and gaming.
Powered by the same RTX Spark Grace Blackwell hardware, the new AI-ready desktops go on sale today, with systems from Acer, Asus, Dell, Gigabyte, HP, Lenovo, and MSI.
Powered by the N1X Superchip
This new line of compact desktops is built around the same RTX Spark hardware seen in recent AI-ready laptops, namely the RTX Spark N1X Superchip. It combines a 6,144-core Blackwell RTX GPU with a 20-core Grace CPU, designed by Nvidia and custom-built with MediaTek. The hardware is purpose-built for local AI, delivering 1 Petaflop of FP4 compute and featuring up to 128GB of unified memory to load and run large local AI models.
It also happens to be quite powerful for creative workflows and complex 3D rendering, supporting the full Nvidia stack: CUDA, TensorRT, NVFP4, RTX Ray Tracing, DLSS (including DLSS 4.5 and DLSS 5), Reflex, and G-Sync. That means that in addition to powering local AI models and agentic AI tools, these compact desktops also support gaming and workstation-grade graphics and computation.
Built for Local, Unmetered AI
As AI technology goes mainstream, the economics of cloud AI use have changed, making it more attractive than ever to run AI locally on privately owned machines.
Comparing one RTX Spark desktop to the cost of cloud API fees with Claude Sonnet 5.5 Medium, one small desktop is capable of generating 61 million output tokens per month for only the cost of the electricity used to run it—about $20. Doing the same work and getting the same number of output tokens from a cloud API through Anthropic would cost $1,200 in API fees. And as models like Qwen3.8-Flash-Next become comparable in performance and intelligence to cloud models, they become an extremely attractive option for businesses wanting to run AI that is affordable, secure, and private.
With the RTX Spark hardware and 128GB of unified memory, each desktop can run a 120B-parameter model with a 1M-token context window.
And when you compare the capability of current open-source models—such as Nemotron 3.5 Super-VL-120B-A12B—and the Day-0 support offered across frameworks like llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, Unsloth, ComfyUI, and Canonical, suddenly the complexity of running AI locally is lower than it has ever been.
The benefits of running AI on the RTX Spark desktop are enhanced with Microsoft's new agentic AI features, such as Windows and OpenShell, MXC sandboxing, and granular PC capability permissions. New software such as NeMo Switchyard for local-cloud routing in Hermes balances workloads between your local models and cloud tools that might have a specific capability you need. Other new tools, like the Nvidia App GPU prioritization feature, allow you to allocate resources for AI use or gaming—or balance between the two—to enjoy your gameplay while still running an agent in the background.
From Workstation to Household and Gaming
But these desktops are not aimed entirely at businesses and professional developers. Microsoft and Nvidia hope that RTX Spark will make these new desktops a household AI appliance. They are designed to run continuously as a low-power home hub for automation, home security, general maintenance, and managing family calendars. As integrations with smart home appliances, sensors, lighting, and other devices mature, it's feasible that this single AI hub could manage an entire household's devices.
Nvidia's multi-device routing, called Nvidia PAIR, can intelligently distribute AI workloads from other household devices across idle PCs on the home network, assigning subtasks and smaller AI agents to Windows laptops, gaming PCs, and even MacBooks, with the RTX Spark desktop as the central coordinating hub that organizes everything. An ecosystem of agent-ready apps is already beginning to grow, with built-in tools and skills for productivity apps like Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and Slack; developer tools like VS Code, GitHub, Docker, and Cursor; and creative software like Adobe Photoshop, Premiere, and Illustrator—all of which now have or will shortly have built-in tools, skills, and MCPs for AI accessibility and agentic task completion.
Because RTX Spark runs on Grace ARM processors and offers graphics capabilities on par with a discrete GPU, gaming capabilities are coming. World of Warcraft: Forever and Marvel Rivals are getting native Windows on Arm support, and DLSS 5 updates are coming for Hogwarts Legacy, War Thunder, and Where Winds Meet. There's even a new beta for the RTX Remix Minecraft Java Mod Kit, bringing powerful tools for gaming and modding games to these compact, gaming-capable machines.
Availability and Pricing
These compact desktops are launching today, joining the thin-and-light RTX Spark laptops that began selling in October. Unlike the laptop lineup—where configurations with lower core counts and smaller memory allotments sell at more mainstream prices—all compact desktops standardize on the top-tier 20-core CPU and 6,144-core GPU, paired with your choice of 64GB or 128GB of unified memory.
While pricing varies slightly by manufacturer, Nvidia says the new desktops start at $4,399 for 64GB configurations.
The first wave of systems hits shelves today—including the Asus ProArt GR1X and MSI EdgeMesa N AI+—with additional models from Acer, Dell, Gigabyte, HP, and Lenovo arriving over the next few weeks.
