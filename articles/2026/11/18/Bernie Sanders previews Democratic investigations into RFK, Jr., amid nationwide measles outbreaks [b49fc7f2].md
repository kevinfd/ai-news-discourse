# Bernie Sanders previews Democratic investigations into RFK, Jr., amid nationwide measles outbreaks

Scientific American · Adam Kovac · 2026-11-18

When Democrats take control of the Senate in the January, Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., will be a prime target for a key committee.
At a press briefing on Wednesday, Senator Bernie Sanders of Vermont, who is the current ranking member of the Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions, hinted that Kennedy’s stewardship of the Department of Health and Human Services amid measles outbreaks that have been so severe that the U.S. lost its elimination status this week would warrant close examination.
“The idea that we have a secretary of health and human services who is into conspiracy theories has led us in a direction where, for the first time in God knows how long, we have lost our status as a measles-free country,” Sanders said. “That is certainly one area that needs investigation. There are many more.”

Sanders, an Independent who caucuses with the Democrats, extended an olive branch to the “Make America Healthy Again” (MAHA) movement.
“To the degree that we can work with MAHA people, we want to see good labeling on food packages that we don't have right now,” he said. “I would like to see junk food advertising off of the TV airwaves. So I think there are some areas that we can work with [them].”
Sanders refused to elaborate on any specific investigations into the Trump administration that may be coming in 2027, but he added that the Senate committee will scrutinize federal ties to the pharmaceutical and health insurance industries.
“You have this absurdity where people are filing claims for health care benefits that they had paid for, and they’re getting rejected. Meanwhile the insurance companies make huge profits,” Sanders said. “We’re also going to want to know why, when the federal government puts huge amounts of money into research and development for prescription drugs, taxpayers pay for that, [but] we end up paying the highest prices in the world for prescription drugs.”
The committee will also focus on legislation pertaining to one of Sanders’s longtime pet issues, universal health care, he said. Regardless of who is named Senate majority leader, Sanders said, he believes a “Medicare for All” bill will come up for a Senate vote within a year of Democrats retaking Congress.
Support for a single-payer Medicare for All option has grown among the general population and the Democratic caucus over the past few years, but any legislation passed in Congress could be vetoed by President Donald Trump.
