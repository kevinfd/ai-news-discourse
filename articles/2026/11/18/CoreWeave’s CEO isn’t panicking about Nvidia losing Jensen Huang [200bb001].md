# CoreWeave’s CEO isn’t panicking about Nvidia losing Jensen Huang

Business Insider · Aditi Bharade, Aditi Bharade (abharade@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-18

CoreWeave CEO Michael Intrator called Huang a visionary, but said the rest of the AI supply chain will continue to be supported by Nvidia, even in Huang's absence. Bloomberg/Getty Images If CoreWeave CEO Michael Intrator is worried about Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia, he isn't showing it. Intrator said that CoreWeave and Nvidia's partnership would continue even in Huang's absence. Nvidia is a major backer and supplier of neocloud companies like CoreWeave. A major neocloud executive doesn't seem phased by Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia. CoreWeave CEO Michael Intrator, at Semafor's "Silicon Valley & The World" event on Tuesday, hinted that he wasn't overly concerned about how Huang's departure from Nvidia would affect CoreWeave's business. He said that Nvidia has been an "extraordinary company," but it also employs "thousands and thousands of amazing engineers and employees" who would continue their partnership. "Those thousands of people integrate with CoreWeave every day to work on how you deploy the equipment, how you build the data centers, how you network the infrastructure, all of these things, and you know, that continues," Intrator added. The moderator asked if Huang's absence — even if just emotionally — would be felt when it came to accessing credit for data center infrastructure. Huang was a huge backer of neocloud companies like CoreWeave. Intrator said that Huang was a visionary who supported not just Nvidia but the data center and AI ecosystem . But the reason Huang propped the ecosystem up was because these elements of the supply chain "were needed and they will continue to be needed, and they will continue to be supported by Nvidia and by the balance sheet that they represent," he said. Intrator's comments came after a tumultuous week for Nvidia, where Huang resigned after multiple allegations emerged over his extramarital affairs. But the pessimism didn't extend to Nvidia's earnings call on Tuesday, where the chipmaker reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier. Nvidia's stock price was up more than 4% in after-hours trading, and Nvidia's leadership said it wasn't considering bringing Huang back. Neoclouds — cloud computing services specifically for AI customers — made headlines in October for their high debt levels to build AI capacity, but the returns on that investment remain unclear. Investors were spooked by the uncertainty, and their stocks took a major hit. Huang, while he was still with Nvidia, maintained that demand for AI cloud infrastructure is "off the charts."
