# What to know about Ring Drone Cam, which can patrol your home for $250

Fox News · Kurt Knutsson, CyberGuy Report · 2026-11-18

Ever leave the house and suddenly wonder whether you closed the back door? You pull up your security camera, but it only shows the driveway. Not much help when you want to see what's happening inside. Well, Ring thinks it has an answer. And this one actually flies. Amazon-owned Ring has unveiled the Ring Drone Cam, a tiny $249.99 indoor security camera that flies from room to room. It can investigate security alerts, check specific areas of your home and send live video directly to your phone.
Earlier this month, the company announced the new drone as part of its new Ring Robotics lineup. And thanks to artificial intelligence, it can navigate your home without someone steering it around. We also talked in depth about this new technology on "The CyberGuy Report Podcast," including what a flying camera could mean for home security. But before you get too excited about having your own miniature security patrol, there are some things worth knowing.

How Ring Drone Cam flies around your home
Traditional home security cameras have one obvious limitation. They only show what's in front of them. Ring wants to change that. The Ring Drone Cam uses a system called AI Pilot to learn the layout of your home. During the initial setup, you carry the drone through your space while it creates a digital floor plan. From there, the system identifies rooms and suggests places worth checking. For example, maybe you want to see whether a window is open. Or perhaps your security system detects movement near the back door. You can save those locations in the Ring app. Then, when you want a closer look, the drone can fly there automatically. You can also create customized routes and schedule patrols with a compatible Ring subscription.
The drone can work with connected Ring cameras and security sensors. If a linked device triggers an alert, Ring Drone Cam can automatically fly to a previously selected location to investigate. According to Ring's official announcement, AI Pilot combines cameras with onboard sensors and cloud processing to navigate around obstacles. So, instead of wondering what triggered an alert, you could actually see what's happening in another part of the house.
Ring Drone Cam records 2K video as it flies
The Ring Drone Cam weighs approximately 5.8 ounces and packs quite a bit of technology into its compact design. It records video at 1920 by 1920 resolution, which Ring describes as 2K. The camera has a 148-degree field of view and can rotate to capture a 360-degree view of a room. You can watch live footage through the Ring app. And if something happens at night, the drone has two built-in LED spotlights that illuminate dark areas. Unlike some traditional security cameras, it doesn't use infrared night vision. The drone also makes noise while flying. That's intentional. Ring says the audible propellers help people know when the camera is moving around.
The drone has no microphone or speaker, so you cannot hear what's happening or talk to someone through it. There's also no built-in siren. And although it can move independently, it doesn't stay airborne indefinitely. Ring advertises up to eight minutes of flight time on a fully charged battery under controlled conditions. When the flight ends or the battery runs low, the drone returns to its charging dock.
The man behind Ring has been working on this for years
If this concept sounds familiar, there's a good reason. Ring originally announced a flying indoor security camera called the Always Home Cam back in September 2020. It was an ambitious idea at the time. Instead of installing cameras throughout your house, you could use one device to check different rooms. Ring founder and chief inventor Jamie Siminoff explained the thinking behind that original concept. In his September 2020 announcement, Siminoff wrote: "We wanted to create one camera that could give users the flexibility of every viewpoint they want around the home." However, bringing that concept to a broad consumer launch took much longer than expected.
More than six years later, Ring is returning to the idea with a new product called Ring Drone Cam. This version uses AI to map your home, navigate around obstacles and fly to specific locations through the Ring app. In the company's November 2026 announcement, Siminoff explained what customers had been asking for. "For years, customers told us they wanted eyes in the places a fixed camera couldn't reach." The new drone reflects that feedback. Ring announced the original Always Home Cam at $249.99 in 2020. The new Ring Drone Cam carries that same advertised price in 2026. So, despite years of development and the addition of AI navigation, the announced price hasn't changed. That's pretty remarkable for a product with this kind of technology.

Can Ring Drone Cam really navigate around everything?
Ring says its AI Pilot system can recognize obstacles and adjust its flight path as it moves. However, certain surfaces can make navigation more difficult. According to Ring's official product information, highly reflective floors, large mirrors and transparent glass can interfere with navigation. Rooms with plain walls and few visual details may also create challenges.
The drone is limited to single-floor operation at launch, although Ring plans to support multiple floors in the future. It also requires Wi-Fi and a suitable location for its charging dock. Ring recommends an upload speed of at least 5 Mbps for optimal performance and 10 Mbps for 2K video quality.
What about privacy with a flying camera inside your house?
Ring says it has built several privacy protections into the device. Ring says the new Drone Cam only records while flying. When it returns to its docking station, the camera stops recording and the device powers down. A visible indicator and audible propellers help let people know when the drone is operating. The company also says recordings will receive protection through its new Throw Away the Key encryption system, known as TAKE. According to Ring's privacy documentation, the company retains video encryption keys for up to 24 hours to support enabled features. It then deletes its copies of those keys, leaving customers with control of the keys needed to access their recordings.
However, TAKE differs from traditional end-to-end encryption because it allows Ring to process video for certain connected features during that initial period. There's another consideration. AI Pilot uses cloud processing as part of its navigation system. So, anyone thinking about buying one should understand how Ring handles the home's digital map and what information gets processed remotely. And if you share your home with someone else, it's worth discussing whether they're comfortable with a flying camera operating indoors.
What Ring Drone Cam costs beyond the $250 price
Here's something else to keep in mind before deciding whether this little drone belongs in your home. The advertised price is $249.99. That includes the drone and its charging dock. However, some features require a compatible Ring subscription. For example, you need a subscription to schedule automatic patrols. You'll also need a compatible plan if you want to save recordings in the cloud and review them later.
Without a subscription, you can still use Live View to check what's happening. But if you want the drone to maintain a video history, there will be an additional recurring cost. Ring says eligible plans can provide up to 180 days of cloud video history, depending on the subscription and settings. And there's one more thing to consider. The drone relies on its battery for flight. With a maximum advertised flight time of eight minutes, you'll need to allow time for recharging between patrols. That makes it better suited for checking specific areas than watching your entire house continuously.

When can you buy a Ring Drone Cam?
Ring says the Drone Cam is scheduled to begin shipping in the U.S. on January 27, 2027. The announced price is $249.99. However, the company still needs authorization from the Federal Communications Commission.
So far, Ring says the device has not received the required FCC authorization and cannot legally be offered for sale or lease until that approval arrives. For now, you can sign up for product availability updates through Ring's official website at ring.com/products/drone-cam. And if you're wondering whether it will actually fly around your house as smoothly as it does in promotional videos, that's something we'll have to evaluate when the product becomes available.
