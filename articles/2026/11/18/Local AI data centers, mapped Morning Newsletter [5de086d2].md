The sheer number of AI data center plans, lawsuits, and controversies can blur together.
To help you get a handle on what’s happening, reporters Erin McCarthy and Frank Kummer made a handy annotated map of the proposals in our area.
Let’s dive in.
The big picture: There are currently three massive AI data centers under construction near Philly; two are nearly completed. Seven more have been proposed or approved. These developments are known as “hyperscale” data centers for their massive size and role in powering the AI boom.
The details: For each data center, McCarthy and Kummer break down all the major points and hot-button issues. That means: describing the site, which company will use the computer power generated, any environmental permits needed, what local utilities the facilities would use, and whether the project has the approvals needed to move forward.

When Sue Quackenbush visited a desolate, sandy road deep in New Jersey’s Pine Barrens earlier this year in search of her missing daughter, she felt overcome with terror.
On Saturday, when investigators discovered Danielle Lopez’s remains in the Pine Barrens two years after she went missing, Quackenbush told Inquirer reporter Jason Nark that some of her terror subsided.
Lopez, 37, was last seen walking alone on Lost Lane, a sandy road pocked with muddy puddles in the heart of the Pine Barrens, miles away from the campground where she had been staying with her boyfriend.
Law enforcement and Quackenbush did not share more details about the discovery or how it relates to their investigation.
“My soul’s purpose was always to bring Danielle home,” Quackenbush said. “Justice comes second.”
