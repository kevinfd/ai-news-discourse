# AI and Data Science Salaries in 2026: What Practitioners Are Actually Earning

KDnuggets · Vinod Chugani · 2026-11-18

AI and Data Science Salaries in 2026: What Practitioners Are Actually Earning
A data-backed breakdown of compensation across roles, experience levels, industries, and geographies, so you know where you stand and where the premium opportunities are.
Data science remains one of the best-compensated fields in tech, and 2026 has brought both clarity and complexity to compensation conversations. Salaries vary so dramatically across sources, roles, and regions that a single headline number like "data scientists earn \$125K" can either discourage practitioners earning far more or mislead those just starting out.
The honest picture is more nuanced. A junior analyst in a mid-market city earns something entirely different from a senior machine learning engineer at a frontier AI lab. A generative AI (GenAI) specialist commands a premium that simply didn't exist three years ago. Remote work has reshuffled the geographic salary map in ways that still aren't settled.
This guide draws on data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, Indeed, Built In, and job-posting analyses. The goal: give practitioners at every stage an accurate, role-specific picture of what the market is paying in 2026, whether you're evaluating an offer, preparing for a negotiation, or planning your next move.
Understanding the Data Science Salary Range
Before getting into specific figures, it helps to understand why salary sources often seem to contradict each other. Platforms differ in who they survey, whether they capture base pay only or total compensation (including bonuses and equity), and whether their data skews toward self-reported tech-hub salaries or broader national samples.
With that in mind, here's where the major sources land for data scientists in the United States in 2026:
- BLS (national median): \$112,590–\$120,230
- Indeed (average base salary): \$130,239
- Glassdoor (average total pay): approximately \$156,000
- Levels.fyi (median total compensation): \$185,000
The spread reflects the difference between all data scientists nationally versus those at companies that participate in high-transparency compensation platforms. For practitioners at established tech companies or in finance, Levels.fyi figures are more representative. For those in healthcare, government, or smaller organizations, BLS and Indeed provide a more grounded baseline.
A useful working range for most practitioners: \$120,000–\$160,000 in base salary, with total compensation reaching well above that at larger companies.
Salary by Experience Level
Experience is the single largest predictor of compensation in data science. The market has matured enough that the gap between entry-level and senior roles is now substantial and well-documented.
Entry Level (0–2 Years)
Base salaries typically run from \$84,000 to \$105,000. At big tech companies, entry-level total compensation can already clear \$130,000 once equity is included. The entry-level market is also more competitive than it was in 2021–2022, with more candidates chasing each opening.
Early Career (2–5 Years)
This is where practitioners see the most significant jumps. Base salaries move into the \$100,000–\$135,000 range. Those who've built expertise in production machine learning or GenAI implementation can reach the upper end quickly.
Mid-Level (5–8 Years)
Experienced practitioners with a track record of shipping models and influencing business decisions earn \$130,000–\$160,000 in base salary. At large tech companies, total compensation at this level often exceeds \$200,000.
Senior and Staff (8–12 Years)
Senior data scientists and staff-level practitioners at well-funded organizations earn \$160,000–\$230,000 in base pay. Senior machine learning engineers at FAANG companies regularly see total compensation of \$300,000–\$400,000 when stock vesting is factored in.
Principal, Director, and VP (15+ Years)
Directors of data science earn \$180,000–\$250,000+ in base salary, with total compensation at top-tier companies ranging from \$350,000 to \$500,000+. Quantitative finance roles at hedge funds can exceed \$400,000 in total compensation at this level.
Salary by Role: The AI Premium Is Real
The data science job family has expanded significantly. In 2026, practitioners aren't all competing in the same compensation band. Your specific role and skill set determine which band you're in.
Data Analyst
The most accessible entry point into the data field. Average base salary runs \$84,000–\$112,000, with tech-sector analysts reaching the upper range. Adding Python to a SQL and business intelligence foundation adds meaningful compensation lift.
Data Scientist
The broadest category. Base salaries average \$120,000–\$130,000 nationally, with significant upside based on industry and company type.
Data Engineer
Data engineers earn slightly more than generalist data scientists on average, around \$136,000 in base salary. That reflects demand for the people who build the pipelines data scientists depend on.
Machine Learning Engineer
One of the highest-paying roles in the data field. Base salary ranges from \$128,000 to \$186,000 across major salary sources, with Levels.fyi reporting median total compensation of approximately \$261,000. Senior machine learning engineers at FAANG companies regularly clear \$350,000+ in total pay.
AI/Machine Learning Engineer
Glassdoor reports median total compensation of \$180,837 for this title, with a typical range of \$148,000–\$225,000. Top earners hit \$271,000 at the 90th percentile.
GenAI and Large Language Model Engineer
The highest-ceiling specialization in 2026. Compensation for dedicated GenAI roles ranges from \$200,000 to \$400,000+, driven by a talent pool that's still shallow relative to demand. The role only emerged as a distinct category in late 2022, so the supply of experienced practitioners remains limited.
AI Research Scientist
Research scientists, typically requiring a PhD, earn \$180,000–\$280,000 in base salary. Total compensation at frontier AI labs can reach \$350,000–\$700,000 when equity is included.
Salary by Industry: Where You Work Matters as Much as What You Do
Two data scientists with identical skills can earn very different incomes depending on their employer's industry. The gap isn't small.
Quantitative finance and hedge funds: \$140,000–\$300,000 base, with significant bonus potential on top. This is the highest-paying industry for data scientists because a better model translates directly into trading profit.
Big tech (FAANG and frontier AI labs): \$150,000–\$200,000 base, with total compensation frequently exceeding \$300,000 when restricted stock units are included. The AI and machine learning sector is seeing salary growth of roughly 12% year-over-year, the highest of any technology discipline.
Healthcare and biotech: \$100,000–\$160,000. A growing sector for data science hiring, with demand accelerating as healthcare organizations build AI-powered diagnostics and clinical analytics tools.
Finance and banking (traditional): \$120,000–\$160,000. Stable compensation with consistent demand for practitioners who can work within regulatory constraints.
Retail and e-commerce: Data science salaries average around \$140,000, reflecting strong demand for demand forecasting and personalization work.
Energy: Around \$150,000 on average, driven by grid optimization and predictive maintenance applications.
Government: Lower base salaries than private-sector peers, but with strong benefits, pensions, and job stability.
Salary by Geography: Where Location Still Matters
Despite the expansion of remote work, geography still shapes data science compensation significantly.
San Francisco Bay Area / San Jose: The highest-paying metro in the country for data science. BLS data puts San Jose's location quotient at roughly 3.5, meaning data scientist employment is far more concentrated there than in the average U.S. labor market. Average salaries reach \$156,000–\$171,000 in base pay. San Francisco median base from job-posting data on company career pages sits at \$210,000.
Seattle: Home to Amazon and other major tech employers, Seattle sits roughly 10–15% above the national average.
New York: Strong finance and tech demand keeps New York at approximately \$125,000 in average base salary, with finance-specific roles pushing higher.
Washington, D.C. / Northern Virginia: BLS data puts this metro near the top nationally, ranging from \$96,000 to \$119,000 in the government-inclusive dataset. Private sector tech salaries in the area run higher.
California (statewide): A \$156,000 mean across the state, driven by the Bay Area and Silicon Valley.
Washington state: \$158,000 mean, among the highest in the country.
Lower-cost metros: Cities like Denver, Chicago, Raleigh, and Austin offer base salaries of \$100,000–\$120,000. Remote work changes this calculus in ways worth understanding.
The Remote Work Equation
Remote work has complicated the geography calculation in ways that benefit practitioners in lower-cost areas. The key distinction is between companies that use location-agnostic pay bands versus those that adjust compensation based on where an employee lives.
For the highest location-adjusted compensation, target companies headquartered in high-cost metros that offer remote work at national or San Francisco-level pay. Remote data scientists earn between \$110,000 and \$192,000 depending on the company's compensation philosophy, with fully remote roles at well-compensated companies paying comparably to in-office roles at the same employer.
Companies with location-based adjustments typically pay 10–15% less than equivalent in-office positions in high-cost metros. That discount on a Bay Area salary, though, often still exceeds what an in-office role in a lower-cost city would offer.
The Skills Premium: What Adds to Your Paycheck
Not all data science skills are compensated equally in 2026. The emergence of GenAI has created a measurable premium for specific technical capabilities.
Large language model and GenAI implementation: A 20–40% premium over base data scientist rates in the U.S. market. This is the most significant skill-based salary driver right now.
Production machine learning and MLOps: Data scientists who can build and deploy production machine learning systems command 20–30% above general data science salaries. That reflects the scarcity of professionals who combine statistical expertise with software engineering depth.
Python with cloud platforms (AWS, Azure, GCP): Python appears in over 80% of data scientist job postings and is now table stakes. Cloud platform experience adds meaningful lift on top.
Domain expertise in high-value verticals: Deep knowledge of fintech, healthcare AI, or supply chain can command a 10–25% premium over a comparably skilled generalist.
Graduate education: A master's degree adds \$10,000–\$25,000 in salary premium over a bachelor's. A PhD adds the most value in research lab environments or quantitative finance.
One pattern worth noting from a 2026 analysis of over 700 job postings: 73% of data scientist postings with AI requirements still use the standard "Data Scientist" title. AI proficiency is becoming an expectation rather than a specialty, though compensation data still reflects a premium for practitioners who can demonstrate it.
What the Market Is Telling Practitioners
A few broader trends from the 2026 salary data are worth factoring into career decisions.
The field is growing, not contracting. The BLS projects 33.5% employment growth for data scientists from 2024 to 2034, roughly nine times the average for all occupations, with approximately 23,400 annual job openings. The U.S. data analytics market is projected to reach \$232.9 billion by 2034, growing at a 25.7% annual rate. Demand is structural, not cyclical.
The entry-level market is selective. The long-term outlook is strong, but hiring in 2026 is more selective than during the 2021–2022 peak. Job-posting activity has returned close to pre-pandemic levels, but hiring momentum remains subdued in some knowledge-work categories. Entry-level candidates face real competition.
Mid-level and senior roles remain undersupplied. The BLS counts roughly 245,900 data scientists currently employed in the U.S., projected to rise to 328,300 by 2034. Experienced practitioners with production skills and business acumen are still in high demand.
Specialization is the fastest path to above-median compensation. Generalist data science skills are increasingly treated as baseline. Practitioners who specialize in GenAI, MLOps, quantitative methods, or a high-value domain see the clearest premiums.
Recommended Resources for Salary Research
Staying current on compensation means benchmarking across multiple sources, since any single platform reflects a specific population of respondents.
- Levels.fyi: Best for total compensation at tech companies, including equity breakdowns by level. The most transparent data for FAANG and mid-to-large tech.
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: Authoritative for national medians and long-term employment projections. Lags the real-time market by 12–18 months but provides the most statistically robust baseline.
- Glassdoor: Useful for company-specific salary data and total compensation estimates. Self-reported, so best for directional comparison rather than absolute benchmarks.
- Indeed: Good for base salary ranges from active job postings. Reflects the current hiring market rather than historical self-reports.
- Built In: Strong for startup and mid-size company data, with good total compensation breakdowns including equity and benefits.
- KORE1 AI Salary Guides: Placement-based data for machine learning and AI engineering roles specifically. More specialized than general job boards.
Final Thoughts
The 2026 data science compensation picture rewards specialization, production skills, and strategic industry positioning more than it did even three years ago. Base salaries across the field remain strong (solidly six figures for anyone past the entry level), but the gap between practitioners who've developed GenAI, MLOps, or domain-specific expertise and those who haven't is widening.
When benchmarking your own compensation, compare against your specific combination of role, experience level, industry, and geography rather than any single national average. A senior machine learning engineer at a hedge fund in New York and a mid-level data analyst at a healthcare company in Nashville operate in different compensation markets, even if both work in "data science."
The structural demand for data professionals isn't in question. BLS projections, the pace of AI adoption, and the volume of enterprise data that organizations are still struggling to extract value from all point to a field with sustained long-term relevance. The practitioners who capture the highest compensation will be those who keep their skills current, move deliberately toward higher-paying specializations, and negotiate with accurate, role-specific market data in hand.
