# With Jensen Huang gone, Colette Kress shows Nvidia is still at the center of AI

CNBC · Kif Leswing · 2026-11-18

Five days after Nvidia's Jensen Huang resigned from the company he co-founded 33 years ago under a cloud of scandal, a nervous base of shareholders eagerly awaited Colette Kress' first public appearance as interim CEO.
Following Nvidia's better-than-expected earnings report on Tuesday, Kress offered some relief to Wall Street, at least temporarily, by proclaiming on the call with analysts that customers are still fully onboard and that there's no slippage in demand. She said leadership from Nvidia's most important supplier, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, visited company offices this week.
"We've engaged with our customers, we've engaged with our suppliers, our partners, they're all 100% behind us," Kress said on the call. "Customers have expressed their support to the business outlook and helping us through that."
While Huang was a singular figure in artificial intelligence with a commanding presence across the globe, Nvidia is still at the center of the AI ecosystem. Customers ranging from hyperscalers and neoclouds to frontier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic remain in persistent need of its technology and capital.
Nvidia shares climbed almost 5% in extended trading on Tuesday after the chipmaker's better-than-expected earnings and strong guidance. That still leaves the stock in a deep slump after a 24% drop so far this month, driven by broad concerns about a slowdown in the AI buildout, a political handoff in Washington, D.C., and the turmoil at the top of the company.
Despite all those headwinds and what Nvidia called the "unexpected transition" in leadership, the business is still booming. Revenue more than doubled in the latest quarter to $115.84 billion, and Nvidia projects roughly 90% growth in the current period to about $130 billion.
More importantly, Kress said the 70% growth forecast for next year that was offered over the summer is actually too low. Now the projection is 75%. That's for a company that's seen revenue multiply by about 15-fold since the launch of ChatGPT in late 2022.
While Huang is gone, "demand did not leave with him," Yorkville Ives analyst Dan Ives said in a note after the report.
Kress maintained her optimistic tone in an interview with CNBC's Jim Cramer after the call.
"The most important thing that we have been seeing is continuing growth in terms of the demand that's out there," she said.
Looking to the future
Kress also said on the call that Nvidia's stepped-up buybacks, announced in September, are still on track, confirming that the board had added $150 billion to the company's authorization for share repurchases.
It's not all on Kress. The consistent message throughout the earnings call was that Nvidia is preparing for life after Huang.
Harvey Jones, the newly named chair of Nvidia's board, opened by saying that Huang had resigned after "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms," the first time the board had given a reason for his removal.
A day earlier, CNBC reported that Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Huang back as CEO.
Kress said on the call that it's "business as usual." In a statement, she said the company's roadmap is "on track," indicating that there haven't been any changes to future chips and systems currently in development. Nvidia said that the Vera Rubin AI system is off to an "extraordinary start," selling $20 billion in the latest quarter.
But Kress did signal a slight change to the company's willingness to backstop certain projects, and said Nvidia is being "disciplined" with how it's thinking about financing startups.
"There's a lot of discussion internally with a lot of the teams on what we think are the best ones that we want to do," Kress told Cramer. "We're going to continue down that path to make sure we choose the right investments for us to work through going forward,"
Plenty of big questions remain.
Nvidia said its quarterly filing would be published in the "coming weeks," whereas it normally lands on the same day as earnings. On the call, neither Kress nor Jones addressed an avalanche of investigations and hearings in Washington looking into allegations that Nvidia was boosting revenue by selling chips to companies that are passthroughs for buyers in China.
China only came up twice during the call. Kress said Nvidia had sold about $2.4 billion worth of approved data center products to China during the quarter, and that Nvidia had stopped selling licensed Hopper chips to China.
Kress also noted that new trade rules released by the U.S. Commerce Department will cut off Chinese access to renting Nvidia chips.
None of that is stunting growth.
"Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing. Our customers and our partners are with us and employees are committed," Kress said when signing off the call. "We've got this."
