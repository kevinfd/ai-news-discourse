# Jeff Bezos’s $41 billion AI startup Prometheus is shrouded in secrecy. We found out who’s building it

Fast Company · Rebecca Heilweil · 2026-11-18

For many in the tech industry, physical AI is the next world to conquer. While developers of large language models like OpenAI and Anthropic seek to push reasoning models distilled on billions of streams of text to their limits, an emerging crop of companies are focusing on an even more ambitious goal: developing AI programs designed to speak the language of physics. Should they succeed, these firms have a stake in everything from building models that might power self-driving manufacturing or enable drug discovery to those that can take on plane design and weapons development.
The promise is enormous. Whoever cracks physical AI could control a slice of the software stack that becomes critical to a host of industries—and worth many, many billions. Already, a growing list of companies are racing to stake their claim. Among the buzziest is Prometheus, a startup backed by Jeff Bezos that’s already flush with cash.
Bezos isn’t just bankrolling Prometheus. He’s also coleading the company, marking his first executive role since stepping down as Amazon’s CEO in 2021. His cofounder is Vik Bajaj, a Stanford professor who previously led science operations at Verily, Alphabet’s health technology venture. Prometheus was incubated in 2024 at Foresite Labs, which Bajaj leads, with support from the venture capital firm Arch Venture Partners. A number of Prometheus employees also appear to have come from firms associated with Bajaj.
By combing through LinkedIn and researchers’ personal websites, Fast Company identified 130 people who say they’re currently working for Prometheus, and assembled what appears to be the first public database of its kind. Prometheus employs approximately 250 people, including numerous accomplished AI researchers, some of whose names have not been publicly disclosed. The company is recruiting not only AI model developers but also experts in fields like aerospace and chemistry, a strong hint that it may one day target manufacturing and healthcare customers. Employees bring expertise in ocean engineering, energy engineering, mathematics, and even high-performance computing.
