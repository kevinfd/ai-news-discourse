# Ashton Kutcher on How Generative AI Will Transform Jobs and Society

Esquire · 2026-11-18

Ashton Kutcher on How Generative AI Will Transform Jobs and Society
Ashton Kutcher discusses generative AI and its ability to create original art and music by mimicking brain processes. He explains how AI will change job roles rather than simply eliminating them, helping people find careers that fit their skills and happiness. Kutcher also predicts an economic shift where kindness becomes more valued as intelligence gets commoditized by AI.
Transcript
- What do you see as the next big thing in tech? I think generative AI will be the next big thing in tech.
- [Announcer One] Just when you think artificial intelligence can't get more intelligent, it does.
- [Announcer Two] Generative AI.
- [Announcer Three] Generative AI.
- Things it can do are mind-boggling.
- Generative AI is a set of neural networks, somewhat similar to the way we think, or our brain thinks, that can produce original content, or seemingly original content, by ingesting copious amounts of data, synthesizing it, and then having an output relative to a query. Does that make sense? The example would be, if I asked an AI to generate a Norman Rockwell painting of Ashton Kutcher, it could do that. I can't search for a Norman Rockwell painting of Ashton Kutcher, because one does not exist. If I asked it to create a song about fashion, as if Will-I-Am wrote it, it could do that. (audience exclaiming)
- [Background Speaker] We're going to all need universal basic income basically, guys, because no one's going to have a job.
- Well, it still goes to similar function as software, where we're all concerned that, like, this is going to eliminate jobs. Somebody said to me, a long time ago, about software, you're either going to have a job telling computers what to do, or being told what to do by computers. And so we all get to decide, in our lives, which people we are going to be. AI will replace some jobs, but we'll also be able to use AI, and ask it, what job should I be doing? And the AI will be able to recommend the job that you're most qualified for, that you'll derive the most joy from, that will deliver you the most income. Likely, all of our jobs are going to change drastically, in the same way that our jobs have changed. If you were an accountant that used to write down, pen and paper, and everything, and balance a checkbook, your job has changed now, and become somewhat replaced by software. But if you were very good at your job, you probably are the person that tells the software what's right.
- [Background Speaker] But could AI at some point, like, become your therapist?
- Yes. I gave an AI a prompt yesterday, asking it, what are the seven things that I could do, that will create the most resilient children? And it gave me seven things that I can do, proactively, to create resilient children. They were all highly accurate, and correlative to a book that I read, on creating resilient children, like a summary, and to the point, the people that are going to get the most benefit out of AI are going to be the people that know how to ask the right questions. Then, in addition to that, I think we've went from the industrial age, and farming, where it was brawn and might that actually was the dominant force of an economy. Now, we've gone to the intelligence economy, where, if you're really smart, and you put things together, and you can pattern recognize very quickly, you become the dominant force in the economy. I believe that we are going to move to an economy of kindness, because intelligence will become commoditized by AI. Thus, the most valuable thing that you could be, as a human being, is kind.
