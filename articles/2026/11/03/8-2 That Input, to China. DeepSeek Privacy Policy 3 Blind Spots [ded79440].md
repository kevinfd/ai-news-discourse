# 8-2 That Input, to China. DeepSeek Privacy Policy: "3 Blind Spots"

note · 伊藤正章｜サバイバルDxクリエイター：荒野のPip-Boy設計局 · 2026-11-03

8-2 That Input, to China. DeepSeek Privacy Policy: "3 Blind Spots"
Introduction
Although a Japanese version of the DeepSeek policy has been published, the fact that data is stored in the People's Republic of China and is subject to Chinese law is a critically important premise for Japanese users.
(1) Points to note when checking "What is collected"
Information collected as confirmed in the DeepSeek policy
DeepSeek explicitly states that it collects information through three channels:
- Information provided by the user
- Information collected automatically
- Information from other sources
Specifically, these are as follows:
- Account information: Date of birth, username, email address or phone number, and password.
- User input (prompts): Any content provided to the model, including text input, voice input, prompts, uploaded files, photos, feedback, and chat history. While the policy states that it "does not extract or mine biometric patterns such as voice prints or facial recognition information from voice input or photos," the input content itself is widely subject to collection.
- Automatically collected information: IP address, unique device identifier, cookies, as well as device model, OS, system language, crash reports, performance logs, logs of features used and operations, and location information.
Points to note when checking
- The scope of "user input" is extremely broad. Because it can include not only chat text but also uploaded files, voice, and photos, it may not be suitable for business use or the input of confidential information.
- Automatically collected information extends to terminal, network, and location information. Even if cookies and device IDs do not seem like "personal information" at first glance, they can lead to the identification or tracking of an individual when combined with other information.
- The limitations of the statement that it "does not extract" voice or facial recognition information. Even if it does not extract them, voice data and photos themselves can be collected and stored, so one should not be overly reassured that "biometric information is not handled." In your answer, it would be good to be able to point out the "specific list for each collection channel," "the breadth of user input," "the traceability of automatically collected information," and "the limited nature of the description regarding biometric information."
(2) The significance of checking "What it is used for"
Purpose of use in the DeepSeek policy
The policy lists the operation, provision, development, and improvement of the service, service provision, management, maintenance, enforcement of terms, and ensuring safety, security, and stability (including the prevention and detection of unauthorized use, fraud, and illegal acts) as purposes of use. According to third-party evaluations, while user input and output are used for model development (training) by default, it is stated that training use can be refused by turning off the "Improve the model for everyone" setting.
The significance of verification for Japanese users
- Predictability
If you can grasp in advance that the prompts you enter and the files you upload may be used secondarily for DeepSeek's model improvement rather than just for generating answers, you can make informed decisions about what information is safe to input.
- Determining whether to opt-out
Knowing that you can change settings if you wish to avoid your data being used for training is important for maintaining control over your own information.
- Alleviating anxiety and triggering appropriate responses
If you understand the specific purpose of use, you can take actions such as "not entering confidential business information" or "minimizing the inclusion of personally identifiable information." In an answer, incorporating perspectives such as "the possibility of secondary use for model improvement," "the existence of opt-out settings," and "decision-making criteria for self-defensive behavior based on input content" will accurately demonstrate the significance of the judgment problem.
(3) How to evaluate "what is not written"
Things "not written or unclear" in the DeepSeek policy
In third-party evaluations, the following points are organized as not stated (Silent) or unclear.
- Data retention period: The policy only states that it will be retained for "as long as necessary," with no specific retention period, deletion timeline after account closure, or settings for short retention of chat logs.
- Details of deletion procedures and methods: There is no clear statement on when data will be deleted after account closure or a deletion request.
- Legal basis for cross-border transfer: While it states that "appropriate protective measures" will be taken, it does not specify the concrete legal mechanism (such as Standard Contractual Clauses).
- List of sub-processors or the existence of data processing agreements.
- Criteria for access by government and law enforcement agencies are also only partially described.
Evaluation
"What is not written" does not mean "there is no risk"; rather, it serves as material for risk assessment due to a lack of transparency. Especially for Japanese users, given the warnings from public institutions that data is stored in China and subject to Chinese law, the ambiguity regarding retention periods and deletion should be evaluated as a risk that "input information could remain indefinitely." Furthermore, if the legal basis for cross-border transfer is unclear, doubts remain as to whether sufficient explanation has been provided from the perspective of providing information when transferring to a third party in a foreign country as required by Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information.
Countermeasures
- Inquire about unclear points directly with the operator
(the privacy contact `privacy@deepseek.com` is provided).
- Refer to third-party evaluations or information provided by public institutions 
(you can use warnings from the Personal Information Protection Commission or privacy evaluations by third parties)
- Comparison with alternative means 
(comparing with services that have established service provision structures domestically and have clear data storage locations and applicable laws) is a possibility. In your answer, if you discuss from both evaluation and countermeasures following the flow of "omission = potential risk," "evaluation as lack of transparency," and "supplementation through inquiries, third-party evaluations, and public information," it will be a substantial answer worthy of 8 points.
Summary (When a Japanese person reads the DeepSeek policy)
- As for collection targets, account information, a wide range of user inputs including prompts, uploaded files, audio, and photos, as well as automatically collected information such as IP addresses, device IDs, cookies, and location information are mentioned, so be aware of the breadth of input content, the possibility of automatic tracking, and the limitations of the statement that biometric information is "not extracted."
- Regarding the purpose of use, in addition to service provision, management, and security assurance, there is secondary use for model improvement (training) by default, and since an opt-out setting exists, it is important to check the foreseeability of secondary use and the availability of opting out, and use this as a basis for judging self-defensive actions.
- On the other hand, retention periods, deletion timelines, legal grounds for cross-border transfers, and lists of sub-processors are unclear. Along with the fact that data is stored in China and Chinese law is applied, evaluate these omissions as risks and a lack of transparency, and supplement them through inquiries, third-party evaluations, public information, and comparisons with alternative services.
