# OpenAI’s ‘celebrity whisperer’ reveals his masterplan for turning Sam Altman into Hollywood’s new best friend

Page Six · Jane Herz · 2026-11-03

OpenAI’s ‘celebrity whisperer’ reveals his masterplan for turning Sam Altman into Hollywood’s new best friend
He conquered high society one schmooze at a time — and now, he’s setting his sights on billionaire OpenAI founder Sam Altman and the enigmatic world of AI.
Charles Porch, the man who has been dubbed as the “Hollywood whisperer” thanks to his incredible black book of high-profile pals, has made a name for himself by brokering connections between Hollywood’s biggest A-listers and some of the tech world’s biggest titans over the past decade.
He transformed Instagram into a coveted platform for exclusive press, pushed even the most private of stars to start sharing their lives online, and, in doing so, was credited with making the app synonymous with A-list intrigue. Now, he’s set his sights on a new tech frontier.
Porch, 45, who joined OpenAI as its Vice President of Global Creative Partnerships in March 2026, has now opened up to New York Magazine about his plans to sprinkle some of the same Hollywood magic on the tech titan as he aims to break down the barrier between the glitzy world of celebrities and the complex world of AI.
“I’m not a business-development guy or the brand guy. I’m the relationships person that’s bringing everyone along on new tech,” Porch told the outlet.
“There’s a strategy around making friends,” he added.
Porch’s new position at OpenAI became a major talking point this summer, when he was revealed as the mastermind behind an off-the-record, alfresco dinner that Gwyneth Paltrow was planning to hold at her Hamptons home in honor of Altman.
The scheduled August dinner made headlines around the world after details about it were leaked, as people slammed the affair as “tone-deaf” and branded Paltrow as a “technofascist.”
In fact, it sparked such controversy that the dinner was actually postponed, instead taking place in September at the home of another — unnamed — “AI enthusiast,” per NY Mag.
Porch was quick to downplay the furor surrounding Paltrow’s soiree, telling the outlet that the internet “made it such a bigger thing than it was.”
“Gwyneth invests in the space. She does these investor dinners all the time. Sam was coming through town and I was like, ‘Let’s just do this,'” Porch explained.
But these dinners are nothing out of the ordinary for Porch — in fact, they’re his entire playbook.
Over the years, he’s become the go-between person to unite socially awkward tech and business founders with glittering Hollywood A-listers. Since his early days working at Facebook, he’s been curating dinners, parties, and other high-end events to help bridge this gap.
In one night, he’s brought together Vogue’s Chloe Malle with TBPN president Dylan Abruscato and “Saturday Night Live” star Chloe Fineman. He noted during his interview that he also throws personal dinner parties for fun, in the hope that they can create connections for the future.
“This is what I’ve been trying to explain to folks: It’s not about deals; it’s about relationships,” Porch told the outlet, as he explained some of the genius behind his business strategy.
However, his new gig with OpenAI presented a few immediate hurdles that even a person as well-connected as Porch would face difficulty overcoming.
Unlike his new VP of global creative partnerships, Altman is incredibly private — and rarely shares information about his day-to-day dealings, let alone his private life. In fact, he only confirmed that he had wed his longtime partner, Oliver Mulherin, in 2024 after photographs from the ceremony began surfacing on social media.
To this day, few details are known about their life together.
But what may help Porch’s efforts to humanize Altman is his carefully-curated lineup of famous pals and his ability to make even a tech app feel like a private club everyone needs to be a part of.
“I think he’s kind of a seed planter. The rest of his team are the ones executing in inbox and doing some of the heavier lifting, but he is so good at making you feel like you want to be a part of something,” influencer Dylan Mulvaney told the outlet about Porch.
Indeed, Porch has been busy making connections since long before his days at Instagram.
In his early days, Porch was an intern at Warner Bros. Records along with Erin Foster, the daughter of musician David Foster. The next thing he knew, he found himself at star-studded events like Kate Hudson’s Halloween party.

“When I was bringing him into my world, which was more industry forward and had sort of celebrity access points, I found that people were always drawn to Charles,” Foster told the magazine, adding that people felt “safe around him” due to the fact that “he didn’t want anything from anyone.”
“He never came across as someone who was a user or someone who was going to speak out of turn or share private information.”
He’s a favorite of Anna Wintour’s, too, and has a close relationship with jewelry designer Jen Meyer.
Privately, Porch married philanthropist Robert Denning in 2025 in a luxurious, multi-day celebration in Paris, France. The wedding, which took place at the hotel Le Bristol, was attended by Barbara Bush, Carole Radziwill, and Harry Styles, just to name a few.
Porch grew up in Charleston, South Carolina, and spent his youth studying at an all-boys choir boarding school in New Jersey, before later attending McGill University.
After working at Warner Bros., he ran strategic relationships at Ning, a now-obsolete startup founded by Marc Andreessen. It was through that job that he met Randi Zuckerberg — Mark Zuckerberg’s sister — and landed a role at Facebook, later pivoting to Instagram when it was bought up by Zuckerberg’s Meta.
He began his post at OpenAI in March 2026, and now, he’s tasked with perhaps what is his most arduous job yet.
“What I keep coming back to is, I think, the usefulness of AI,” Porch explained to the magazine. “What it’s transforming for people is undeniable. And the more conversations I have, I just — I feel it coming, people being able to talk about it more.”
Porch compared AI usage to that of the early days of GLP-1 medications, explaining that many users are skeptical at first, and then hooked as soon as they use it.
“What I see every day is that people are externally anti-AI but completely in the closet on how they’re using it,” he said.
“It’s almost like they’re at an AI protest in the morning and then a meeting with me in the afternoon…”
