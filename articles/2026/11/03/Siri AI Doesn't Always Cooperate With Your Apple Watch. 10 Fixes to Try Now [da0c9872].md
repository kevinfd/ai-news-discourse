# Siri AI Doesn't Always Cooperate With Your Apple Watch. 10 Fixes to Try Now

PCMag · Lance Whitney · 2026-11-03

Apple's iOS 27 introduced the long-awaited Siri AI, and in my experience, the Apple Intelligence-powered assistant works relatively well on the iPhone, iPad, and Mac (albeit with some hiccups here and there). But on the Apple Watch? Not as much. I’ve encountered slow responses, failed requests, dictation errors, and more. If you own Apple's smartwatch with watchOS 27 and have also been frustrated by Siri AI, don’t give up. Here are some issues people have been facing, and my suggestions to make the AI work more reliably from your wrist.
1. Enable Voice Activation
Saying the wake word “Siri” or “Hey, Siri” to your watch should activate the voice assistant. If that’s not working, your first step should be to ensure voice activation is enabled, which you can do with the Watch app on your iPhone or directly on the watch.
On your iPhone, open the Watch app and select Siri > Listen for from the My Watch section. If this option is turned off, change it to “Hey Siri” or “Siri or Hey Siri” according to your preference. I chose “Siri or Hey Siri” so that I can use either phrase. After you do that, you may be prompted to train Siri on your voice by repeating some commands.
On your watch, go to Settings > Siri > Listen for and change the option to “Hey Siri” or “Siri or Hey Siri” if this option is turned off. Now try saying “Siri” or “Hey Siri” to your watch to see if Siri responds.
2. Press the Digital Crown
Even with voice activation turned on, Siri may not always respond the way you expect. If you have your iPhone, iPad, or Mac nearby, and these devices also have Siri voice activation enabled, saying the wake word may get a response from the wrong place.
To get past this conundrum, you can instead press the Digital Crown on your watch to summon Siri. Enable this by going to Settings in the Watch app or directly on your Apple Watch, select Siri and then turn on Press Digital Crown. Now, you can just press the Digital Crown on your watch when you want to talk with Siri, and the voice assistant should respond.
3. Raise Your Watch to Speak
Another way to get Siri to pay attention to you is to raise your wrist to your mouth and then just speak your request. With this option, you don’t even have to use a wake word to kick things off. To enable this in the Watch app on your iPhone or directly on your watch, head to Settings > Siri and then enable Raise to Speak. The next time you want to chat with Siri via your watch, just bring your wrist close to your mouth and then start speaking.
4. Find a Better Connection
While Apple tries to offer as much on-device processing as possible, it's not always possible for every query. That means your device will need an internet connection to receive responses to certain questions and requests. If you're in a dead zone, the AI might not work. If you're having problems and notice a poor internet connection, that may be the reason. You'll need to wait until you have a better connection to try again. It's worth noting that even if you have a cellular version of the watch, you may find that the AI works better over Wi-Fi.
5. Make Sure Siri Is Listening
Have you ever given Siri a long request or complicated question only to discover that the AI wasn’t listening or paying attention? Before kicking off a conversation with a lengthy topic, make sure Siri is awake and responsive. On the Apple Watch, you should see the Siri chat bubble on the screen. Even then, you may want to start off by simply saying hello. After Siri responds, you can then segue to the subject on your mind.
6. Break Up a Long Request Into Shorter Chunks
Even if Siri on your watch is listening, it may simply time out if you keep rambling on with a long request or question. For that reason, break up a lengthy request or question into shorter chunks. Remember that it’s a conversation. State your initial request, then let Siri respond. It may provide a helpful response or ask for more details. You can then chime back in with more information. You may need to go back and forth a few times, but at least Siri should stick around for the entire conversation.
7. Cancel a Request
Sometimes Siri on the Apple Watch just seems to hang with no signs of life. Is it stuck? Is it thinking about your question? You never know. But rather than waiting indefinitely for Siri, your best bet is to cancel the process and try again. To do that, press the Digital Crown to stop Siri and then press it again to restart.
8. Return to a Previous Conversation
Sometimes you want to view or resume a previous conversation with Siri on your watch, iPhone, iPad, or Mac. Your chats are synced, so you can access the same ones across all your supported Apple devices. To access past conversations on a watch, tap the Digital Crown to open the new dynamic app grid, which displays a small number of previously used apps. Tap the center icon, and you’re taken to your conversation history.
From here, swipe through the list and select the conversation you wish to view. You can then call on Siri by voice or press the Digital Crown to continue the conversation. Siri will then pick up where it left off.
9. View Your Conversation on the Screen
Siri may sometimes misunderstand what you say, resulting in an incorrect response or no response at all. One way to confirm that it understood you correctly is to see your own words and Siri’s response on the screen. To view the actual words of your conversation on your watch screen, go to Settings in the Watch app on your phone or on the watch itself. Select Siri > Siri Responses and enable Always Show Siri Captions and Always Show Speech. The next time you chat with Siri on your watch, you’ll see the words for both ends of the conversation.
10. Keep Your iPhone and Apple Watch Updated
Whatever faults or foibles that currently plague Siri on the Apple Watch will hopefully be addressed with future updates. For that reason, make sure you update iOS and watchOS to the latest versions. Go to Settings in the Watch app on your phone or directly on your watch. Select General > Software Updates, and then install the latest update.
