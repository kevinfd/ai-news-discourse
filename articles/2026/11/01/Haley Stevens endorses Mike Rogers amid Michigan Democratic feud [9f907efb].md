# Haley Stevens endorses Mike Rogers amid Michigan Democratic feud

Axios · Andrew Pantazi, Alex Isenstadt, Holly Otterbein · 2026-11-01

Rep. Haley Stevens (D-Mich.) made a stunning last-minute endorsement of Republican Mike Rogers on Sunday, two days before Michigan's closely watched Senate election . Why it matters: Her endorsement gives Rogers a striking photo op as his party tries to peel away Black and Jewish voters in a tight race that could determine control of the Senate . Driving the news: Stevens appeared Sunday alongside Rogers at Detroit's Perfecting Church, where she praised his support for Black-owned businesses. Stevens later posted an endorsement on X. In a video, she said Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed told her she was no longer welcome in his campaign. "And he's right, and that's why I am endorsing Mike Rogers for the United States Senate today," Stevens said. Stevens' appearance with Rogers was arranged at the last minute after the two spoke Sunday morning, per a person familiar. She had previously planned to attend a Detroit church service with El-Sayed and Rep. Jim Clyburn (D-S.C.). How it happened: The dispute escalated last week after Stevens' fiancé, Jason Turkish, announced he would vote for Rogers. Stevens demanded El-Sayed condemn an outside group's ad linking U.S. support for Israel to higher grocery prices and remove a campaign ad featuring Tucker Carlson. On Friday night, after El-Sayed's campaign said Stevens would not be welcome at his events, Rogers called Stevens and told her he would welcome her support, according to a person familiar with the conversations. The next day, El-Sayed explicitly rejected the outside ad's contents, prompting Stevens to publicly welcome his remarks. On Sunday morning, Stevens called Rogers back to say she would endorse him, the person said. She told Rogers she remained a Democrat and believed the party would still win a Senate majority. The intrigue: A video posted by Detroit journalist Sam Robinson showed Stevens entering the church and praising Rogers . Rep. Debbie Dingell (D-Mich.), who helped organize the original planned appearance with El-Sayed, told CNN she asked Stevens not to attend because the dispute had become distracting. Stevens narrowly lost the August Democratic primary to El-Sayed but endorsed him afterward. The other side: A person close to El-Sayed argued Stevens' endorsement could actually help him by reinforcing his anti-establishment appeal. "It's good for Abdul," the person said, portraying Stevens as a symbol of the Democratic establishment, Congress and support for the Israeli government. The endorsement "shows Abdul is the candidate of change and for the people of Michigan," the person added . What she's saying: Stevens told Politico that El-Sayed had made clear she wasn't welcome in his campaign. " Abdul has run a magnificent campaign," she told Politico's Adam Wren. "He just was clear that I wasn't a part of it. I mean, I respect him. I wish him well. He very well could win. It's a close race. I mean, I'm voting for the other guy, and maybe some others are too." What he's saying: President Trump welcomed Stevens' endorsement in a Truth Social post Sunday afternoon, calling it "BIG" and praising her "bravery." Trump said Stevens has a huge following and "virtually unparalleled" respect among Michigan's Black and Jewish voters. "Haley said what few people are willing to say," Trump wrote, calling the endorsement an act of loyalty to Michigan. Trump said at an Iowa rally later Sunday: "I'd love her to join the Republican Party. Actually, I think that could happen maybe." Flashback: In August, Trump called Stevens a "Radical Left Socialist" during the primary vote count. What we're watching: Michigan voters choose between Rogers and El-Sayed on Tuesday, with Democrats facing a crucial test of whether they can reunite their coalition.
