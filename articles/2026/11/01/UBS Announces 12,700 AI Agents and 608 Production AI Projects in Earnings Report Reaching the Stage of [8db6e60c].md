Major Swiss bank UBS announced in its third-quarter earnings report that "the number of personal AI agents used by employees daily has exceeded 12,700."
There are 608 AI projects currently in production, an 81% increase year-over-year.

It would be a waste to just dismiss this news as "they're a major bank, so they have a different budget."
What caught my attention wasn't the size of the numbers.
It's that in the most formal setting of an earnings report, they are counting and reporting AI by "number of projects" and "number of agents."
And that these agents are not just one per company, but are being distributed to each individual employee.
UBS's 12,700 AI agents and 608 projects indicate the stage of distribution to every employee
Let me start with the conclusion.
AI in large companies has moved past the stage of "experiments secretly tried by some departments."
We have now entered the stage where "each employee has an agent tailored to their own work."
In the earnings announcement UBS released on October 28, 2026, the AI figures were listed together.
Writing them out as they appeared in the official announcement, it looks like this.
- 9 large-scale end-to-end AI transformation initiatives underway
- 608 AI use cases in production (up 81% year-over-year)
- Over 1,090 applications in development
- Over 12,700 AI personal agents used by employees in daily operations
- Over 18,000 developers using AI tools in software development
- Over 30 million insights generated by the internal tool "STAAT" for advisors year-to-date
This part is seriously important.
9 large-scale initiatives and 12,700 personal agents are running simultaneously.
Large-scale transformations decided from the top, and small agents used by each person on the front lines.
I see the fact that they are running these two things side-by-side as the biggest takeaway here.
Let's look at the numbers just a little more.
There are over 18,000 developers using AI tools in software development, and the internal tool called STAAT has produced over 30 million insights year-to-date.
In other words, AI is not a "special project," but is integrated into the flow of daily work.
Developers open AI when writing code, and advisors open it before talking to customers.
This state of being "integrated into the daily flow" is exactly the form we want to aim for in our own work.
AI in large companies has gone from departmental experiments to tools for every individual employee.
Counting AI in earnings reports: The meaning of 608 projects and an 81% year-over-year increase
There is one more point I found interesting.
It is that they are counting AI by "number of projects" and comparing it to the previous year in their earnings report.
In the earnings presentation for the third quarter of the previous year, 2025, it was explained that there were 340 AI projects in production.
This time, it is 608.
That is about 1.8 times more.
To be honest, I believe this is where the biggest difference in AI utilization lies.
Many companies say, "We use AI too."
But when asked, "How many are running in production?", not many companies can answer with a number immediately.
If you don't count them, you won't know if you've grown or stalled.
If you don't know, you can't decide what to entrust to AI next.
Conversely, if you just count them, the number 608 itself becomes a map for finding the "next one."
Counting isn't difficult.
For example, just counting "how many types of tasks were completed last month without human intervention thanks to AI" is enough.
The number of types and how many times they were used in a month.
If you know these two things, you can compare them to the previous month.
The moment you can compare them, AI utilization changes from a matter of feeling to a matter of work that can be improved.
This also connects to the concept of "remaining lives" in the Uniko Constitution.
There is a limit to the number of decisions a person can make in a day.
Worrying from scratch every time about "what should I entrust next" is a way of wasting those remaining lives.
If you have a list, the number of times you have to worry decreases.
To add one more thing, numbers also speed up internal conversations.
Discussions like "Is AI really useful?" tend to become a clash of opinions without numbers.
But if you can say, "We entrusted 12 tasks last month, and it became 15 this month," the conversation moves to "which three should we do next?"
When you have numbers, the discussion changes from "whether to do it" to "which one is next."
I believe this is the greatest benefit of counting.
On the other hand, I should also note what has not been disclosed.
How many of the 608 projects are for customers versus internal use?
What kind of mechanism is used to manage the 12,700 agents?
These details are not in the announcement, so I will not speculate in this article.
With AI utilization that isn't counted, you don't even know if it's growing.

The flip side of the story from the five major US banks the day before: Stop external AI, distribute it internally
In yesterday's article, I wrote about how five major US banks have started blocking logins to accounts by third-party AI agents.
I feel that this UBS story is looking at the same landscape from the exact opposite side.
Banks are cautious about "AI of unknown origin" coming from the outside.
This is because it is problematic for an AI that has been entrusted with a customer's password to act on its own.
On the other hand, they are distributing agents to each employee within the company.
At first glance, it seems contradictory.
But it makes sense.
What they are stopping is "unmanageable AI," and what they are distributing is "AI that can be managed within the company."
Regarding personal agents, it is reported that employees adjust and use them according to their own work flows.
While each individual can change the form to suit their own work, it operates on the company's internal system.
The point is that the scope of free use and the scope visible to the company overlap.
Even in a small company, this way of thinking can be used as is.
Instead of employees using AI on their own personal accounts, the company decides and distributes, "You may use this AI for this job."
Just by doing that, the amount of invisible AI decreases significantly.
What we can learn from this is that it is not a binary choice of whether to use AI or not.
The important thing is to think about the following three points separately.
- Whose account is the AI running on?
- What job is the AI being used for?
- How many are running (are they being counted)?
AI for which these three things can be stated can be distributed.
For AI that cannot, first stop it and take inventory.
What banks are doing is just drawing this obvious line on a large scale.
Stopping AI and distributing AI are two sides of the same management coin.
Applying the number 12,700 to your own work
Let's play with the numbers for a moment. 
UBS announced that as of September 30, 2026, they have 97,593 full-time equivalent employees. 
Simply dividing the 12,700 agents means there is about one agent for every eight employees.
Of course, this is just a simple division, as one person might have multiple agents. 
Even so, it shows that 'everyone at a large company is already mastering AI agents' is not the case. 
In other words, we are still in the middle of the process. 
That is precisely why there is room for individuals and small companies to catch up.
What I recommend is not waiting for a large-scale AI implementation across the entire company. 
Choose one of your routine tasks and carve it out into an agent for yourself. 
Increase them one by one and keep count. 
UBS's 608 projects are, after all, just the accumulation of one project at a time.
Here, let me talk about the planning side and the ToDo side of the Unico Constitution. 
The side that decides 'what to do' and the side that does the hands-on work to 'how to do it.' 
When you carve out your routine tasks into agents, the time you spent doing the hands-on work is freed up. 
Use that freed-up time to decide 'what to delegate next.' 
If you repeat this, your work will gradually shift toward the planning side. 
While you wait for the company to distribute AI, not a single step of this accumulation will progress. 
That is why I believe it is better to start from your own desk rather than waiting.
For example, these are tasks that are easy to carve out.
- Report memos that are compiled in the same format every week
- Summarizing documents read from a fixed perspective
- Initial sorting of inquiries
- Preliminary research on information gathered before meetings
The point is not to build it big from the start. 
One agent for one task. 
Once it works, add one line to your ledger. 
Just by doing this, in half a year, you will be able to say, 'We have this many tasks delegated to AI.'

Create a ledger to count the tasks you have delegated
Finally, I will write about the most modest and most effective thing. 
It is to create a single ledger of tasks delegated to AI.
You don't need a fancy system. 
It is enough to just list the following items on the first sheet of a spreadsheet.
- Name of the delegated task
- AI or tools being used
- Whose account is it running on?
- When should a human verify it?
- The start date and the procedure for stopping it
Having this leads to two benefits. 
First, you can see what work to delegate next. 
When you list them out, you can notice, "Similar tasks to this are still being done manually."
Second, you can stop them immediately when you want to. 
As with the bank story from the previous day, the day will surely come when external circumstances change and you need to rethink how you use AI. 
When that happens, if you have a ledger, you won't be confused about which ones to stop.
It is enough to review the ledger just once a month. 
You only need to look at three things. 
Stop tasks that weren't used last month. 
For tasks that were used frequently, think about whether you can expand them to similar work. 
For tasks where verification was missed, decide again on the timing for human review. 
Stopping is just as important as increasing. 
If unused agents remain, you will lose track of which ones are actually running again.
