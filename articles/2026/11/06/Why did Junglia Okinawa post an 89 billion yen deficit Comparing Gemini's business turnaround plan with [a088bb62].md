# Why did Junglia Okinawa post an 89 billion yen deficit? Comparing Gemini's business turnaround plan with ChatGPT and DeepSeek

note · シアルフィ · 2026-11-06

Why did Junglia Okinawa post an 89 billion yen deficit? Comparing Gemini's business turnaround plan with ChatGPT and DeepSeek
Japan Entertainment, which operates the theme park "Junglia Okinawa" that opened in July 2025, reported a final deficit of 8.9 billion yen for the fiscal year ending June 2026. Due to worsening cash flow, it appears they are also looking for new investors.
The total project cost for Junglia was approximately 70 billion yen. Is it possible to turn around a facility that has received such a massive investment?
When we asked AI to evaluate the potential for business revitalization, Gemini responded that "if the past investment and debt can be separated and the facility acquired at a lower cost, there is a possibility of reconstruction."
On the other hand, ChatGPT and DeepSeek point out that there are significant hurdles to actual reconstruction, such as the time required for debt restructuring and negotiations with financial institutions.
In this article, we will compare the responses from three AIs—Gemini, ChatGPT, and DeepSeek—to consider whether a business turnaround for Junglia Okinawa is truly possible.
Junglia Okinawa's 8.9 billion yen deficit and missed visitor targets
On October 30, 2026, Japan Entertainment, the operator of "Junglia Okinawa," held a press conference to explain the financial results for the first year of operation and the current state of management.
As a result, the operating company reported a final deficit of 8.9 billion yen and appears to be seeking new investors.
*Note: While the parent company, Japan Entertainment Holdings, also reported a final deficit of approximately 17.3 billion yen, this article focuses on the 8.9 billion yen deficit of the theme park operating company.
First, let's have the AI organize the reasons for the deficit.
Three reasons for Junglia Okinawa's deficit
Junglia's deficit is not simply a case of "it's in the red because it just opened." In addition to failing to achieve the initially anticipated ability to attract customers, it seems the business structure was not set up to generate profit even with low attendance.
Far from Naha, it could not be incorporated into the Okinawa sightseeing route
First, an important point: it seems it takes two hours one way from Naha City, the center of Okinawa. It is not a place you can visit easily. I don't know the local situation, but I think it was not possible to create a flow that allows for easy drop-ins.
There was a gap between pre-opening expectations and the actual experience
Furthermore, while there was extensive promotion at the time of the opening in 2025, it seems that visitors did not get the satisfaction they expected. Although it is not possible to directly quantify the feelings of visitors, it appears to be reflected in figures such as reviews and the number of repeat customers.
Initial investment of 70 billion yen and heavy fixed costs put pressure on management
Junglia is said to have a total project cost of about 70 billion yen, and there are many costs that arise because it is a large theme park. It seems to be a structure with high fixed costs where it is difficult to retain profits.
Will new investors appear for Junglia Okinawa?
So, is it possible to attract new investors while Junglia is in the red? If Gemini were an investment candidate, how would it evaluate Junglia?
Business revitalization is not bound by past initial investment amounts
When a new sponsor invests in an existing operating company, it is also necessary to consider past borrowings and liabilities. On the other hand, if a company separate from the existing one can be established and the facilities and business can be bought cheaply, it may be possible to restructure by separating past investment amounts and liabilities.
In other words, what is important for a new sponsor is not the fact that it cost 70 billion yen in the past, but how much the business can be acquired for, and how much profit it can generate in the future.
Even if the initial cost was 70 billion yen, if the business can be acquired for 10 billion yen, it is calculated that it can be recovered in 5 years if it generates 2 billion yen in profit per year.
If Gemini were to take control of management, what kind of restructuring plan would it create?
Gemini's business restructuring plan is cost reduction and facility efficiency
The policy of structural reform itself seems decent at first glance. However, can it be trusted as is?
Let's have ChatGPT and DeepSeek read Gemini's response.
ChatGPT points to issues with debt forgiveness by financial institutions and land ownership
ChatGPT first responded that Gemini's answer is logical as a method for corporate restructuring and business acquisition.
※The above is a direct transcription of the AI's response.
※Regarding the land lease agreement for Junglia, it may be necessary to transfer contractual status or obtain the lessor's consent.
The operating company anticipates approximately 638,000 visitors in the second year and a final deficit of approximately 1.36 billion yen. However, this is the current outlook, and they aim to advance structural reforms by next spring to create a system that can secure a profit even at this level of attendance.
Therefore, it cannot be said that Gemini's plan for profitability is completely detached from reality. However, whether it can truly become profitable through structural reform still requires verification.
So, what does DeepSeek think?
DeepSeek points to the negotiation costs and time with creditors
DeepSeek also reaches the same conclusion as ChatGPT, although the wording is different.
A point different from ChatGPT's response is that DeepSeek mentions the negotiation costs and time to 'not assume debt.' It states that it usually takes several months to over a year, requiring coordination among creditors and interest adjustment with old shareholders.
If it takes over a year just for debt forgiveness negotiations, the reconstruction of Junglia will move further and further away.
※Note that 'over a year just for debt forgiveness negotiations' is DeepSeek's subjective view. The time required for debt restructuring varies depending on the number of creditors, collateral relationships, methods of business transfer, and the agreement status of financial institutions.
Summary: Business turnaround for Junglia Okinawa is difficult with general theories alone
The method answered by Gemini is correct as a 'general theory of business turnaround schemes,' but it seems to be content that underestimates the time actually required for negotiations and the feasibility of debt reduction.
Furthermore, while the current management has projected losses for next year as well, ChatGPT and DeepSeek are more skeptical than Gemini's response, which suggests that new management could achieve profitability quickly.
In this instance, I also believe the opinions of ChatGPT and DeepSeek are correct. It is interesting how the optimism or pessimism of the outlook changes depending on the AI.
