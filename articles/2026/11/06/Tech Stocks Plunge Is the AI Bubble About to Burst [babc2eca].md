# Tech Stocks Plunge: Is the AI Bubble About to Burst?

Bluewin · Redaktion blue News, Keystone-SDA · 2026-11-06

Tech Stocks in Free Fall
Is the AI bubble about to burst? Here's what you need to know right now
Fears of stricter regulation of artificial intelligence have triggered a global sell-off of technology stocks on the stock markets.
Keystone
Just two days after the midterm elections, tech stocks in the U.S. are plummeting, and stock markets worldwide are following suit. The post-midterm recovery rally is short-lived. Why are tech stocks now on a downward spiral?
Here's what it's all about
- Fears of stricter AI regulations are triggering a massive sell-off of tech stocks on the stock markets.
- Chip companies, in particular, are losing billions, and Swiss suppliers are also coming under pressure.
- Experts do not see the AI bubble as having burst, but they warn of further price volatility and an uncertain year-end rally.
Euphoria gives way to panic: Fears of increased regulation of artificial intelligence (AI) have triggered a sell-off in tech stocks on Wall Street. Two days after the Democrats’ success in the U.S. congressional elections—which initially sent stock markets higher—stocks in the chip industry, considered the biggest beneficiaries of the AI hype, plummeted.
On Thursday, the Nasdaq 100 tech index suffered its biggest single-day loss since the 2020 COVID-19 crash, plummeting to April levels and losing about 2.3 trillion dollars (approx. 1.88 bio francs) in market value. The sell-off swept through stock markets worldwide, including the German stock market, which continued to decline on Friday. Is the AI boom over now? And should retail investors be worried about their savings?
Why are tech stocks under so much pressure?
Following the U.S. Democrats’ landslide victory in the congressional elections, financial markets are on edge. This is because the Democrats are more skeptical about the potential of AI than the Republicans, who have traditionally advocated for the reduction of government regulations. Tech companies must brace themselves for more headwinds regarding their massive AI data centers.
Even during the election campaign, opposition began to form against the construction of ever-more data centers to fuel the AI boom. Even Republicans imposed moratoriums on construction. Data centers have come under criticism for their immense electricity consumption, which could also drive up energy prices for the public. Added to this are concerns about noise, air pollution, and water shortages, as well as fears that data centers create only a few permanent jobs.
What would stricter regulation mean for the AI boom?
Data centers are an essential component of AI infrastructure and consist of numerous standardized metal racks containing semiconductors. If investment in data centers were to slow down or even decline, it would have significant consequences for chip manufacturers.
As a result, such companies on Wall Street were hit particularly hard. Nvidia shares plummeted 9.3 percent on Thursday; major players in the storage sector, such as Seagate, Western Digital, and SanDisk, even plunged by as much as 22 percent. Their products are indispensable for data centers. Micron’s stock lost 17.5 percent. Fears of stricter regulation also hit the business empire of tech billionaire Elon Musk: Tesla stock fell by nearly 11 percent, and shares of the space and AI company SpaceX dropped by 9 percent.
Are Swiss stocks also affected?
Swiss stocks would also be directly affected. For example, stocks such as ABB and the chip supplier VAT had recently benefited greatly from the AI boom. On Friday morning, they rebounded somewhat or stabilized. However, they are still trading at comparatively high levels.
Is the AI bubble about to burst?
There have long been concerns that the massive, billion-dollar investments by U.S. tech giants are not translating into profits as quickly as hoped. Fears that an AI bubble will burst have been circulating on the stock market for more than a year—yet stock prices have continued to rise. On top of that, the stocks of the “Magnificent Seven”—including Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Alphabet—carry significant weight on global stock markets. The boom in tech stocks has long been the main driver of the stock market rally, despite all the wars and crises.
"On Wall Street, exactly what investors have been fearing for months has come to pass: that the concentration risk stemming from a few tech heavyweights will come back to haunt the market and drag the major indices down with it should they experience a sharp decline," commented market analyst Jochen Stanzl of Consorsbank.
Is the success of AI companies fundamentally in question?
Industry experts and economists overwhelmingly disagree. The current crisis in the stock markets is not seen as a failure of the technology itself, but rather as a painful correction of exaggerated market expectations. The fundamental benefits of AI in automation, data analysis, medical research, and software development remain undisputed, and demand among companies for practical applications continues to grow.
Rather, it is the timeline within which the massive investments in hardware and data centers must pay for themselves that is in question. While stock prices had recently priced in nearly flawless, exponential profit growth, companies are now being caught up by the reality of infrastructure bottlenecks and political hurdles. This does not fundamentally undermine the business model, but the phase of modest growth and investors’ blind faith in every AI announcement is giving way to a phase of sober cost-benefit analyses.
Is my ETF portfolio at risk?
For individual investors, the price slide is particularly noticeable because major global indices such as the MSCI World have become heavily weighted toward technology stocks in recent years. Due to the enormous market capitalization of Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft, and others, top tech stocks have recently often accounted for more than 20 to 25 percent of a standard global equity ETF. Many retail investors are seeing the consequences of the tech slump directly in their portfolios.
According to consumer advocates and asset managers, anyone who has been saving for retirement through a broadly diversified portfolio over many years should nevertheless remain calm. Fluctuations of 10 to 20 percent are within the normal range of volatility in the stock markets, and for ongoing savings plans, a dip even provides an opportunity to buy shares at temporarily lower prices.
However, investors who have specifically focused on sector ETFs in the semiconductor or AI sectors must be prepared for continued high price volatility and should assess whether their portfolio is sufficiently balanced with other sectors such as industrials, consumer goods, or healthcare.
So what about the year-end rally on the stock market?
Hopes for a carefree year-end rally have been dealt a severe blow by the tech shock. After the DAX initially hit a record high on hopes of a more lenient stance from the U.S. government, Germany’s leading index turned negative. “The election euphoria has given way to disillusionment,” said Salah-Eddine Bouhmidi of the brokerage IG. Although November and December are historically considered strong months for the financial markets, a noticeable recovery around the turn of the year would require rapid signs that the crisis is over.
According to market observers, for prices to rebound, reassuring statements from U.S. policymakers regarding future regulatory plans or surprisingly robust economic and quarterly data would be necessary. If, on the other hand, political uncertainty remains high and major investors hold back on new investments, the traditional year-end rally could fall through and give way to a period of heightened nervousness—especially since there are plenty of other concerns, such as the Middle East conflict, inflation, and high interest rates.
