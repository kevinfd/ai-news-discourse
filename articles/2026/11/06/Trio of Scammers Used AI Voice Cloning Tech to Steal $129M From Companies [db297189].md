# Trio of Scammers Used AI Voice Cloning Tech to Steal $129M From Companies

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-06

A group of scammers exploited AI voice-cloning software to steal over $129 million from companies across the globe.
Starting in 2024, the trio of suspects abused the technology to impersonate corporate executives and attorneys at major law firms to trick victim companies into wiring them funds, according to federal investigators.
“They would explain that they needed an employee’s assistance on a confidential financial transaction,” the Justice Department said. But in reality, the suspects used AI programs to mimic a corporate executive's voice and even deepfake their physical appearance during calls with targeted employees.
Security experts have long warned that AI voice and face cloning could be easily abused to enhance impersonation scams. And Thursday’s DOJ announcement shows they are extremely lucrative. The trio in question scammed $4.8 million from a hospital system in Missouri, nearly $2.7 million from a bank in Rhode Island, and $11.5 million from a UK financial tech company.
The first suspect, 41-year-old Jacon Soall of Spain, would impersonate attorneys from the UK. The other two suspects, 54-year-old Sharon Hazan of Israel and 42-year-old Guy Sheetrit of Israel and the United Arab Emirates, mainly focused on laundering the funds.
The group and their unnamed co-conspirators convinced victims to wire funds to bank accounts all over the globe. The money was then laundered by converting it into cryptocurrency.
The 15-page unsealed indictment adds that the group typically targeted employees in accounting roles who had the authority to initiate large wire transfers. “Once the defendants and their co-conspirators identified a Victim Employee, they typically initiated the fraud by sending the Victim Employee an email through an electronic signature company such as DocuSign,” the court document says. The emails would pretend to come from a company's CEO and claim assistance was needed to complete a confidential financial transaction, such as an acquisition.
The targeted employee would also be asked to sign a nondisclosure agreement. Using the AI programs, the group would then impersonate fake attorneys to further urge the targeted employee to “keep the transaction secret from his or her fellow employees,” the indictment says.
Investigators in Europe arrested each suspect earlier this year in Spain and the UK. The Justice Department has filed for the extradition of all three back to the US. The suspects now face up to 104 years in prison if found guilty of all the charges, which include aggravated identity theft, wire fraud, and money laundering.
