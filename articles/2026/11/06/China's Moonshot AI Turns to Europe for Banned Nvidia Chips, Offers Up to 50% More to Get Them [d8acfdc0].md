# China's Moonshot AI Turns to Europe for Banned Nvidia Chips, Offers Up to 50% More to Get Them

International Business Times Singapore · Bhaswati Guha Majumder · 2026-11-06

Chinese AI startup Moonshot AI is exploring deals to lease access to more than 8,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs through European data centres, highlighting how Chinese AI developers are finding ways around US export controls. The Kimi model maker is reportedly willing to pay 30% to 50% more upfront than non-Chinese customers to secure access to the restricted computing power.
People familiar with the matter told CNBC that discussions took place this year for Moonshot to rent computing capacity from neocloud operators hosting Nvidia graphics processing units (GPUs) at facilities in Europe.
The arrangements could give the Chinese AI lab access to advanced chips that Washington has effectively prohibited from being imported into China.
The development highlights a growing loophole in U.S. restrictions. While Washington can restrict the physical shipment of advanced Nvidia chips to China, Chinese companies can potentially access the same hardware remotely through overseas cloud infrastructure.
Moonshot Targets European Data Centres for Blackwell Access
Three sources told CNBC that Moonshot was looking to lease computing capacity powered by Nvidia's Blackwell systems.
Discussions reportedly included clusters containing 8,000 or more Blackwell GPUs, with one source describing the Chinese AI lab as "very aggressive" in its pursuit of additional computing power.
Two sources said Moonshot was particularly interested in European GPU clusters for inference, which involves running trained AI models to serve users. One source reportedly indicated that the company was interested in securing computing capacity for model training.
The latest discussions follow an earlier controversy involving Moonshot's access to restricted Nvidia hardware. In July, the White House said the Chinese AI lab had accessed Nvidia Blackwell chips through a facility in Thailand.
Discussions about securing computing capacity in Europe had been taking place since the White House raised the Thailand allegations.
Moonshot, Nvidia, the White House, the U.S. Department of Commerce, China's embassy in the UK and its Ministry of Foreign Affairs did not respond to requests for comment.
30%-50% Upfront Premium Could Secure Access
Securing access to advanced AI computing infrastructure has become increasingly expensive as demand for Nvidia GPUs continues to grow. Neocloud operators typically lease computing capacity to customers, known as offtakers, who pay fees and an upfront amount to secure their contracts.
It was also reported that Moonshot had been proposed to pay 30% to 50% more upfront than a non-Chinese customer would pay for comparable computing capacity.
The higher upfront payments could make Chinese customers more attractive to European cloud operators and financing companies seeking to protect themselves against the possibility of future restrictions on remote access arrangements.
Moonshot's push for additional computing power comes as Chinese AI models gain traction globally. Founded in Beijing in 2023, the company released a new model in July that it said had narrowed the gap with leading systems from OpenAI and Anthropic.
The launch of its Kimi K3 model also exposed the company's computing constraints. Moonshot said demand had stretched its available capacity and temporarily paused new subscriptions to prioritise existing members.
"We're adding capacity as fast as we can and will reopen new subscription spots in batches," the company said in a LinkedIn post.
US Lawmakers Seek to Close the Cloud Computing Loophole
The growing use of overseas data centres to access restricted American chips is increasing pressure on Washington to expand its export controls.
The US House of Representatives passed the bipartisan Remote Access Security Act (RASA) in January. The proposed legislation seeks to extend export restrictions to cloud-based access to critical hardware and software. However, it has yet to pass the Senate.
The US House Select Committee on China also criticised the arrangements, calling for Congress to close the loophole.
It said: "Chinese AI labs are leasing advanced chips from U.S. and European companies, circumventing U.S. export controls. Congress should pass the bipartisan Remote Access Security Act to close this dangerous loophole and win the AI race against China. Allowing China access to the best chips in the world undermines the security of America and Europe and must be stopped."
Meanwhile, Nvidia has reportedly told cloud providers that at least 51% of their services must go to non-Chinese customers, according to the Financial Times.
Defending the importance of its global infrastructure, an Nvidia spokesperson said: "America cannot win the AI race unless America's AI infrastructure wins worldwide - if the world's clouds rely on foreign-made chips, the model and application layers will be foreign-built as well."
Nvidia also said in August that Washington had licensed some sales of its older H200 chips, but "such sales were restricted" by the Chinese government, which has sought to strengthen domestic chipmakers.
With Chinese AI developers gaining ground against American competitors, Moonshot's reported European expansion highlights a growing challenge for Washington. Restricting where advanced chips can be shipped may not be enough if companies can still rent computing power from abroad.
