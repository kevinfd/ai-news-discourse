# Apple will monitor your public iCloud links now, starting with iOS 27.3

ZDNet · Taylor Clemons · 2026-11-06

ZDNET’s key takeaways
- Starting with iOS 27.3, Apple will monitor public links shared via iCloud.
- Only iCloud links marked “Anyone with the Link” are affected.
- Apple may ban users’ accounts who violate its “harmful content” policy.
Be careful what you share in your iCloud. Apple announced it will start monitoring public iCloud links for harmful content — and potentially disable accounts that violate company policy.

What is Apple looking for?
If you try to upload harmful content, Apple says it reserves the right to “move, refuse, modify, remove, or delete” your content at any time. Furthermore, Apple outlines possible actions it may take, including:
- Blocking or disabling your account
- Blocking the ability to create a new account with the associated email address or phone number.
- Involving law enforcement
Apple’s announcement did not explain what “harmful content” would mean in practice. While there are obvious violations like drug sales and child sex abuse material, the new policy’s vagueness is concerning.
Limited access links are still safe
Once an iCloud link is shared under the “Anyone with a link” designation, end-to-end encryption is removed, even if either party has enabled Advanced Data Protection, which allows Apple to view the link’s content.
If you select “People you choose,” which limits access to users who have been invited or approved, the encryption remains in place, and Apple won’t be able to see what you’re sharing.
The new policy does not lay out an appeals process if you think your account or content has been falsely flagged as harmful. However, Apple does have a web page that provides information on how to appeal for other reasons. So there is a framework in place to push back if you think you’ve been caught in the dragnet by accident.
