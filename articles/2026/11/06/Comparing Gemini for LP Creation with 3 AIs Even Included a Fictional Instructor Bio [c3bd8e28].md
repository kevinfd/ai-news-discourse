# Comparing Gemini for LP Creation with 3 AIs | Even Included a Fictional Instructor Bio

note · 徳武輝彦｜Utage×ウェビナー×Ai · 2026-11-06

Comparing Gemini for LP Creation with 3 AIs | Even Included a Fictional Instructor Bio
"It seems like you can even create LPs with Gemini now." 
Seeing news like that, didn't you think you could leave your entire LP creation to AI? 
I thought the same thing, so I actually tried it out.
Hello. I am Teruhiko Tokutake, a two-time UTAGE Award winner and developer of the One Webinar Funnel®.
I compared Gemini's LP creation with ChatGPT and Claude using the exact same prompt. 
The one that got the layout looking right the fastest was Gemini. 
However, it even went ahead and wrote up a bio for an instructor that I hadn't even asked for.
To give you the result first, none of the three produced an LP that could be published as-is.
In this note, I will reveal everything: the prompts I used, the differences between the three AIs, where the AIs failed and what I had to fix, and the order to use when creating a webinar registration LP.
Gemini's new feature: "View and edit within the chat"
On October 29, 2026, Google announced that you can now create documents, PDFs, slides, spreadsheets, code, etc., within the Gemini app chat and preview or edit them directly. 
Google's announcement (English)
The main points mentioned in the announcement are the following three:
- When in full-screen mode, you can directly retype the text.
- You can select text and ask Gemini to question or correct it.
- PDFs can be used on PCs and smartphones, while other files can be used on the browser version for PCs.
When I actually tried it, buttons for switching between "Code" and "Preview" and an export button appeared for HTML files like LPs. 
However, the HTML preview is for viewing only. 
Even when I selected text, "Ask Gemini" did not appear.
How I tested it: I entered the exact same prompt into all three AIs
If you change the prompt when comparing, you won't know if the differences are due to the AI's quirks or the prompt itself. 
Since even a one-word difference changes the output, I entered the exact same text into all three.
The subject was a 90-minute individual session for "Slim-Brain Dieting." 
The prompt I entered was this:
The models I used and the formats they produced were as follows:
- ChatGPT: Plus plan, "medium" reasoning depth. Provided it as an HTML file.
- Gemini: 3.1 Pro. Appeared with a preview inside the chat.
- Claude: Opus 5.5. Appeared with a preview in the artifact window on the right.
Since I used my everyday account, Claude also read my past conversation notes to create it. 
Please view this as a comparison of my "usual user experience."
For the first view, Gemini looks the most like a "proper LP."
Gemini boldly stated, "Dieting in your 40s isn't about 'willpower,' it's about losing weight with your 'brain'," and placed a sign-up button right below it. 
It is designed so that the moment you open it, you know who it is for and what it is about.
ChatGPT asked gently, "You've gained it back again... shall we find that 'eating habit' together?" 
However, the final "?" spilled onto its own line, so it needed a visual fix.
Claude wrote, "Don't you want to end the 'I've gained it back again...' cycle this time?". 
It used the customer's own common phrases as a headline, making it easy for women in their 40s to feel like it's about them.
I write about how to choose from the catchphrase candidates generated by the AI in this note.
Where the AI failed: It "made up" things it didn't know
The biggest difference between the three AIs appeared around the sign-up button.
Gemini wrote at the end, "Let's start your final diet here." 
For the instructor profile, it said, "I myself have suffered as a lifelong dieter in the past." 
This is a background I never mentioned in the prompt.
Below the button, it said "(Join at a special price)." 
There is no mention anywhere of what it is special compared to.
ChatGPT placed a box saying "Please check before publishing" and returned it with the fees and schedule left blank. 
Instead, it had many disclaimers, making it easy for the reader's interest to cool off halfway through.
Claude created a table for "Session Details" and left the participation fee and number of attendees blank with [Input Required]. 
The instructor profile was also left blank with a note saying "In the teacher's own words."
Gemini looks closest to a finished product because it fills in things it doesn't know. 
But the one that fills things in is also the one that causes trouble if you publish it as is. 
Fabricated background stories will destroy your credibility the fastest if you publish them without noticing.
If I were to score them, there was no LP that could be used as is
I scored them out of 10 points each across five categories: catchphrase, impact on the customer, structure, usability as is, and ease of editing. 
The total is out of 50 points.
- ChatGPT: 31 points
- Gemini: 27 points
- Claude: 34 points
As for whether they are "ready to use as-is," all three scored 3 to 4 out of 10 points. 
Gemini's total is lower because it takes time to find and remove exaggerated claims and fabricated background stories one by one.
The design, however, was actually the quickest to take shape among the three.
When I write LPs or newsletters, I throw the same questions at ChatGPT, Gemini, and Genspark, and cherry-pick the best parts.
I use ChatGPT when I want to add a human touch and emotion, and Gemini for researching the latest information or writing logical, well-organized text.
I use Claude for prep work like summarizing long texts or rewriting.
I use AI drafts assuming they have about 80% accuracy. 
I fix the weird parts myself before publishing.
I have summarized in this note how I divide the work, letting AI handle 80% and having humans take care of the remaining 20% and improvements.
Things I fixed: 3 things to look for before publishing
Phrasing that promises results for health or beauty products may violate the Pharmaceutical and Medical Device Act or the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations.
Even if the words were written by AI, the person who publishes them is responsible.
Here are the phrases from this output that I judged could not be used as-is, and the direction for fixing them.
- "Lose weight with your 'brain'": It states losing weight as a certainty. Change it to describe the session content, such as "Learn the triggers that make you want to eat."
- "The last diet of your life": This could be read as never gaining weight again. Change it to "This time, let's rethink how you continue."
- "Join at a special price": This looks like a double-price claim. Do not write about price on the button.
- "I am also a lifelong dieter": A background story not verified with the person themselves. Replace it with the instructor's own words and true background.
ChatGPT and Claude included a sentence like "Results may vary by individual" from the start.
Even so, I verify whether urgent language like "Limited number of spots available per day" is true before keeping it.
I was able to fix the slides by selecting the text and using "Consult Gemini"
In the same conversation where I created the LP, I asked, "Based on the content of this LP, please create 5 slides for a webinar in Google Slides format."
What came out was 6 slides. 
The first slide contained a photo of a wristwatch that had nothing to do with the content.
You need to verify both the number of slides and the photos rather than trusting them as-is.
The subtitle of the first slide was "Automatically lose weight with brain mechanisms."
So I selected "lose weight" and asked, "This is an exaggeration that promises results, so please rewrite it in a way that doesn't make a promise."
In about 10 seconds, it changed to "Achieve your ideal body effortlessly through brain mechanics." 
It is convenient that only the selected line changes without having to redo other parts.
However, "Achieve your ideal body" is still a way of implying results. 
Even after the AI makes corrections, you should check the final version with your own eyes. 
For export, I was able to choose from three options: Google Slides, PDF, and PPTX.
I wrote about which parts I did not leave to AI when I created 250 webinar slides using AI in this note.
If you are using it for a webinar registration LP, use this order
The LPs this time were all pages for getting people to sign up for a 90-minute individual session. 
If it is an LP for webinar lead generation, the goal is to get them to register and watch the webinar. 
If the goal changes, the wording on the buttons and the promises made to the reader also change.
The difference in registration rates directly translates to a difference in sales. 
If 200 people see it and the registration rate is 3%, that is 6 people, and if it is 10%, that is 20 people. 
If the product is 500,000 yen, the registration rate of the initial LP alone creates a difference of about 1 million yen in sales versus zero.
If you make requests vaguely, you will only get low-accuracy results back. 
The shortcut is to provide a high-accuracy template and your own information beforehand, and ask it to "create my version using this template."
I wrote about where to look for numbers like registration rates and where to start making corrections in this note.
Summary
- Gemini can now view and edit files within the chat. However, it only views LPs (HTML)
- Even with the same instructions, the LPs from the three AIs were completely different. Scores: ChatGPT 31, Gemini 27, Claude 34 (out of 50 points)
- Gemini gets the appearance ready quickly, but it included exaggerations and a fabricated background
- The three things to look for before publishing are exaggerations, fabrications, and baseless "hurry" tactics
- For slides, I was able to correct them line by line by selecting the text and choosing "Consult Gemini"
- For webinar LPs, change the goal to "registration" and provide a template
Start by opening the LP created by your AI and marking the three things: exaggerations, fabrications, and words that pressure the reader.
