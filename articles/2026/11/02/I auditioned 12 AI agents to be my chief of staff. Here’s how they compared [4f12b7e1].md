# I auditioned 12 AI agents to be my chief of staff. Here’s how they compared

Fast Company · Jeremy Caplan · 2026-11-02

This article is republished with permission from Wonder Tools, a newsletter that helps you discover the most useful sites and apps.
I’ve never had a secretary before. Or a chief of staff. With these AI agents, I suddenly feel like I have both. This week, among other things, they:
- Researched and booked Amtrak train tickets for me
- Briefed me about important overnight emails and upcoming meetings
- Surfaced an important contract I had overlooked
- Turned my bookshelf into a visual reading list
Unlike older chatbots, these agents can take action proactively without waiting for a prompt. They can go online and research or buy things, make reservations, or fill in forms. They can look through various documents, transcripts, and emails to help you finish tedious projects.
Agents are the biggest step forward for AI this year. I’m testing a dozen to compare their reliability, features, and design: Thine, Instinct, ChatGPT’s Dots, Town, Hark, Muse, Poke, Ambi, Sentience, Extra, Slashy, and Claude Duet.
My early favorites: Dots, Slashy, and Hark
- Dots has helped me spot calendar conflicts and find and prepare materials I need for work. I use it several times a day.
- Slashy has changed how I manage email. It’s a strong alternative to Superhuman, the email app I’ve used for years. Slashy puts an AI agent inside my inbox.
- Hark has a standout design. It’s been reliable for shopping and reservations.
Other observations
- Business agents will be a separate category. Town, for example, is easy to use and well designed, but its features are tuned to small businesses and midmarket companies.
- These are early impressions. I’ll share more as the tools develop. I’m working on a post about Slashy and how to use AI to help with email overload. The inbox is where I see early agents being most helpful.
One is enough
Start with one agent that does what you need. I’m testing a bunch for now, but ultimately I’ll prioritize just a few with distinct strengths.
What they cost
Some agents, like Instinct, Hark, and Muse, start out free. Others require a paid subscription.
- Dots is so far available only on ChatGPT’s Pro plan, which starts at $100/month.
- Town starts free, but the Pro plan is $49/month.
- Hark starts free. Upgrade for $20/month for more AI usage or $100/month for full power.
- Thine is free to start. It’s $15/month or $120/year for extra capabilities.
- Pricing for other agents is still taking shape. You can test many for free. Before connecting your accounts, check on ongoing costs and how each service uses your data.
Connect your email, calendar, and files
Thine and Dots became much more useful as soon as I connected them to my email, calendar, documents, and Granola meeting transcripts. Now they have a broad view of my projects for context, as well as a detailed understanding of my emails and tasks. Drawing on meeting transcripts, they even understand what I’ve promised to do.
