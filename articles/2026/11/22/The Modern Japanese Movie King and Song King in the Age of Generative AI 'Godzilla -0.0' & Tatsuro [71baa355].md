# The Modern Japanese Movie King and Song King in the Age of Generative AI: 'Godzilla -0.0' & Tatsuro Yamashita's 'JOY 2'

note · 縄文ねこ · 2026-11-22

The Modern Japanese Movie King and Song King in the Age of Generative AI: 'Godzilla -0.0' & Tatsuro Yamashita's 'JOY 2'
In an era where anyone can create 'plausible works' using generative AI, entertainment created by real humans with soul is what truly strikes a chord. Following our previous 'Diva' Chisato Moritaka, this time we look at the modern Japanese 'Movie King' and 'Song King'—the film 'Godzilla -0.0' which approaches with overwhelming destructive power, and Tatsuro Yamashita's live album 'JOY 2' which knocks listeners out with blissful grooves. I will share my personal thoughts on them!
I went to see 'Godzilla -0.0'.
 'Godzilla is truly the treasure of Japanese cinema and a world-class Movie King.' 
Just a few minutes into the film, it plunges straight into the first shocking scene. 
This is a common introductory sequence for the Godzilla series, but this time the level is different!
Suddenly! The appearance of 'that guy,' Godzilla's eternal rival.
'That guy,' whom Director Yamazaki reveres, is the other protagonist of this film.
I, too, was completely knocked out early in the film by the super-strong, ultimate, and evil 'that guy'!
Although its appearance could be anticipated from the advance trailers, 
the sudden arrival is a brilliant opening and script that will make anyone groan in admiration. 
Yes! This Godzilla comes down to the 'wonderful quality of the script.' 
How to depict the sequel to the previous work, how to make it interesting as a film, 
and whether it can be made into a film worth watching—it seems this script was finally completed after thoroughly thinking through these points.
And once again, the monster is super-strong, ultimate, and evil, surpassing the previous one.
It mercilessly destroys Matsumoto City, Director Yamazaki's hometown. 
When I saw the overwhelming figure of 'that guy' straddling the National Treasure Matsumoto Castle, the city's symbol, I strongly felt that capturing this very scene was one of the major purposes of this film. 
The VFX, which surpasses the previous work that won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects, is of overwhelming quality, 
fully allowing you to experience the terror of having Godzilla and 'that guy' right in front of you. 
This is truly the pinnacle of monster movies. 
If it's not an IMAX screen but a regular one, 
sitting as close to the front as possible to watch on a large screen applies to this Godzilla as well. 
This has been my personal ironclad rule for watching monster movies for a long time. 
This time, I was in the 4th row, and since there was no one in front of me, 
I was lucky enough to watch it from the front row. 
With only the large screen and the two giant monsters in my field of vision, it was full of impact even on a regular screen.
There are scenes that might divide opinion, but 
'War and Peace', one of the major themes of this film, 
was likely something the director wanted to depict using his unique method of expression. 
I would like to applaud Director Yamazaki's sentiment of 
'not seeking the tragedy of Hiroshima in New York.' 
I was able to feel the wish for peace that runs through the first Godzilla film.
The use of sound effects and background music is also wonderful, 
and the direction where the main Godzilla theme song finally plays in full scale at the very end of the ending was brilliant. 
I recommend watching this Godzilla all the way through to the end. 
At this point, I'm looking forward to the third installment, but...?! 
'There will not be a third time. It ends here.' Does this catchphrase point to New York, following Hiroshima and Nagasaki? Does it point to a sequel (the third installment) of 'Godzilla -0.0'?? 
Does it include both??? 
The mystery only deepens...
Song King: Tatsuro Yamashita 'JOY 2'
And speaking of the 'King' who knocks us out with overwhelming entertainment, there is one more person outside the screen who cannot be forgotten.
Just as the Movie King 'Godzilla -0.0' struck the hearts of the audience within minutes of starting, 
the greatness of this 'JOY 2' comes down to the first track of the album, 'SPARKLE'. 
First, I recommend listening to it at the highest possible volume or with headphones on. 
I listen to it in my car with the volume turned up. 
The moment that legendary guitar cutting rings out, 
it instantly changes the atmosphere of the place, with an overwhelming sense of groove that carries the listener away into a blissful world of sound. 
From the very first note, 'This is the music of a king', there is a tremendous persuasiveness that leaves you no choice but to bow down.
Precisely because we live in an era where anyone can easily create 'plausible music' using generative AI, the honed craftsmanship of living humans poured into this 'SPARKLE,' and the heat of vocals that shine even more with the passing of years, strongly shake the listener's soul. 
I think the words about live performances with 'perfect vocals and perfect playing' that Jon Anderson, the vocalist of Yes, aimed for, and 'vocals becoming an instrument,' also apply to Joy 2. 
'SPARKLE' is, I believe, a performance comparable to 'Siberian Khatru' on Yes's masterpiece live album 'Yessongs.' Of course, comparing two albums with a time difference of over 50 years on the same playing field is too harsh on Yes... After all, the performance venues, performance equipment, recording equipment, etc., must have evolved overwhelmingly in the 50 years since.
Indeed, if the Godzilla on the screen overwhelms viewers with its destructive power, 
Tatsuro Yamashita's 'SPARKLE' knocks listeners out with overwhelming euphoria and rays of sound.
No matter how much generative AI evolves, what truly shakes people's hearts is the moment when the creator's obsession and living soul collide, as these 'two kings' have taught me.
If it were possible, 
'Godzilla -0.0' is something I would like to see in the best IMAX environment. 
And I hope to win tickets to Tatsuro's live concert.
Afterword
I kept it vague by calling it 'that thing' to avoid spoilers this time, but it's already been completely spoiled online.

'Godzilla-0.0' seems like it becomes an even more amazing film in an IMAX theater! 
If I get the chance, I want to go see it in an IMAX theater too (I'm repeating this because it's important).
