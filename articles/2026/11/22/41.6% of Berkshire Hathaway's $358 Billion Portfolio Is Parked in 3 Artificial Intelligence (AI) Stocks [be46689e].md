# 41.6% of Berkshire Hathaway's $358 Billion Portfolio Is Parked in 3 Artificial Intelligence (AI) Stocks

The Motley Fool · Anthony Di Pizio · 2026-11-22

41.6% of Berkshire Hathaway's $358 Billion Portfolio Is Parked in 3 Artificial Intelligence (AI) Stocks
Berkshire's new CEO has aggressively bought one particular artificial intelligence stock this year.
By Anthony Di Pizio –
Key Points
- Warren Buffett's simple investment strategy propelled the Berkshire Hathaway holding company to market-beating returns for 60 years.
- Buffett didn't invest in tech very often, but at least three of the companies in Berkshire's portfolio are using artificial intelligence to supercharge their legacy businesses.
- Berkshire's new CEO, Greg Abel, has aggressively increased the conglomerate's position in one of those three companies during 2026.
Warren Buffett served as the chief executive officer of the Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -0.47%)(BRKB -0.36%) holding company from 1965 to 2025. By the time he retired, he had turned it into a $1 trillion conglomerate with numerous wholly owned subsidiaries, a $350 billion stock portfolio, and over $300 billion in cash.
Had an investor parked $500 in Berkshire stock in 1965 and held on until Buffett stepped down, it would have grown into a whopping $24 million. The same investment in an S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) index fund would have grown into just $199,851 over the same period.
Buffett achieved those returns by focusing on companies with steady growth, reliable profits, and experienced management teams. He never chased the hottest trends, so you wouldn't find him piling money into artificial intelligence (AI) stocks. However, three of Berkshire's top holdings are using AI to supercharge their legacy businesses, and they represent 41.6% of the value of the conglomerate's stock portfolio.
Berkshire's new CEO, Greg Abel, has significantly increased the conglomerate's position in one of them this year. Here they are.
Warren Buffett. Image source: The Motley Fool.
1. Alphabet: 8.2% of Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio
Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%)(GOOGL -2.70%) is the parent company of Google, YouTube, Waymo, and a host of other subsidiaries. It has become a leader in the AI race by integrating it into legacy platforms like Google Search, and by helping businesses tap into the technology via Google Cloud.
In Google Search, the AI Overviews feature creates AI-generated responses to users' queries and places them above the traditional search results, complete with links to the relevant sources. If users wish to expand on their original query, they can open AI Mode and enter further questions into the chatbot-style interface. Alphabet says these features are fueling increased Google Search usage, driving the platform's advertising revenue to all-time highs in recent quarters.

Google Cloud, on the other hand, provides enterprises with access to computing capacity, ready-made foundation models, and other tools to help them create and deploy AI software. Its revenue surged by 103% during the recent third quarter (ended Sept. 30), but growth could accelerate even further given the platform's $561 billion order backlog from customers who are waiting for more data centers to come online.
Berkshire initially bought Alphabet stock in the third quarter of 2025, while Buffett was still at the helm, but Abel has increased the position sixfold this year. Berkshire now owns 78.8 million Class A shares and 27.2 million Class C shares worth a combined $29.6 billion.
2. Coca-Cola: 11% of Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio
Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) is the world's largest beverage company, but building a portfolio of 200 brands and managing supply chains across more than 200 countries wouldn't be possible without the help of technology. The company has invested heavily in AI over the last few years, using it to run marketing campaigns, improve manufacturing processes, and even create new drinks.
In 2024, Coca-Cola signed a deal to spend $1.1 billion on the Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) Azure cloud platform over five years, where it will access tools like the Copilot virtual assistant to support its AI transformation.

Coca-Cola is one of Berkshire's oldest holdings. Buffett spent approximately $1.3 billion acquiring 400 million shares between 1988 and 1994, and the conglomerate still holds every single one. The position is worth $39.5 billion today, and it will pay Berkshire $848 million in dividends this year alone.
3. Apple: 22.4% of Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio
Apple (AAPL -1.90%) is gunning to become the world's biggest distributor of AI to consumers. It boasts an installed base of more than 2.5 billion active devices around the globe, which mostly include iPhones, iPads, and Mac computers, and it's fitting them with increasingly powerful chips to run the Apple Intelligence suite of AI features and applications.
Apple Intelligence includes writing tools that can summarize and draft emails or text messages for the user. It can also prioritize notifications based on what each user deems most important. It even features an upgraded version of the Safari internet browser, which can alert the user to changes to their favorite web pages, so they know immediately if a product they were following is back in stock or is on sale.

Apple remains one of the most successful investments of Buffett's entire career. He spent approximately $38 billion accumulating shares between 2016 and 2023, and by the beginning of 2024, the position was worth over $170 billion. But it accounted for more than half the value of Berkshire's entire portfolio at that point, so Buffett and his team offloaded 75% of the conglomerate's stake by the end of 2025 to lock in gains and reduce concentration risk.
Nevertheless, it remains Berkshire's single largest position with a portfolio weighting of 22.4%, so the conglomerate will still do exceptionally well if Apple dominates the consumer AI market.
