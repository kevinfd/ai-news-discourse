# CarPlay Is Much More Useful With Siri AI. 10 Things to Try on Your Next Drive

PCMag · Lance Whitney · 2026-11-22

With iOS 27, Apple has transformed Siri into a more capable, powerful, and versatile AI assistant. Beyond using Siri AI directly through your iPhone, you can also use it in the car via Apple CarPlay. And unlike the old Siri, you can use the new version for more than just providing directions, playing music, and making phone calls.
The new Siri can handle complex requests, answer open-ended questions, and engage in back-and-forth conversations. In my experience, the new version also handles ordinary tasks more effectively than its predecessor, which sometimes challenged or stumped it.
How to Get Started
First, Apple's new Siri requires a phone compatible with Apple Intelligence, which means an iPhone 15 Pro or newer. You also need to be running iOS 27 or higher, so go to Settings > General > Software Update and install any available updates.
For now, Siri AI remains in beta, so you'll also need to join the program first. To do this, open Settings > Siri and tap Try Siri AI (Beta). Apple's iOS will then need to build and optimize the search index, a process that kicks off automatically but can take time. It has also been known to drain battery life, but once the process is complete, you'll be good to go.
With that done, you'll need to connect your phone to the car through Apple CarPlay. Now, here are 10 things I like to ask Siri AI to do, and how it handles them more smoothly than the original.
1. Give Me Driving Directions to a Contact
In the past, I’d often ask Siri for directions to a person or place in my contacts list. But instead of consulting my contacts, it would often give me random matching names that it found on the web. And much of the time, none of those would be the right person.
In contrast, Siri AI always checks my contacts list when I ask it for directions to a specific person or business. For example, I’ve asked Siri AI for directions to the offices of doctors who are stored as contacts, and it’s found the right person and delivered the correct directions each time.
2. Tell Me About My Current Location
Sometimes I’ll be driving through a new location and want to learn something about it. In that case, I can ask Siri AI to tell me something interesting about where I am, and it will invariably provide history, points of interest, and other details about the area.
For example, I drove to Binghamton, New York, in August to attend a Rod Serling festival. While driving to the hotel, I asked Siri to tell me something interesting about the town, and it told me why Binghamton is the carousel capital of the world, how the city played a role in aviation and computing, and how it’s the birthplace of a regional sandwich known as the spiedie.
3. Answer a Messy, Corrected Question
We all sometimes naturally stumble over words or correct ourselves when speaking, and that certainly can happen when talking to an AI. Where the original Siri might have trouble with messy speech, Siri AI is built to handle corrections and more natural language. Here, I might say something like: “Siri, what’s the weather oh, wait, no, I meant the temperature, or rather, what’s the weather forecast for tomorrow.” The new Siri can parse all that to understand my actual question.
4. Play Specific Music From My Library
Asking Siri to play a specific album, artist, or song from my music library sounds like a simple request, but the old voice assistant would often get confused about where to get the song, in part because I’ve set up multiple music sources through CarPlay. As one example, I might tell it to play Beatles music, but instead of playing it from my library on my iPhone, it would fire up the Beatles Channel on Sirius XM. Siri AI automatically pulls the music I request from my iPhone’s library unless I specify a specific source.
5. Tackle a Multi-Part Request
Unlike the original, Siri AI is designed to handle complex, multi-part requests. This means you can combine several tasks in one shot, and the AI version will tackle each one individually to carry out the entire request. As one example, I recently asked Siri to find a recipe for lentil soup on the internet, create a shopping list with the ingredients, and then text me the list. It followed each step correctly and ended with the list of items in Messages.
6. Find Information Without Specifying the Source
Siri AI is designed to work with the content on your iPhone. If you want it to find information locked away in one of your apps, you can ask it to retrieve the details without specifying a source. For example, I might ask Siri AI to find the name and address of a restaurant that someone recommended. Even if I don't remember if the information is in an email or text, it won't be a problem. The AI will look across the apps on my phone until it finds a match.
7. Explain a Complex Topic
Siri has always been able to serve up answers to general questions, but the older version might give up, hand off the job to ChatGPT, or provide a response that's too brief to be helpful if the request is too complex. However, Siri AI is much more adept at answering such questions with just enough details.
For the sake of comparison, I asked regular Siri to explain Einstein’s special theory of relativity. It provided a short, one-paragraph answer, taken from a single source on the web. I then asked Siri AI, which served up a full and thorough explanation of the topic. It then asked me if I wanted to explore how this differs from Einstein’s general theory of relativity.
8. Ask About an iOS Feature and Activate It
Ask the old Siri a question about a built-in feature or setting, and it would typically show you web-related results. In contrast, Siri AI explains the item in question. From there, you can even tell it to enable, disable, or otherwise modify the option. Here, I asked Siri AI how to silence group text alerts on CarPlay. It explained three ways to do that: 1) Turn off the switch to show text notifications in CarPlay; 2) Silence alerts for a specific conversation; and 3) Use Driving Focus mode. I then asked Siri to turn on Driving Focus mode, which it did.
9. Teach Me a Language
For this task, I asked Siri to teach me a language. The classic Siri told me it could search the web for that when I’ve finished driving. Meanwhile, the AI-powered Siri asked me what language I wanted to learn (Italian), then offered me basic, conversational phrases in both Italian and English, explained what each one meant, and continued the lesson by giving me more words and phrases.
10. Have a Back-and-Forth Conversation
Though Siri AI isn’t as chatty as other chatbots like ChatGPT and Gemini, it’s certainly more conversational than its predecessor. To test this, I told it I wanted a back-and-forth conversation about a specific topic. It asked me which topic, and I suggested American films from the 1930s. It gave me an overview of the topic and then asked if I wished to narrow it down. I told it I wanted to talk about screwball comedies. From there, we focused on It Happened One Night, one of my favorites from the era. We were able to discuss various aspects of the film, leading to an interesting conversation.
