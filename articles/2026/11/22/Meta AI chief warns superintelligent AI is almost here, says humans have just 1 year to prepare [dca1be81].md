# Meta AI chief warns superintelligent AI is almost here, says humans have just 1 year to prepare

India Today · Krishna Goswami · 2026-11-22

Meta AI chief warns superintelligent AI is almost here, says humans have just 1 year to prepare
Following a major math breakthrough by Anthropic's Claude, Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang warns that humanity has just a year to prevent a "handover" of society to self-improving machines.
Artificial intelligence is no longer just a tool for writing emails or generating images. According to Meta’s Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang, machines are now on the doorstep of "superintelligence" AI vastly smarter than the brightest humans and the world has only about a year to prepare.
In a lengthy post on X (formerly Twitter) titled “Superintelligence is Arriving,” Wang warned both the tech industry and the public. The post quickly crossed 2 million views. “Superintelligence is arriving. How humanity acts over the next year will have great consequence on what future world we live in,” he wrote.
Why the sudden alarm?
Wang’s warning follows a reported major math breakthrough on November 20, when Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpge announced he had used Claude over weeks to solve the Riemann Hypothesis — a famous 167-year-old puzzle about prime numbers carrying a $1 million Millennium Prize.
While traditional human peer review is still pending and the Clay Mathematics Institute officially lists the problem as unsolved, independent mathematicians at MIT and the University of Waterloo ran computer checks confirming the 17-million-line mathematical code compiled without errors.
Wang, who spent much of his life in math and science, called the feat “completed with the help of an alien mind that we don’t understand.” Growing up in Los Alamos, New Mexico, he noted that “many physicists would agree that the discovery of the atomic bomb is a homework problem compared to the Riemann Hypothesis.”
Intelligence is no longer the bottleneck
For years, critics argued AI lacked real reasoning. Wang says that barrier is gone. He compared this moment to December 2024, when OpenAI’s o3 model aced the American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME) — a tough teen math contest harder than most coding or business work.
Calling that an “oh shit, everything is about to change” moment, he noted that over the past two years, fixing technical gaps nearly solved coding and unlocked strong cyber and agentic capabilities.
Now, AI has tackled a problem humans struggled with for over 150 years. Wang’s MIT professors would agree the hardest problems in nearly every other field are "easier." The remaining gaps stopping AI from beating top experts everywhere are “no longer intelligence; they are domain-specific know-how, plumbing, and experimentation.”
The biggest fear: AI improving itself
Since AI research is less demanding than the Riemann Hypothesis, Wang warned that models “are clearly going to be intelligent enough to automate all of AI research and be clearly superhuman at improving themselves.” Alongside accelerating biology, cybersecurity, economics, and weapons research, progress could outpace human monitoring.
He questioned whether humanity can control “fully unconstrained recursive self-improvement” or machines “1000x smarter and more capable than humans in every field.”
No handover of society to AI
Wang is not demanding a shutdown, but he slammed “lazy thinking” treating Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) as inevitable. “We should not be okay with a ‘handover’ of society to AI,” he wrote, urging labs to leave the “default path” and prioritise safety, alignment, security, and human empowerment.
The post caps a tense week in Silicon Valley. Days after Anthropic’s blockbuster Nasdaq IPO on November 19, OpenAI publicly urged rival labs on Sunday to agree on shared safety limits until better monitoring tools exist, turning the AI race into a race to keep humans in control.
