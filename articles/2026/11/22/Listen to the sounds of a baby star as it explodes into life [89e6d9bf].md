# Listen to the sounds of a baby star as it explodes into life

Scientific American · Mary Randolph · 2026-11-22

The nominations for the upcoming Grammy awards may be in, but the stars we’re listening to are even bigger than Olivia Rodrigo and Bruno Mars.
This week, NASA released an album created from observations of baby stars across the galaxy performed by the Chandra X-ray Observatory. The album includes four sonifications of actively forming stars in which data are mapped to sounds, turning stellar formation into a musical journey.
Listen to each “star song” below.

Stars are almost constantly forming and dying in Messier 82, which is full of hot gas from the explosions of young, giant stars. X-ray data are captured by high-frequency sounds here, and infrared can be heard as a low drone sound. In between those pitches, visible light is mapped to different musical sounds.
Just below the Orion constellation’s belt, more than 1,000 young stars twinkle in this sonification. Even the dust and gas in an infrared image are converted to sound as low musical notes and distorted strings here.
In the Carina Nebula’s sonification, individual baby stars each get their own string sound, and other instruments reflect the mountainous shape of the galactic region.
Consider this the emo track, with dark dust clouds bringing silence in the middle of the song. Beyond that, young stars and a variety of dust observations create an eerie sound experience.
