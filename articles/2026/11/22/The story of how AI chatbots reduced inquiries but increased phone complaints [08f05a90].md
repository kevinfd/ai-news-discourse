# The story of how AI chatbots reduced inquiries but increased phone complaints

note · Minihyde · 2026-11-22

The story of how AI chatbots reduced inquiries but increased phone complaints
A company that sells home appliances online installed an AI chatbot at its support desk. It was trained on frequently asked questions and provides immediate answers 24 hours a day. Two months after implementation, inquiries to the chat dropped by 40%. At the monthly meeting, the manager in charge displayed a graph showing a '78% self-resolution rate' and received applause.
That same month, complaints to the phone support line had doubled. A man in his 70s, speaking to a new operator who had just picked up the receiver, said this first thing: 'I was made to talk to a machine for 30 minutes. Put a human on the line.'
Why did customer dissatisfaction increase even though inquiries decreased?
Reason 1: AI chatbots are very strong at 'questions they can answer'
Shipping costs, return deadlines, password resets. For frequently asked questions, AI answers accurately and immediately, which is surprising. However, the questions from people who are truly in trouble are usually outside the scope of 'frequently asked questions'.
Reason 2: Because 'the conversation ended' is counted as 'resolved'
The reason a chat is closed is not necessarily because the problem was solved. People who couldn't find an answer and gave up and closed the chat are also counted as the same 'finished' status. In terms of numbers, those who gave up are indistinguishable from those who were satisfied.
