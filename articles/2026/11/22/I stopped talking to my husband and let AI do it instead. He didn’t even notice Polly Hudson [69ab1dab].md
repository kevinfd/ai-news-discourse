# I stopped talking to my husband and let AI do it instead. He didn’t even notice | Polly Hudson

The Guardian · Polly Hudson · 2026-11-22

I’ve just ventured deeper into my phone’s settings than anybody has ever been before. A software update is always discombobulating, but the latest one brought a lot of unwanted AI nonsense (that mental image you just got of me waving my fist at the sky: spot on). Even when you manually switch off Apple Intelligence, check the box to make Siri the same dummy she’s always been, and deselect the option to offer replies to texts and emails, it makes no difference. Harrumph.
Days later, bored on the bus, I began answering my husband’s texts with the terrible suggested AI responses, to see if he’d notice. (We’ve been married a long time; this is how I get my kicks now.)
Mortifyingly, notice he did not. Even when I told him, “You’re a classic!” and laughed “Haha I know right” with nary a comma.
There was worse to come. Because that-which-shall-eventually-rule could tell I’d used its proposed sentences, it was encouraged, and proposed more, which I was tutting and scoffing at until it recommended a reply that ended with the nickname I call my husband. Not darling, honey, or whatever – something unique.
A chill descended in my soul as the penny dropped that these weren’t generic phrases everyone gets – they were specific. My phone was impersonating me, showing me who I really was, and – long story short – I was awful. Cheesy, exclamation-mark-addicted, and full of bantz with a definite z.
Humbled in a way it is probably impossible to ever fully recover from, I realised that in conducting this experiment, I had utilised the very artificial intelligence I was battling to remove just hours before. I’d hate-used it, yes, but I’d used it all the same. Further proof, not that it was needed, that resistance is futile. Luckily I surrendered immediately and have always been fully on board with AI, so when the day comes, I’ll be absolutely fine. Haha I know right.
Polly Hudson is a freelance writer
