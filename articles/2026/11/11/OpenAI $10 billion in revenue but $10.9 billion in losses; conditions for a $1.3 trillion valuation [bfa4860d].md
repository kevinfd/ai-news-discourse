# OpenAI: $10 billion in revenue but $10.9 billion in losses; conditions for a $1.3 trillion valuation

OpenAI: $10 billion in revenue but $10.9 billion in losses; conditions for a $1.3 trillion valuation
OpenAI's quarterly revenue has reached $10 billion (1.6 trillion yen) for the first time. Even so, its losses are $10.9 billion, exceeding its revenue. Rapid growth and massive losses are expanding simultaneously.
These figures for the July-September 2026 quarter were reported by The Information and others as part of an investor presentation. The $10.9 billion is an adjusted operating loss excluding stock-based compensation expenses, which differs from operating losses under general accounting standards.
◆ Revenue is 2.6 times that of the previous year
Revenue for the July-September quarter increased by approximately 50% from the previous quarter and was about 2.6 times that of the same period last year. Simply multiplying $10 billion by four results in an annual pace of $40 billion (6.4 trillion yen). This is an annualized figure, not an annual result.
In addition to paid individual subscriptions for ChatGPT, API usage by companies integrating AI into their own services has grown. According to The Information, revenue from enterprise business doubled in the July-September quarter, accounting for more than half of total company revenue. The expansion of enterprise products, such as the programming assistant Codex, is also supporting growth.
◆ Inside the $10.9 billion loss
Adjusted operating losses expanded from $10.1 billion in the April-June quarter to $10.9 billion (1.744 trillion yen) in the July-September quarter. On the other hand, the ratio of losses to revenue improved from approximately 150% to 109%. This is because revenue growth outpaced the increase in losses.
Calculating backward from $10 billion in revenue and $10.9 billion in adjusted operating loss, the adjusted expense equivalent reaches approximately $20.9 billion. The previous quarter was approximately $16.8 billion. While revenue increased by about $3.3 billion, the expense equivalent increased by about $4.1 billion, widening the deficit.
If expenses were to remain at this level, the revenue required to break even would be approximately 2.1 times the current level.
Training AI models and generating responses through inference require massive computational resources. The use of GPUs and data centers, research and development, and personnel costs are factors driving up expenses. Since inference costs also increase with usage, simply growing revenue is not enough.
According to The Wall Street Journal, stock-based compensation expenses for the July-September quarter were $2.6 billion. This is not included in the adjusted loss mentioned above. Furthermore, operating loss is an accounting figure and does not necessarily indicate the actual cash outflow.
◆ Anthropic has $17 billion in revenue
Anthropic, the company behind Claude, stated in its IPO filing that its revenue for the July-September quarter was approximately $17 billion. This is an increase of about 47% from the previous quarter, reaching approximately 1.7 times that of OpenAI. The results of its early lead in enterprise programming assistance are reflected in its revenue scale.
Anthropic's operating loss was $567 million. However, this is an accounting figure that includes stock-based compensation. It cannot be directly compared to OpenAI's adjusted loss, which excludes stock-based compensation. Anthropic reports an adjusted operating profit of $2.463 billion, but it cannot be confirmed that the adjustment items are the same as OpenAI's.
◆ $1.3 trillion valuation and SoftBank Group
OpenAI proceeded with a new funding round at a valuation of $1.25 trillion (200 trillion yen) and received $11 billion of that by November 6. Against the annualized July-September revenue of $40 billion, the valuation is approximately 31 times that amount.
This multiple includes not only the growth power of a roughly 50% increase in quarterly revenue but also the expectation that it will eventually curb expense growth and generate profits. How much can it lower the costs associated with inference? Along with enterprise expansion, this is a condition that supports the $1.3 trillion valuation.
(9984) SoftBank Group completed an additional $30 billion investment in OpenAI on October 1. As of that date, the cumulative investment amount was $64.6 billion, with an equity stake of approximately 13%. It was reported that they also participated in the new funding in November, but the additional amount has not been confirmed.
The fair value of SoftBank Group's OpenAI holdings as of the end of September was $99.6 billion. This figure does not include the subsequent $10 billion investment in October. If the $1.3 trillion valuation is multiplied by the 13% stake as of October, it would be 26 trillion yen.
While the rise in OpenAI's valuation could lead to unrealized gains on assets held, it does not mean that shares have been sold and cash received. The $10.9 billion loss at OpenAI will not be recorded as an equivalent amount in SoftBank Group's consolidated earnings.
OpenAI has surged to an annualized revenue of $40 billion. The next challenge is how much it can curb costs, including computing resources, relative to revenue growth. Whether it can generate profits commensurate with a $1.3 trillion valuation depends on that.
