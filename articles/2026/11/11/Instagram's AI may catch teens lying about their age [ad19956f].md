# Instagram's AI may catch teens lying about their age

Fox News · Kurt Knutsson, CyberGuy Report · 2026-11-11

My own niece has told me for years that she and her friends could get around age restrictions on Facebook and Instagram when they were younger. They knew how easy it was to make themselves appear older online. And I suspect plenty of parents have heard similar stories. So when I heard that Meta was expanding its AI technology to identify teens who lie about their age, my first thought was, it's about time.
On November 9, Meta announced a worldwide expansion of its AI age-estimation technology. The system looks for accounts that may belong to teenagers, even when someone has entered an adult birthday. Meta says the expansion will place millions more young users into Instagram's built-in Teen Account protections.
The move comes as lawsuits and growing public pressure push Meta to take greater responsibility for protecting young users. After years of concerns about children getting around social media age restrictions, this could be one of the company's most significant attempts yet to address the problem. But how does Instagram's AI figure out someone's age? And can parents trust it to get things right?

How Instagram AI age checks work
For years, entering a birthday was one of the easiest ways to tell Instagram how old you were. The problem was that the app had to rely heavily on what someone told it. Now Meta is using artificial intelligence to look for clues that suggest an account belongs to someone under 18. Instead of relying solely on the birthday listed on a profile, the company's age-estimation technology analyzes account activity and other signals to estimate whether someone may be a teenager.
Meta has previously explained that its age-detection technology can look for clues such as birthday celebrations, references to school grades and information shared in posts or comments. The company has also described separate technology that analyzes photos and videos to help identify accounts belonging to children under 13. According to the company, that technology examines general visual clues without using facial recognition or identifying specific people.
The important thing to understand is that these systems estimate age. They don't establish someone's exact birthday. If Instagram's AI determines that an account likely belongs to someone between 13 and 17, the company can automatically apply Teen Account protections. That means a teenager who entered an adult birthday could suddenly find their account operating under stricter safety settings.
What happens when Instagram identifies a teen?
Instagram's Teen Accounts have been around since 2024. However, those protections depend on the platform correctly identifying younger users. The new worldwide expansion is designed to close that gap. When Meta identifies an account that likely belongs to a teenager, it can apply safeguards that include:
- Stronger privacy: Teen Accounts generally start with private settings that restrict who can see posts and interact with an account.
- Messaging restrictions: Instagram limits direct messages to people a teen follows or already has a connection with.
- Content filtering: The platform reduces exposure to sensitive material and places teens into age-appropriate content settings.
- Limits on unwanted interactions: Instagram restricts who can tag or mention teens and filters potentially offensive comments.
- Time management tools: Teen Accounts include usage reminders and overnight notification restrictions, with additional limits available through parental supervision.
Teens under 16 generally need parental permission to make built-in safety settings less restrictive. Instagram has also introduced 13+ content settings inspired by PG-13 movie ratings. Those filters are designed to reduce the amount of mature material younger users encounter.
We previously covered how Instagram's PG-13 restrictions give parents more control over what teens see. Of course, no content filter catches everything. And these protections can only help if Instagram correctly identifies the accounts that need them.

Instagram's AI age checks are going worldwide
Meta began testing AI technology to identify teens using adult birthdays in the United States in April 2025. The company later expanded the system to additional countries, including Australia, Canada and the United Kingdom. In May 2026, Meta announced further expansion into the European Union and Brazil. Now the technology is going global.
According to Meta, millions more teens will receive additional safety protections, even if they originally claimed to be adults. The company is also expanding age-detection technology on Facebook, with a similar rollout planned for Threads later in 2026.
Meanwhile, Meta says it has started using Apple's age-range signal in Australia. With the right permissions, Apple's system can share someone's age range with participating apps. That gives Meta another way to check whether someone meets an app's age requirements. The big question is whether all this technology will finally make it harder for kids to get around the rules.
Why Meta is finally getting serious about teen safety
The timing of this announcement is worth paying attention to. For years, parents and child safety advocates have raised concerns about social media platforms and their effects on young users. Meanwhile, Meta has faced lawsuits alleging that its platforms contribute to harmful experiences for children and teenagers. Meta reportedly reached settlements worth up to approximately $18 billion with nearly every U.S. state and territory in August 2026. The agreements address lawsuits brought by attorneys general over alleged harm to teens. Under those agreements, Meta committed to strengthening how it identifies teenagers and children younger than 13.
The settlement also includes stronger controls for teens in participating U.S. jurisdictions, including a default two-hour daily limit across Facebook and Instagram that requires parental permission to disable. Additional provisions address nighttime access and parental oversight. Those commitments are separate from the new worldwide AI expansion, although they reflect the broader pressure Meta faces.
And there's an important point here. Meta has known for years that younger users sometimes enter false birthdays. The challenge has been figuring out how to identify them reliably. The company has introduced safety features over time. But public criticism and legal action have increased the pressure to show that those features actually work. Meta's latest announcement suggests the company is putting more resources behind age detection. The results will determine whether this becomes a meaningful turning point. We reached out to Meta for comment on its expanded AI age checks, but did not hear back before our deadline.
Could Instagram's AI get someone's age wrong?
That's another concern worth examining. AI can make mistakes, particularly when it tries to draw conclusions from someone's online activity. An adult might regularly post photos of younger relatives. Someone could also share old school pictures or birthday celebrations from years ago. That doesn't mean one post will automatically convince Instagram that an adult is a teenager. Meta uses multiple signals when making age estimates. However, mistakes remain possible.
Meta says people who are incorrectly placed into Teen Account settings can take steps to verify their age and restore the appropriate settings. Depending on the situation, Instagram may require proof of age through identification or an age-estimation service. And there's another side to the problem. The technology could miss teenagers who successfully make their accounts appear to belong to adults.
That's why parents should still review their children's account settings rather than assuming Instagram has correctly identified their age. Meta's November 9 announcement did not provide a new accuracy figure for the worldwide expansion. I'd like to see more information about how often the system gets these decisions right and how quickly people can correct mistakes.

What parents can expect from Meta
Starting in November 2026, Meta says parents in the United States will begin seeing an informational video in their Instagram feeds. The video will explain how Meta identifies teenagers and places them into age-appropriate protections. It will also link to guidance from internet safety advocate Larry Magid, president and CEO of ConnectSafely.
The guidance explains Meta's approach to identifying younger users and how parents can help ensure their children's ages are accurate. Parents worldwide can also access age-assurance resources through Meta's Family Center. For families, this creates an opportunity to have a conversation about why social media platforms ask for someone's age in the first place.
Tips to help keep your teen safer on Instagram
Even with stronger AI protections, I wouldn't leave everything up to Instagram. There are several practical steps parents can take right now.
1. Check your teen's birthday on Instagram
Ask your teen to open Instagram and go to their profile. Tap the menu, open Accounts Center, then look for Personal details and Birthday. Make sure the birthday is accurate. Instagram may require verification if someone attempts to change an account's age from under 18 to an adult age.
2. Review their account privacy settings
Open Instagram's settings and look for the account privacy controls. Check whether the account is private and review who can follow your teen. Also review who can send messages. You may find that some protections are already enabled through Teen Accounts.
3. Turn on parental supervision
Instagram's Family Center gives parents additional tools for managing a teen's experience. Parental supervision requires both the parent and teen to agree to the connection. Once connected, parents can review available safety controls and manage certain account restrictions.
4. Review screen time controls
Instagram offers daily usage reminders and additional restrictions for teens. Parents can also use supervision settings to manage screen time. On an iPhone, open Settings, then Screen Time to review available app limits. For Android devices, Google Family Link offers parental controls and device management options.
5. Check the content settings
Instagram's 13+ content setting is designed to limit material that may be inappropriate for teenagers. Parents can review these protections and choose stricter settings where available. Remember that filters may still miss content, so it's worth asking your teen what regularly appears in their feed.
6. Talk about why age restrictions exist
This may be the most useful step. Ask your teen whether they have ever felt tempted to enter an older birthday to unlock features or avoid restrictions. Explain that providing the correct age helps Instagram apply protections intended for younger users. And make sure they know they can talk to you if they encounter something uncomfortable online.
