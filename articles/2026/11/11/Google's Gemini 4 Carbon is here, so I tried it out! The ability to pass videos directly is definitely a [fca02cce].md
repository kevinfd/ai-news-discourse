# Google's Gemini 4 Carbon is here, so I tried it out! The ability to pass videos directly is definitely a game-changer! The performance is also powerful

note · まさお · 2026-11-11

SYSTEM NOTICE
Google's Gemini 4 Carbon is here, so I tried it out! The ability to pass videos directly is definitely a game-changer! The performance is also powerful
Since Google's Gemini 4 Carbon became generally available on November 10th, I've been using it extensively with the API and Cursor.
When I passed in one of my previously published videos in its entirety, it pointed out a spot with background noise that I hadn't even noticed. When I played back that section, there really was noise in it.
What I tested was showing it videos and having it perform my usual tasks.
I used a paid API key and Cursor. In Cursor, I set the thinking budget to the highest level, High.
First, I passed in videos and audio without any processing and had it write its impressions. Separately, I had it perform the tasks I give to new models every time to compare its output with other models.
It was stronger at reviewing videos. As for creating things, it wasn't enough to make me switch from the Fable model I usually use.
Just by passing it a video, it returned the parts that needed fixing.
The point I felt was strongest was being able to show it videos directly. In addition to the noise issue at the beginning, when I asked it where to cut to keep it under one minute, it told me exactly where to make the breaks.
When I asked it by providing a YouTube URL, it pointed out ways to reduce tangents and how to handle slides that only appeared for a split second. It also touched on volume and editing tempo. When I intentionally passed it a video with a lot of noise before editing, it returned a table summarizing the parts that should be cut.
This is helpful because doing the same thing with Claude is a hassle. According to Claude's official documentation, input is limited to text and images. When I want to show it a video, I have to go through the trouble of making it readable in my own way.
It is valuable to have someone who looks at the finished video itself, rather than just text or screen captures, and gives feedback. If you are someone who creates videos or audio, I think it's a good idea to keep your current model for the creation role and add Carbon just for the review role. I personally intend to incorporate it into my automation system as a tool for evaluating deliverables.
If you switch your creation work over to it, you will lose out on the fine details of the final product.
My impression of having it perform my usual tasks was that it was a refined version of the traditional style. While I feel a sense of novelty in Fable's output that I haven't seen before, Carbon looks like an extension of the previous generation. The introduction pages and slides have a large amount of text, which feels overwhelming to the reader.
For the MMORPG introduction page, while Fable was able to depict the details, Carbon's game screen section looked cheap.
The 3D fighting game looks dated. The computer opponent is so strong that there is almost no opening for me to make a move.
The 3D marble run was of a quality that previous models couldn't create, and the presentation was well-done. Even so, if you keep the balls flowing, it gets stuck halfway through.
The thumbnail was created without breaking. What concerned me was the thickness of the title text.
When I had Fable look at Carbon's work, it said that the attention to detail in moving and verifying things was lacking. It is one step better than the same Gemini's Argon, and the parts that were broken in Argon were fixed.
There are also rows in the comparison table that Google itself released at the time of the announcement where other companies come out on top.
What I'm losing to other companies in is tasks that involve having agents write code and computer operations, so I'm concerned about that too. Since Fable was better even in the details, I will continue to focus on Fable for my daily production for the time being.
If you only call it when you get stuck, you can keep wait times and costs down.
Another way to use it is to assign it only to difficult spots where you are stuck. Reading Google's official documentation, the difference between Carbon and Argon is not the price. What is different is how much it thinks in a single response.
Because it thinks more, Carbon has a longer wait time until it returns a response and uses more tokens. The official documentation also states that Argon is sufficient for most daily development and research. Carbon is treated as a model to bring out when you hit the most difficult problems.
When I tried it with an API key, the cost changed significantly at the stage of how much it thinks, even though it was the same type of task. Therefore, I will not send everything to Carbon, but will use it by calling it from the API only for difficult spots. If I am to use them differently within Gemini, it will be Argon for daily use and Carbon when I get stuck.
If you want to try it, use a paid API key or Cursor.

Carbon is a model that has taken the strengths of the work I want to entrust to Gemini to the next level. Even so, Fable remains my main go-to, and I won't be switching over. I will keep it on hand as a partner to show videos and audio directly, and as one to call via API only when I get stuck.
