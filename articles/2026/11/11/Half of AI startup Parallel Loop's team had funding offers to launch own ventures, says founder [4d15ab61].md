# Half of AI startup Parallel Loop's team had funding offers to launch own ventures, says founder

Moneycontrol · Reshab Shaw · 2026-11-11

15
Parallel Loop, which has a team of around 30 employees, is building an AI-focused workforce comprising engineers and technology professionals who have previously worked at companies such as Ola and CRED
  November 12, 2026 / 10:39 IST  
Nearly half of the employees at Parallel Loop had investment offers to start up on their own before choosing to join the firm, founder Prateek Jogani said, highlighting the growing demand for artificial intelligence talent.
Bengaluru-based Parallel Loop, which has a team of around 30, is building an AI-focused workforce comprising engineers and technology professionals who have previously worked at companies such as Ola and CRED.
“Like half of our teams had offers of investments who ended up joining us,” Jogani told Moneycontrol in an interview.
The company, which raised $6.6 million in seed funding led by 3one4 Capital last month, is positioning itself as an AI operating partner for enterprises, combining forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) with AI tools to help businesses modernise their technology systems.
The company's biggest competition for talent does not come from traditional IT services firms such as Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) and Infosys.
“I have never found them to be any competition from a hiring perspective as of today. My competition is actually the frontier labs,” he said.
The engineers Parallel Loop seeks to hire are often considering opportunities to lead AI companies or starting their ventures.
“My challenge is if I have an offer from OpenAI and I have an offer from Parallel Loop, what makes them join Parallel Loop as compared to OpenAI?” he said.

Why engineers are choosing jobs over entrepreneurship
Prateek said the changing economics of AI startups is also influencing engineers' decisions to pursue entrepreneurship.
Unlike traditional software businesses, AI startups face the risk of products becoming obsolete as major developers introduce new capabilities.
“In the world of AI, this pain has aggravated because tomorrow a dev (developer) day will happen in OpenAI and 50 percent of the startups will go away because it has just become a native part of the model,” he said.
The 50 percent figure was used to illustrate the potential disruption from AI model upgrades rather than as a measured estimate of startup failures.
Jogani, who described Parallel Loop as his fourth entrepreneurial venture, said engineers increasingly recognise the difficulty of building standalone AI products in a market where underlying technology is advancing rapidly.
Working across multiple enterprise AI projects can provide engineers with broader exposure and help them develop skills that remain relevant as models evolve, he said.
3one4 Capital on backing potential founders
Anurag Ramdasan, Partner at 3one4 Capital, said the venture capital firm is not concerned about employees eventually leaving the startup to launch their own businesses.
“As VCs, we have to be pro-entrepreneurship. We don't have a choice,” Ramdasan said, adding while some employees may aspire to become founders, starting a company requires the right timing and long-term commitment.
Starting a company requires more than the ambition to become a founder, he said. Employees joining Parallel Loop may eventually pursue entrepreneurship but may believe that the right time to start a company has not yet arrived.
The comments come as India's major IT services companies increasingly focus on building specialised AI engineering teams.
TCS, Infosys, HCLTech and Wipro have outlined initiatives to develop AI capabilities and deploy engineers who can work directly with enterprise customers.
