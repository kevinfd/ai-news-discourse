# Will AI create more jobs than it kills? Why 2030 projects a net gain of 1.7 million globally

Zee Business · Anubha Shriwas · 2026-11-11

Written By  Anubha Shriwas
Published: 4:17 PM, Nov 12, 2026 | Updated: 4:58 PM, Nov 12, 2026
Discover why AI is projected to create 10.6 million jobs by 2030. Find out which industries will be hit hardest by this. 
AI will remove some roles, delay others, and reshape many more. | Image Credit: Magnific
Fears that AI will take away jobs have been growing for years, but a new report offers a more hopeful picture. According to Gartner, AI is projected to create 10.6 million new jobs worldwide by 2030, while removing 8.9 million. That leaves a net gain of 1.7 million jobs.
This report also says job losses directly linked to AI will affect at most 1.8 per cent of workers globally. Even so, over 20 per cent of workers will have to change how they do their jobs. Read ahead to understand what this means for jobs in the future, which industries are most affected, and why deferred jobs are not lost jobs.
Gartner’s forecast shows AI creating more jobs than it eliminates. The 10.6 million new roles outnumber the 8.9 million lost ones, giving a net gain of 1.7 million jobs worldwide by 2030.
The report was presented at the Gartner IT Symposium/Xpo in Barcelona. Helen Poitevin, Distinguished VP Analyst at Gartner, said the most advanced organisations see AI as a way to reinvent operations, not just replace workers.
AI is already changing hiring. In 2026, business leaders delayed the creation of 1.3 million positions. In the same year, 2.2 million jobs were lost, and 1.6 million were gained.
By 2030, about 5 million people will have faced delayed hiring because executives postponed new roles. John Lovelock, Distinguished VP Analyst at Gartner, explained the difference, saying, “Deferred jobs are not the same as lost jobs.” These positions are not removed. They just never get created.
Gartner projects that between 0.4 per cent and 1.8 per cent of workers globally will lose their jobs directly because of AI by 2030. That is a small share of the global workforce.
The bigger change is in how people work. Just over 20 per cent of workers, or 435.5 million employees, will have to adapt. Their tasks, methods, and responsibilities will change, even if their jobs remain.
Gartner says the impact is uneven across geographies, job functions, and industries. It looked at three types of industries.
- Asset-intensive industries: These deal in physical products or run on heavy physical infrastructure, and they employ the largest share of workers globally. By 2030, they will see 6.4 million jobs created and 6 million lost. AI-driven job losses will affect 0.4 per cent of the global workforce in this sector.
- Public sector: Gartner says AI investments here are unlikely to be enough to fix serious skills shortages caused by other factors. The sector will see 0.4 million job gains and 0.9 million job losses by 2030. AI-driven losses will total 0.4 per cent of the public sector workforce.
- Technology sector: Nearly 70 per cent of tech workers have high exposure to AI. By 2030, the sector will create 1.9 million jobs and lose 0.4 million. AI-driven job loss will affect 1.8 per cent of the global tech workforce, the highest share among the three.
Poitevin warns that the worst mistake an executive can make is to apply lessons from one industry or role to another. Each industry faces its own risks, and what works in tech may not work in public services or manufacturing.
The report suggests the bigger story is change, not mass job loss. AI will remove some roles, delay others, and reshape many more. For workers, learning new skills and adapting to new tools may matter as much as the headline numbers.
