# AI-Generated Complaints, Requests Flood Government Offices. It's Not Going Well

PCMag · Jon Martindale · 2026-11-11

Public bodies in the UK are struggling to handle an influx of AI-written complaints and correspondence, which are often long and full of hallucinations, The Guardian reports.
AI chatbots may be useful tools for quickly whipping up an email based on a few bullet points or a single sentence, but when someone else has to read through it to find the point of the email, it can slow them down, especially if it references non-existent sources. That's the problem facing many public bodies, including lawyers' offices, local councils, schools, and politicians, who say they're struggling to respond to everyone, let alone effectively take action on their behalf.
“Half the emails we receive are some form of AI,” Hollie Wickens, a senior case worker in a Labour MP's office, tells The Guardian.
This AI slop is causing "AI clog," and it's only growing worse as AI agents begin sending out correspondence of their own. Researchers at Berlin's Centre for Digital Governance, the Cooperative AI Foundation, and GovAI have dubbed this so-called "agentic flooding" a serious concern for public-facing bodies.
Many of these submissions are legitimate; complaints about social care services, missed garbage collections, and potholes have surged. Those problems existed before AI, but the tech has made it easier to spin up a message and find the right person to send it to. It's also leading to a rise in appeals, as people can quickly put together a counterargument to a fine or warning. That said, lawyers told The Guardian that people often cite cases that don't exist or laws that apply in other countries or jurisdictions.
Although some are calling for additional funding to handle the new caseload, others have suggested a fee or more complex processes for submitting complaints. Others have argued for banning AI-submitted correspondence altogether or fighting fire with fire, by using AI to filter correspondence to find those that are legitimate and not full of errors.
