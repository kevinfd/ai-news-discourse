# Anthropic is public, now comes the hard part

Axios · Matt Phillips · 2026-11-20

Data: FactSet; Chart: Matt Phillips/Axios With its IPO done and dusted, Anthropic's shareholders will be watching its progress toward becoming a business that doesn't need constant capital infusions from investors and vendors. Why it matters: Anthropic — along with OpenAI — is at the heart of the industry that's dominated the equity and debt markets for the last four years. Much of the AI infrastructure boom is ultimately aimed at supplying computing power to these two companies. Yes, but: The bill for that data center investment will start to come due over the next few years. And Anthropic and OpenAI are going to have to pay for quite a bit of it. How it works: Anthropic has covered much of its computing costs thus far by selling parts of itself off to compute vendors like Amazon and Alphabet, and by raising more cash from other investors. But eventually the company's core business will have to generate enough money to cover those costs on its own. Case in point: Anthropic's filings say it is on the hook for roughly $900 billion in spending commitments — mostly for data centers and computing power — with about half of that due before the end of 2030. After the IPO, Anthropic is expected to have roughly $200 billion in cash and short-term investments in the bank, which should buy it some time. But its sales will have to be a lot higher to just cover computing obligations, let alone the profits ANT's new shareholders are expecting. By the numbers: As the chart above shows, Anthropic posted a loss of roughly $23 billion on revenue of nearly $17 billion in the third quarter. It's true that much of that loss stems from a non-cash accounting charge of almost $22 billion. But even the more favorable metric of operating income — which strips out the paper losses and gains that can obscure the company's core earnings power — still shows an operating loss of more than half a billion dollars in the third quarter. The bottom line: Anthropic still has a long way to go before it is going to be standing on its own feet, financially speaking.
