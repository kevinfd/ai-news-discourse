# Tech wreck drags Anthropic into the red on its second day of trading

CNBC · Leonie Kidd · 2026-11-20

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.
Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.
The Anthropic honeymoon is over.
Despite its debut-day rally, the broader markets plunged around the stock, and it's now catching the tech cold - down in premarket trading on its second day as a public company.
Happening now: Debut downer
It's day 2 and Anthropic shares are under pressure.
The stock is falling in premarket trading, at one point down over 6%.
The stock had closed 17% higher in its first day of trading, easing back from a near-40% jump earlier in the session.
Speaking exclusively to CNBC's Andrew Ross Sorkin after ringing the bell at the Nasdaq, CEO Dario Amodei defended the company's massive $900 billion in AI commitments, saying, "If you look at the amount of cash we have on the balance sheet and the amount of revenue that we're already making per year, suddenly those commitments don't seem so large."
Nasdaq futures are back in the red, underperforming both the Dow and S&P in early trading.
- Analysis: Jim Cramer blames Nvidia's woes for trumping excitement over the IPO, wiping out wealth faster than the debut is creating it.
- Pro: Steve Grasso, CEO of Grasso Global, is urging investors to wait for Anthropic to trade at a discount before jumping in, saying, "Be patient. I wouldn't buy it all at once. Wait for that discounted price."
- Big Cap: Ross Gerber, CEO of Gerber Kawasaki, is bullish, saying, "For investors, it's going to become a blue-chip stock like Google."
Big Movers: Tech Wreck
Nvidia is lower in premarket trading, alongside AMD.
- Shorts: AMD shares came under pressure after short seller Hunterbrook Media claimed the company's exposure to China was higher than disclosed.
It's a deeply negative handover from Asia and Europe.
Japan's Softbank suffered its worst trading day in over two years as the AI sell-off swept through the Asian tech sector. Shares in CXMT, SK Hynix and others ended the day deep in the red.
In Europe, chip stocks like ASML, Infineon and others suffered heavy selling.
- Coming up: "Squawk Box" will speak to ARK Invest CEO & CIO Cathie Wood at 6 a.m. ET. We'll see if she still "always feels better when the market is worried."
Exclusive: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent
The debate over the export of U.S.-made chips to China has divided the Trump Administration's cabinet. The Nvidia scandal has further exposed this split and fanned the flames between the two camps.
- The backdrop: Earlier this week, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent claimed "Commerce has the pen," amid the controversy over Nvidia's export relationships with China-related firms.
- What next?: Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is due to appear before the Senate Banking Committee in December, as well as Nvidia's former CEO Jensen Huang.
- Investigation: House Democrats are seeking records from Cantor Fitzgerald, run by Lutnick's sons, amid the probe into why he allegedly resisted cracking down on certain companies.
- White House: President Trump will give a news conference following his trip to China, which is due to take place at 12 p.m. ET today.
Crypto Watch: Bitcoin blues
Bitcoin hit a 4-month low amid the broader tech sell-off, putting further pressure on cryptocurrencies.
- Coincidence?: CNBC's Tanaya Macheel will report on how mega IPOs could be sucking the liquidity out of Bitcoin, with it falling 23% in the 30 days leading up to Anthropic's (ANT) IPO, which nearly mirrored its 22% slide in the month before SpaceX went public
- Contrarian?: Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse has told CNBC how the crypto sell-off could reverse after Anthropic's IPO, citing a rally after SpaceX's listing.
And Finally...
Gold's bad year is getting worse
Precious metals investors can be forgiven if they feel like the past year has given them whiplash.
Following a torrid run in which the price of gold hit new records 53 times in 2025, it hit a historic high of $5,589.38 an ounce on Jan. 28, only to take an almost equally historic nosedive. On Wednesday, gold fell below $3,800 for the first time since September 2025, roughly a third below its peak.
A convergence of factors pushed gold to historic highs earlier this year, according to Suki Cooper, global head of commodities research at Standard Chartered, the London-based multinational bank. Now a different convergence is bringing the price down.
