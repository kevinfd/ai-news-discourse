# Satya Nadella says Jensen Huang is 'singular' — but founders are 'not gods'

Business Insider · Thibault Spirlet, Thibault Spirlet (tspirlet@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-20

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella says Nvidia should be able to thrive without Jensen Huang. Spencer Platt/Getty Images; Sean Gallup/Getty Images Satya Nadella said founders like Jensen Huang are "singular." But founders are "not gods," he added. The Microsoft CEO said Huang built an Nvidia that can thrive long after he is gone. Jensen Huang is "singular," but founders aren't indispensable, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella has said. "Founders are unbelievable in the sense that they create something from nothing, so that is pretty magical. So therefore yes, they're singular," Nadella told Andrew Ross Sorkin at The New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday. "But they're not gods," he added. Huang resigned as CEO last week after Nvidia's board opened an investigation into whether he was "the target of a foreign intelligence effort." Huang also apologized for being unfaithful to his wife. Nadella said a founder's real test is whether they built a company that can stand the test of time and outlast them. "Singularity is about the magic you create, but it's also the institutional strength you build," he said. "I think that's what Jensen actually has done with Nvidia." Nadella did not take a position on whether Huang should return as CEO, but many Nvidia employees, venture capitalists, and tech leaders — including Elon Musk — have called for his reinstatement. Pershing Square Capital Management founder Bill Ackman said the board was not acting in the best interests of investors by resisting calls to reinstate Huang, while Musk compared Huang's resignation to ousting NBA legend Michael Jordan . Another camp has also emerged : critics who say Huang's supporters are excusing a powerful man's conduct while minimizing the damage to those around him. Nvidia's board, which appointed Colette Kress as interim CEO, has said it has no intention of reinstating Huang as CEO. That is despite Ben Reitzes, a tech analyst at Melius Research, warning interim leadership of the possibility of losing its best engineering talent following Huang's exit. The company's shares fell by 3.3% to close at $145.98 on Thursday, down from an all-time closing high of $239.24 on October 6, before Huang's resignation .
