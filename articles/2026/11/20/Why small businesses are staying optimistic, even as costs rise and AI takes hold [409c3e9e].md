# Why small businesses are staying optimistic, even as costs rise and AI takes hold

Business Insider · Kathleen Elkins, Kathleen Elkins (kelkins@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-20

More than half of small business owners surveyed by Gusto said they raised prices in the past year. MoMo Productions/Getty Images More than half of small-business owners raised prices in the past year, according to a new survey. Owners are more optimistic about their businesses than the US economy. The survey also found that more owners are using AI in meaningful ways. Rising costs are forcing small-business owners to make difficult decisions about what to charge customers. More than half of owners (52%) raised prices in the past year, according to Gusto's latest State of Small Business report. Half expect to do so again next year. Still, the report's broader picture is one of cautious optimism. Payroll and HR service Gusto surveyed 1,731 small-business owners and found that, while entrepreneurs remain wary of the broader economy and rising costs, they feel confident about what they can control. Nich Tremper, Gusto's principal economist, shared three takeaways about the state of small business today. 1. Owners feel better about their companies than the economy More than half (61%) of owners are optimistic about their own businesses, but just 18% feel the same about the US economy — down from 29% in 2025. "There's uncertainty, and they're worried about the overall economy," Tremper said. "But when they look at the things that they can control, they're mostly feeling confident." Courtesy of Gusto That confidence is rooted in results: 86% of owners said their business is meeting or exceeding the expectations they had when they started, while roughly 7 in 10 said customer spending and demand shape how they view their business. 2. AI is moving beyond the experiment phase Most small businesses (67%) now use AI for at least some tasks. And owners aren't just turning to the technology to draft social posts or emails. Many are applying it to more central parts of their operations, such as understanding customer data and planning for demand. "AI is really deepening into the business," Tremper said, adding that it's one of the biggest shifts he's noticed between 2025 and 2026. "Whereas somebody might just be using AI to run marketing a year ago, they're now looking more at, 'How do I understand my data? How do I understand what my customers want, and how do I meet them there?'" Courtesy of Gusto That shift tracks with how Kyle Ray, founder of Houston-based Geek Window Cleaning, has adopted AI. Ray uses ChatGPT to analyze his company's P&L and pressure-test ideas for improving margins — what he has called an " AI CFO " or "AI COO." He also uses AI-powered tools to handle customer texts and answer calls that come in after hours or on weekends, allowing his small team to respond more quickly without hiring additional customer service staff. "Even though it's just me — I don't have a CFO or a CEO or a COO, and I'm wearing all those hats — I can create ones in ChatGPT," Ray previously told Business Insider. 3. Cost pressures haven't gone away. More than half of owners (52%) raised prices in the past year, and half expect to do so again next year. Meanwhile, 59% said rising costs for materials and supplies negatively affected their business. "Small businesses are not immune to the cost pressures that everybody has felt the last couple years," Tremper said. "All businesses are navigating this continued inflationary period that we're in." Smaller companies often have less negotiating power with suppliers than their larger competitors, he added: "They don't get to negotiate huge contracts with their suppliers. They are folks that are going to Home Depot and paying the price at Home Depot as they would as a consumer." Still, the report suggests owners are adapting — and, overall, remain optimistic. Even in an uncertain economy, "small business owners know that they are important in their community," Tremper said. The survey found that their customers continue to show up. "That's the reason that they're optimistic about the future," he said. "It's because they know that they're providing a service that is highly valued." Read the original article on Business Insider
