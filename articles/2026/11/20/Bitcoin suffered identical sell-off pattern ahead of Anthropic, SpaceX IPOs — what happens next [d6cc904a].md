# Bitcoin suffered identical sell-off pattern ahead of Anthropic, SpaceX IPOs — what happens next?

CNBC · Tanaya Macheel · 2026-11-20

Bitcoin has suffered nearly identical sell-offs ahead of two of the biggest public offerings this year, leaving investors wondering if competing demand for investor capital could be weighing on the cryptocurrency and perhaps delaying its recovery.
The price of the bitcoin dropped 22.7% in the 30 days before Anthropic's IPO and fell 22.3% in the month leading up to SpaceX's June 12 listing. Ether declined 25.3% in the 30 days preceding Anthropic's debut, and 28% in the same window ahead of SpaceX.
"Liquidity events can elevate correlations across asset classes quickly," Matthew Sheffield, chief investment officer at the ether treasury firm Sharplink, told CNBC. "The first 15 min of [Thursday's] Anthropic IPO put that on full display. Tech stocks, value stocks, oil, gold, crypto all sold off as traders sold assets to buy shares in the secondary market."
"Stores of value function as such over long horizons, but in the short term are still liquidity driven," Sheffield said. "We saw this in early Covid when the market fled to cash. Stock, bonds, commodities ... it didn't matter, you were being sold for cash."
While investors appear to be rotating capital out of crypto to make room for history-making IPO investments, that can't be said with precise certainty. Crypto has also been under pressure from ETF outflows, leverage, macro uncertainty and broader risk-off sentiment.
Still, the post-IPO price action may offer a data point in understanding where bitcoin's price goes next. With Anthropic now public, investors are watching to see whether bitcoin and ether find a floor, and if institutional demand returns.
"[After] the SpaceX IPO, bitcoin didn't truly find its final bottom until after the IPO was completed, and it didn't actually reverse the downtrend until 70 days later," said Matt Cole, CEO and chairman of Strive Asset Management, a bitcoin treasury firm. "My view is that [for] bitcoin, the most likely scenario is that it chops around here."
The expiration of IPO allocation restrictions can influence the timing of capital flows into digital assets, Sheffield said.
"Post SpaceX we saw the wealth effect in full force," Sheffield said. "Many brokers make traders wait 15-30 days before they are allowed to flip shares they received as allocations. … If you went long ETH versus SpaceX on the day of the IPO, on day 31 was when you would have become profitable."
Bitcoin's next critical test is between $60,000 and $62,000, according to Nicolai Sondergaard, a research analyst at Nansen. On Friday, it was sitting at about $62,000.
As of Friday, bitcoin was hovering at the upper end of a critical $60,000 to $62,000 support range. Its next move could determine whether it can stabilize or if it will face another leg lower and revisit its July low of $57,737.35.
Throughout November, bitcoin has been struggling from a combination of dollar strength, institutional selling, and ongoing leveraged bitcoin liquidations. The declines began in late October, as concerns over artificial intelligence valuations and spending fueled a broad tech sell-off. They were compounded by the $6.3 billion Binance hack and weakening institutional ETF demand.
Bitcoin is in the early stages of forming a bottom, according to Cole. Retail fear is elevated amid recent outages, quantum concerns and other short-term uncertainties, but bitcoin should continue to benefit from macro forces like rising debt and weakening fiat currencies, which should drive price higher, he said.
"Capital markets are open, … retail fears are high, and when you combine those things it's much more likely to be a dip-buying opportunity across the board, including bitcoin, than the start of a great financial crisis," said Cole. "It'll likely age as a volatile time to consider allocating to risk, not the opposite."
"The winter for bitcoin is basically at the end, but after winter does not come summer, it comes early spring," he added. "Anytime anyone's been around early spring, what you notice is it gets really cold still, it still rains – it's not necessarily great weather, and I think that's the environment we're in for bitcoin."
