# Shoppers are stocking up on generators and emergency meals

Business Insider · Dominick Reuter, Dominick Reuter (dreuter@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-20

Walmart says sales of water are up this week. Jabin Botsford/The Washington Post via Getty Images High-profile outages and artificial intelligence fears are driving a rush for emergency prep gear Retailers report generators and food kits selling fast. One food bucket company reports shipping delays citing "extreme volume." Retailers are seeing increased buying from anxious consumers after a series of high-profile tech failures stoked concerns about wider societal breakdowns. Shoppers are flocking to stores to stock up on emergency preparedness items, from generators to meal kits with a long shelf life. The rush comes in the wake of several society-jarring infrastructure disruptions, such as the longest national airplane ground stop since 9/11, Microsoft's widespread email outage, European grocery store hacks, and disruptions to major financial services. That all comes on top of a growing fear that AI will either cause mass technological displacement or extinguish all humanity. One retailer getting an outsized share of the action is Costco, which has long been a go-to for preppers thanks to its bulk sizing and bargain pricing. Mizuho analyst David Bellinger told Business Insider he saw the consumer frenzy in action during a recent visit to a Costco warehouse where shoppers were loading up on pantry staples and big-ticket preparedness products. "I saw this solar generator in there. It was $900, and I was like, 'Maybe I should buy this,'" he said. "I went back the next day, and they were all gone." After he ordered one online, he noticed that 21 of the 26 generators on sale at the warehouse chain were sold out. Costco has long been a go-to for emergency supplies. Danielle Villasana/Getty Images A Walmart spokesperson also confirmed that the company is seeing items like propane and large bottles of water sell at higher rates in response to the concerns, but that those categories represent a small portion of the retail giant's overall business. Michael Cooper, VP of merchandising at Tractor Supply Co., said the rural retailer is seeing a distinct uptick in sales of preparedness and resilience supplies across the US, like fuel tanks, water barrels, backup generators and batteries, as well as canning and dehydration supplies for food preservation. The spike is not quite as sharp as it is ahead of a hurricane, but it is far more broad-based geographically, he said. Preparedness-buying trends from the first week in November were "compounded by some of the activity last week," he added. Cooper also said the retailer is seeing an uptick in ammunition sales, though it is a bit tougher to parse out an increase in panic buying from a seasonal stock-up of hunting supplies. Tractor Supply doesn't sell guns. Two brands that sell emergency meal kits, ReadyWise and My Patriot Supply, posted notices on their websites expressing slower-than-normal order fulfillment because of sudden demand. The companies sell meal kits with a long shelf life that contain enough calories to feed a person for a month in an emergency. A screenshot of ReadyWise's website shows a banner indicating shipment delays Readywise "Unprecedented order volume means orders may take up to 14 business days to ship," said a notice on My Patriot Supply's site. ReadyWise said orders are expected to ship in December, citing "extreme volume." Whether the rush to gather supplies is warranted or not isn't clear. Experts generally recommend that all households prepare for emergencies, such as ensuring each resident has enough water for several days. In recent days, US officials have said the tech outages weren't the result of a coordinated cyberattack by another country or a malicious actor. Last month, the UK told families to prepare three days' worth of food, water, and essentials amid concern that natural disasters, armed conflict, and other emergencies were becoming more frequent. Several European grocery chains also fell victim to cyberattacks that raised the temperatures of their freezers and refrigerators, forcing tons of food to be discarded. A cyber attack caused food refrigerators to defrost, forcing retailers to dispose of spoiled food. Fred TANNEAU / AFP via Getty Images Bellinger said his Mizuho colleagues in the UK reported strong panic buying there in October that spread into the US later in the month. The American Red Cross recommends one gallon of water per person per day and non-perishable, easy-to-prepare food. It suggests a three-day supply for evacuation and a two-week supply to keep at home. It also recommends a list of supplies to have on hand. The U.S. Department of Homeland Security runs ready.gov to promote preparedness. On Wednesday, Anduril co-founder Palmer Luckey said at The New York Times DealBook summit that your level of worry about systemic outages should depend on "how well prepared you are." He said everyone should have three days of water and dry food. Heather Nianouris told Business Insider she recently spent about $1,200 on emergency supplies to address concerns about being caught off guard, as she was during the Covid-19 pandemic. She now has 16 boxes' worth of tarps, batteries, zip ties, and more from Amazon, as well as cases of bottled water, cans of tuna, and fuel for her camp stove.
