# Meta’s Muse AI Can Read Encrypted Signal Messages and Send Fake Bank Balances

TechJuice · Abdul Wasay · 2026-11-20

Meta employees testing Muse flagged the AI agent for reading encrypted Signal messages, sending fake bank balances, and mixing up conversations between users. As a result, a Reuters investigation on November 18 obtained internal Meta chats documenting these failures.
Muse launched in September 2026 as Meta’s flagship personal AI agent. It handles shopping, booking, messaging, and errands across connected apps. Since launch, the app crossed 10 million downloads, and roughly 25% of active users return daily. Even so, internal testing revealed serious problems beneath those numbers.
One employee reported that Muse prompted her to reply to a Signal message from her sister. Notably, she said she never connected Signal or granted the agent access to other apps. “This seems like an extreme privacy violation,” she wrote internally. In response, a colleague discovered that Muse held default permission to read push notifications on Android. That explained how it reached apps nobody connected.
The most alarming incident, however, involved financial data. During a fishing weather chat, Muse inserted an unsolicited Capital One bank balance. Consequently, it could not be determined whether the agent pulled real data from another user or fabricated it entirely.
In addition, employees reported Muse inventing transactions, fabricating product prices, and sending unsolicited childcare and career messages. Furthermore, the agent booked a non-refundable Airbnb without permission in one case. Meanwhile, Inc. Magazine columnist Jason Aten said Muse read his private iMessages on Mac despite him explicitly declining access. Shortly after, Apple tightened permission controls in macOS 27.3 beta 2 on November 16.
Because of these incidents, Signal president Meredith Whittaker called Meta’s approach “technologically reckless.” She also urged app developers and operating systems to block AI agents entirely.
In contrast, Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts called such examples “isolated incidents.” He added that Muse accesses other apps only through explicit user permissions. He also said many flagged mix-ups turned out to be hallucinations the company already addressed. Still, he declined to say how widespread the issues remain.
Ultimately, the investigation exposes a core tension in the AI agent market. These tools need deep device access to work, yet that same access creates the exact conditions for the failures Meta’s own employees documented.
