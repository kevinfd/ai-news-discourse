# Trump floats major tax on U.S. companies constructing data centers overseas

Axios · Julianna Bragg · 2026-11-20

President Trump on Friday said he would block U.S. companies from building data centers overseas or impose a "tremendous tax" on them if they do. Why it matters: Trump's push to keep AI infrastructure in the country comes just weeks after data center expansion became a midterm-election flashpoint , as voters raised concerns about the environmental and financial impacts they pose. What they're saying: Trump acknowledged that the announcement may come as a surprise, saying he had not yet discussed it with his own officials. "They're making so much money they don't know what to do with it," he said of the data centers being built across the country. Trump also claimed data center expansion would help lower Americans' costs and taxes. He also encouraged Americans to negotiate deals with companies proposing data centers in their communities. "They have plenty of money," he said. "Take it." Reality check: It's unclear how Trump would implement the proposed tax, since Congress has the Constitutional authority to impose federal taxes. Trump did not specify whether he would seek congressional approval or use existing executive powers to penalize companies that attempt to build data centers globally. Thought bubble from Axios' Ina Fried : Even companies that build data centers in the U.S. often need to have them in other places in order to have reliability, low latency and comply with various laws that require data to be processed in a specific region or country. Friction point: Trump's remarks followed a trilateral summit with China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin, but seemed torn between praising Xi and warning about the threats China poses to the U.S. "We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen," Trump said Friday. Yes, but: Trump also said Xi respected and congratulated the U.S. on its achievements in what he calls "super intelligence."
