# Anthropic researcher says Claude solved maths’ greatest riddle

The Hindu · AFP · 2026-11-20

A mathematician at AI company Anthropic said Friday (November 20, 2026) that he had used the firm’s Claude chatbot to prove the Riemann Hypothesis, a problem that has eluded mathematicians for more than 160 years.
“I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered proof of the Riemann Hypothesis,” Levent Alpoge wrote in a four-page note released Friday (November 20, 2026).
The claim has not been independently verified, and the proof has not yet been reviewed by outside experts.
First proposed by German mathematician Bernhard Riemann in 1859, the hypothesis concerns the distribution of prime numbers and is widely regarded as the most important open question in pure mathematics.
It is one of seven Millennium Prize Problems selected by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, which offers $1 million for a solution to each.
Under the institute’s rules, a solution must be published in a peer-reviewed journal and withstand two years of scrutiny.
Mr. Alpoge said the method also resolves a broader version of the problem known as the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis.
He said he and Claude had produced a version of the proof in Lean, a programming language that allows mathematical arguments to be checked by a computer, and that he was releasing only that version for now.
A full written argument would be extremely long, he said, adding that he had not yet mastered all of the mathematics involved and planned to write it up properly.
Mr. Alpoge said he had consulted Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei before announcing the result and acknowledged that its timing could be viewed cynically, without elaborating, though he was probably referring to this week’s record IPO.
In August, Anthropic said an unreleased version of Claude had improved a long-standing result related to the Riemann Hypothesis, while stressing that it had not proved it.
Anthropic did not immediately respond to a request for comment from AFP.
The claim comes two months after rival OpenAI said an AI model had solved another Millennium Prize Problem, on the equations that govern how fluids move.
Mathematicians say that proof appears correct but has yet to be fully vetted.
Mr. Alpoge, who had worked on related fluid problems with New York University mathematician Tristan Buckmaster, was at the centre of a dispute over credit for that work. OpenAI denied wrongdoing.
