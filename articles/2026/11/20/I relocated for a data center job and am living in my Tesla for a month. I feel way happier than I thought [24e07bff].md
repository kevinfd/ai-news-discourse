# I relocated for a data center job and am living in my Tesla for a month. I feel way happier than I thought.

Business Insider · Agnes Applegate, Agnes Applegate (aapplegate@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-20

Corey Cline has been living out of his Tesla while working at a data center in Omaha. Courtesy of Corey Cline Corey Cline moved 140 miles to Omaha, NE, to start a new role at a data center. After his living situation unraveled, Cline decided to live in his car until his new lease starts. He sleeps in his Tesla with Camp Mode on at a free charging station or in the back of hotel lots. This as-told-to essay is based on a conversation with Corey Cline, a 48-year-old AI infrastructure engineer at a data center in Omaha, Nebraska. His words have been edited for length and clarity. I recently moved 140 miles from Grand Island, Nebraska, to Omaha for a data center job. But I can't move into my new apartment until December 1, so until then, I'm living out of my Tesla. Taking this job was a leap of faith. But I wanted it badly enough to know I could figure it out. Right after I got to Omaha, I started staying at someone's house and put the rest of my things in a storage unit. However, my temporary living situation unraveled pretty quickly after I got here. I'd heard of some people who worked at data centers and lived out of their vehicles for a little while, and the more I thought about it, the more living out of my car for 30 days sounded like a better option than finding a new place to stay. So, for the past few weeks, I've been living out of my 2018 Tesla Model S. I kind of like it. I feel happier than I thought I would. I can't say I prefer it to apartment living — I haven't been into my new spot yet — but 30 days feels like a minimal sacrifice to be here.

 I got this data center job and relocated quickly I was married for a long time and got divorced in 2024. That kind of changed everything for me. After my divorce , I was renting a house in Grand Island and working as a Tesla Specialist and IT administrator at a car dealer. When I got this data center contract, I felt super fortunate to be working on IT in an environment that's at the center of what's high-tech and current. The negative buzz around these data centers on social media doesn't really get to me. There've been buildings with computers in them for quite a long time, and nobody really threw up much of a fuss about it before. When I first got to Omaha a few months ago, I had very different work hours from the person I was staying with. I had to be up in the middle of the night sometimes, and I started getting anxious toward the end of the day, knowing they were upset that they weren't getting any sleep because I was staying there. I could tell the arrangement was wearing on both of us, and I needed that anxiety to go away. I've had a number of people in my life and on Reddit, where I shared my story, tell me I could stay with them, but I feel bad doing that and don't want the same problems to occur. I decided to live in my Tesla for the next month My car is comfortable enough for five or six hours of sleep, and getting a motel room seemed like a waste of money. Also, I know I'm moving into an apartment soon, so that took the whole "oh my god I'm living in my car" feeling out of this experience. I see it more as a challenge to myself, and after getting some advice on Reddit, I've built a routine that works for the next few weeks. All of my stuff is in a storage unit, which costs about $120 a month. I got a membership at Planet Fitness to shower and work out there, which costs me around $15 a month and isn't too far from the data center. I also got a Rocket Car Wash membership for $10.99 a month because even if I'm living in my car , I still don't want it to look like I'm living in it. The data center, laundromat, and gym are all within a few miles of each other. Then there's a free charging station at a Hy-Vee grocery store, about 20 miles south of Omaha, where I plug my car in most nights and sleep there. Sometimes if I'm too tired, I'll pull into a hotel parking lot or somewhere like that that's well lit. Cline's 2018 Tesla Model S. Courtesy of Corey Cline My daily routine sleeping in my car Most days, I wake up around 3 or 3:30 am, go to the gym, do a quick workout, take a shower, and then burn some time between the gym and driving to work by sitting in the car watching YouTube. I head to the data center between 6 and 6:30 a.m. A lot of my clothes are packed in the back of the car. The data center has lockers, so I hang up some of my nicer shirts there to change into for work. I have a duffel bag I keep in the car with all my toiletries and such, too. After work, I like to stay busy, so I'm not just sitting in the car. I do laundry at the laundromat once or twice a week. After I drive to the Hy-Vee, I probably spend $15 to $20 grabbing something to eat there. I've done a few fast-food and restaurant outings, but the check afterward really sobers me up a bit. If I need anything that I don't have in the car, it's either in my storage unit, which is pretty close to the Hy-Vee, or it's at the data center . I usually go to bed around 9 or 10 p.m., and I sleep in the driver's seat, reclined all the way back, with a giant pillow and blanket. The pillow I have is about four feet long, and I smush it around the sides of my head so I don't move too much at night. I also keep camp mode on so the Tesla stays at a certain temperature all night. I was worried it would affect my well-being and mental health, but I feel happier Living in a car is more work than apartment living. I have to drive to different locations for laundry, showering, and other needs. But I don't have to clean a house right now. I don't have to pay any apartment bills . There's more incentive to just save money until I move into my brand new apartment next month. I've already lost some weight, and I have no excuse for not being in the best shape of my life after this because I have to go to the gym every day. The only time I doubt sticking this out and getting a short-term rental or something is when I talk to other people about what I'm doing. But their limits aren't mine, and they automatically see this as something bad, when it isn't. I've told a few people that I have contact with a lot, but otherwise I've just been low-key about it. I don't want to hear the criticisms. I don't want people to feel obligated to offer me a place to stay. I'm good where I'm at, and I'll ask for help when I need it, but I haven't needed any yet. If I were doing this long-term , then I'd be more open to hearing people out. But hearing advice from people who've never done it and don't understand the circumstances feels like a waste of energy. This is the first time I've done anything like this, and that's why it kind of makes me feel good. I'm not ashamed of it. We've all just been taught how to do things a certain way, and anything outside that is often seen as weird, but 30 days from now, I'll be in an apartment like everybody else.
