# iOS 27.3 Apple Intelligence, Siri AI usage limits: What to know

Mashable · Matt Binder · 2026-11-20

iOS 27.3 Apple Intelligence, Siri AI usage limits: What to know
All good things must come to an end implement usage limits.
Just a few months after Apple finally launched the long-awaited Siri AI, its upgraded voice assistant powered by Apple Intelligence, the company will now implement daily usage limits. These new restrictions will be implemented along with the launch of iOS 27.3.
What does this mean? Basically, if a user hits those limits, they will need to upgrade their paid iCloud+ account or wait for the daily limits to reset. However, not every Siri AI request counts towards users' daily limits.
So, what counts towards your daily Siri AI limits? How much does it cost to upgrade? Lets break down everything you need to know about the new Siri AI usage limits.
What AI features will have daily usage limits?
According to Apple, "Siri AI and Apple Intelligence features are available for free for users with a supported device set to a supported language." However, "some features rely on powerful server models and have limits."
Apple says the following features will have daily usage limits:
- Siri AI: Advanced tasks
- Images: Intelligent Photo Editing Tools, which include Clean Up, Extend, and Spatial Reframing, as well as Image Playground
- Workflows and automation: AFM Cloud and AFM Cloud Pro models in Shortcuts
- Third-party apps that use Apple Foundation Models in Private Cloud Compute
What are the daily limits?
Apple has not published exactly what the daily usage limits are for Apple Intelligence or Siri AI. The company's Support page simply describes it as a "generous daily usage limit."
If a user reaches their limit, they can subscribe to an iCloud+ plan to increase their daily usage limits.
Notably, each of those above categories has its own unique daily usage limits. These are not shared between those features, which means a user can hit the daily limit for one but not the others.
Daily usage limits are connected at the Apple account level and are implemented across all Apple devices, including iOS devices, Mac, and Apple TV.
How will I know if I hit my daily limits?
When a user reaches their daily limit, they will receive a prompt after making a request that counts towards those limits. Advanced requests are paused until the daily limit resets or the user upgrades to an iCloud+ plan.

"Daily Request Limit Reached," the prompt reads on iOS. "You can continue with basic requests."
The prompt also includes a button to upgrade to an iCloud+ plan to increase those daily limits.
How much does an iCloud+ plan increase daily AI usage limits?
As previously mentioned, Apple isn't sharing exactly what those Apple Intelligence and Siri AI limits are. However, they have shared just how many times those "generous" limits are increased when upgrading to an iCloud+ plan.

- 200GB: $2.99 per month – Up to 2x daily usage limit
- 2TB: $9.99 per month – Up to 5x daily usage limit
- 6TB: $29.99 per month – Up to 10x daily usage limit
- 12TB: $59.99 per month – Up to 20x daily usage limit
The 50GB iCloud+ plan, which Apple sells for $0.99 per month, does not include any increase in daily usage limits for Apple Intelligence and Siri AI.
If you are already subscribed to an iCloud+ plan, these limit increases will be included in your current subscription as an added benefit to your current paid plan.
When do daily usage limits reset?
Apple says that all feature categories will have their daily limits reset at approximately midnight in the user's current time zone.
According to Apple, users who are travelling internationally will have their limits reset "around midnight in the central time zone of the country or region of your Apple Account." Users who share an iCloud+ plan with a Family Sharing group will see their limits reset at around midnight of the time zone of the iCloud+ plan purchaser. Users who aren't signed into their Apple Account will see their limits reset around 24 hours after they first used that feature category that day.
Unused limits from the previous day do not roll over.
What Siri AI tasks count towards Siri AI's daily usage limits?
Starting with iOS 27.3, Siri AI will also receive limits for certain "advanced" tasks. Apple says that basic tasks will continue to be unlimited and will work even when the daily usage limits for advanced tasks are reached.
You may be wondering what a "basic" task is and what is an "advanced" task, the latter of which will have daily usage limits.
Apple provided the following examples as a basic task, which users will be able to utilize without limits:
- "Set a timer for three minutes."
- "Text Mark that Annika's party is at 123 Main Street.”
As for advanced tasks, Apple provides these examples:
- "Text Mark the address for Annika's party, and tell him what time we need to leave from home to make it on time."
- "Add a summary of my San Francisco trip note to this email draft."
Based on those examples, it appears advanced tasks involve multi-step requests that require server-side processing.
Beyond Apple's official published examples, Apple leaker pdfu has uncovered Apple's more detailed instructions given to Siri AI telling the assistant how to decide if a user has reached their limits for advanced tasks. In an X post, the leaker explained Siri will take limits into account if a user request:
- uses information from one app, from what is on screen or from a message, in a different app, or needs a result from one step used in a different step or app (such as a looked-up fact typed into a field)
- contains two or more separate asks in one sentence, even in the same app (extra details of one action, such as a cc or a time, are still one ask)
- asks about or summarizes the content of a photo, a camera view or an attachment (editing, searching and albums are fine)
- deletes, archives, junks, moves, marks or changes a set (all, every, these or a whole category) of the user’s emails, notes, reminders, messages, photos, files, contacts or events
- sets a device action or a notification to run on a time or trigger (not alarms, timers, reminders or calendar events), notifies someone when a person arrives or leaves, or builds or edits a Shortcut or automation
- is deep research or a multi-step task done on the user’s behalf
- asks you to compose or create new content (a story, poem, essay, plan, recipe, image, or an email or message from a topic rather than wording the user gives), or to rewrite in a tone other than friendly, professional or concise
A user asking a third-party AI assistant like ChatGPT for an advanced request is never limited, even if a user reaches their daily usage limit for the day.
According to pdfu, tasks like "proofreading, rewriting in a friendly, professional or concise tone, summaries as key points, a list or a table, typing or sending wording the user gives, a single direct operation in any app, even when Siri must look the target up first (the closest bakery, my cousin), 'this' or 'that' within its own app, running a Shortcut by name, a reminder for yourself by time or place, Find My, sharing your ETA or location, and all your home accessories (lights, doors, locks), alarms, timers or device settings" all fall under basic tasks.
So far, Apple hasn't released much more on these new daily usage limits for Apple Intelligence and Siri AI. Hopefully, before iOS 27.3 comes out, Apple provides users with more specific information regarding what does and doesn't count towards their daily usage limits.
