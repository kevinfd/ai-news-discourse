# Tech takes a beating as Anthropic surge fails to ease AI worries

Channel NewsAsia · CNA · 2026-11-20

Tech takes a beating as Anthropic surge fails to ease AI worries
Anthropic's record IPO failed to settle nerves on trading floors as they deal with the fallout from the crisis engulfing AI titan Nvidia.
HONG KONG: Tech-rich Seoul, Tokyo and Taipei led losses across Asia on Friday (Nov 20) as Anthropic's sparkling debut failed to muster a recovery in sentiment towards the AI trade, with nerves frayed by worries over crisis-hit Nvidia, profitability and regulation.
After its record-breaking initial public offering, Claude-creator Anthropic soared 17 per cent on its first day as ordinary investors sought to grab a piece of the new kid on the block.
But the rally did nothing to drag other, more established firms higher as the uncertainty that has hung over the tech sector in recent months sapped confidence, sending Wall Street deep into the red.
Magnificent Seven members who led a scorching surge over the past two years continued a retreat that has characterised trade since the summer, as investors fret over AI profitability, eye-watering spending, rising borrowing costs and geopolitics.
Microsoft, Apple, Amazon and Tesla were all well down, along with Nvidia - which is battling to deal with the fallout from a spy scandal that has led to the resignation of pivotal leader Jensen Huang.
The losses continued on US futures markets, with Anthropic dropping more than 4 percent.
Traders have also been spooked by the US Commerce Department's move to close a loophole allowing Chinese companies to access Nvidia's powerful AI chips remotely, adding to worries that a new Democratic-led Congress will impose further regulations
The selling followed through to Asia, where Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei - home to the region's leading AI-linked firms - tumbled between 5 and 6.2 per cent.
Japan's Nikkei 225 was dragged by losses of 11 per cent in Kioxia and 14.5 per cent in tech investor SoftBank, while TSMC shed 4.8 per cent and MediaTek tanked 10 per cent as Taiwan's main stock market was dragged more than 6 per cent lower.
SK hynix and Samsung took the brunt as South Korea's Kospi dived more than 5 per cent.
Hong Kong, Shanghai, Sydney, Singapore, Bangkok, Jakarta and Manila also took a pummelling.
The flight from risk boosted the safe-haven yen, helped by a surge in Japanese inflation last month that stoked bets on another central bank interest rate hike.
US Treasuries were also boosted, with yields on most term-lengths dropping. Yields fall as demand sends prices higher.
"With volatility elevated, equity futures breaking support and high-beta assets under pressure, traders will increasingly look for signs of capitulation, stabilisation or a catalyst capable of reversing sentiment," said Chris Weston at Pepperstone.
"The risk is that selling becomes self-reinforcing. As volatility rises, risk limits tighten, leveraged positions are reduced and systematic strategies can add to the pressure.
"Whether Anthropic's IPO proves to be a defining moment for the AI trade remains to be seen. For now, the immediate priority is watching how markets settle into the weekend and whether buyers can regain control next week."
Investors are keeping tabs on developments in the Nvidia case as California announced it was investigating whether the firm misled the public and investors about its chips ending up in China, despite federal export controls.
Huang resigned last week after reports alleged he had multiple extramarital affairs, including with a woman named Alice Huang who is a suspected Chinese spy.
Alice Huang, who is no relation, is the former director of Megaspeed, a cloud computing company based in Singapore that was investigated by local officials in 2025 over potential export control violations.
Jensen Huang has agreed to attend a closed-door questioning session about national security issues with Congressional officials on Dec 1.
Oil prices rose on worries about fresh violence in the Middle East and after the International Energy Agency said the crisis in the Middle East had created the "largest threat" to energy security in history.
The IEA said in its annual World Energy Outlook that consumers were increasingly footing the bill as the world runs out of ways to absorb the impact of the conflict on energy supplies.
