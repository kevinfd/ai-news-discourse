# Deloitte suspended by WA government after AI report debacle

ABC News Australia · Courtney Withers · 2026-11-25

Deloitte suspended by WA government after AI report on university mergers
In short:
Deloitte has been banned from working for the WA government for three months after it produced a report riddled with AI-generated errors.
WA Tertiary Education Minister Tony Buti said he was "embarrassed" by the report, and the government is trying to recoup its cost.
Deloitte labelled the report's errors "minor".
The WA government has suspended accounting firm Deloitte from the bulk of its government work for up to three months over an AI-riddled report it produced on a controversial university merger plan.
Deloitte was enlisted to deliver a taxpayer-funded cost-benefit analysis of its proposed merger of three of the state's public universities — Curtin, Murdoch and the University of Western Australia.
But last month it was revealed that the report was littered with errors generated by artificial intelligence, prompting the government to order the firm to refund its $166,000 fee.
Now the government has gone a step further, banning Deloitte from most government work for up to three months.
"While the errors contained in the report do not affect the modelling or the findings, they are nonetheless disappointing," a state government spokesperson said.
"The WA government had made the decision to suspend Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu from the Audit and Financial Advisory Services Common Use Arrangements and Information and Communications Technology Services Common Use Arrangements."
Minister embarrassed
The ABC understands the firm was told about the decision today.
The action follows comments from Tertiary Education Minister Tony Buti earlier this week saying the refund requested for the report had not yet been paid back.
"We are demanding that the refund be looked at, but if that doesn't take place, there are other measures that we can put in place," Mr Buti said on Monday.
"I'm incredibly disappointed at Deloitte's [conduct] … I'm very embarrassed by what happened, but incredibly disappointed in their performance."
Mr Buti said discussions had "taken place" but noted a "satisfactory outcome" had not been reached with the firm.
Deloitte labelled the errors in the report "minor" at the time they surfaced and said they did not affect the findings of the cost-benefit analysis.
However, the firm has previously been caught using AI in consultancy reports for government.
Last year, Deloitte used AI to produce a $440,000 federal government report that contained similar errors and made-up quotes.
The firm apologised, reissued the report and has paid back nearly $100,000.
The ABC has contacted Deloitte for comment.
The government has said data from Deloitte's merger report, along with new information, will be "interrogated by the university reform business case".
Plan continues
Mr Buti has continued to pursue the proposed university merger despite concerns raised by two of three parties — Curtin and Murdoch.
Students and staff have also been vocal in their objection, taking to the steps of parliament last month to protest.
According to the WA government website, potential benefits of the merger include "creating a globally competitive university of scale that receives more research funding, has increased domestic student numbers, higher regional representation and better course completion rates".
"We often as politicians get criticised for thinking short term," Education Minister Sabine Winton told reporters on Tuesday.
"It is energising to be part of a government that is having a long-term think around what is best for this state, and I welcome that contest of ideas."
