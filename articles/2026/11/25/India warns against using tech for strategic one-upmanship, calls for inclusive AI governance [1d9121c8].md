# India warns against using tech for strategic one-upmanship, calls for inclusive AI governance

The Hindu · PTI · 2026-11-25

India has voiced concern over the instrumentalisation of technology for strategic one-upmanship through export control and licensing regimes, calling out the tendency to treat countries of the Global South as mere consumers as it underscored that technological transformation must not deepen existing inequalities.
Addressing the Security Council’s high-level open debate on Maintenance of International Peace and Security on ‘Conflict Resolution in an Era of Rapid Technological Change’, India’s Permanent Representative to the UN Ambassador Harish Parvathaneni said AI systems can inadvertently perpetuate biases related to gender, language and socio-economic background.
“We note with concern the emerging medium- and long-term trends of technology denial and the instrumentalisation of technology for strategic one-upmanship through export control and licensing regimes, and a tendency to treat countries of the Global South as mere consumers without any agency or stake in how emerging technologies such as AI are developed, are regulated and are deployed,” Mr. Parvathaneni said.
“AI must not reduce human beings to mere data points or raw material. India supports inclusive international cooperation on technology governance, including through the United Nations, with due regard to national priorities and developmental needs,” he said.
He emphasised that technology should serve humanity, strengthen international cooperation, and advance the cause of lasting peace.
“Technology may inform human judgement but cannot and should not replace it,” he said.
India also asserted that amid the rapid integration of artificial intelligence into military and intelligence spheres, responsibility for use of lethal autonomous weapons systems must always rest with humans.
“Rapid technological advances are fundamentally transforming the nature of conflict and the international security landscape. Artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, and emerging digital technologies present unprecedented opportunities but also introduce new vulnerabilities and risks across the entire conflict cycle,” Mr. Parvathaneni said.
He added that the rapid integration of artificial intelligence into military, intelligence, and civilian spheres has fundamentally transformed the landscape of international peace and security.
India stressed that the application of emerging technologies in conflict settings must be responsible, human-centric, respect sovereignty, and adhere to international law.
“Emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems raise legitimate legal and ethical questions. India is committed to addressing these within the framework of the Convention on Prohibitions or Restrictions on the Use of Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW), which seeks to balance military necessity with humanitarian considerations,” Mr. Parvathaneni said.
He underscored that human judgement and oversight remain essential, taking into account the operational context and characteristics of the weapons system. “Responsibility for the use of such systems must always rest with humans,” the Indian envoy said, adding that leveraging technology for protection must be a cross-cutting and cross-sectoral measure.
“It must be used for the protection of civilians and of peacekeepers and should be leveraged for the protection of humanitarian personnel.”
India called on the international community to address threats posed by misuse of emerging technologies such as AI.
“The risks associated with AI are real, and we must address them without any hesitation. The potential misuse of these emerging technologies by terrorists and non-State actors is a matter of grave concern. Incomplete, inaccurate, or deliberately manipulated data through AI can undermine decision-making,” he said.
He told the Council that India has made robust advancements in digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, and emerging technologies, reflecting a clear emphasis on protecting critical information infrastructure and ensuring the continuity of essential civilian services.
Addressing the Security Council Open Debate, Secretary General Antonio Guterres voiced concern that today’s wars have become “proving grounds” for AI-enabled targeting.
Asserting that killer robots must have no place in the world’s future, Mr. Guterres reiterated his call to urgently launch negotiations for a legally binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapons systems.
“AI is driving greater autonomy across military functions. From intelligence gathering to target selection, it is reported that algorithms are increasingly shaping life-and-death decisions,” he said, adding that these capabilities are within the reach of both States and non-State armed groups.
“And the potential integration of AI with nuclear command, control and communication systems is a recipe for catastrophe. As long as nuclear weapons exist, the decision to use them must rest with human beings alone,” Mr. Guterres said.
“When civilians are killed, or human rights are violated, the answer must never be ‘the algorithm did it’,” Mr. Guterres said.
The UN chief added that humans must always retain control over life-and-death decisions – guided by international law and ethical principles.
“Technology companies must reckon with the legal risks they may face if their services contribute to violations of international law. And no country should design, develop, deploy, or use military applications of AI in armed conflict that violate international law, international humanitarian law, or international human rights law,” he said.
