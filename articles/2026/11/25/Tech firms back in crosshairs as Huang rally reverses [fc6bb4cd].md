# Tech firms back in crosshairs as Huang rally reverses

Channel NewsAsia · CNA · 2026-11-25

Tech firms back in crosshairs as Huang rally reverses
Taipei, Tokyo and Seoul - home of most of the region's key AI-linked firms - were all down, a day after a surge on the back of the news of Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO.
HONG KONG: Technology firms once again tumbled on Wednesday (Nov 25) as the euphoria that greeted the return of Nvidia's Jensen Huang was washed away by the now-common panic and uncertainty over the AI boom.
News that the poster child of the artificial intelligence explosion had returned less than two weeks after resigning over extramarital affairs - including with an alleged Chinese spy - sent Asian markets into a swoon on Tuesday.
Traders were reassured about the ongoing presence of a fixer and visionary to an industry that has fuelled a tech revolution and a global economic boom.
But the mood appeared to have already soured as analysts pointed to the reality that there were still huge worries about the colossal sums pumped into the AI buildout and when they would see returns, if any.
After explosive gains on Tuesday, Asian stock markets were back in the doldrums.
Taipei, Tokyo and Seoul - home of most of the region's key AI-linked firms - sank eight per cent, more than three per cent and more than five per cent, respectively.
That came a day after surging 9 per cent, 3.9 per cent and over 6 per cent, respectively.
The losses were led by Taiwan's TSMC and MediaTek, Japanese chip giants Kioxia and Advantest, and South Korean titans SK hynix and Samsung.
There was also heavy selling in Shanghai, Sydney and Singapore. However, Hong Kong, Wellington and Manila edged up.
The rout followed a return to reality on Wall Street, where Nvidia - which had rocketed more than 31 per cent Monday - shed almost six per cent, while AI giant Anthropic lost 6.5 per cent to fall below last week's US$83 IPO price.
AMD, Intel and Micron also took a hammering after the previous day's surge.
The Nasdaq shed 1.5 per cent and the S&P 500 lost 0.5 per cent but the Dow rose, with analysts pointing out that traders were rotating into other sectors.
"DEAD-CAT BOUNCE"
"The champagne from (the) Nvidia resurrection had barely lost its fizz before Wall Street was back reaching for the sick bags," said SPI Asset Management's Stephen Innes.
"What looked like the mother of all relief rallies has already acquired the rather less flattering characteristics of a dead-cat bounce, with the Nasdaq taking another beating (and) Nvidia surrendering a sizeable chunk of its extraordinary gains.
He said: "Professional investors (were) apparently using the opening provided by the Jensen Huang comeback to unload technology exposure rather than rebuild it".
"And that, unfortunately, is what happens when a market rallies on the back of forced buying rather than genuine conviction."
And BNZ's Jason Wong added: "Despite Huang’s return, some key issues continue to overhang the AI sector, including whether investment in the sector will provide an adequate return and the threat of regulation under a new administration from next year."
Nvidia also remains under scrutiny from investigators including at the FBI and on Capitol Hill for allegedly helping Chinese firms gain access to the firm's top-line chips.
Investors will be keeping an eye on the release of the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge of inflation later in the day, which could play a role in its interest rate decision next month.
With price gains still far above target, officials are widely expected to hike, but their post-meeting statement will be pored over for an idea about the bank's plans for the new year.
In company news, Chinese electronics giant Xiaomi jumped 8 per cent in Hong Kong on optimism for the performance of the company's higher-end smartphones even as an AI-driven memory chip supply crunch hits profits.
