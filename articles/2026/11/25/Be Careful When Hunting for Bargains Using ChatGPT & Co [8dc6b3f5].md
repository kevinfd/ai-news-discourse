# Be Careful When Hunting for Bargains Using ChatGPT & Co.

Bluewin · Martin Abgottspon · 2026-11-25

Found an error?
Report now
Poor AI advice can quickly lead to purchases that amount to nothing.
Gemini @ blueNews
More and more people are using ChatGPT and similar tools to search for discounts while shopping. This is especially true on Black Friday. Yet, when it comes to prices, discounts, and retailers—of all things—the AI is surprisingly often off the mark.
- AI chatbots are helpful when it comes to choosing the right product, but they often get prices and discounts wrong.
- In a test, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity directed users to risky or fake online stores in up to 15 percent of their responses.
- You'll find real savings mainly on vacuum cleaners, TVs, and headphones, while smartphones and game consoles are hardly any cheaper.
Ask ChatGPT for the best Black Friday deal on a robot vacuum, and in just a few seconds, you’ll get a neat list of models, prices, and retailers. This comes across as professional and saves time. Whether the prices are accurate, the discount is genuine, and the store even exists, however, is another matter entirely.
AI really shines when it comes to questions rather than numbers. Which robot vacuum can handle carpets and pet hair, and what do buyers consistently complain about in hundreds of reviews? ChatGPT, Gemini, and others answer these kinds of questions faster and more clearly than any product page.
As soon as real Swiss francs are on the line, the facade crumbles. In the same test, Claude was repeatedly swayed by discounts off the list price, even though the product had been significantly cheaper in stores for some time. Other prices were out of date, shipping costs were missing, and in some cases, the AI estimated price trends instead of looking them up. Even OpenAI admits that its shopping assistant can make mistakes regarding price and availability.
On top of that, there’s something new. Since the end of August, ChatGPT has also been displaying ads in Switzerland—both in the free version and in the affordable Go plan. The ads appear clearly labeled below the response, and OpenAI emphasizes that they do not influence the answers. Especially when searching for deals, it’s still worth taking a close look to distinguish between recommendations and paid placements.
- Get advice instead of letting someone else do the buying for you. Have the AI generate a shortlist with exact model numbers, and then buy it yourself.
- Request the source and date. A good prompt might be something like, “Search the web for current prices at Swiss retailers, including shipping; for each price, list the source and date retrieved, and do not estimate price trends.”
- Check price trends yourself. On Toppreise.ch and at Digitec and Galaxus, you can see how much a product has cost over the past few months and set up a price alert.
- Street price instead of list price. What matters is what a device actually cost in the weeks leading up to the purchase, not the crossed-out list price.
- Go directly to the stores. Don't blindly click on links in the chat; instead, visit familiar retailers yourself. For stores you're not familiar with, it's worth looking up other customers' experiences, and if possible, pay by invoice, as recommended by BACS.
The situation is even more delicate when it comes to retailers. In September, the brand protection firm Podqi analyzed 6,795 shopping responses from ChatGPT, Gemini, and “Perplexity,” as The Verge reports. Perplexity directed users to risky sites—such as fake brand stores or abandoned domains—in 15 percent of its responses, ChatGPT in 8 percent, and Gemini in 4 percent. In about nine out of ten of these cases, there was no warning at all.
At the same time, scammers are stepping up their game. The Federal Office for Cybersecurity (BACS) issued a warning on November 10 about a surge in fake online stores that, thanks to AI, offer error-free text, realistic product images, and credible reviews—and in some cases copy the layout of well-known Swiss retailers. For a chatbot, such sites are likely no easier to spot than they are for humans.

- Now click on the bell icon. You can now enter the price (or lower) at which you'd like to be notified.
