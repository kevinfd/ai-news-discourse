# Treasury CIO Is Vibe Coding a ‘Civilization’ Clone During Treasury Department Hack

404 Media · Jason Koebler · 2026-11-25

As the Treasury Department dealt with the fallout of a hack that took down some of its services, its Chief Information Officer was pushing updates to Legatum, his vibe coded Civilization clone.
Sam Corcos is the cofounder of the A16z-backed glucose monitoring health startup called Levels; He became the Treasury Department’s Chief Information Officer as a DOGE hire near the beginning of Trump’s second term, and has been a leading AI evangelist within the administration since. Corcos’s department is in charge of cybersecurity for the Treasury Department, and so it is possibly concerning that while the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) is dealing with a “cybersecurity incident” that has taken a critical bank compliance reporting panel down, he has been actively pushing balance patches, new theme music for the Aztec faction, and critical tweaks to the in-game player assistance tooltips for Legatum, his vibe-coded “turn-based strategy game in the tradition of Civilization VI.”
Legatum is similar to Civilization VI “on purpose,” according to a readme on Corcos’s Github. “If you have played Civilization VI you already know the basics: found cities, pick research, queue production, fight on hexes […] Legatum is built largely with AI coding agents [both Claude and OpenAI’s Codex], and all of its art is AI-generated. Most of the art comes from OpenAI's image models through ChatGPT.”
Legatum is also open source, and has been getting updated in real time while I am writing this article and after a notice went up on the FinCEN website that said it was “investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems.”
While newer AI models can do a lot on their own and can often work for hours or days without much human intervention, shoddy or lazy prompting by Corcos has resulted in a situation where Claude is pushing updates that come as the result of direct feedback from Corcos about improvements that can be made to the game. Some of the most recent updates include direct quotes from Corcos about how he’s feeling about the game, indicating that he is actively involved in its development.
One of the most recent updates, pushed after the FinCEN hack, includes, for example, a new special unit for Egypt, which came at the explicit direction of Corcos. “I like Chariot archers for egypt, their current special unit is lame,” Corcos told Claude. That update was pushed while a Treasury Department website displayed a warning noting that “as a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline.”
“FinCEN is working diligently to return all systems to normal operations as soon as possible,” FinCEN added. Luckily, Legatum players will not have to wait any longer for more faction-appropriate music as Corcos and his AI have been working diligently to improve the game’s “production value,” according to a Github commit pushed after midnight on Wednesday. This new update includes “Music by era, the Aztecs' own music, the sea, and gunpowder-age battle sounds.”
The federal government has suffered a series of high-profile outages and hacks in recent weeks; the FinCEN hack followed earlier hacks at the Central Intelligence Agency, the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence that were carried out by hackers using the Artex artificial intelligence hacking tool, according to the New York Times. In addition to his work as a hobbyist game vibecoder and Treasury CIO, Corcos was involved in several General Services Administration projects, including one at Login.gov, which we previously reported was to have AI integrated throughout.
Corcos previously seized control of several U.S. government Github repositories and was accused earlier this fall of deleting comments that pointed out regulatory compliance violations associated with the way the US Web Design System was attempting to automate accessibility testing. At the time I’m writing this, Corcos has not deleted a user-reported issue with Legatum that states “treasury CIO is too busy vibe coding instead of doing his job.” That issue is full of people clowning on Corcos.
Earlier this fall, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Corcos he could no longer work on AI policy after he held several unauthorized meetings with OpenAI and Anthropic, according to Politico. In those meetings, “Corcos portrayed himself as an industry ally and warned that other government officials, reacting to fears over the latest AI models, were attempting to impose a burdensome licensing regime that would hamper innovation.” Politico did not report on whether Corcos had sought feedback from OpenAI and Anthropic on Legatum, which “is not affiliated with, endorsed by or derived from Sid Meier’s Civilization,” a notice on its GitHub reads.
Crucially, players can play Legatum in a web browser: “Nothing to install.” Something you can’t do in a web browser, at the moment, is reliably file certain compliance documents about the Banking Secrecy Act with the Treasury Department; “FinCEN is extending deadlines for filers impacted by the disruption,” it said. I did play Legatum for several minutes in my web browser but then got bored and decided to go searching through Corcos’s GitHub instead.
Corcos’s most recent tweet reads “I believe it is reasonable to ask employees what they are working on,” in response to the news that some federal workers would be ordered to send updates about what they are working on. Corcos, it seems, has been working on making sure that the walls in Legatum are better in the mid-to-late game and to fix graphical glitches with walls: “feels like we could do better,” he told Claude.
The Treasury Department did not respond to a request for comment.
