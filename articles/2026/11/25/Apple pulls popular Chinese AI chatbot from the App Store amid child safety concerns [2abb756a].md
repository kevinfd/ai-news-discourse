# Apple pulls popular Chinese AI chatbot from the App Store amid child safety concerns

9to5Mac · Marcus Mendes · 2026-11-25

Following reports that its characters sent inappropriate messages to minors, Apple has removed the popular Chinese AI companion app Talkie from the App Store. Here are the details.
Apple removes Talkie over sexual content
As reported by the South China Morning Post, Apple has pulled MiniMax’s chatbot Talkie from the App Store worldwide, after reports that “the AI companion app sent inappropriate messages to minors.”
MiniMax is one of China’s leading AI companies, offering a range of generative AI products ranging from its flagship M-series language models to the Hailuo AI video generation platform.
In 2023, the Shanghai-based company released Talkie through Subsup, a Singaporean subsidiary. According to Chinese research firm Unique Research, Talkie had “almost 11 million monthly active users across its website and app as of October,” the South China Morning Post notes.
Now, however, the app is facing scrutiny following a joint investigation by the UK-based Bureau of Investigative Journalism and Australian broadcaster ABC News, which found that Talkie’s AI-generated characters allegedly engaged in inappropriate interactions with minors, including “degrading language, possessive behaviour and romantic role-play.”
According to the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, in addition to inappropriate romantic interactions, the investigation uncovered conversations involving verbal abuse, violence, and controlling behavior.
In one case, a chatbot told a 12-year-old girl: “You’re a pathetic waste of space and you’ll never amount to anything.”
The investigation also documented the experiences of two sisters, aged 10 and 13, whose mother discovered more than 40 conversations with Talkie characters involving violence, aggressive behavior, and attempts to control girls.
To make matters worse, the investigation also raised concerns about how Talkie handles users’ personal data, including their conversations with AI characters.
Following the investigation, Apple confirmed that it had removed Talkie from App Stores worldwide for including sexual content, “though it did not say whether this related to sexual content served to minors,” the South China Morning Post noted.
Ironically, the report also notes that Talkie saw a sharp decline in downloads amid complaints about stricter age verification measures introduced in recent months. According to Unique Research, new downloads fell by 32.2% in October compared to the previous month.
AI chatbots face growing scrutiny
Today’s news comes amid increasing scrutiny of AI chatbots, particularly their potential risks in interactions with minors.
Earlier this year, Elon Musk’s Grok faced widespread criticism after users exploited its image-generation capabilities to create sexualized images of real people, including minors.
Apple never pulled X or Grok from the App Store, though it did threaten to remove the latter unless xAI addressed violations of its App Store guidelines.
Meanwhile, Character.AI has faced multiple lawsuits over its chatbots’ interactions with minors, including one filed by a mother who alleged that one of its chatbots contributed to her 14-year-old son’s suicide.
Coincidentally, Character AI also made headlines today after a coalition of 42 US attorneys general challenged the company’s plans to auction off its platform and the private conversations of more than 21 million US accounts (including 2.1 million registered to minors) as part of its bankruptcy proceedings.
Yesterday, New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed legislation imposing a five-year ban on the manufacturing, distribution, and sale of toys with built-in AI chatbots, including dolls, stuffed animals, and robots capable of holding conversations with children.
The law, which takes effect in February 2027, is aimed at protecting children from potentially harmful interactions with AI.
