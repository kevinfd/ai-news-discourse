# ‘Free Pop Is Over’ for OpenAI After Anthropic’s Rout, Tech Analyst Luke Lango Says: But a Late-2027 IPO Could ‘Ride the Recovery’

AOL · Radhika Anilkumar Nadig · 2026-11-25

‘Free Pop Is Over’ for OpenAI After Anthropic’s Rout, Tech Analyst Luke Lango Says: But a Late-2027 IPO Could ‘Ride the Recovery’
OpenAI can no longer count on an easy debut, market experts told Benzinga, after Anthropic, PBC (NASDAQ:ANT) fell about 28% from its IPO high, and Chinese artificial intelligence stocks show how fast a hot listing can fade.
OpenAI Faces a Tougher IPO Debut
“For OpenAI, the free pop is over,” tech analyst Luke Lango said in emailed comments to Benzinga on Tuesday. “Public markets will price OpenAI against where Anthropic trades, not its debut-day hype.”
Anthropic priced its record IPO at $83 on Nov. 19, raising $81.3 billion, and hit a record high of $115.10 before dropping below the offer price this week.
Lango said first-wave AI model makers “trade on scarcity first and fundamentals second, and scarcity always runs out,” producing a predictable pattern of a pop followed by a drop.
Anthropic trades at roughly 40 times trailing sales and under 20 times its run rate, he said, which is “pricey, not a meme valuation.”
According to Jay Ritter, director of The IPO Initiative at the University of Florida, OpenAI “will have to lower its expectations” for its share price if Anthropic does poorly.
The next two months matter more than the first few days of trading, he added, though an Anthropic decline driven by OpenAI winning share with a superior product would be bullish for the ChatGPT maker.
OpenAI could go public next year, and Lango believes the AI slowdown should bottom in mid-2027, so a late-2027 debut “could ride the recovery instead of fighting the selloff.”
China’s AI IPO Boom Offers a Cautionary Tale
Chinese AI model makers Z.AI Co. Ltd., also known as Zhipu, and MiniMax Group Inc. offer an early look at how a hot AI listing can unwind.
Both surged after their January Hong Kong debuts and have since fallen sharply from their peaks. As of Wednesday’s close, Zhipu was more than 90% below its June high, though still about 106% above its IPO price. MiniMax has fallen 93% from its record high and nearly 75% from its first-day close, trading about 47% below its IPO price.
Lango called both “retail frenzies” and said Zhipu’s valuation, briefly above 1,000 times sales, lost its premium once lock-ups expired, though he believes “China is winning the giveaway game, and America is winning the money game.”

Hot IPO, Cold Market
Lango called Anthropic’s listing “a hot IPO in a cold market” and said the drop is “price discovery, not a collapse.”
Facebook “dropped by about 50%” in the four months after its 2012 IPO, then became “an enormous success,” Ritter noted.
IPOs overall carry roughly a 50% chance of a negative return over three years, he said.
Price Action: On Wednesday, Anthropic closed 1.08% higher at $82.79 and climbed 1.76% to $84.25 in extended trading.
