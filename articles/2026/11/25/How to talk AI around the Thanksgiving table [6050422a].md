# How to talk AI around the Thanksgiving table

NPR · Kevin Roose · 2026-11-25

How to talk AI around the Thanksgiving table
You're gathered with family this Thanksgiving when the topic of AI comes up. How can you have a productive, fact-based conversation?
SCOTT DETROW, HOST:
Thanksgiving dinner conversations can be fraught in some families. It is best to avoid certain topics, like politics or religion, and 2026 has given us a new land mine of small talk - AI. Machine Gods is here to help. Machine Gods is the new podcast from NPR about the AI future. In this excerpt, hosts Kevin Roose and Casey Newton offer some friendly advice for how to navigate AI dinner table talk.
(SOUNDBITE OF MUSIC)
CASEY NEWTON, BYLINE: Casey here with a quick disclosure at the top. As regular Machine Gods listeners know, my fiance works for Anthropic.
KEVIN ROOSE, BYLINE: All right, Casey...
NEWTON: Yeah.
ROOSE: ...We wanted to do something a little seasonal. We wanted to help our listeners with their Thanksgivings.
NEWTON: Yes. You know, many podcasts will do advice around the holidays, and we got a question that it felt like we might actually be in a good position to answer.
ROOSE: So listener, Blythe (ph), wrote in to ask us, what are the three concise AI talking points you would have ready to share with your family at Thanksgiving, presumably, as we head into 2027? Let's first of all, start by examining the premise here.
NEWTON: Yeah.
ROOSE: Is AI something you should be discussing with your family at Thanksgiving?
NEWTON: (Laughter) Listen, when it comes to discussing AI, I try to operate in a consent-based culture. So I am not out there just volunteering my opinions about AI to people. Now, it so happens that it will often come up in conversation. So I have given this some thought, and the first thing I would suggest you might want to tell your family, Kevin, is to try it. And what I mean by that is, have you actually sat down in front of ChatGPT recently and asked it to do something? And I say all of this not as an unrelenting promoter of the AI industry, but because it remains the best way I know how to wrap your brain around what is happening.
ROOSE: Yes. That was my first piece of advice, too, is, like, show, don't tell.
NEWTON: Yeah.
ROOSE: Something that you've been saying recently that has really stuck with me is that you really have to see a computer using itself.
NEWTON: (Laughter) Yes.
ROOSE: There is something about the computer use that these agents can now do, where they can take over your browser and fill out a form or build you a website where it really does feel like a light bulb goes off in a lot of people's heads - not everyone's.
NEWTON: Yeah. And look, I don't think this is going to, you know, necessarily make people feel differently about AI. You might really hate AI. You're very fearful of the world that it is bringing into being. I still think - and that is world - you want to watch a computer use itself because it is just going to, frankly, give you more ammunition as you're sort of out there in the world talking about your own beliefs on the subject.
ROOSE: OK. What is the other thing that our listeners should take away?
NEWTON: So every family is going to be coming to this conversation with a different level of knowledge about AI, with different sorts of feelings about AI. And I think wherever you might find them, the job that you could take on for yourself is just to sort of ask them about what lies just a little bit beyond their frame of reference. So if you're at a family where truly no one has ever used ChatGPT, the entire conversation really could just be, here, I'm going to hand you my phone. Ask it a question. Have it write you a poem. Have it build you a website. And just kind of get their imaginations going.
If, on the other hand, your family is, you know, protesting the local data centers, very well-informed, you may want to talk to them about recursive self-improvement. You might want to say, hey, listen, I understand. We're really, you know, concerned about what's happening in our community, and I'm worried that what is happening inside this data center might put our family at risk, and so let's talk about that.
So if you're like Kevin and me, and you believe this is the biggest story in the world, figure out where your family is and then try to fill in some of the other parts of the story. Because unlike many conversations that come up at Thanksgiving dinner, Kevin, this one just is not actually boring.
ROOSE: Yeah. And it's not also polarized in a...
NEWTON: Yeah.
ROOSE: ...Predictable way. Like, your Republican uncle might be really worried about recursive self-improvement, and your progressive aunt might have concerns about the environmental impact of data centers. And maybe there is some way to kind of find some common ground there and be a peacemaker rather than an agent of division at your Thanksgiving dinner.
NEWTON: Oh, just imagine unity at the Thanksgiving table...
ROOSE: (Laughter).
NEWTON: ...Around being mad at the world. Yeah. Actually, that checks out. And I actually have maybe one final tip about AI and Thanksgiving that I would like to offer to all of humanity, Kevin, in a spirit of goodwill.
ROOSE: What's that?
NEWTON: Here's what I would say. If anything goes wrong at your Thanksgiving dinner, you look around the table and you say to your family members, I used an AI recipe, and I'm sorry.
ROOSE: (Laughter) I like that. We can...
NEWTON: Throw the AI under the bus. It can take it.
ROOSE: It can take it.
NEWTON: It can take it.
ROOSE: There you have it, folks.
NEWTON: Happy Thanksgiving.
ROOSE: Happy Thanksgiving. I'm thankful for you this year.
NEWTON: I'm thankful for you, as well, Kevin.
(SOUNDBITE OF MUSIC)
DETROW: Kevin Roose and Casey Newton - their new podcast from NPR is called Machine Gods. And we will note, Anthropic is the financial supporter of NPR.
