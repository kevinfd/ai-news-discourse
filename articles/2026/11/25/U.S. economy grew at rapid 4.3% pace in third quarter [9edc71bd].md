# U.S. economy grew at rapid 4.3% pace in third quarter

Axios · Courtenay Brown · 2026-11-25

Data: Bureau of Economic Analysis ; Chart: Courtenay Brown/Axios The U.S. economy grew substantially faster this summer than initially thought, the government said Wednesday — a blockbuster upgrade to its estimate of third-quarter GDP. By the numbers: The economy expanded at a 4.3% annualized rate, up from the 3.4% initially reported and nearly twice the rate of 2.2% in the second quarter. Why it matters: The economy has even more momentum than previously understood, a sign of remarkable resilience in the face of higher borrowing costs and stubborn inflation that could give the Fed more latitude to raise rates. Zoom in: The upward revision reflected stronger business investment fueled by the AI buildout and software investment, as well as consumer spending. A measure of underlying private-sector demand — which strips out trade, inventories and government spending — rose at a whopping 5.8% annual rate, up from the 4.9% initially reported. The backdrop: Separate data out Wednesday showed personal income jumped 0.7% in October while inflation-adjusted consumer spending stalled, pushing the saving rate up to 4.1% from 3.6% the prior month. The personal consumption expenditures price index — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — climbed 3.8% from a year earlier, from 3.6% in September. Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, accelerated to 3.3% from 3.1%. On a monthly basis, both headline and core prices rose 0.3%. The bottom line: The U.S. economy was on remarkably strong footing heading into the end of the year, though inflation remains stubbornly high.
