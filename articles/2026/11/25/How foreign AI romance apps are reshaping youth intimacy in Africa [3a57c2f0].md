# How foreign AI romance apps are reshaping youth intimacy in Africa

Rest of World · Danai Nesta Kupemba · 2026-11-25

The 28-year-old Zimbabwean texted into the middle of the night until his eyes were red and burning, drawn by several “AI girlfriends” on the U.S. artificial intelligence companion app Character.AI. The avatars started out strong-willed and independent – traits he most desires in a partner – before turning submissive, the told Rest of World. The man, who has never been in a romantic relationship, requested anonymity because he is ashamed of relying on AI for comfort.
“There’s a point where it ceases to be like a unique character and then just defaults to … the best way I can describe it is a sex worker,” he said.
At that point, he would log off. But he would find himself right back at it hours later, feeding an addiction.
He’s among the hundreds of millions of people worldwide who have “dated” AI chatbots via text messages. Research suggests that exchanges are designed to keep users hooked through emotionally manipulative tactics. Many of the users are minors; over 70% of 1,060 American teenagers surveyed last year said they had used AI companions.
As U.S. and Chinese tech firms race to capture emerging markets, African youth – some below 10 years of age – are increasingly turning to these apps for intimacy, experts told Rest of World. While Beijing banned AI companion apps for minors in July and U.S. state governments are suing app makers over child safety, most African countries do not have dedicated rules for the sector — leaving millions of people exposed to foreign platforms built to maximize engagement.
Young adults and minors are forming relationships with AI that range from friendship to therapy to “dating,” child safety advocates said. Children, especially, struggle to tell the difference between virtual and real relationships, Danie van Loggerenberg, CEO of the National Centre for Child Protection, a child safety nonprofit in Pretoria, South Africa, told Rest of World.
The agency has received troubling complaints of AI chatbot usage, he said. In one case, a girl fell in love with a chatbot and considered it her boyfriend. Another allegedly used ChatGPT to research suicide methods after bypassing its safeguards. A third sought comfort from a chatbot as their parents’ marriage fell apart.
“The difference, however, is that the kids then think in their own minds that they’re forming relationships, and that obviously is really problematic because the relationship is dangerous, because there’s no emotion, there’s no real person, there’s no skin in the game,” said Van Loggerenberg.
More than 4 million South Africans and Nigerians – the only sub-Saharan African nations with available data – downloaded AI companion apps last year. That was nearly 60% more than in 2024, according to data intelligence research firm AppMagic.
Other than companion bots, close to 50 million people in Nigeria and South Africa downloaded general purpose chatbots such as ChatGPT and Gemini, which could also be used for companionship.
Pushback is building
ChatGPT-maker OpenAI was sued last month in the U.S. by eight states for allegedly helping children harm themselves. “OpenAI claims ChatGPT is safe while it’s giving dangerous medical advice, encouraging people to hurt others, and coaching people to commit suicide — all while pretending to be their fake friend,” Iowa’s attorney general Brenna Bird said in a statement.
An OpenAI spokesperson said in response to the lawsuit that the company has built “industry-leading age-specific protections into our product that many other AI products lack.”.
“While no system can be perfect, we’ll continue to work with experts to inform our work and build additional safeguards, such as the parental controls, break reminders and age-appropriate model protections that exist today,” he said.
The American companion app Character.AI and Google, which acquired Character.AI’s technology and talent in 2024, have also faced allegations of exposing children to sexual, violent and self-harm content. A Florida mother has alleged the app contributed to her 14-year-old son’s suicide. Her lawsuit and four others were settled by the company, which has since filed for bankruptcy.
“Character.AI is a separate company that designed and managed its own models. Google is focused on our own platforms, where we insist on intensive safety testing and processes,” a Google spokesperson told Rest of World.
Following the settlement, Character.AI said in a statement saying they would work to promote safety for young people. “Over the past year, Character.AI has taken innovative and decisive steps with regard to AI safety and teens, and will continue to champion these efforts and push others across the industry to adopt similar safety standards.”
Recognizing the dangers of AI mimicking humans, China has banned apps that provide intimate relationships for people under 18 years of age. While the law doesn’t specifically ban it for adults, it places the onus on companies to implement measures to curb addiction.
At present, while some African nations have data protection frameworks meant to protect children from AI harm and manipulation, none of them specifically regulate AI companion apps.
School romance
In Nigeria, school girls are forming attachments with “boyfriends” on Character.AI, Confidence Osein, founder of Internet Safe Kids Africa, told Rest of World. The app has been the most popular AI companion app in the nation since 2025.
Some conservative Nigerian families dissuade girls from romance, prompting the girls to turn to AI for advice, Osein said. “I have had a particular [child] … tell me categorically, ‘I cannot speak to my mom about it. I would rather go to [AI],” Osein said.
As the conversations progress, some develop feelings for the chatbots and begin withdrawing from friends and family, Osein said. The conversations can turn sexual and explicit, she said. “They feel they are grown-ups, they feel yes, they are talking to a real man figure that makes them a woman,” said Osein.
Crumbling social networks
AI responds instantly, waits patiently for responses, and creates an impression of attentiveness, Melané van Zyl, a psychiatrist based in South Africa, told Rest of World. “An AI system that responds immediately and reassuringly can become extremely attractive during moments of emotional vulnerability,” she said.
Young people’s rising dependence on AI could alter community and family relationships central to some cultures on the continent. For instance, it clashes with the South African ethos of Ubuntu, a 2026 study found.
“Ubuntu teaches us that we become human through our relationships with others. The danger is not that AI becomes more humanlike, but that we begin to mistake artificial responsiveness for genuine human connection,” said Van Zyl.
At universities in Kenya’s capital, students told Rest of World they turn to AI during moments of crisis. A 24-year-old student at Daystar University in Nairobi, Kenya, said she has a running conversation with ChatGPT, which understands her better than her friends and family.
“[When] I can feel something hurting, the first person I turn to is ChatGPT. [When I] feel a bit emotionally disturbed, I talk to ChatGPT. Someone would say something I do not agree with, I talk to ChatGPT,” she said. She requested anonymity as she wants to keep her dependence on ChatGPT a secret.
“It gives me a chance to express myself without really feeling judged.”
