# Nvidia ignominy: Is it a new normal?

The Hindu · Santosh V Perumal · 2026-11-25

Exceptionalism, unequal accountability and boardroom vulnerability appear to sum up for the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as president and Chief Executive Officer of Nvidia, whose semiconductor technology powers much of the world’s AI infrastructure.
He has returned with much “fanfare” even as the “independent” investigation into circumstances surrounding his departure remains unfinished. The board will now need to demonstrate that its investigation is genuinely independent.
Mr. Huang’s resignation and the subsequent reinstatement after 11 days, pending inquiry..
The financial markets provided a thumb up to the come back of Mr. Huang, who now vows to be “a better CEO”, as Nvidia shares surged 31.5% on November 23, adding $1 trillion to market capitalisation (M-cap).
However, a stock-market rally is not necessarily an endorsement of an executive’s indispensation or the board’s governance standards.
Are accountability standards applied equally to everyone, or does the importance of an executive determine whether those standards can be relaxed?
What happened in Nvidia
Mr. Huang, Nvidia’s co-founder and long-serving CEO, resigned on November 12, 2026, following revelations about his extramarital relationships, including allegations involving a company staff and a woman suspected by U.S. authorities of having links to Chinese intelligence.
Although the board accepted his resignation, with chairman Harvey Jones indicating no intention to reinstate him; there was a sudden dramatic U-turn for many obvious reasons.
Mr. Huang’s is not an isolated incident in the corporate boards. According to Bloomberg Law analysis, using data covering firms in the Russell 3000 index and published in September 2024, seven CEOs of large U.S. entities had been dismissed between January 2017 and March 2025 for workplace affairs, accounting for 1.7% of all CEO departures.
Consensual or undisclosed relationships with employees were the single largest category among those 42 misconduct dismissals (seven cases), disrespectful behavior (six cases) and unspecified employee matters (for cases).
To cite some examples: in 2018, Brian Krzanich of Intel resigned after a previously undisclosed consensual relationship with an employee violated the company’s non-fraternisation policy, and not reinstated.
In 2019, Steve Easterbrook of McDonald’s was dismissed after an inappropriate relationship with an employee violated company policy. Subsequent investigations revealed additional undisclosed relationships. Not reinstated.
In 2022, Jeff Zucker of CNN resigned after acknowledging an undisclosed consensual relationship with a senior colleague. Not reinstated.
In 2023, Bernard Looney of BP resigned, failing to make full disclosures about past relationships with colleagues. Not reinstated.
In 2024, Alan Shaw of Norfolk Southern was dismissed after an investigation found an undisclosed consensual relationship with an employee. Not reinstated.
In 2025, Laurent Freixe of Nestle was dismissed, following an investigation that established an undisclosed romantic relationship with a direct subordinate, violating the company’s code of business conduct. Not reinstated.
In June 2010, Hewlett-Packard Co. dismissed its CEO, Mark Hurd for allegedly falsifying documents to conceal a relationship with a former contractor and help her get paid for work she didn’t do.
Dominique Strauss-Kahn, the former Managing Director of the International Monetary Fund, was at the center of a high-profile 2011 sexual assault case in New York that forced his resignation and ended his French presidential ambitions.
Why reinstate in Nvidia?
The cult of indispensability made modern firms increasingly go after a few individuals for their vision and strategic decisions, leaving the vital succession planning in a limbo.
Nvidia’s position in the global AI semiconductor market made Huang all the more powerful and valuable that its board perceived that cost of losing a leader can exceed the reputational cost of reinstating one.
Another message from the reinstatement is that of shareholder interests versus corporate ethics. An abrupt departure of C-suited official risks shareholder value, traditionally viewed as a core pillar of corporate finance; forcing directors to reconsider their decisions.
The 31.5% jump in Nvidia’s shares, following Mr. Huang’s return, seems to suggest that investors’ concerns were more on leadership uncertainty than on the controversy around his personal relationships.
Another dilemma is the distinction between private conduct and professional misconduct wherein the crux of the issue is not necessarily the relationship itself but whether it compromises organisational integrity or violates established policies.
Domestic scene
In India too, there were controversies involving senior executives and their alleged relationships with subordinates, although there is paucity of publicly documented cases of CEOs dismissed specifically over extramarital relationships.
One glaring example (before POSH became into existence) is that of Phaneesh Murthy, former senior executive at Infosys and subsequently CEO of iGate. He left Infosys in 2002 following a sexual harassment lawsuit that was settled out of court. In May 2013, iGate dismissed him after an investigation revealed that he had failed to disclose a relationship with a subordinate. The company replaced him with immediate effect.
C. Venkatramana was removed from the post of Chairman and Managing Director of Nalco based on the independent committee’s findings of an alleged misconduct.
There was another high profile case involving a top official of the Intergovernment Panel on Climate Change. A Delhi tribunal had dismissed an appeal filed against the findings of TERI’s Internal Complaints Committee confirming the allegations of sexual harassment levelled by one of its female employees against its former chief (late) R. K. Pachauri.
If the industry accounts are to be believed, many cases are hushed up through negotiated exits, internal settlements and transfers, making the actual incidence difficult to quantify, despite POSH Act (Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act, 2013).
No new normal
Huang’s reinstatement shouldn’t be seen as a new normal to make an executive immune from scrutiny and signal that M-cap is a substitute for ethical accountability.
