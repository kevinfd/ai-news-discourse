# Futures sink further into the red as tech worries resurge

CNBC · Leonie Kidd · 2026-11-25

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.
Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.
The return of Jensen Huang to the helm of Nvidia has also seen the return of the "key-man risk" concerns, as he has defied calls to adjust his succession planning.
Investors and policymakers could take more convincing to restore long-term confidence in the group, despite its crucial position in the AI economy.
Happening now: Nvidia in the red
Nvidia shares are still under pressure in premarket trading, weighing on Nasdaq futures and the rest of Wall Street.
Reinstated CEO Jensen Huang held his first all-staff meeting on Tuesday afternoon, apologizing for his actions, but also defying calls to address succession planning. According to CNBC sources, he said a succession plan would create unnecessary hierarchy.
- Inside Nvidia: For more on Huang's thanking of CFO Colette Kress, his revelation that he is in therapy, and his promise to do a better job, you can read all the details here.
- Analysis: The volatility around tech stocks has reignited concerns of a big tech spending squeeze.
The shifting sentiment towards the stock has also thrown the spotlight back on the White House.
CNBC's Garrett Downs and Kevin Breuninger report on how President Donald Trump is finding himself increasingly isolated over his move to return Huang to his position as Nvidia CEO.
- What next?: President Trump has said he is open to pardoning Huang if needed, as the Nvidia CEO prepares to appear before Congress next week.
Big Movers: Tech under pressure
The declines across the futures have accelerated through the morning.
Nvidia, Anthropic, and chip-related stocks are all adding to the downward pressure.
Nasdaq-listed semiconductor group Ambarella is plunging in premarket moves, down almost 40%. This is after the group shared fourth-quarter revenue guidance that was well below the Street's expectations.
Overnight, Asian technology stocks tumbled once again, with SoftBank down as much as 15% during the session.
- Analysis: Deutsche Bank analysts believe "we're back to the rotation trade rather than the indiscriminate sell-off we saw towards the end of last week," adding that "the current moves don't have any of the characteristics that accompanied the outright bear markets of recent decades."
Economics: A raft of data
Stock market volatility has dominated investors' attention, but today there is also a raft of economic data to process.
Watch out for the following from 08:30 a.m. ET:
- Second reading of real GDP, with Dow Jones consensus expectations for a 3.6% gain.
- PCE data for October, with Dow Jones calls for a 0.3% gain on the month, 3.8% year-on-year.
- Durable Goods orders, with expectations for a 1.1% rise.
- Weekly Jobless Claims come a day early due to Thanksgiving. The Dow Jones consensus is for 200,000 new claims.
Earlier this week, New York Fed President John Williams indicated that higher rates would be likely "over time" to tackle inflation.
- Why it matters: The Fed's next meeting is on December 8-9, with a quarter-percentage-point hike almost fully priced in.
IPO: Public problems
The wild ride of the past couple of weeks has proved tough for the world's biggest newly listed companies.
Anthropic fell below its IPO price during Tuesday's session, off about 30% from its IPO day highs.
The stock closed below $83 a share after falling over 6.5%.
- Analysis: Some are still positive, with Canaccord Genuity initiating a buy rating and a $135 price target for the stock. Wedbush has an outperform rating with a $120 price target.
- Bear view: But others see more pain ahead, with the biggest options trade on Tuesday targeting an 11% move lower.
SpaceX also fell on Tuesday, down 5.7%. That puts the stock more than 40% lower from its post-IPO high.
- Analysis: Mizuho is initiating the stock as outperform, saying, "Best Ideas. SPCX (Outperform): the clearest case of vertical integration in the group. It is the dominant launch provider for itself and the industry, and it uses that to build out its Starlink infrastructure."
The Financial Times is reporting that Nscale — the data center company — is considering staying private as the tech sell-off makes management reconsider its $35 billion IPO.
And Finally...
Trump Account contributions from friends and family pass $1 billion mark
Families and friends have collectively contributed more than $1 billion to Trump Accounts since the July 4 launch, bringing total deposits to more than $9 billion, the U.S. Department of the Treasury said Tuesday in an announcement exclusively provided to CNBC.
The $1 billion in Trump Account deposits come from more than 3 million individuals, according to the agency. That total does not include the government's seed money or philanthropic gifts from donors such as Michael and Susan Dell.
