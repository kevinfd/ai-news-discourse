# Disney is making a $20 million bet on Character.ai. Here’s what it’s really buying

Fast Company · Inc. · 2026-11-25

Never pay peak hype prices for unproven technology. Chatbot startup Character.ai filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Delaware after facing severe legal, regulatory, and operational pressures.
The company was once valued in the billions as a darling of the Silicon Valley consumer AI boom. Now it is putting its assets up for a court-supervised sale that will wrap up by early December. If a buyer does not step up to purchase and run the whole platform, the service will shut down completely.
Standing at the front of the line in bankruptcy court is the Walt Disney Co. Through its affiliate Disney Worldwide Services, the entertainment giant stepped in as the opening bidder with a $20 million offer for a nonexclusive, perpetual license to key Character.ai intellectual property.
If you run a growing company, look closely at the sequence of events that led to this moment. Disney did not get swept up in fear of missing out during the height of the chatbot frenzy. Instead, the company executed a ruthless, patient acquisition strategy that every business leader should study.
Enforce your boundaries before you negotiate
When a fast-moving startup uses your assets without permission, you do not have to rush into a reactive partnership. Before Disney ever put a bid on the table, it threatened Character.ai with legal action over copyright infringement and unauthorized chatbots using its characters.
Other media executives panicked about falling behind in artificial intelligence. Disney simply held the line on its intellectual property. It watched the startup burn through cash trying to figure out an expensive consumer business model. At the same time, Character.ai faced mounting scrutiny over child safety and teen mental health lawsuits.
You lose all negotiating power the moment you act desperate for a new trend. Protect your core assets first. Let unproven competitors absorb the early regulatory blows and compute costs on their own dime.
