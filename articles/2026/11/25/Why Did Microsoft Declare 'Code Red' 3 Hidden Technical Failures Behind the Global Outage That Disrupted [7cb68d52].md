# Why Did Microsoft Declare 'Code Red'? 3 Hidden Technical Failures Behind the Global Outage That Disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot

Jagran Josh · Aishwarya Samant · 2026-11-25

Why Did Microsoft Declare 'Code Red'? 3 Hidden Technical Failures Behind the Global Outage That Disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot
Microsoft has declared Code Red after a major global outage disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot. Discover the three technical failures behind the disruption and Microsoft's new one-hour recovery target.
Microsoft Outage 2026: The global tech industry went into a tech meltdown on the day Microsoft and its applications went down. Imagine the number of people working through Microsoft, left with nothing to do but refresh their pages and emails.
A few days ago, on November 12, Microsoft services, including Outlook, Teams and Copilot, went down and stopped working due to a bug. Many people initially thought it was a cyberattack. But no, it was later confirmed that an internal technical issue was the cause.
Now, Microsoft has declared a "Code Red", its highest-priority internal response. This step has been taken after a major 24-hour global outage disrupted Microsoft 365 services on November 12–13, 2026.
The outage affected Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft platforms, causing problems for businesses, airlines, trading firms and government organisations across several countries.
But what caused such a major breakdown at one of the world's biggest technology companies?
Let's decode the Code Red and the Global Microsoft Outage glitch.
What Caused Such A Major Tech Breakdown At Microsoft?
According to internal memos reviewed by Reuters, three technical problems came together and triggered the crisis. From an old software bug to overloaded servers, the incident exposed major gaps in Microsoft's systems and emergency response.
What Happened During Microsoft's Global Outage?
The reports suggest that the outage disrupted Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams and Microsoft 365. According to users, they faced problems accessing emails, sending messages and using connected services.
Later, after a detailed check-up and in-depth analysis, Microsoft confirmed that the incident was not caused by a cyberattack. This was an internal problem that took over 12–14 hours to resolve on a primary basis. But the cause was so big that the company had to conduct a deep check-up of its internal systems to identify the problem at the front end.

What Is Microsoft's New Code Red Recovery Plan?
Microsoft has introduced a recovery plan to prevent future outages. Its key steps include:
- One-Hour Recovery: Fix major global outages within one hour.
- AI Agents: Use AI to detect problems and find their causes faster.
- Better Traffic Management: Prevent local server failures from affecting global services.
- Daily Reviews: Hold daily meetings and weekly progress checks with engineering leaders.
- Faster Updates: Speed up urgent security fixes and global deployment.
The goal is to make Microsoft's services more reliable and prevent another major global outage.
