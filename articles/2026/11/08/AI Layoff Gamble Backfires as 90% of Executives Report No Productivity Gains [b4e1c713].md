# AI Layoff Gamble Backfires as 90% of Executives Report No Productivity Gains

CoinCentral · Maisie Morrison · 2026-11-08

TLDR
- An Atlanta Fed paper found nearly 90% of surveyed executives saw no measurable productivity or jobs change from AI.
- Meta has scaled back some plans to replace workers with AI agents.
- Challenger reported 121,401 US job cuts tied to AI in the first ten months of 2026.
- AI cuts make up about 20% of all announced job reductions this year.
- Challenger said weak economic conditions have overtaken AI as the top reason for layoffs.
Several large companies are changing course on plans to replace workers with artificial intelligence. New research from the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta shows most businesses have not yet seen clear productivity gains. The findings come as US employers keep citing AI in layoff announcements.
The AI layoff gamble is backfiring.
Companies that rushed to replace workers with AI are now cleaning up the mess, per The Guardian.

An October investigation by The Guardian found that firms that adopted AI quickly are now facing hidden costs. Employees are spending time fixing mistakes made by AI systems. Some of those errors involve security problems.
Meta, Microsoft and McKinsey Adjust Their AI Plans
Meta has scaled back some plans to replace staff with AI agents. The company found that workers were spending more time correcting technical and security errors. It is also rebuilding parts of its management structure after earlier cuts to middle-management roles.
The Guardian reported that Meta has stopped using AI adoption as a factor in employee performance reviews. The company has not said how long the changes will last.
Microsoft has faced its own problems with AI output. Executives have warned about a rising amount of low-quality AI-generated material inside organisations. Ryan Roslansky, a senior Microsoft executive, criticised cases where one worker uses AI to write a document and a colleague uses AI to summarise it.
McKinsey and Microsoft have also reportedly placed limits on some employee AI tools. The reported reason is to manage rising costs. Walmart has drawn complaints from staff about AI scheduling and task systems. Workers cited unrealistic targets, inaccurate alerts and errors from automated decisions.
Fed Research Shows Limited Gains So Far
A March 2026 working paper from the Atlanta Fed looked at nearly 6,000 businesses. The firms were based in the United States, United Kingdom, Germany and Australia. About 70% of them were already using AI.
Nearly 90% of executives said AI had not measurably changed productivity or employment levels over the past three years. Even so, firms expected AI to lift productivity by about 1.4% over the next three years.
A separate Atlanta Fed paper found productivity gains in some sectors. Finance and professional services showed the clearest improvements. The gains were uneven across the wider economy.
AI Cited in More Than 121,000 US Job Cuts
Companies have kept cutting jobs while the results remain unclear. A Challenger, Gray & Christmas report released on November 6 counted 121,401 AI-linked job-cut announcements in the first ten months of 2026. That is about 20% of all announced cuts.
Challenger noted that a company citing AI does not prove the technology replaced those workers. Some firms may be restructuring, cutting costs or shifting money toward AI infrastructure.
The report also said weaker market and economic conditions have overtaken AI as the leading reason for layoffs. October job cuts fell to a three-year low, according to Challenger.
The Atlanta Fed research reported that AI is still being used in areas such as software development, data analysis and customer support. Its latest findings were drawn from executive surveys across four countries.
