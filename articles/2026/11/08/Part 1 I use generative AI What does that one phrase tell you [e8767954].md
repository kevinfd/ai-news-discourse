# Part 1 | "I use generative AI": What does that one phrase tell you?

note · Digital MICE（デジタル マイス · 2026-11-08

Part 1 | "I use generative AI": What does that one phrase tell you?
Revisiting how each individual employee uses it, hidden behind usage rate figures
"Do you use generative AI?"
When conducting surveys for company employees, you might set up a question like this.
If you collect answers as "Yes" or "No," you can calculate the generative AI usage rate.
For example, suppose the result shows that 60% of employees use generative AI. This is a figure that is easy to explain in management meetings and easy to use for year-over-year comparisons.
However, I would like to add one more question here.
"How do you use generative AI?"
Even with the same answer of "I use it," the content might be very different.
People who use it are not all the same
For example, suppose there is an employee who only asks generative AI a question once a month. On the other hand, there might be an employee who uses it every day as a sounding board for their work. Someone who uses it to summarize text. Someone who thinks of ideas for projects. Someone who gets help creating materials. Furthermore, there might be people who use mechanisms that automatically carry out multiple tasks. In a survey, all of these employees are classified as "people who use generative AI."
However, their frequency of use, purpose, and involvement in their work are all different.
A single figure like usage rate cannot fully grasp these differences.
Are you typing on a keyboard?
When looking into how generative AI is used, what I want to focus on is the input method.
Are they typing text on a keyboard? Are they asking questions via voice from a smartphone? Or are they using a combination of text input and voice input?
Even if the action of using generative AI is the same, the situation and experience of using it change depending on the input method.
For example, a keyboard might be convenient when creating materials on a PC.
On the other hand, voice interaction might be useful when you are on the move or want to organize your ideas.
Understanding these differences is also important for companies to prepare a suitable usage environment for their employees.
Is it a PC or a smartphone?
Next, I want to check the devices being used.
Generative AI can be used not only on PCs but also on smartphones and tablets.
Some people might use a PC for work and a smartphone for private life.
Also, there might be people who use the same generative AI service for both work and private life.
What is interesting here is the possibility that there are differences not only in the devices but also in the AI services themselves being used.
The generative AI specified by the company and the generative AI that employees are accustomed to using daily are not necessarily the same. I would like to think about this point again in the final installment of this series.
Do not judge users solely by age
Comparing the usage status of generative AI by age group is also useful.
However, I would like to avoid assuming from the start that younger generations use it more actively and older generations use it less.
By combining not just age, but also job type, device, input method, and past experience with digital services, more specific characteristics might become visible.
What I value on a daily basis isnot drawing conclusions from a single piece of data, but making judgments by combining multiple pieces of data.
This is a common way of thinking when analyzing the actual usage of generative AI.
AI implementation starts with knowing the actual usage of employees
When a company introduces generative AI, the information systems department and legal department often take the lead in considering security, contract terms, costs, and so on.
Such considerations are important.
At the same time, grasping the voices of the employees who actually use it is also essential.
What kind of AI are they using? Which features do they value? What do they want to use it for in their work?
By understanding the actual usage of employees, it becomes possible to make more specific considerations regarding AI selection, training, and the development of usage rules.
Also, to make the introduction of generative AI a company-wide initiative, cooperation not only with the information systems department but also with the corporate planning department and each business division is important.
Relatedly, I want to think about AI implementation and company-wide consensus building
On our company's note, we examine internal communication when formulating AI policies from the roles of each department. In particular, the corporate planning department is expected to play a role in connecting the actual usage on the front lines with management policy and overseeing AI utilization across the entire company.

What to investigate after "usage rate"
There is value in surveying the usage rate of generative AI. However, isn't that figure just a starting point for understanding how employees actually use it?
Through this series, I would like to examine the profile of employees who use generative AI from various angles.
Input methods, devices, age groups, prompt reuse, and AI agents.
Furthermore, what kind of user profiles emerge when we cross-reference this data?
And how can that analysis be connected to a company's AI selection, employee training, and the development of AI policies?
Beyond the single phrase "I use generative AI" lies information that companies need to know.
