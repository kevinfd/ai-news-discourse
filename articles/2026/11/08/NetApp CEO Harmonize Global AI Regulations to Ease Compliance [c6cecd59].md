# NetApp CEO: Harmonize Global AI Regulations to Ease Compliance

Rediff Money · Rediff Money Desk · 2026-11-08

NetApp CEO: Harmonize Global AI Regulations to Ease Compliance
x
NetApp CEO George Kurian urges global harmonization of AI regulations to simplify compliance for businesses. India is NetApp's fastest-growing market.
Key Points
- NetApp CEO George Kurian calls for global harmonization of AI regulations to ease business compliance.
- Divergent regulatory frameworks complicate operations, requiring companies to meet multiple sets of rules.
- Kurian suggests policymakers adopt an approach similar to cyber resilience standards for AI.
- India is NetApp's fastest-growing market in Asia, with a large global capability center in Bengaluru.
- NetApp is the number one player in India's all-flash storage market, serving key sectors.
Businesses are required to meet not only AI-specific regulations across different jurisdictions but also various industry-specific requirements, including the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), he told PTI.
"We would encourage policymakers around the world to harmonise on a few things, just like the world harmonised on cyber resilience standards, which allowed the entire industry to actually deliver more capability," he said.
He further urged policymakers to adopt a similar approach towards AI, including issues related to data sovereignty, to simplify compliance and enable businesses to develop and deploy AI capabilities more effectively.
"Having very different regulatory jurisdictions on AI complicates business because now you have to not only comply with one set of regulations related to AI, but also meet another set of regulations," he said.
About sovereignty norms, he said, "I would say the Europeans are the most stringent in their sovereignty requirements, including requiring local models."
Other countries, constrained by factors such as cost and resources, are taking a more pragmatic approach, increasingly relying on open-source and open-weight models to make AI services affordable and accessible to a wider section of users.
Kurian also said that India is NetApp's fastest-growing market in Asia, with the BFSI, manufacturing, IT services and public sectors – particularly central and state government departments – as key drivers of the company's revenue growth.
The San Jose-based data infrastructure company has also grown its global capability centre in Bengaluru, with the majority of global R&D processed out of the centre.
With a team of about 4,000 engineers, the centre is the largest for NetApp and is core to the global innovation work for the company across technology areas.
"India has been the fastest-growing market in Asia, and we are always looking to grow faster," he said.
NetApp's global capability centre in India has become the company's largest and has consistently grown faster than the rest of the business, the India-born CEO of the company said.
Kurian, who has been driving the company's business growth as CEO since 2015, said NetApp is the number one player in India's all-flash storage market and is giving its local teams greater scope to expand the business through investments by the company and its partners.
NetApp has a broad customer base in India, including large banks and financial institutions, central and state government departments, manufacturing companies and IT services firms, he said.
