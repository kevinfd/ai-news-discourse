# Mozilla Firefox head says the company is ‘ramping up' the time it devotes to cybersecurity issues

Business Insider · Brent D. Griffiths, Brent D. Griffiths (bgriffiths@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-26

Firefox head Ajit Varma said there is still enough time to stay ahead of the AI cybersecurity wave. Alex Broadway/Web Summit via Sportsfile via Getty Images Mozilla's Firefox head says his team has stepped up its efforts to fix cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Ajit Varma said his team is working with both OpenAI and Anthropic. Varma said he still thinks there's "enough time" to fix bugs before the next AI advancements roll out. Mozilla's Firefox chief says the pace of AI advancements is still giving his team "enough time" to make sure it addresses any cybersecurity vulnerabilities. "What we've seen is that we are devoting a lot more of our own resources in order to fix these things," Mozilla's Ajit Varma told Business Insider during a recent interview. "So I think if you look at a year ago, I think it was about 10% of our time, and now, when I look at the latest, it's 27% of our effort is now spent on addressing these." Varma's comments came as top US government officials cautioned that the recent string of outages affecting everything from Microsoft Outlook to JPMorgan Chase reflects companies' efforts to fix software issues faster than ever. Home Depot's chief information officer recently told Business Insider that the retailer wants to cut the time it takes to deploy a fix "to hours." Varma said both OpenAI and Anthropic are giving Mozilla, which is a nonprofit, "a lot of tokens for free." The Firefox head said the frontier models "are finding more and more complicated vulnerabilities within our browser." The AI leaders are eager to work with Mozilla. So much so that Varma said that in March his team noticed that someone with an Anthropic email address had submitted a bug. Since Firefox is completely open source, anyone could do so. Once Mozilla's team got in touch, they realized that the Anthropic employee had found a lot more vulnerabilities. "They were excited about testing their models with Firefox, because we have spent decades building a very secure and very tested browser that's open source, has a lot of contributions and a lot of scrutiny around it," Varma said. Beyond cybersecurity, Varma said, is finding other ways to deploy AI tools to make "Firefox even faster" and prevent issues like memory leaks. He said he encourages his team to view AI as a tool and to "triage" what truly needs human attention. "We are trying to encourage people to say there's a lot of routine activities that we don't necessarily need humans to do and that AI does really well because it's relentless; it doesn't get tired," he said.
