# Human military relevance is more important than economic relevance

LessWrong (curated) · cousin_it · 2026-11-26

A very simple point that I've had to explain to many people, so I thought I'd make a post on it. Some classes of people in the past were economically necessary for their labor, but militarily irrelevant and coercible: slaves, serfs. Others didn't do any economic work, but were militarily important: the aristocracy! Living fun lives, partying, traveling, making and enjoying art. Some of them felt a bit of ennui due to not working, but mostly it wasn't a big problem. The reason aristocracy gave way to democracy wasn't because of rising productivity of the masses: a slave in a diamond mine can be very productive, but still remain a slave. Rather, it was because firearms lowered the cost of training a soldier, and thus made the masses valuable as potential soldiers . That gave the masses a bargaining chip which led to better economic and political standing. If Alice wishes for a world where her labor (e.g. as a programmer) remains economically valuable, but all military jobs go to AI, then humans will lose the "potential soldier bonus" we've had for a couple centuries and revert to a powerless, exploited position. The economic importance of our labor won't save us. Whereas if Bob wishes for a world where all jobs in the economy are done by AI, but humans remain militarily important, then humans will be the aristocracy in that world and lead lives of leisure and fun. If I could magically make that future happen, I would. Of course it's all academic: all military jobs can probably be done by AI even sooner than other jobs. There's already surveillance and drones. But still I wanted to write it up, because so many people today think economic irrelevance is the main issue, when it isn't. Discuss
