# I’m a Silicon Valley couples therapist. Here’s what Jensen Huang's daily therapy sessions might entail.

Business Insider · Agnes Applegate, Agnes Applegate (aapplegate@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-26

Phil Boissierie has been a couples' therapist in Silicon Valley for almost 20 years. Courtesy of Phil Boissierie At an Nvidia all-hands, reinstated CEO Jensen Huang said he'll be in therapy for 90 minutes a day. Phil Boissierie is a Silicon Valley therapist who works with couples in high-profile careers. He hasn't met Huang but says therapy could include managing public pressure and relationship repair. This as-told-to essay is based on a conversation with Phil Boissierie, a 50-year-old marriage and couples therapist at The Relationship Therapy Group in Silicon Valley. His words have been edited for length and clarity. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang recently said he was doing 90 minutes of therapy every day as part of his effort to move forward. I've been a couples therapist in Silicon Valley for 20 years. Ninety minutes of therapy a day is not particularly common, nor is it a traditional model. Usually, if someone's doing that much therapy every day, it's because they're likely trying to work through a lot quickly and create some behavioral change. If a behavior is compulsive or hard to change, doing extended periods of therapy every day can be helpful for building traction. I can't say for sure what he's doing since I don't know Jensen, but here's what that frequency of therapy for a high-profile CEO in a situation like this would probably look like. There will be a lot to work through Working through how to manage public pressure, understand repetitive behavior, and repair relationships are all things I would discuss in the first few sessions of intensive therapy like this. The pressure, the eyes on him , the eyes on the company — all of that is huge, alongside all the damage that's been created in his relationships with his wife, his family, and the other people he's been involved with and working with.

 Any therapy plan has to do with what the particular person is going through, and I'm not going to speculate, but in my practice, a lot of times when we see someone having multiple affairs , that doesn't just come out of nowhere. It often has to do with family of origin, early life experiences, and things they're working through, which become the fabric of who we are and how we operate. It takes intentional work to create those changes. I also always recommend to people that when a disclosure comes out about an affair, doing work as a couple right up front is really important. If Jensen and Lori Huang are also doing couples work, I think that would be really, really smart and not overkill. The 90-minute frequency, every day, helps you get through a lot In a traditional 50-minute therapy session, it's common for people to settle into things, then, right as they're doing some heavy lifting and reaching an insight that's particularly challenging or emotional, the session's over. So, by allowing 90 minutes, you're able to settle in and cover a lot more. Working every day in therapy like this could help him pick right back up where they left off the day before, not only face what's happening that day, what's coming up, and all the pressures, but also get a jumpstart on that intensive work of answering questions like, "Why did this happen? How do I stop this from happening again?" Hopefully, that would be a big part of his motivation for doing this much therapy. This also signals something to his company and to people watching Sometimes coming back into the workplace after such a big public situation requires satisfying people. I think that could also be a part of this. It demonstrates to those watching that someone is working hard on themselves. If multiple affairs are happening, if that is what's happening here, it's easy for people to say, "Look, if you're involved in all of this, how are you paying attention to what's going on at the company?" I would imagine if he came out and said, "Hey, I'm going to do an hour a week of therapy, or I'm going to do two hours a week," that just wouldn't suffice for people. If you're going to make the behavioral change, repair the relationships, and take the helm of the company , yeah, you do have to make changes all over the place to be healthy again.
