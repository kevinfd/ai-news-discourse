# Anthropic chief denies wanting humans to 'surrender' free will to AI

Euronews · Christopher Alessi · 2026-11-26

Dario Amodei's comments come amid a growing debate over how to maintain human autonomy in the face of superintelligent AI systems.
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei on Wednesday denied wanting humanity to cede its free will to artificial intelligence, amid recent veiled criticism from fellow tech executives.
Several rival AI leaders, including from Meta, have highlighted the importance of defending human agency as AI evolves. Amodei’s comments followed an Axios report this week that suggested Anthropic’s leadership takes the view that humanity should “surrender” some decision-making power to superintelligent AI systems.
"No, I do not believe that we should 'surrender human free will' to AI systems, and it's absurd to suggest that I do," Amodei wrote in a lengthy post on X. "We should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination at the altar of efficiency or security," he added.
The debate over human agency comes after Anthropic said last week that its Claude AI model had helped solve what’s widely thought to be the “biggest maths problem of all time”.
The news prompted Meta’s top AI executive, Alexandr Wang, to warn on X on Sunday that the mathematical achievement marked “the beginning of a significant change” in how humans interact with the technology. He added: "We should not be okay with a 'handover' of society to AI.”
Fears that AI tools could slip out of human control have haunted Silicon Valley since a string of incidents this summer, including the hacking of Hugging Face by autonomous AI agents developed by OpenAI.
Anthropic’s Amodei has previously made comments suggesting humans should be willing to relinquish some autonomy to AI. In February, he told The New York Times that in "life-critical, safety-critical cases, we really want to turn it over" to AI. And in late 2024, he predicted that AI systems "will be the leaders, and they'll be ordering humans or other AI systems around."
Anthropic’s initial public offering last week was the largest on record, with the company’s value at one point surpassing $2 trillion in value, despite concern over an AI bubble and growing fears the technology threatens lives and livelihoods.
