# A teenage entrepreneur skipped the advice 'follow your passion.' His company did 6 figures in revenue in a month.

Business Insider · Kathleen Elkins, Kathleen Elkins (kelkins@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-26

Stavya Nilawar founded his creator-marketing agency, Nilawar, with about $35. Courtesy of Stavya Nilawar Stavya Nilawar, 19, built a creator marketing agency that generated $119,000 in October 2026. He chose AI clients for their growing budgets, not because he loved the industry. His advice: Pick a business model you enjoy, then find a niche with demand. When seeking career advice, there's a refrain that comes up often: "Follow your passion." Stavya Nilawar, 19, thinks that approach can be a "huge limiter" for aspiring entrepreneurs. Rather than building a business around a personal passion, Nilawar selected an industry where he saw demand, companies with marketing budgets, and room for a service provider like him. His creator-marketing agency, Nilawar , connects brands with content creators for sponsored segments and dedicated videos. The agency, which he launched with about $35, works with AI companies including CodeRabbit and Wispr Flow, and typically earns a cut of each project fee. The business brought in $119,000 in revenue in October 2026 , according to business-account statements reviewed by Business Insider. Nilawar's first attempt at entrepreneurship was much more closely tied to his interest in social media. He started a social-media marketing agency, visiting local businesses to film and edit short-form videos for their accounts. He'd create about 30 videos a month for clients and earn roughly $750 for his work. "Quickly, I realized doing that all myself — the editing, the recording, and going to the actual place — it was so much time," Nilawar, a sophomore at Babson College, said. "That time cost was insane." He knew he wanted to work in media, but he didn't necessarily want to be the person on camera or have a business dependent on his physical presence. "I didn't want to be the main figure," he said. "But I knew that I could work with other people who can be the main figure." So when he launched Nilawar, he chose a model that suited him: running an agency that connects brands with creators. The next decision was which kinds of brands to serve. He picked AI for the business opportunity, not personal interest Nilawar decided to focus on AI. He saw that AI companies were growing quickly and often had budgets for creator partnerships — the type of work his agency could facilitate. "I was not passionate about AI at all, to be honest," he said. He believes founders should look beyond whether an industry sounds exciting or aligns with their personal interests and, instead, seek niches with customers who have both a clear need and the ability to pay. For him, that meant working with AI companies instead of focusing on an industry he was personally passionate about. "The business vehicle you choose should be something you like," he said. "But then after that, the niche you do should always be business-oriented." Read the original article on Business Insider
