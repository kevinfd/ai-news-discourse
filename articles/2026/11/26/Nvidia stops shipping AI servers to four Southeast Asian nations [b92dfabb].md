# Nvidia stops shipping AI servers to four Southeast Asian nations

The Hindu · AFP · 2026-11-26

U.S. tech titan Nvidia is not allowing orders of computer servers containing its artificial intelligence chips to be shipped to four Southeast Asian countries, the Financial Times reported Thursday (November 26, 2026).
The article comes after Washington tightened export controls for the sensitive components in the wake of an espionage scandal that has engulfed Nvidia, forcing out longtime CEO Jensen Huang before his reinstatement on Sunday (November 22, 2026).
Citing national security concerns, the United States has since 2022 restricted export to tech rival China of Nvidia's industry-leading advanced chips that are used to train and run AI tools.
Washington last week moved to close a loophole in those controls, to crack down on chip smuggling, but also the large grey market of Chinese companies renting computing power from data centres in third countries, often in Southeast Asia.
The Financial Times report said Nvidia had instructed manufacturing partners to stop shipping computer servers to buyers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia.
The British newspaper, citing three people with knowledge of the matter, said it was unclear whether the halt was temporary.
It named U.S. giants Dell and Supermicro, who package Nvidia AI chips into servers for data centres, as two affected partners.
Buyers affected include Singapore-based cloud computing company Aolani, which had planned to provide access to the Nvidia hardware to Chinese TikTok owner ByteDance through leasing agreements, the Financial Times said.
An executive at a cloud provider told the newspaper that Washington's new rules had caused "sheer chaos" in the region during a 90-day period in which to apply for a newly required licence.
AFP has contacted Nvidia, Dell, Supermicro, Aolani and ByteDance for comment.
The Nvidia scandal erupted following reports of Huang's affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company under investigation for the alleged transfer of Nvidia chips to China in circumvention of US.. rules.
Alice Huang, no relation to Mr. Jensen, is also reportedly suspected by some U.S. officials of spying for Beijing.
Massive investments from U.S. and Chinese tech giants have driven a data centre construction boom in Southeast Asia, especially in the countries surrounding hyper-connected Singapore.
Data centre capacity in Southeast Asia is projected to triple from 2025 levels by 2030, driven by a tenfold surge in AI use, according to KPMG.
