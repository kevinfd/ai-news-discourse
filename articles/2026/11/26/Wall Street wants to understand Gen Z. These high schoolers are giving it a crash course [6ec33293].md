# Wall Street wants to understand Gen Z. These high schoolers are giving it a crash course.

Business Insider · Lauren Edmonds, Lauren Edmonds (ledmonds@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-26

High school students Emile Chilingirian and Jasper Gould cofounded a finance newsletter. Jasper Gould Jasper Gould cofounded Passing the Torch, a finance and economics newsletter with 28,000 subscribers. The newsletter offers something many others don't: An authentic Gen Z perspective. The newsletter has attracted Wall Street bros as well as young folks interested in finance. Finance analysts sift through heaps of consumer data, conduct large-scale surveys, and meticulously track trends in their quest to understand Gen Z. High school senior Jasper Gould had a better idea. Gould and Emile Chilingirian of New York City cofounded Passing the Torch , a finance and economics newsletter that's amassed 28,000 readers since its official launch in 2025. The newsletter offers an unfiltered glimpse into the minds of Gen Z , the latest generation to drum up spending power. Passing the Torch reports on a wide breadth of topics, from how Zoomers perceive automated vehicles and robotaxis to a deep dive into Pershing Square Capital Management's $65 billion now-defunct takeover bid for Universal Music Group . This month, the duo published an article titled "AI Stocks Just Started Taking Accutane," which discusses the recent tech stock sell-off. Subscribers pay $15 a month, or $150 annually. "We are a proprietary data source of a very special order, and I think people recognize that," Gould, 18, told Business Insider. That's one reason the newsletter has garnered a fan base on Wall Street. "When we looked into the subscriber logs, we saw a concentration at firms like Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan Chase, and Blackstone," Gould said. 'Let's make a business' Although Gould and Chilingirian are now in the media business, they got their start in the nonprofit sector. They cofounded Brooklyn Youth Charitable Investment Group, an organization that teaches financial and leadership skills to high schoolers, in 2024. The nonprofit has 100 active members and over 1,000 students have participated so far. To gain investors, the pair did cold reach-outs while juggling school and their personal lives. "There was a period of time in 10th grade where we were getting three hours of sleep a night, and I'm very happy we were doing so," Gould said. "If you are intentional about your relationships, if you really invest in them, and if you have something going for you like being in high school, that's really all it takes to warm things up." That helped them secure guest speakers from major institutions like Deutsche Bank, the White House, and Apple, Gould said. As the nonprofit grew, Gould and Chilingirian decided to create an Instagram account to share more tips and advice for young folks interested in finance. The account gained over 37,000 followers in less than two years, which inspired Gould and Chilingirian to think bigger. "A lot of the things we're saying are really valuable to investors, so let's make a business," Gould said. They said they didn't have to purchase ads or flood X to find readers. "It was word of mouth, stakeholders in the nonprofit, and people who knew us," Gould said. "In our pursuit of fundraising and working on this organization, we sent a lot of emails, and we had a lot of people in our network." On-the-ground journalism There are certainly challenges to running a media business, including competing in an oversaturated media market and making money amid dwindling online ad rates. There's also the fact that they're still in high school. "The biggest issue is getting people to take us seriously, which generally isn't an issue after the first phone call, but it's always a battle leading into it," Gould said. While the pair conduct traditional research and speak with experts, their youth allows them to take an anthropological approach to tracking Gen Z's tastes. "Just go to a party and ask someone," Gould said. "They don't realize that what they're about to tell us is going to end up making some investor or some company millions of dollars." Although neither founder has graduated from high school yet, they've already envisioned how their brand will continue leveling up. These days, that includes offering their deep insights into Gen Z to companies through consulting. "I'm sure there are some analysts at some of the pod shops that are learning how to read TikTok and Reddit as valuable sources of information, but I'm seeing it in the hallways every day," he said.
