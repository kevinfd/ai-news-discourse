# Bye bye, gift cards: People are putting more effort into gifts this year

Business Insider · Alex Bitter, Alex Bitter (abitter@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-26

The number of people planning to give gift cards this holiday season is at a 20-year low. : Jeffrey Greenberg/Universal Images Group via Getty Images People are planning to buy fewer gift cards as holiday gifts this year. Gift card purchases are expected to hit a 20-year low, according to the National Retail Federation. It shows that shoppers are getting more thoughtful about their gift-giving. Expect to get fewer gift cards this holiday season. The share of consumers who are planning to buy someone on their list a gift card is at the lowest in more than two decades, according to a survey of likely Thanksgiving Weekend shoppers conducted by the National Retail Federation, or NRF. Just 39% of shoppers surveyed plan to buy a gift card this year. Two decades ago, a majority did. The gift card 's slide in popularity can be attributed to factors from generational divides to giving fewer gifts, retail experts said. But it also shows a societal shift: If shoppers are going to bother buying a gift, it might as well mean something. "While gift cards are flexible, they are neither imaginative nor are they particularly personal," Global Data retail analyst Neil Saunders said. "A lot of people now want to give more meaningful gifts." The NRF found something similar in its survey, said Allison Zeller, the group's vice president of industry and consumer insights. The top reason respondents said they weren't giving gift cards was that they wanted to give gifts more tailored to the recipient. There was also an age divide, with baby boomers reporting they were more likely to give a gift card than Gen Z shoppers were, Zeller said. Thanks to the internet — and now AI tools — shoppers also have fewer excuses for not finding a good gift. That's a shift from two decades ago, when more shoppers stuck to in-store shopping during the holiday season. "You were limited in what you could buy based on what was in your town," she said. This year, many consumers are also trying to save money on holiday gifts. One-fifth of respondents to the NRF's survey, for instance, said that they were planning to buy gifts for fewer people . That could point to another reason people are buying fewer gift cards, said Julian Givi, an associate professor of marketing at West Virginia University who has studied gift-giving and consumer behavior. For years, gift cards were an easy choice if buyers didn't know the recipient that well — think about that cousin you only see twice a year, Givi said. "If you cut them out, you're probably cutting out the gift card spending," he told Business Insider. Shoppers who are trying to save money on holiday gifts are also more likely to buy a gift at a big discount during shopping days like Cyber Monday, Givi said. Those kinds of deals can give the gift recipient the impression that the giver spent more than they actually did, he added. Gift cards, meanwhile, make the purchase price obvious.
