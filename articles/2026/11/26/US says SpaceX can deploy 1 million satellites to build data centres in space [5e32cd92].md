# US says SpaceX can deploy 1 million satellites to build data centres in space

Euronews · Christopher Alessi · 2026-11-26

The US Federal Communications Commission authorised Elon Musk's rocket company to create a data centre constellation to power AI on earth.
The Trump administration on Wednesday gave Elon Musk’s rocket company SpaceX the green light to deploy one million satellites that it said would create data centres in space, as governments increasingly look to alternative means to power artificial intelligence.
The US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) lauded its authorisation as a historic first amid the AI race. It said that SpaceX's one million "orbital compute modules" — forming a data centre constellation called Starmind — would process massive amounts of data that would be used back on Earth.
"America is leading the world into the era of super intelligence," FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, who was appointed by President Donald Trump, said in a statement. He added that Wednesday's move "opens a bold new frontier".
A lengthier authorisation order published by the FCC noted that SpaceX must deploy at least "one authorised space station" by 2033 — and then gradually add more and more through 2040. Musk welcomed the decision over social media.
In its ruling, the FCC maintained that SpaceX's proposed Starmind constellation operates consistently with its orbital debris rules and that deploying the satellites "serves the public interest". But it acknowledged that it had received a “substantial number” of public comments leading up to its authorisation on Wednesday, including petitions to deny SpaceX's request.
The world's richest man has long vowed to form vast, solar-powered data centres in space to help run AI tools on Earth. But scientists and industry experts have stressed enormous technical and financial obstacles, as well as a host of environmental concerns like atmospheric pollution caused by launches and reentries.
Proponents of data centers in space argue that capturing the sun’s energy would ease pressure on power grids and cut demand for sprawling computing warehouses on Earth, as well as the resources like water needed to cool them.
European data centres in space?
The European Union is also exploring how to construct data centres in space.
The European Commission’s 16-month, €2 million Horizon Europe ASCEND feasibility study, which was conducted by an industry-led research consortium, found that building solar-powered data centres in orbit is technically and economically viable. It proposed an initial 10 MW deployment by 2036 scaling to 1 GW by 2050.
A review by the European Parliament of the ASCEND proposal in April noted that data centres in space could ease constraints on terrestrial centres, including their high electrical demands. But it also noted that the "main barrier is economic, rather than technical, with future launch costs a key determinant".
Meanwhile, China launched an initial cluster of 12 AI computing satellites in May 2025 as the first phase of its planned 2,800-satellite "Three-Body Computing Constellation" led by ADA Space and Zhejiang Lab.
