# Banks look to dump AI debt

Semafor · PLenz · 2026-11-26

Banks are increasingly shopping around their AI portfolios as they look to offload some of their exposure to AI-related debt. Banks including Citi and JPMorgan are marketing portfolios of loans, people familiar with the deals tell me, to manage risk or, in some cases, ready themselves to take on even more.
In some instances, banks are pitching structures that would keep the loans on their balance sheets while shifting the risk of losses to investors. That financial engineering tool, known as synthetic risk transfer, has been a popular way for banks to move risk off their books while maintaining close relationships with the corporate borrowers.
Some of this naturally occurs in December when banks are eager to close out the year with a clean bill of health, but it’s notable that banks lending into the buildout are joining hyperscalers in trying to push AI obligations off their books. There’s been so much AI-related debt issuance, it’s crowded out Treasurys and some investors are starting to feel tapped out. Others say they are offloading risk so they can lend more, not to pull back. But either way, the risk isn’t so much disappearing as moving somewhere harder to see.
