# Global stocks rally as investors shrug off U.S. tariffs and AI jitters to 'fish for bargains'

CNBC · Chloe Taylor, Lee Ying Shan · 2026-11-26

Global stocks rallied on Thursday, staging a tentative recovery from an intense AI-driven sell-off and looked past a fresh set of tariffs unveiled by the White House on Wednesday.
U.S. markets will be closed Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday and will have a shortened trading session on Friday. During Wednesday's regular session, the Dow gained 0.33%, the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 0.62% and the S&P 500 shed 0.25%, with the major averages paring losses seen earlier in the trading day.
In Europe on Thursday, equities rose sharply. The pan-European Stoxx 600 was 1.6% higher by 12:20 p.m. in London (7:20 a.m. ET), with all sectors and major bourses in positive territory.
Germany's DAX led gains on a rise of 1.9%, while the Stoxx 600 Technology index jumped 2.5%. The regional tech index has lost around 12% so far this month, amid jitters around a potential AI bubble bursting and the ousting — and return — of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO.
German semiconductor firm Infineon was more than 4% higher in afternoon trading, as chip stocks notched a boost. ASMI and ASML each gained just over 3%, while Be Semiconductor added 3.5%.
Asia-Pacific markets also rose on Thursday, with regional tech stocks also rebounded sharply, led by Japanese chip-related companies.
South Korea's Kospi gained 0.29% to close at 4,711.21, while the small-cap Kosdaq index advanced 0.78% to 655.71 after the country's central bank hiked benchmark lending rates to a 2-year high in its final meeting of the year.
Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.86% to 57,499.16 while the Topix edged 0.11% higher to 3,750.4. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 gained 0.82% to 8,763.8.
Hong Kong's Hang Seng index was up 1.52% in its last hour of trade, while mainland China's CSI 300 edged higher marginally, closing at 3,900.55.
SoftBank Group surged more than 7%, while chip-testing equipment maker Advantest rose 4.57%. Lasertec and Disco gained , while Tokyo Electron climbed 0.59%
In South Korea, memory chip giants SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics advanced more than 2% and 0.35%, respectively. The gains followed a bruising session Wednesday amid renewed concerns over the sustainability of artificial intelligence spending.
'Fishing for bargains'
Russ Mould, investment director at AJ Bell, labeled it a "solid day" for regional markets, suggesting investors were hunting for opportunities in the wake of an intense global sell-off.
"The rollercoaster stock market is back on an upwards path as a new round of Trump tariffs aren't as bad as feared and the US takes the day off to celebrate Thanksgiving," he said in a note on Thursday morning. "It was notable that investors cast their net wide for opportunities, fishing for bargains in the rubble following the tech sell-off and looking at other sectors in the quest for diversification."
But Mould warned investors not to be "too complacent."
"Oil prices heading higher is not good for efforts to tame inflation, neither is the prospect of retaliation by countries on the receiving end of new US tariffs," he said.
The Trump administration on Wednesday unveiled a fresh barrage of tariffs on more than a dozen trading partners, including China, the European Union and India.
Wall Street gets a breather
Daniela Hathorn, senior financial market analyst at Capital.com, said in a Thursday note that the mood in markets was "cautious rather than uniformly negative."
"European markets are attempting to rebound after yesterday's decline, while U.S. cash markets are closed for Thanksgiving, limiting the chance to test whether Wall Street's late recovery can hold," she said. "The main focus now will be trying to determine how many further increases the Fed considers necessary in early 2027 and whether AI shares can withstand that rate path while investors continue to question the returns on heavy spending."
U.S. inflation data on Wednesday reinforced expectations of further monetary tightening by the Federal Reserve. The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 3.8% year on year in October, accelerating from 3.7% in September, while core inflation came in slightly above expectations at 3.3%.
Mark Haefele, chief investment officer at UBS Global Wealth Management, said in a note on Thursday morning that his team believes investors should maintain strategic allocations to equities.
But he advised market participants to "review current allocations and use market moves as an opportunity to rebalance, refocus, and strengthen portfolio resilience."
