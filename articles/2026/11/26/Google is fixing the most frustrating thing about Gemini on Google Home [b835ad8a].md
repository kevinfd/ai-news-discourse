# Google is fixing the most frustrating thing about Gemini on Google Home

Android Police · Jade Bryan Jardinico · 2026-11-26

Gemini's adoption on Google Home hasn't been the smoothest. Early on, users complained about unreliable smart home controls and painfully slow, turtle-paced commands with Gemini.
There were also more frustrating issues, including basic commands completely failing and responses being interrupted.
Fortunately, Google has been slowly and painfully addressing many of those Gemini complaints. Now, its latest Google Home update takes another step toward making Gemini a more capable and reliable home assistant.
Gemini is now less frustrating on Google Home
Google released its second November Google Home update (via Android Authority), packed with enhancements for the Gemini assistant and smart home device integration.
Perhaps most important, Google is making smart home commands "substantially" faster when using Gemini for device controls. The company says "responses & actions for the most common commands should feel snappier."
The release also improves how Gemini handles follow-up requests. It can now remember and better understand the context of ongoing queries.
For example, if you ask it to switch on the bulb in the kitchen, it remembers that you turned it on during the previous command. This avoids Gemini wrongly interpreting the status of the light and giving you a dead-end answer.
Similarly, Google is fixing the bug where Gemini gives you a "nothing is playing" response when you ask it to stop a currently playing music or video track.
Perform actions while chatting with Gemini Live
The newly improved Gemini Live for Google Home, available for Home Premium accounts, is starting to roll out to users.
Google is upgrading Gemini Live with a new speech-to-speech architecture for a more responsive conversation and better natural language understanding.
You can chat with Gemini Live more naturally, including interrupting it mid-sentence or changing topics. Plus, you can perform smart home control actions, like turning on your TV, while conversing and without ending the session.
If you have kids, you might also find the new "Hey Google, tell me a story" command useful for bedtime or daytime reading. Google is rolling this out now, letting listeners customize how Gemini sounds and adjust the pacing.
New siren feature arrives on Nest and onn cameras
In addition to these Gemini-based enhancements, Google is also giving the Google Home app a host of upgrades and fixes. That includes a new remote siren feature added to support outdoor cameras. Live viewers can activate or interrupt a 30-second alarm, which can help deter intruders or call attention.
It supports the wired 2nd-gen Nest Cam Outdoor, the onn Outdoor Camera Plug-in, and the onn Floodlight Camera Hardwired.
The two onn models are also getting the Familiar Faces feature, which is already available on Nest cameras.
The search giant adds that it is fixing notification previews and improving video playback on Android. It also updated the Google Home Speaker setup process on both iOS and Android, adding visual cues and prompts.
Google continues to polish Gemini on Google Home, which is set to fully replace the classic Google Assistant sometime in the second half of 2027, assuming there will be no major quirks ahead.
