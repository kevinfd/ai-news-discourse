97% Probability of an AI Bubble | A 3-Minute Explanation of Why It Does Not Mean a 97% Chance of a Crash
The "97% probability of an AI bubble" refers to the "probability that AI-related stocks will be in a bubble state in September 2026," calculated by researchers at the UK's London Business School (LBS) and other institutions by analyzing articles from The Economist using AI. It is not the probability of a market crash.
The figure "97% probability of an AI bubble" is the kind of sound bite that spreads easily. In this article, we summarize in three minutes how this 97% figure was calculated and why it should not be interpreted as a "97% chance of a crash."

What was announced?
On November 25, LBS published an introductory article on the research. The study was conducted by Marco Grotteria of LBS, along with Afonso Monteiro and Constantine Yannelis of the University of Cambridge.
- They had an AI (large language model) analyze approximately 170 years' worth of articles from The Economist
- Based on the "tone of the language" in the articles, they created a monthly Bubble Index
- Based on that index, they estimated the probability of AI-related stocks being in a bubble state in September 2026 at up to 97%
- The index level for September 2026 was on par with or higher than the peak of the dot-com bubble in March 2000
The LBS article also notes that the Nasdaq index dropped by more than 18% from early October to late November 2026.
How did they calculate 97%?
It has long been said that bubbles are "only recognized in hindsight." Therefore, the researchers focused on how journalists were writing at the time.
- They initially built the index using articles from The Economist from 1843 to 2013
- They extended it to the present by adding articles from 2014 through September 2026
- For each article, the AI scored aspects such as "mentions of speculation," "regulatory warnings," "concerns that stock prices are overvalued," and "depictions of euphoria"
- These were combined into a monthly bubble index
According to a summary by Superpower Daily, the AI scored 795,964 articles, of which approximately 486,000 were market-related.
The key takeaway is looking at the "rate of increase" rather than the "volume" of warnings. According to Grotteria, The Economist frequently mentions bubbles even in calm market environments, meaning that only sharp surges well above normal levels serve as a meaningful signal.
Trends in AI stock prices are also summarized in the article published the day Nvidia's Huang returned as CEO.
Why does it not mean a "97% crash probability"?
Because the study itself shows that "newspapers and magazines exaggerate crashes too much."
- Articles wrote about major crashes occurring within six months of claiming "it's a bubble" as if they had an average probability of 20%
- The rate at which they actually occurred was 5.7%—an exaggeration of roughly 3.5 times
- Even so, the index had the power to distinguish bubble months from non-bubble months (according to a summary by Superpower Daily, the index was higher during bubble months in 74% of the compared pairs)
In other words, 97% is the strength of the signal indicating that "we seem to be in a bubble state right now"; it is not a forecast that "stocks will crash with a 97% probability."
The researchers also state that whether this index is useful depends on whether one views the "loss from missing a bubble" or the "loss from a false alarm" as more serious. Their view is that it is extremely useful for countries and central banks that place greater weight on avoiding missed warnings.
Does this mean AI itself is doomed?
No, it does not. Mr. Grotteria stated, "Just because it is a bubble does not mean AI technology is useless. The technology may be very productive, but investors are still in the process of learning its true value."
The researchers also wrote, "Even if speculation is occurring, it does not mean the technology lacks economic value," and "An AI bubble and a genuine technological revolution can coexist."
During the dot-com bubble, stock prices fell sharply as well, but the internet itself remained an integral part of our lives. This study also urges us to distinguish between the value of AI as a tool and the stock prices of AI-related companies.

How should we interpret this?
- "97%" is the probability of being in a bubble. Do not confuse it with the probability of a crash
- The figures in the article are as of September 2026. Subsequent price movements are not included
- The usefulness of AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini is a completely separate matter from the stock prices of AI-related companies

Frequently Asked Questions
Q. Does 97% mean that "AI stocks will fall by 97%"?
A. No. It is "the probability that AI-related stocks will be in a bubble state" as of September 2026. It is not the magnitude of a decline or the probability of a market crash.
Q. How can magazine articles reveal a bubble?
A. It examines how much more intensely reporters write that things are "too expensive" or "experiencing mania" compared to usual. According to the research, the rate of increase in warnings served as a better clue than the total volume of warnings.
