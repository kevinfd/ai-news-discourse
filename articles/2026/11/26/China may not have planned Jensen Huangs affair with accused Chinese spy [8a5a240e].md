# China may not have planned Jensen Huangs affair with accused Chinese spy

Mashable · Amanda Yeo · 2026-11-26

China may not have planned Jensen Huang's affair with accused Chinese spy
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was returned to the job earlier this week, the board reinstating him amidst plummeting stock prices and apparent intervention from President Donald Trump. Now doubt is being cast on allegations that one of Huang's affair partners was a Chinese spy.
Huang has admitted to cheating on his wife with at least four partners as of this week, however his relationship with Alice Huang (no relation) has drawn the most scrutiny. A former executive at Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed, Ms. Huang is suspected of being an intelligent agent who shared information with the Chinese government and smuggled Nvidia chips into China, circumventing U.S. restrictions.
As such, Nvidia opened an independent investigation once Huang and Ms. Huang's relationship came to light, as well as established a committee to oversee the company's national security policies and export control compliance. Huang will also be interviewed by the U.S. House Foreign Affairs Committee, House Select Committee on China, and House Intelligence Committee on Dec. 1, and testify before the U.S. Senate on Dec. 3.

However, the Wall Street Journal and New York Times now report that the U.S. has no evidence that the Chinese government directed Ms. Huang to begin an affair with Nvidia's CEO. Instead, current evidence indicates that the pair's extramarital entanglement may have been a matter of ordinary human infidelity rather than deliberate international espionage. U.S officials reportedly informed congressional staffers of this in a briefing on Nov. 24, the day after Nvidia reinstated Huang.

This isn't to say that U.S. agencies didn't find any link between Ms. Huang and the Chinese government at all. U.S. officials reportedly stated that China's Ministry of State Security asked Ms. Huang to provide them with some unspecified information, which she allegedly did in accordance with Chinese law. They also claimed that her husband, businessman Kang Wei, was a Chinese intelligence asset. However, while the full extent of Ms. Huang's relationship with the Chinese government remains unclear, there is reportedly nothing to suggest that she passed on any significant information.
The Chinese government has consistently rejected claims that Ms. Huang was an intelligence agent. In an Oct. 30 press conference, Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Guo Jiakun called such reports "unsubstantiated" and "nothing but smears against China." He later reiterated China's stance after Huang revealed his first affair on Nov. 8.
"On the so-called 'Chinese spy,' we have made our position clear," Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson Mao Ning said in a Nov. 12 press briefing. "China's sci-tech progress relies on independent innovation and open cooperation. The U.S. should stop fabricating the 'Chinese spy' narratives as a pretext for imposing technology restrictions."
The U.S. government currently restricts Nvidia's chip sales to China, citing national security concerns. Several other categories of foreign-made devices are also banned from entering the U.S. due to national security, including WiFi routers and robot vacuums.
