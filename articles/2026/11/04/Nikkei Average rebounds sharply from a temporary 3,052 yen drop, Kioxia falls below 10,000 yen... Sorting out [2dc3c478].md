# Nikkei Average rebounds sharply from a temporary 3,052 yen drop, Kioxia falls below 10,000 yen... Sorting out a day of AI regulation concerns [November 4 Japanese Stocks]

note · 田村祐一 × 西村剛 日本株研究所 · 2026-11-04

Nikkei Average rebounds sharply from a temporary 3,052 yen drop, Kioxia falls below 10,000 yen... Sorting out a day of AI regulation concerns [November 4 Japanese Stocks]
On November 4, Japanese stocks saw the Nikkei Average fall for the third consecutive day, closing at 62,188.63 yen (-1.42%), down 893 yen.
As vote counting for the US midterm elections progressed, concerns over AI regulation intensified, and the index was sold down to 60,030 yen—a drop of 3,052 yen—during the session, but it recovered 2,159 yen by the close of the afternoon session.
In this article, based on the day's market commentary video, we will organize the details of the decline and the outlook on the US midterm elections.
Additionally, we will summarize the supply and demand for Kioxia and SoftBank Group, the differences in strength between AI semiconductor stocks, and points to check from tomorrow onwards.
Commentary: Yuichi Tamura (Head of Investment, Fairtrade Co., Ltd.)
Nikkei Average drops 3,052 yen at one point, recovers 2,159 yen from the low
The closing price of the Nikkei Stock Average was 62,188.63 yen (down 893.23 yen, -1.42% from the previous day).
After falling to 60,030 yen at 13:37, buying interest emerged, leading to a significant recovery.
The TOPIX was at 3,895.38 points (-0.89%), falling below the 3,900-point mark.
However, it also recovered 103 points from its low.
In the US market the previous day, the NASDAQ rose by +0.83%.
AMD's strong earnings confirmed the strength of AI demand, while the NY Dow was down -0.38%.
Since stock indices in Shanghai, Hong Kong, and South Korea were positive, Japan was the only market in Asia to buck the trend and decline.
The dollar-yen rate was 158.15 yen, a yen depreciation of 0.28 yen from the previous day.
The fact that stock prices did not stop falling despite the weaker yen is a characteristic of this day.
Tamura views the sharp recovery in the afternoon session as a movement that could be called a 'reverse Trump shock'.
His assessment is that while risk-aversion movements intensified momentarily due to the Republican Party's loss, prices recovered quickly.
US midterm elections and AI regulation, 3 companies account for 60% of the decline
The core of the selling was concern over AI regulation following the results of the US midterm elections.
It has been reported that the Democratic Party is dominant, winning the House and leading in the Senate.
There are voices within the Democratic Party calling for restrictions and regulations on the construction of AI data centers, and the power burden is also being viewed as an issue.
On the other hand, the President is a Republican and holds veto power.
Tamura believes that laws that would halt investment are unlikely to pass.
Japan has many companies involved in the manufacturing equipment and components for AI semiconductors, and Tamura's analysis is that this election result is likely to be a headwind.
Looking at the breadth of the market, 1,107 stocks (71.3%) declined on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market.
Out of 33 sectors, 25 declined, while only 8 sectors rose.
Only some domestic demand sectors, such as air transport and real estate, bucked the trend and rose.
The air transport sector is benefiting from lower crude oil prices.
Non-ferrous metals, electric appliances, and oil and coal products were among those that declined.
The stocks that pushed down the Nikkei Average's decline are as follows.
- Advantest: -258.90 yen
- Tokyo Electron: -172.93 yen
- SoftBank Group: -114.08 yen
These three companies alone accounted for -545.91 yen, making up about 60% of the decline.
Fast Retailing mitigated the decline by +162.62 yen.
Nikkei Average temporarily fell below the minor crash line
The Nikkei Average is at a position of -12.02% from its closing high of 70,683.98 yen on 10/6. 
The intraday low of 60,030 yen briefly fell below the -15% "minor crash line" of 60,081 yen. 
However, it recovered by the close.
Tamura sees the current market as being on the verge of either heading toward a rebound or seeing further declines. 
He does not strongly believe a crash is coming, but his position is that it is important to organize the levels to watch in case it does happen.
There are two levels to check. 
Whether the Nikkei Average falls below 60,081 yen at the close, or whether the TOPIX falls below 3,777.48 points, which corresponds to -10%, at the close. 
The TOPIX is -7.19% from its closing high of 4,197.20, and it is 117.90 points away from this line. 
In both cases, a momentary breach during the day is not considered reaching the level; we look at the closing price.
The divergence between individual AI semiconductor stocks has widened
The median decline for the 11 major AI semiconductor companies from their October highs to the November 4 close was -33.7%. 
This is significantly higher than the Nikkei Average's -12.0%.
Those with smaller declines were Advantest (-17.7%), Lasertec (-19.4%), and Tokyo Electron (-25.2%). 
Tamura explains that these are companies responsible for inspection before AI chip shipment or for equipment processes. 
Tamura's view is that their high global market share and lack of competition are the reasons why their declines were smaller.
On the other hand, SCREEN fell -46.3% and Kioxia fell -45.6%, both dropping over 45% from their October highs. 
Tamura believes that in both cases, the realization of a peak in growth after their earnings reports led to the selling.
Tamura believes that from now on, the market will be one where we check figures separately for each company, rather than just grouping them as AI semiconductors. 
For example, free cash flow for SoftBank Group, and profit margins or operating rates for Kioxia.
Also, Kioxia and SoftBank Group have fallen below their July lows.
Kioxia (285A): Briefly fell below 10,000 yen, unrealized profit is 7.54%
Kioxia closed at 10,735 yen, down 610 yen (-5.38%). 
During the day, it was sold down to 9,635 yen and briefly fell below 10,000 yen, but it recovered by 1,100 yen.
Tamura believes that the impact of the overall decline in AI semiconductors was greater than any Kioxia-specific news on this day.
The estimated cost distribution for the last 30 days is as follows.
- Percentage of people holding with unrealized profit: 7.54% (previous day 1.69%)
- Estimated unrealized profit/loss ratio for all holders: -22.22% (previous day -21.43%)
- Benchmark for relief selling: 13,428 yen
- Benchmark for support level: 10,562 yen
While the percentage of unrealized profit improved, the unrealized profit/loss ratio worsened. 
It is currently hitting new recent lows, and supply and demand are still in a poor state.
13,428 yen is the price range that serves as a benchmark for relief selling.
SoftBank Group (9984): Third consecutive day of decline, reached 3,967 yen during the day
SoftBank Group fell for the third consecutive day, closing down 141 yen at 4,269 yen (-3.20%).
During the day, it was sold down to 3,967 yen, but recovered by the close.
The Nikkei Average was pushed down by 114 yen.
Tamura believes that because the company invests heavily in AI, concerns over AI regulation led to the selling.
The estimated cost distribution for the last 30 days is as follows.
- Percentage of holders with unrealized gains: 11.83% (12.56% the previous day)
- Estimated unrealized profit/loss ratio for all holders: -16.16% (-14.88% the previous day)
- Benchmark for relief selling: 4,599 yen
- Benchmark for support level: 4,269 yen
The support level is at almost the same level as the closing price, and the layer of holders becomes thin down to the 4,100 yen range.
On the upside, 4,599 yen is the benchmark for relief selling.
Tamura sees this as a phase where the price could swing in either direction.
Checkpoints for tomorrow and beyond
- Policies after the US midterm elections: Will concrete measures for AI/data center regulation emerge, or will it end with just headlines?
- US AI/semiconductor stocks (AMD, NVIDIA, SOX): Will the US confirm the sharp drop in Japanese stocks?
- Nikkei Average: Can it hold today's low of 60,030 yen, can it establish itself at 62,000 yen, or will it close below 60,081 yen?
- TOPIX: Can it recover to 3,900 points, or will it close below 3,777.48 points?
- Will high-priced semiconductor stocks like Advantest and Tokyo Electron stop falling?
- Kioxia: Will it stop setting new recent lows, and what is the distance from 13,428 yen?
- SoftBank Group: Will it move closer to 4,599 yen or the 4,100 yen range?
