# What is Steam's Generative AI Disclosure? Differences Between Pre-Generated and Live-Generated Content and How to Find Them

note · AIゲーム探検家 · 2026-11-04

What is Steam's Generative AI Disclosure? Differences Between Pre-Generated and Live-Generated Content and How to Find Them
For games distributed on Steam, developers must declare whether they use generative AI via a questionnaire. The content of this declaration appears in the "AI-Generated Content Disclosure" section on the store page. There are two categories of declaration: "Pre-generated," which is created by AI during development, and "Live-generated," which is created by AI while the game is being played.
I have summarized the differences between these two categories, what is required of developers, and where players should look on the store page, based on the official Steamworks documentation (verified October 27, 2026) and one Steam store page.
If you are looking for games where AI acts as a conversation partner, what is written about live-generated content in the disclosure section serves as a clue. This is not a record of actually playing Steam games, but rather content based on official documentation and an examination of one store page display.
What does Steam's "AI-Generated Content" disclosure ask?
The AI section of the Steamworks content questionnaire is described as "related to the use of AI in creating content included with the game and its consumption by players." This covers artwork, sound, story, localization, and more.
It is also noted that the use of AI tools to improve development efficiency is not the focus of this item. The focus is on content that is included with the game and consumed by the player.
Differences between pre-generated and live-generated content
There are two categories.
- Pre-generated: Any type of content created using AI tools during development that is included in the game and consumed by the player
- Live-generated: Any type of content created using AI tools while the game is running
According to official documentation, Valve reviews AI-generated content in the same way as non-AI-generated content during the pre-release review. Live-generated content has additional requirements, and developers must describe in the questionnaire what measures are being taken to ensure the AI does not generate illegal content.
Rules for developers when including live-generated AI
Games with live-generated content may incur ongoing costs for each input or output when using AI services. The official FAQ describes how to handle such cases as follows:
- The developer manages access to external services on behalf of the player
- Costs are collected from the player using payment methods supported by Steam
The official documentation lists four examples of how to collect these costs: incorporating the necessary costs into the main price of the game, handling them via microtransactions, allowing unlimited access through a recurring subscription, or unlocking usage limits via a one-time purchase DLC.
Additionally, regarding sexually explicit content rated "Adults Only" that uses live-generated AI, the official stance is that "at this time, it is not desirable to release sexually explicit content rated Adults Only that uses live-generated AI." The reason is that the legal and customer-related risks are extremely high.
Where should you look on the store page?
The disclosure appears after the store page description and before the system requirements, under the heading "AI-Generated Content Disclosure." Below the heading, it states, "The developer describes their use of AI-generated content in the game as follows," followed by the text written by the developer.
I checked one store page for "ARC Raiders" displayed in Japanese. It contained an English statement from the developer to the effect that while they may use procedural or AI-based tools during production, the final output reflects the team's creativity and expression.
In the one case I verified, the text written by the developer was displayed in this field. The official documentation also states that regardless of whether it was written in the questionnaire, Steam products must comply with content rules. The presence of this field does not mean that the use of AI is safe.
Things I could not verify
- I have not confirmed the total number of games on Steam that have a disclosure field, as there is no official figure.
- I only checked one store page. I have not individually verified whether it appears in the same location for other games.
- I have not been able to confirm whether pre-generated and live-generated content are listed separately within the disclosure field. I have also not confirmed what is actually written for games that use live generation.
The verification date is October 27, 2026. Official documentation and store page displays are subject to change.
