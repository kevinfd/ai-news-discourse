# What are you doing with 'ChatGPT'? Understanding your 'AI Age' through how you use it - Part 2: 2022, the era of 'asking questions'

note · ウサコッツ パパ · 2026-11-04

What are you doing with 'ChatGPT'? Understanding your 'AI Age' through how you use it - Part 2: 2022, the era of 'asking questions'
In the previous installment, I provided a timeline of when AI technologies and usage methods were introduced to the public, offering a benchmark to help you determine where your own usage habits fall. In that framework, the practice of asking an AI questions and receiving answers is categorized as '2022'.
For many people, asking questions and receiving answers was likely their first way of interacting with AI. In this installment, we will look back at what happened in 2022 and examine what was already understood at the time about the practice of simply asking questions.
2022 began with 'AI illustrations'
In 2022, the AI tools that continued to be released to the general public were not for text, but first for creating illustrations.
On April 6, OpenAI announced DALL·E 2, an image generation AI. OpenAI explained that it could create images by combining concepts, attributes, and artistic styles. Initially, a waitlist registration was required, but that requirement was removed on September 28, making it available to everyone.
On July 12, Midjourney entered public beta. Midjourney is a service within the chat platform Discord where, by typing the '/imagine' command followed by a description of the image you want to create, it returns four images.
Then, on August 22, Stable Diffusion was released.
What these three have in common is the format where you provide a text description of what you want to create, and the AI returns an image. In terms of asking with text and receiving a result, it can be seen that the same usage pattern as the later ChatGPT reached the general public in the field of art first.
I installed and used Stable Diffusion on my gaming PC around the time it was released. However, it wasn't really usable, often producing results like six fingers or four legs. I paid to use Midjourney. This was slightly better, but it still generated strange images. Also, having many restrictions was a characteristic of Midjourney.
Debates sparked by AI art
AI that creates art also sparked debate within the same year it was released.
In the United States, a work titled 'Théâtre D'opéra Spatial,' created with Midjourney, won first place in the digital art category at a 2022 Colorado state fair.
The prompt was written by Jason Allen. It was reported that other digital artists voiced their opposition, and the judges stated they did not know Midjourney used AI. However, they later stated that they would have awarded it first place even if they had known.
In Japan, the beta version of the illustration-creating AI service 'mimic' was released on August 29. However, concerns about the risk of misuse were raised, primarily by illustrators, and the service was suspended the following day, on the 30th. The developing company released a revised beta 2.0 version in November.
Regarding AI art, it can be seen that as early as 2022, questions were being raised not just about what AI could create, but also about whose work could be used and how.
November 30, ChatGPT is released
On November 30, 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT.
According to reports at the time, OpenAI explained that they built ChatGPT to allow for interaction in a conversational format. They described it as being capable of answering follow-up questions, admitting its mistakes, challenging incorrect premises, and rejecting inappropriate requests.
For training, a method called Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) was used to improve answers based on human evaluation. This method follows the same line of research as InstructGPT, an AI that follows instructions, which OpenAI presented in a paper in March of the same year, 2022. The title of the paper means training language models to follow instructions using human feedback. And ChatGPT was made available for free as a research preview period.
I have been using ChatGPT since around this time. Initially, unless I provided instructions in English prompts, it wouldn't return decent answers, and the conversation wouldn't flow well; it certainly didn't feel like something I could rely on for work.
4. The spread immediately after release
A few days after the release, OpenAI co-founder Sam Altman posted the following on X (published in an Entrepreneur India article on December 5): "ChatGPT launched on wednesday. today it crossed 1 million users!"
Later, Reuters reported, citing UBS estimates, that the number of monthly users reached approximately 100 million in January 2023, about two months after the release. As I wrote last time, this figure is an estimate based on data from an analytics firm, not an announcement from OpenAI.
ChatGPT at this time was a service in the form of a dialogue where you would type text into an input field and read the returned answer. In my previous criteria, I designated 2022 as the year for the usage style of asking questions and receiving answers, referring to this period.
5. In the research world, the 'way of asking' was starting to be questioned
Within 2022, before ChatGPT was released, it had already been shown in the research world that even with the same AI, the answer changes depending on how you ask.
In January, Google researchers demonstrated in a paper that having the AI write out the chain of thought before providing an answer significantly improves its complex reasoning capabilities. The method involves showing a few examples of the intermediate thought process within the prompt.
In May, Takeshi Kojima and others published a paper showing that AI can reason even without examples just by adding the phrase "Let's think step by step" before the answer. The paper reported that in a test called MultiArith using the InstructGPT model (text-davinci-002), the accuracy rate increased from 17.7% to 78.7%.
In other words, as of 2022, it was already known in research that the quality of the answer changes depending on how the question is written. It is in the following year, 2023, that this became a focus of attention as a 'skill' among general users.
6. What was known from the beginning, as long as you only use it to ask questions
At the time of ChatGPT's release, OpenAI itself had already indicated its limitations. According to reports at the time, OpenAI wrote that ChatGPT might return answers that sound plausible but are inaccurate or nonsensical. There was also a warning that while it would refuse some inappropriate requests, it might still respond to harmful instructions or exhibit biased behavior.
About a week after the release, an incident related to these limitations occurred. Stack Overflow, a site for programming questions and answers, temporarily banned posts created with ChatGPT in early December. According to reports, the reason was that while the rate of correct answers from ChatGPT was low, they looked like good answers at first glance. It was also reported that thousands of posts were flooding in, and the mechanism for maintaining answer quality could not keep up.
Combining OpenAI's explanation and Stack Overflow's decision, one can read that with a usage style of just asking questions and receiving answers, the task of verifying whether the answer is correct remains with the user.
7. Re-evaluating 2022 usage from today's perspective
So, is the usage style of asking questions and receiving answers already outdated? Let's consider this as one perspective.
The usage style of receiving answers to questions is the form in which it reached the public in 2022, and in this series, I consider it the most basic form. It is considered a form that is still used today in situations such as the starting point for research or checking the meaning of words.
On the other hand, the limitations that were known as of 2022 can be compensated for on the user's side. For example, the following points:
- Verify the numbers, dates, and names that appear in the answer against the original source material
- Ask the AI to follow up with the basis or source of the answer
- Do not pass on or publish AI answers to others as they are
- For questions that require a reasoning process, ask the AI to think step-by-step before providing the answer
The first three are procedures organized in this series based on the limitations written by OpenAI itself and the reasons why Stack Overflow stopped allowing answers to be posted. The fourth is the writing method from the paper in May 2022 introduced in '5.'. However, the figures in the paper are results from a specific model and specific tests at that time. Since it is not guaranteed that the answers will be just as good with current AI, please compare the results yourself when testing.
8. Is your 'AI Age' still stuck in 2022?
Please reflect on how you use it by asking yourself the following questions.
- Do you only use AI when asking questions about things you want to know?
- Do you use the answer returned from a single question exactly as it is?
- When an answer is incorrect, do you rephrase your question and ask again?
The third point, asking again, means you are testing in your usage that changing how you write a question changes the answer. In this series, we consider this the gateway to the 2023 way of using AI: 'refining instructions'.
If you fit the first two but not the third, then by the standards of this series, your usage is from '2022'. That in itself is not bad. However, there is a possibility that you are evaluating AI without knowing the ways of using it that have emerged since 2023.
9. Things to keep in mind
The limitations of ChatGPT introduced in this installment were written at the time of its release in November 2022. This installment does not cover how much has changed with subsequent AI. You need to be careful not to judge current AI based on 2022 evaluations.
Also, regarding the discussion surrounding AI art, this installment only covers what happened in 2022. The handling of copyright and similar issues is not covered here. When using such services, please check their terms of service and the official information available at that time.
10. Next time: 2023
After the release of ChatGPT, what began to attract attention was the way of writing questions itself. Next time, we will take up the 2023 era of 'refining instructions' and look at what was happening when prompt engineering began to attract attention as a professional skill.
