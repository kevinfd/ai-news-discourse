# Centre’s AI regulation consultation paper nearly ready, say sources

Moneycontrol · Ishaan Gera · 2026-11-04

15
The government has examined how other countries regulate artificial intelligence and consulted stakeholders, including academics
  November 04, 2026 / 16:47 IST  
The Centre’s consultation paper on regulating artificial intelligence (AI) is nearly ready, people familiar with the matter told Moneycontrol, as the government prepares to seek public feedback on safeguards for the technology.
The government considered international approaches to AI regulation and consulted a range of stakeholders, including academics, while drafting the paper, a senior official said.
Union Electronics and Information Technology Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw announced the consultation on October 8, saying the government would release the paper within a month. He identified AI safety as the main priority, flagging deepfakes and the potential psychological harm to users. Vaishnaw also said the consultation would cover skilling and ways to ensure the benefits of AI are widely shared.
On October 23, Vaishnaw said the paper would be released in the coming weeks and would seek views from citizens, industry and users of AI. He said the risks posed by the technology had reached a point where the government should consider legislation and new regulations.
The consultation follows the release of the India AI Governance Guidelines in November 2025, which set out principles and practical recommendations for developers, companies and regulators.
What the existing AI guidelines recommend
The guidelines set out seven principles for developing and deploying AI, including keeping people in control of AI systems where possible, assigning responsibility based on the risk of harm and giving users and regulators enough information to understand how systems work.
The committee that drafted the guidelines found that existing laws could address many AI-related risks. It nevertheless recommended a review to clarify who bears liability when an AI system causes harm, how data protection rules apply to AI development and how copyright law applies to material used to train models.
It also called for common standards to verify the origin and authenticity of digital content, an issue that has gained prominence with the spread of deepfakes.
The guidelines proposed an AI Governance Group to coordinate policy across government, supported by a Technology and Policy Expert Committee. They also envisaged an AI Safety Institute to support research and testing.
