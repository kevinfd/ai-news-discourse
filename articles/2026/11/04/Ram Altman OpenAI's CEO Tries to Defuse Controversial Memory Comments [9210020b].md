# Ram Altman? OpenAI's CEO Tries to Defuse Controversial Memory Comments

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-04

Can "Ram Altman" save us from the memory shortage?
On X, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman briefly changed his name to the new moniker while addressing controversial comments he made last week, in which he suggested that PCs outfitted with loads of DRAM are a waste of memory. “I have been arguing for the world to ramp up memory production for a long time,” he tweeted on Tuesday night.
Altman engaged in some damage control for comments he made last week in the Cheeky Pint podcast. In the interview, he seemed to object to users running AI locally on PCs built with high memory specs. “But from a global allocation perspective, actually, it’s quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world’s limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer. And be used 5% of the time,” he said. “You want maximum utilization in the cloud if you’re trying to deal with this very supply-constrained environment.”
Altman’s apparently self-serving comments sparked a wave of backlash from AI enthusiasts and PC gamers, who were quick to point out that his company has been a major driver for the ongoing memory shortage, which has led to price hikes across consumer electronics. Some users have also been poking fun at OpenAI’s CEO with memes and photos, including a fake GoFundMe for his company’s memory needs. On Tuesday, one user tweeted at Altman with an image of a trail of RAM sticks leading to a box trap.
The image received an “lol” from Altman on X. A user then asked him to change his profile name to “Ram Altman,” which he briefly did, according to screenshots.

Altman then explained: “but only because...i actually do want the world to have tons of memory and people to put as much as they want in their computers, and it is fucking annoying that this was taken out of context.”
One user then asked: “why u want my ram then?” Altman simply replied: “i want you to have so much ram, lets build more.”

Still, not everyone is convinced. “Then don’t order 40% of the world’s RAM at once and then claim your for people having RAM,” wrote one user in response. “I’m sure this is a futile attempt to PR damage control,” said another user on Reddit.
The harsh reception comes amid persistent concerns about AI’s impact on society and the memory crunch. In yesterday’s election, US voters overwhelmingly supported measures to block and restrict new AI data center construction.
Perhaps in response to the concerns, Altman also went out of his way to tweet on election night: “People should always be in control of the future…. People should not cede control of the future to superintelligence, and that requirement should ultimately constrain AI progress.”
