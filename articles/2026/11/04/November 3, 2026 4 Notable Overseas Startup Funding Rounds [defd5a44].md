November 3, 2026: 4 Notable Overseas Startup Funding Rounds
Around November 3, funding for four companies in the AI and defense tech sectors was announced. These include Fal, which operates an inference infrastructure for generative media; Traversal, which uses AI for incident response; Lazarus AI, which advocates for "independent AI" that keeps data within the company; and Corridian, which develops large unmanned underwater vehicles. While the funding amounts and stages differ, when lined up, three trends emerge: "infrastructure to run AI," "tools to use AI in the field," and "new industries where AI is entering."
1. Fal: $1.3 billion in Series F, valuation of $16 billion
Inference infrastructure for generative media
Fal is a company that provides inference infrastructure for running generative models for images, video, and audio. In addition to operating its own models, it also works on fine-tuning existing models to make them faster and higher quality. Its customers include video production companies, advertising agencies, e-commerce businesses, game developers, and marketing teams, all of whom run models in their production environments. According to reports, Bessemer lists Adobe, Canva, Shopify, World Labs, and Amazon as customers.
Series E and F announced simultaneously
In its official blog, the company announced two rounds together: Series E, led by Sequoia Capital, and Series F, led by Bessemer Venture Partners. NVIDIA joined the Series F round as a strategic investor. While the blog does not mention the amounts or valuation, reports state that the Series F round was $1.3 billion with a valuation of $16 billion. The Series E round was completed earlier this year for approximately $350 million, bringing the total for both rounds to about $1.65 billion. In addition to NVIDIA, Sound Ventures and T. Rowe Price also participated in the Series F round.
Valuation doubled in just over half a year
Reports indicate that the valuation has doubled from $8 billion in April. In the Series D round in December 2025, the company raised $140 million at a valuation of $4.5 billion. It has been reported that the annualized revenue exceeds $800 million, but this was conveyed by Bloomberg citing people familiar with the matter, and Fal itself has not disclosed the figures.
Regarding the use of funds, the official blog mentions investment in the entire Fal ecosystem for 2027, new research, new forms of creation, and team building. Reports suggest that expanding inference capabilities and strengthening research and product teams—specifically securing computational resources—are the most pressing challenges.
2. Traversal: $120 million at a $1 billion valuation, AI finds the cause of failures
AI that addresses production environment failures
Traversal is a company that provides "AI SRE (Site Reliability Engineering)" for enterprises. Its goal is to diagnose, repair, and prevent the recurrence of failures in production systems. Its core technology is described as a "Production World Model" and a "Causal Search Engine." On November 3, it announced that it had raised $120 million at a $1 billion valuation. Its total funding to date is $173 million.
Investors and customers
The co-leads were Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint Ventures, and Khosla Ventures, with participation from Sequoia Capital, Battery Ventures, and Amex Ventures. Kleiner Perkins increased its investment, and Sequoia made an additional investment from its growth fund. When it emerged from stealth in June 2025, it raised $48 million led by Sequoia and Kleiner Perkins, and received a strategic investment from Amex Ventures in December of the same year.
Customers include Nubank, Harvey, PepsiCo, DigitalOcean, and American Express. It is said to have been operating in enterprise production environments for about 15 months since leaving stealth.
Growth metrics according to company announcements
According to figures released by the company, its ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) has increased ninefold this year, product usage has increased nearly 100-fold, and monthly active users have increased about 20-fold. The number of investigations in the previous quarter exceeded 150,000. The company claims to have supported over 4,000 engineers and handled over 1,000 major incidents, 50,000 minor incidents, and 5,000 critical production changes. Note that these are figures based on a paid press release and have not been verified by a third party.
"AI is bad at identifying the cause of failures" benchmark
The company created "ORCA-bench" in collaboration with Cornell Tech and Columbia. It reports that across 1,079 root cause identification tasks, the best results from five state-of-the-art agents showed a 25.3% accuracy rate for medium-difficulty failures, dropping to 10% when reports were ambiguous, with a hallucination rate reaching up to 40%. It is important to note that these figures were presented in the context of demonstrating the necessity of their own product.
The founding team consists of CEO Anish Agarwal, Raj Agrawal, Raaz Dwivedi, and Ahmed Lone, a former quant from Citadel Securities. Agarwal and Dwivedi are also faculty members at Columbia and Cornell Tech. The funds will be used for AI deployment across the entire software development lifecycle and for hiring in research, engineering, and sales.
3. Lazarus AI: Raising $43 Million with a Focus on "Independent AI"
Boston-based Lazarus AI announced a $43 million funding round on November 3. The round type, lead investors, and valuation were not disclosed. Participating investors include QBE, Mucker Capital, Motivate Ventures, State Farm Ventures, Wave Function Ventures, Indelible Solutions, Joyance Partners, AllegisNL Capital, One Way Ventures, AngelList, Techstars, and Plug and Play.
The "Independent AI" the company promotes allows customers to operate on their own servers, sovereign environments, or their own cloud, giving customers control over data management and the right to approve model changes. CEO Alex Panait stated, "Customers can keep sensitive data within their own walls, approve all model changes, and verify the basis for every result."
There are three products. The "Applied Intelligence System," which analyzes complex documents and indicates the supporting pages, has increased item-level accuracy by 12.5 points compared to standalone models in 150 customer use cases, according to the company. "Clearwing," which finds and reproduces vulnerabilities in source code and provides fixes, is designed to leave implementation to engineers. "Constructor," which extracts business logic from legacy systems to generate modern code, is being piloted with two major mainframe manufacturers.
Pricing is not based on token usage, and it is claimed that applications do not need to be rebuilt even if models or hardware change. In projected high-volume use cases, the company claims it can reduce annual operating costs by 78% compared to usage-based cutting-edge models. Customers span insurance, healthcare, government, finance, and technology, and are located in North America, Asia-Pacific, and Europe. The funds will be used for strategic partnerships, research and development, and new products.
4. Corridian: $38 Million in Seed Funding for "Silent Logistics" with Large Unmanned Underwater Vehicles
Founded by former General Dynamics executives
Boston-based Corridian raised $38 million in a seed round and publicly announced its business for the first time. The round was led by Point72 Ventures, with participation from Balerion Space Ventures, Harpoon Ventures, DCVC, and Marlinspike. The valuation was not disclosed.
Founded in September 2025, the four founders—CEO Mike Guay, President Greg Barrow, CTO Kyle Arace, and COO Mark Bodnar—all previously led one of the largest portfolios of underwater autonomous systems in the U.S. at General Dynamics. Past programs have been deployed by the U.S. Navy and the Australian Navy, among others. Advisors include retired Admiral Lisa Franchetti, who served as the 33rd Chief of Naval Operations.
What they are building
They are building extra-large autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) capable of carrying ISO-standard containerized cargo and traveling thousands of nautical miles without a crew. Characterized by stealth and high survivability, the immediate focus is on payload and effect delivery, intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR), and logistics in contested environments. The flagship vehicle is scheduled to be unveiled in 2027, and the company states that its supply chain is independent of the "submarine industrial base."
Guay stated that relying on surface or air domains for large-scale logistics is a "serious vulnerability" and positioned the company's efforts as "stealth logistics." The funds will be used for the development of the flagship vehicle, the establishment of research and development facilities, and the hiring of an underwater engineering team. Customer names have not been disclosed.
