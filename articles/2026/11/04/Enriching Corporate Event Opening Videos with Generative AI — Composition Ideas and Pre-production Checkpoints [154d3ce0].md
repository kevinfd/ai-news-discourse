Enriching Corporate Event Opening Videos with Generative AI — Composition Ideas and Pre-production Checkpoints
The venue lights dim, and the first video plays on the screen. The impression of an event can change significantly in these first one to two minutes.
Whether it is an internal kickoff, an anniversary ceremony, a company-hosted seminar or conference, or a product launch, an opening video helps shift the attendees' mindset to 'it's about to begin'.
On the other hand, we often hear comments like, 'We can't spend that much budget every time,' or 'It ends up being just a video showing the company name and logo.'
That is why we at Glis would like to propose opening videos that incorporate generative AI. In this article, we have summarized from the perspective of a video production company what changes when using AI, what kind of events it suits, and what you should check before you start creating.

What changes in an opening video when you incorporate AI?
You can create a 'worldview' that cannot be captured in live-action footage
Shots zooming in on Earth from space, futuristic cityscapes, or abstract visuals symbolizing a company's journey. Trying to capture such footage in live-action would require large-scale sets or CG production, making it difficult to afford for a video intended for a single event.
With generative AI, you can now create these types of images from text or reference images. We feel the biggest change is that it has become easier to express the event's theme as a 'visual worldview'.
You can compare multiple visual concepts before you start creating
In meetings for opening videos, it is not uncommon to decide on a direction using only words like 'cool' or 'exciting.' When relying only on words, it is easy for the final result to be 'not what I had in mind.'
With AI, you can create several video drafts with different directions and decide while actually comparing them. Since you can show the visuals to stakeholders for internal review, it becomes easier to reach a consensus.
Even for annual events, it is easier to recreate according to the theme
For events that happen every year, such as kickoffs or anniversary ceremonies, you cannot just play the same video as last year. Since parts created with AI are easier to recreate by changing themes or color palettes, it becomes easier to prepare visuals tailored to each event's theme.
It is suitable for events like these
Internal events (kickoffs, award ceremonies, anniversary ceremonies)
You can convey company policies and the vision you are aiming for through the visual worldview. This is a suitable way to use it when you want to unite the employees' feelings.
Company-hosted seminars and conferences
It can also be used for loop videos played while waiting for the start, or short videos inserted between sessions. It could also be played at the beginning of an online broadcast.
Exhibition stages, product launches
You can showcase the background of a new product or service, or future scenarios where it will be used, through evocative imagery.
Recruitment events, induction ceremonies
You can convey the company atmosphere and shared goals to students and new hires through memorable visuals.
What to leave to AI and what to create by hand
Using AI doesn't mean everything can be done automatically. We distinguish between tasks where AI excels and tasks that are better handled by humans.
Tasks well-suited for AI
- Backgrounds and imagery that establish the world-building
- Short clips for scene transitions
- Brainstorming ideas to determine the visual direction
Tasks better handled by humans
- The structure and core message of what is being communicated
- Displaying company names, logos, and text (AI can sometimes distort text or fine details, so we insert these accurately during editing)
- Footage of actual employees, products, and the venue (we combine these with live-action shots)
- Music, sound effects, and syncing audio with visuals
- Final rendering tailored to the venue's screen specifications
Combining AI-generated footage with live-action shots and accurate logos and text to create a single, polished video. We believe this 'integration and finishing' process is where our video production experience truly shines.
Things to confirm before you start
The shape and size of the venue screen
Depending on the venue, you may encounter wide LED displays or multiple screens. Confirming the aspect ratio and resolution with the venue before you start production will help prevent the need for rework later.
Terms of Service for Generative AI Tools
Whether generative AI tools can be used commercially and how the created videos are handled varies by tool and plan. If you are showing a video at an event where people from outside the company will be watching, checking the terms of service of the tools you use is essential.
Rights and What Not to Imitate
Avoid creating videos that resemble real people, other companies' characters or logos, or famous works. The Agency for Cultural Affairs' "AI and Copyright" page is a useful reference for understanding the relationship between generative AI and copyright.
AI and Copyright (Agency for Cultural Affairs)
If you use AI to process and feature the faces or voices of employees, proceed only after obtaining their consent.
Whether to Disclose 'AI-Generated' Content
It is also reassuring to decide internally whether to inform participants that the video was created using AI. Depending on the nature of the event, adding a brief note in the end credits or elsewhere can help prevent misunderstandings.
How to Create an Opening Movie | AI Video Generation
From here on, I will introduce the steps for creating an opening movie in order. When we produce them, we generally follow this flow.
Step 1: Summarize the Purpose and the 'Desired Feeling After Watching' in One Sentence
The first thing to decide is not the look of the video, but the purpose of the event. Along with that, try writing down in one sentence how you want the participants to feel after watching it.
For example, for a kickoff, it might be 'Everyone accepts the new policy as their own.' For an anniversary ceremony, it might be 'Taking pride in the journey so far and looking forward to the next ten years.' This one sentence will become the standard for all subsequent decisions.
Step 2: Determine the Composition
Next, decide 'what to show and in what order' within the video. Opening movies are often structured in a flow like the following, for example.
- Hook (the first few seconds to shift the atmosphere of the venue)
- Theme (conveying what you want to communicate at today's event through visuals and short words)
- Climax (the point where the music and visuals are at their peak)
- Title and Logo (displaying the event name and connecting to the MC or the first speaker)
It is more memorable if the length is not too long. Rather than packing in everything you want to say, focusing on a single message makes it more likely to leave an impression after it ends.
Step 3: Create and Compare Video Concepts Using AI
Once the composition is decided, create video concepts for each scene using AI. By lining up several concepts with different directions and comparing them internally, you can prevent the "this isn't what I had in mind" scenario.
At this stage, the criteria for choosing a concept should be the single sentence from Step 1. Choose based on "whether the viewer will feel that emotion" rather than "whether it looks pretty."
Step 4: Finalize and Verify at the Venue
Combine the chosen AI-generated footage with live-action footage, company names, logos, text, and music to finalize the video. Since text and logos can sometimes become distorted when created by AI, it is standard practice to add them accurately during editing.
Finally, before the actual event, play the video on the venue's screen to check it. There are many things you can only understand at the venue, such as screen brightness, volume, the timing of the lights dimming, and the pause between the end of the video and the moderator starting to speak.
What determines the final result is "composition," not AI
After reading this far, you may have realized that AI is most useful in Step 3.
Thanks to generative AI, it has become much easier than before to prepare impressive video assets. Even so, if you just line up the assets, the opening tends to end up being "pretty, but I can't remember what the video was about."
What determines whether the viewer's emotions are moved after watching is the composition, specifically the following parts:
- What to show in the first few seconds to change the atmosphere of the venue
- The order and wording used to convey the message
- Where to align the climax of the music with the transitions in the video
- How to transition from the end of the video to the moderator or speaker
Composition is not something that is completed by the video alone. It must be considered in conjunction with the entire flow of the event, the lighting, and even the moderator's first words. While AI speeds up asset creation, we believe that the experience of someone who understands both events and video is indispensable when it comes to thinking about this composition.
