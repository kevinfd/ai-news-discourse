# US Voters Reject AI Data Centers, Elon Musk Finds Solace in Orbital Alternative

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-04

US voters overwhelmingly rejected new AI data center construction during Tuesday's midterm elections, dealing a blow to the tech industry. But SpaceX CEO Elon Musk seems to be half-joking that the results justify his push to put data centers in orbit.
In 10 US states, including Illinois, Kansas, and Ohio, 41 ballot measures related to blocking, restricting, or imposing 180-day moratoriums on new AI data centers were up for vote on Tuesday, according to Ballotpedia. And it currently looks like nearly all are on track to pass.
Although votes are still being counted, “every reported ban, moratorium and voter approval requirement passed, and every reported question that would have helped a project failed,” according to The Gridsight, a service that helps AI data center builders plan in local communities.
Meanwhile, Rebud Consulting reports that one ballot measure in Harwood, North Dakota, that's more friendly to data centers appears on track to pass. But it's not an automatic green light; it requires an “independent review, paid for by the developer, and to hold a public hearing and adopt written findings before approving large industrial projects such as data centers.”
The results reflect ongoing opposition to local AI data center construction, which has faced complaints about pollution, water consumption, excessive noise, rising electricity prices, along with concerns about the technology's disruptive impact on society. A poll in August found that 75% of Americans don't want a data center built near them, with 64% “strongly opposed”
The other big news is that Democrats won big in Texas, securing a US Senate seat, and winning the state’s governor and attorney general races. That’s sparked speculation that SpaceX and Tesla’s operations in Texas will face more scrutiny, regulations, and investigations, as Musk has long antagonized Democrats. In this election cycle, the trillionaire reportedly contributed $474 million in campaign donations to Republican candidates and groups.
Last night, AI industry analyst Dylan Patel highlighted Texas, tweeting: "I am now bullish [on] space datacenters."
Musk then tweeted a similar take: “Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now.” SpaceX is proposing to operate up to 1 million data-center satellites through its upcoming Starmind constellation. However, Musk will need to overcome numerous technical challenges, including cooling the data center in the vacuum of space and making the satellites economically competitive with ground-based data centers. Other groups, such as astronomers and environmentalists, oppose Starmind and orbital data center projects over fears about exorbitant rocket launch pollution and how the satellites could alter the night sky.
