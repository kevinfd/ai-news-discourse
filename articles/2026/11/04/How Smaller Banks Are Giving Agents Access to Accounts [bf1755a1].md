# How Smaller Banks Are Giving Agents Access to Accounts

The Information · Yueqi Yang · 2026-11-04

Greetings! When it comes to letting your AI agent—such as Muse or Instinct—manage your personal finances or go shopping, your choice of bank will make a big difference. Some of the biggest banks in the U.S., like JPMorgan, are restricting AI agents’ access to consumers’ personal bank accounts, saying letting them in is too risky.  But some smaller banks are going in the opposite direction, giving AI assistants more access to consumer banking which they see as a potential area of growth. That includes Lead Bank, a Kansas City, Missouri-based bank that works with fintechs like Stripe, a major payment provider for AI agent apps. Increase Bank, owned by fintech entrepreneur and early Stripe employee Darragh Buckley, is planning to offer banking for agents too, along with cards that agents can use.  These banks are conscious of the risks. Lead Bank allows its fintech partners to issue bank account numbers for customers, reserved for AI agents. That in turn enables the agents to perform banking tasks like moving money through ACH and wire transfers while allowing the customers to limit what the agents can do in their accounts.  The bank also says it has controls including transaction and spending limits and can immediately lock an agent out from the account if needed.  “This ensures agents can only move money within explicitly defined parameters, rather than having unrestricted access to customer funds,” a spokesperson for Lead Bank said.  In the case of Increase Bank, it plans to offer isolated bank accounts for agents that will be separate from the rest of the human customer's money, and the customer will be able to decide which agents can access the account. The agent would be able to access its account through an API, with Increase Bank confirming that the agent has permission.  “We don’t need the entire industry to coordinate banking for agents. It's sufficient to have just a small cohort of technology-forward banks” to enable agents to get bank accounts, said Buckley, who already set up a personal bank account at Increase Bank in his name for his agent.  Then there’s Natural, a payment startup backed by venture firm Forerunner that has raised over $40 million in total. Natural is bringing banking products such as payments and credit lines to AI agents. Natural isn’t a bank but it’s offering a way for digital wallets used by agents to hold money in an FDIC-insured account. Natural uses Column Bank to hold the money, and each wallet is a sub-ledger into customers’ individual accounts.  Natural in turn offers its digital wallet to AI companies to use in agents, enabling the agents to send and request money. For example, Natural works with Conduit, a startup that offers AI agents used by short-term rentals and hotels to help manage their businesses. With the digital wallets, Conduit’s agents can accept payments, pay vendors and contractors, and store money.  Banks are also helping agents make payments by issuing one-time use virtual cards, which helps keep the agents from accidentally splurging. Cross River Bank, which works closely with payments giant Stripe, said agent card payments powered by the bank have reached more than $40 million across over 200,000 transactions over the past three months, with airlines being the top consumer spend category.  Still, letting AI agents into consumer banking raises questions about liability and cybersecurity—if an agent makes a payment a customer didn’t intend, for example, it’s unclear who is on the hook for reimbursing the customer. Wells Fargo, for its part, has warned it could restrict or block some AI tools and recently updated its terms to say that if a customer uses an AI agent to access their bank accounts, the customer is responsible for the agents’ actions.   But Natural co-founder and CEO Kahlil Lalji argues that banks trying to block agents face an uphill battle. Agents with access to a user’s personal email can still see things like verification codes to log in, making them hard to keep out.
