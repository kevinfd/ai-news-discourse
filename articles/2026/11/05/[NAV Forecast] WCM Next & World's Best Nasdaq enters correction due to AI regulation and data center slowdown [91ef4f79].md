[NAV Forecast] WCM Next & World's Best: Nasdaq enters correction due to AI regulation and data center slowdown concerns after midterm elections; both funds expected to drop significantly (11/6 Analysis)
Good morning, this is TOMMY_JUNE.
Today's US market saw a sharp decline as concerns over stricter AI regulations and a slowdown in AI infrastructure development spread rapidly following the midterm elections, where the Democratic Party secured a majority in Congress and won in states where opposition to data center construction is strong. Semiconductor and data center-related stocks were heavily sold off, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) plunging over 12%, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite recording its largest drop in 19 months at -5.52%, confirming its entry into a correction phase. The S&P 500 also fell sharply by -4.41% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average by -3.44%, with all three major indices plummeting. In a risk-off environment where the Nikkei 225 also fell by -1.79% in the Japanese market, compounded by the strengthening yen (USD/JPY 157.66, -0.49%), extreme downward pressure is being applied to the NAV forecasts for both funds.
1. NAV Forecast Summary (Announced 11/6)
These are estimated values based on confirmed returns in overseas markets. While currency factors are uniformly negative (-0.42%), the decline in stock prices of constituent holdings, driven by the sharp drop in the AI infrastructure and semiconductor sectors in the US market, is having a significant impact.
2. Contribution Analysis (Stock Price Factors)
On a local stock price basis, the market is experiencing a severe, near-total decline, centered on AI infrastructure and semiconductor-related stocks.
■ WCM Next Generation
Major stocks in AI infrastructure, power, and semiconductors, such as Seagate Technology (-16.25%) in data center storage, Comfort Systems USA (-11.58%) in HVAC and facility infrastructure, as well as Samsung Electronics (-7.57%) and top constituent Siemens Energy (-4.70%), all plummeted. All top 10 holdings made negative contributions, leading to a significant subtotal contribution drop of -2.17%.
■ World's Best
The portfolio was dragged down by significant declines in Texas Instruments (-10.70%), which was hit directly by the semiconductor stock crash, as well as large-cap tech stocks like Microsoft (-3.58%) and Visa A (-3.18%). Although AIA Group (+2.47%), which rose in the Hong Kong market, and the highly defensive Coca-Cola Europacific (+1.25%) secured gains, they were unable to offset the overall downward pressure (-2.17%).
3. Deep Dive: Factors Behind Notable Stock Movements
Here is an explanation of the recent background for stocks that showed significant movement in last night's market.
- Seagate Technology (STX) Return: -16.25% 
  - [Plummeted due to concerns over headwinds for data center construction] Following the US midterm election results, concerns emerged regarding a slowdown in infrastructure development due to Democratic victories in states where opposition to data center construction is strong and the potential for stricter AI regulations. The stock, which had been bought heavily year-to-date on expectations of growing demand for high-capacity HDDs for AI data centers, was hit by a wave of profit-taking and caution, recording its largest drop since April 2025 and becoming the largest negative contributor (-0.49%) within WCM Next.
- Comfort Systems USA (FIX) Return: -11.58% 
  - [Hit by concerns over a slowdown in AI data center capital expenditure] As a representative stock in AI infrastructure with a large backlog of orders for HVAC and electrical installation for data centers, it had been a leader in the sector. However, it plummeted along with other data center and power equipment-related stocks (such as GE Vernova) due to the assumption that the pace of new data center construction would hit the brakes. The drop of over 11% significantly pushed down the fund's NAV (contribution rate -0.46%).
- Texas Instruments (TXN) Return: -10.70% 
  - [Caught in the broad semiconductor sector crash] The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) plunged over 12%, and as panic selling spread across the entire semiconductor sector due to Qualcomm's lower-than-expected revenue forecast and caution regarding AI regulations, the company also saw a sharp decline of 10.70%. It recorded the largest negative contribution (-0.35%) among the top 10 holdings of World's Best.
- Siemens Energy (ENR) Change: -4.70% / Samsung Electronics Change: -7.57% 
  - [US AI and Power Infrastructure Concerns Spread to European and Asian Markets] Concerns over tighter US AI regulations and a slowdown in power demand for data centers have spread globally. In the European market, Siemens Energy, a leader in power infrastructure that had been performing well, saw its losses widen toward the end of the day to -4.70%. In the Korean market, Samsung Electronics also plummeted by -7.57% due to caution regarding US AI regulations, leading to a broad decline among WCM Next's top holdings.
4. Today's Summary: Investment Perspective
Today was a day where new political and policy risks—specifically "tighter AI regulation" and "concerns over a slowdown in the pace of data center construction" following the US midterm elections—erupted all at once, causing a total collapse in the AI infrastructure, semiconductor, and power sectors that had been driving the market.
WCM Next was hit directly as its core portfolio holdings in AI infrastructure, data centers, and semiconductors took a beating, with its NAV expected to drop significantly by -317 yen (forecast: 10,087 yen) from the previous day. World's Best also faced a tough result, with its NAV expected to drop by -229 yen (forecast: 8,647 yen) from the previous day due to adjustments in semiconductors, high-tech, and financial stocks, despite some support from Asian stocks and certain consumer staples.
With the Nasdaq Composite Index falling more than 10% from its October 6th peak, confirming its entry into a correction phase, market sentiment has cooled significantly in the short term. However, AI development and infrastructure construction themselves are not coming to a complete halt, and this can be seen largely as a process of position adjustment as excessive expectations are stripped away. Since high volatility is expected to continue until the specific regulatory trends at the congressional and state levels become clear and institutional selling runs its course, we must calmly monitor the movements as the market seeks a floor.
