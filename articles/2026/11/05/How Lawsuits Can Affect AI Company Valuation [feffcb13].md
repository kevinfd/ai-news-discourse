# How Lawsuits Can Affect AI Company Valuation

Forbes · Tomas Milar · 2026-11-05

Tomas Milar is the Founder and CEO of Eqvista, an equity management platform.
In the first half of 2026, nearly three-quarters of the alleged investor losses originated from AI-related lawsuits. That’s a significant amount of damages claimed against AI companies, despite the fact that this represents only 13% of all securities class action filings in the period.
AI is disruptive, and other industries are seeking significant financial compensation, which could impact the scope of AI. But how much can these lawsuits affect AI companies and their valuations? That’s the question I’ve been sitting with over the last couple of weeks. Here’s what I have found.
Lawsuits rarely affect AI company valuations through damages alone. The bigger question is how they change the assumptions behind future cash flows.
Legal fees, settlements and licensing raise costs, while injunctions or data restrictions can constrain revenue. Uncertainty may slow customer adoption and weaken growth. This increases the perceived risk. As a result, investors may then assign a lower revenue multiple, reducing the company’s valuation even before any damages are paid.
How Do Markets React To Lawsuits Involving AI Companies?
We cannot estimate the private market’s reactions to AI company lawsuits; there simply isn’t enough data. However, we can make an educated generalization by looking at public market indicators. Specifically, we can look at the Disclosure Dollar Loss (DDL) Index and Maximum Dollar Loss (MDL) Index to gauge how lawsuits are impacting AI valuations.
In securities lawsuits, a key observation you should make is the impact of unfavorable facts coming to light because of corrective disclosures. DDL measures the drop in market caps of defendants because of such disclosures. MDL, on the other hand, measures the drop from peak to end-of-period market cap for defendants.
MDL rose from $1 trillion to $1.86 trillion in H1 2026. DDL stood at $529 billion in H1 2026, the highest since 2016 and a 77% increase from H2 2025. A Harvard study indicates that AI filings accounted for 70% of the MDL and 73% of the DDL. So, the correlation between market cap losses and AI-related lawsuits is definitely significant.
Despite this, AI startups have been able to raise revenue multiples of 25x to 50x or even more at Series B and beyond in 2026. This creates an apparent contradiction. Litigation risk and investor optimism seem to coexist. But why?
The changes in DDL and MDL reflect how markets are reacting to AI-related lawsuits. But there’s a disparity in initial reactions and long-term expectations. Investors may continue assigning high valuations when they believe future growth will outweigh the expected cost of litigation.
Where AI Litigation Can Put Company Value At Risk
In my opinion, the trends in alleged investor losses in AI-related lawsuits should not be ignored. Just because investors are firmly standing behind AI companies doesn’t mean that the friction between AI and IPs will smooth out on its own. AI-related copyright filings jumped from 16 to 70 between 2023 and 2025.
In a multi-district litigation involving OpenAI and Microsoft in the Southern District of New York, the core contentions were regarding how copyrighted material was used to train AI models and the distribution of altered copyrighted materials.
This case is representative of an important class of legal issues that arise with AI. And there are two action areas that could resolve a chunk of these issues.
First is licensing. To an extent, publishers and stock media libraries already offer licensing deals, providing access to huge content corpuses for AI training. You can also find platforms and licensing partners that help you procure training data from various different rights holders. The question is whether a meaningful licensing market exists. AI startups need to take that issue more seriously.
Second is output originality, which is a little trickier. Models need tighter controls and better training to reduce the risk of regurgitating training data in their outputs.
Why Litigation Risk Belongs In AI Valuation Due Diligence
Given the mounting market cap reductions and alleged investor losses resulting from AI lawsuits, gauging exposure to lawsuits has become extremely important for investors as well as founders. The likelihood of a securities class action against a U.S.-listed company is on pace for 4.4% in 2026, higher than the 3.8% average for 2012–25.
At every stage of development, AI founders must review the legality of their training methods and their outputs. Before committing capital, investors should perform a similar check as part of their due diligence.
What If My AI Company Is Already Being Sued?
If your AI company is in the middle of a lawsuit, you need to find a credible explanation for the following questions:
• How much of your commercial value is exposed to legal issues?
• Could the lawsuit affect future revenue, margins or growth?
• What additional legal, licensing or compliance costs could arise?
• Could the dispute restrict access to data needed to train models or impact your IP rights?
• How could the outcome of the lawsuit(s) affect the assumptions used in the company’s valuation?
These questions may heavily influence the economic damages you would be liable for, if at all. Instead of relying on internal estimates, ideally, you should rely on expert witnesses with experience in AI valuation.
The Real Question Is How Much Value Is Exposed
The current trends in AI-related lawsuits call for structural changes in how AI is developed and how investors should pick AI startups. In my opinion, the imperative for AI startups and content rightsholders to work together could not be higher. The trends observed in this article may not completely disintegrate the value created by AI. Instead, a bigger portion of that value will flow to rightsholders.
In this transition period, some legal friction between AI and rightsholders is to be expected. While this friction is sustained, credible expert witnesses will play a crucial role in protecting you from outsized claims.
