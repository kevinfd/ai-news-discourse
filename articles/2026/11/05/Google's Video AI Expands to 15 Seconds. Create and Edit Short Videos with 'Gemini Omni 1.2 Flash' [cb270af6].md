# Google's Video AI Expands to 15 Seconds. Create and Edit Short Videos with 'Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash'

note · 永野翔馬｜分身Aiで会社を回す経営者 · 2026-11-05

Google's Video AI Expands to 15 Seconds. Create and Edit Short Videos with 'Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash'
I want to convey the new cafe menu not just with photos, but with short videos as well. Showing a steaming cup, highlighting the product's features, and finally inviting customers to the shop. When thinking about that flow, a difference of a few seconds feels surprisingly significant.
On November 5, 2026, Google announced the video generation AI 'Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash.' They noted that the maximum length of videos that can be created has increased from 10 seconds to 15 seconds, and that 720p video output is 30% cheaper than the previous model. This update is relevant to work involving prototyping short videos and refining them through conversation.
From 10 seconds to 15 seconds: The flow you can convey expands
You could already generate and edit videos with the previous Omni 1.1 Flash. This time, the length you can create at once has increased. 15 seconds is 1.5 times longer than 10 seconds. However, this does not mean the video quality itself has become 1.5 times better.
According to official specifications, you can create videos from text or reference images, and it also supports output with audio. Standard generation and editing range from 3 to 15 seconds, and you can choose between portrait and landscape orientations. However, the method of specifying the first and last images to connect the middle is limited to a maximum of 10 seconds. Not all creation methods result in 15 seconds.
If it were me, I would think of a structure that grabs attention in the first few seconds, then shows the product's features, and finally adds a closing remark. I believe the value of the increased duration lies in making it easier to create a sequence of what to show, rather than just dragging it out.
You can continue to edit through conversation even after creating it
Google explains that they have improved the ability to maintain the shape of products and brand colors from reference images, as well as the ability to edit videos through ongoing conversation. It is a way of using it where you adjust the same video by saying things like 'make the lighting warmer' or 'change the background.' This is based on official explanations and is not the result of my own comparative testing.
They also provide guidance on a method of testing ideas at a lower resolution and then upscaling only the adopted videos to 1080p or 4K. Rather than aiming for a finished, high-quality piece from the start, you can first compare whether the structure and movement fit. In a professional setting, this order seems useful.
Consider usage and pricing separately
For developers, it is provided via the Gemini API and Google Cloud, which are the gateways for integration into services. It is noted that it will be provided sequentially for Google AI Ultra in the Gemini app. You should check if it is available in the plan or interface you are using before trying it out.
Calculating based on the announced unit price of $0.07 USD per second at 720p, a 15-second video output costs approximately $1.05 USD. This is a rough estimate for the output portion only. The price list also has different unit prices for input and text output, and you need to consider costs for upscaling and re-creation. This is separate from the monthly fee for the Gemini app.
Google explains that there is still room for improvement regarding fast sports movements, instructions to suddenly change content midway, and complex object movements. If you are introducing actual products, humans need to verify that the shape, quantity, and movement do not differ from reality.
If it were me, I would start with a 15-second prototype
For a cafe announcement, the first thing I would decide is the goal of 'conveying the delicious moment of the new menu.' I would provide the product photos I am allowed to use and the order I want to show them in, and leave the short prototype to the AI. This is a usage plan based on the current specifications and is not an implemented case study.
With the created video, I would check if it looks different from the actual product and if the purpose of the announcement is conveyed in the first few seconds. I would finish text and pricing in an editing process where they are easy to verify. If you keep the adopted structure and the points of correction, they can also serve as material for thinking about the next announcement.
The first step is not to complete an advertisement right away, but to create a 'prototype that narrows down what you want to convey to one thing' using a single photo that is ready to be published. If the entry point for video production becomes easier, even small shops could increase their opportunities to compare expressions.
