# The midterms weren’t the rebuke of AI money many expected

Fast Company · Tekendra Parmar · 2026-11-05

The midterms are shaping up to be a record-breaking election for Democrats, who have flipped states including South Carolina and Texas. But the election has also shattered records for private political spending. By some estimates, super PAC spending exceeded $2 billion this cycle, more than 50% above previous election cycles.
One of the dominant narratives heading into the midterms was that Big Tech—and the artificial intelligence sector in particular—would spend hundreds of millions of dollars electing AI-friendly lawmakers, much as the cryptocurrency industry had done in previous elections.
Indeed, Leading the Future, the largest pro-AI super PAC network—backed by VC firm Andreessen Horowitz, OpenAI cofounder and president Greg Brockman, and Palantir and 8VC cofounder Joe Lonsdale—invited comparisons to the crypto super PAC network Fairshake, drawing on some of the same donors and political operatives. (Both networks operate through partisan affiliates: Leading the Future has Think Big for Democrats and American Mission for Republicans, while Fairshake has Protect Progress and Defend American Jobs, respectively.)
But as public sentiment toward AI soured and Democratic Party insiders warned candidates against accepting industry money, the narrative began to shift. A Fast Company analysis of election data suggests the midterms were hardly the rebuke of AI money that some had anticipated. More than 86% of the candidates backed by Leading the Future have won or are currently leading their House of Representatives or Senate races. That’s roughly on par with Fairshake, which supported 87 candidates this cycle, 77 of whom have won or are currently ahead.
The two industries’ political efforts also appear to be converging. Leading the Future and Fairshake backed 21 of the same candidates, including Republican Kevin Hern, who won his Senate race in Oklahoma. Hern also received support from the Anthropic-funded Public First, which has positioned itself as a counterweight to the industry’s other political spending operations.
Big Crypto outspent the AI industry but achieved similar results
While Big Crypto outspent the largest AI industry super PACs this election cycle and backed more candidates, the groups saw broadly similar results. Fairshake, the cryptocurrency super PAC funded by Coinbase, Ripple, and Andreessen Horowitz, spent about $154 million this cycle, compared with $34.8 million by Leading the Future and $24.4 million by Public First. Yet roughly comparable shares of the candidates each group supported won their elections.
Of the 12 Senate candidates backed by Leading the Future, nine won their races, compared with five of Fairshake’s eight candidates. Both Senate candidates supported by Public First—Hern in Oklahoma and Republican Pete Ricketts in Nebraska—also won. In the House, Leading the Future’s candidates won 31 of 38 races, compared with six of 11 for Public First and 63 of 79 for Fairshake.
