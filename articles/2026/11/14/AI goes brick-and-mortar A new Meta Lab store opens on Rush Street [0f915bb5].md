# AI goes brick-and-mortar: A new Meta Lab store opens on Rush Street

Chicago Tribune · Jane Miller · 2026-11-14

Just in time for the holidays, Meta Lab’s first Midwest retail location opened Friday on Rush Street in Chicago. Featuring over a hundred different styles of AI glasses and two different VR headsets, the AI giant is going all in on experiential shopping to connect with consumers.
“The technology is really, really cool for people to try,” said Emily Lewis, director of retail for Meta. “While there’s incredible virtual tools, and people really love our online experience, the ability to put a pair of glasses on your face and feel how you feel in it … is really important.”
The Meta Lab storefront, with its matching bright blue walls, windows, and doors, stands in stark contrast next to its brick-walled Lululemon neighbor. Within the store, customers can find rows of distinct styles of glasses on display, next to large wooden drawers filled with extra stock, and floor-length mirrors for trying on.
The Chicago location, the company’s 13th store, comes alongside a plethora of Meta Lab openings across the country. In the past six weeks, stores opened in Houston; Atlanta; New York City; Honolulu; and Scottsdale, Arizona.
“We are incredibly excited about Chicago,” Lewis said. “It welcomes 55 million visitors a year to the city.”
Rush Street, with its proximity to the Magnificent Mile, a spot that has recently seen a resurgence in retail openings following the pandemic, is “an iconic area to be in,” Lewis said.
“There’s not many better retail destinations in the U.S.,” Lewis said.
The dozens of AI glasses featured in the store have gone viral for their ability to capture photo and video hands-free, allowing people to answer calls with the tap of a finger and, in some versions, see the equivalent of a phone screen projected inside the lenses.
The Meta Ray-Bans, at $799, use a muscle-reading technology wristband, allowing users to select the apps visible within the glasses’ lenses using specific hand motions, without touching a screen.
All of the styles of AI glasses offered allow users to give the device vocal commands, like asking for directions, or to play music.
Partnerships with brands like Ray Ban and Oakley, as well as collaborations with the likes of Kylie Jenner, have given the glasses a chic new look, so there’s “something for everyone,” Lewis said. From tinted sunglasses to clear everyday pairs, users can also order prescription lenses to go with the technology.
“We’re really pushing the boundaries on styles now,” said Michael Wells, manager of training at Meta Labs.
The most popular pair of glasses is the Meta Glasses Adventurers, available for $299. A close second is the Meta Starfire Kylie Glasses, at $399.
The store’s grand opening comes ahead of the holiday season, with Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals to come, Lewis said.
It also comes against the backdrop of a class-action lawsuit filed in September against Meta, in which plaintiffs alleged that in order to develop the unreleased NameTag facial recognition system intended for its AI glasses, Meta violated the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act by harvesting and exploiting the biometric data of its users.
Meta is seeking exemption from the lawsuit, and when asked how the company plans to gain local consumer trust amidst the situation, a spokesperson said, “Meta and our products of course adhere to all applicable Federal, state and local laws.”
With regard to concerns that the glasses can film other people without their knowledge, Wells explained that a blinking indicator light on the side of the AI glasses serves as a signal to other people that the camera is on.
“Anytime that there is any tampering, any type of blocking (of the indicator light), this automatically cuts the camera off,” said Wells. “Privacy is built in as a priority.”
Some pairs of the AI glasses don’t have cameras at all, and only have audio capabilities for phone calls and music, Wells said.
By opening the storefront, Meta hopes people can test out the various technologies, from the AI glasses to the Meta Quest VR sets, helping the company understand what users best connect with, and allowing users to find the best fit for themselves, Lewis said.
An engraving machine at the store also allows people to customize their glasses’ cases when they purchase something.
“All of our stores very intentionally have seating that’s comfortable. We want people to really feel like they can dwell and spend time and get to know us and the product,” Lewis said. “I think it is so new for so many people that we really want to itch that curiosity.”
