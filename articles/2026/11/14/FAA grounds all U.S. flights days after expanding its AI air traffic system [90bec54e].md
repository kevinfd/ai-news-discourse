# FAA grounds all U.S. flights days after expanding its AI air traffic system

Scientific American · Ramin Skibba · 2026-11-14

On a typical Friday November evening, millions of passengers would be hopping on flights for pre-Thanksgiving weekend trips and journeys home after work. But today on November 13, domestic and international flights throughout the U.S. completely ground to a halt at about 4 P.M. EST.
The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) cited a “temporary telecommunications outage,” in a post on X, and all flights have been grounded “out of an abundance of caution.”
The sweeping aviation disruption comes three days after the FAA expanded its Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories system, known as SMART. The FAA previously tested the system in the National Capital Region, the airspace around Washington, D.C., in September. The artificial intelligence-driven system is intended to synthesize data on weather patterns, flight paths, traffic flow and controller staffing metrics in order to “reduce delays and cancellations,” according to the FAA’s description. As of November 10, it was deployed across all 22 air traffic regions in the country.
On supporting science journalism
Such nationwide groundings are exceedingly rare. The last time the FAA stopped flights was for 90 minutes on January 11, 2023, to address an outage in its Notice to Air Missions (NOTAM) system, which informs pilots and operators about potential safety hazards. Before that, flights were halted immediately following the September 11, 2001 terror attacks.
The FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure is managed by L3Harris, a Silicon Valley-based aerospace and defense company and a contractor for federal government agencies. L3Harris did not immediately respond to Scientific American’s requests for comment. The FAA issued a brief statement, saying that “FAA technicians are investigating the issue. We will provide updates.”
Ruth Stilwell, a former air traffic controller and aerospace policy expert, speculates that the outage may be due to a software problem, such as a software update gone awry in the Voice Switching and Control System, which manages communications between pilots and air traffic controllers. It is not Microsoft software, Stilwell emphasizes, referring to the widespread Microsoft product outage that occurred on Thursday into Friday. CBS News reported that a software update to the private communications system may have triggered a system-wide reboot, causing it to crash.
“It would be unusual for them to do a software update during the day, but the FAA is very short-staffed,” Stilwell says. Department of Government Efficiency-related cuts laid off hundreds of FAA workers last year.
"This is yet another example of how brittle our air transportation system is in our country," says Missy Cummings, at George Mason University and a former fighter pilot whose research focuses on responsible AI in transportation. "This has been a problem over several administration, and the Trump administration made the situation much worse with all their cuts."
Even if the problem, software-related or otherwise, was small in scale, it’s standard procedure to ground planes while attempting to communicate through other channels with pilots who have already taken off, Stilwell says.
“The ground stop was the right thing to do,” Stilwell says. “Planes in the air are the top priority. The safety of those flights is way more important than anybody’s delay. The planes on the ground are safe.”
