# Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Capital One customers report outages in latest tech hiccup

New York Post · Over-Mix-1957 · 2026-11-16

Big bank customers report outages; JPMorgan Chase blames software bug with potential ripple effects
See more of our coverage in your search results.
Customers at banks including Chase, Bank of America, Capital One and Wells Fargo said they experienced outages coast to coast on Monday — days after technical snafus wreaked havoc for flight passengers and Microsoft customers.
There were nearly 5,000 reports of outages at Chase and about 1,400 between BofA and Wells Fargo, according to Downdetector.com.
JPMorgan Chase said a software bug in systems it runs in-house slowed service for some customers – and may have affected other banks.
“It is taking longer than usual to service some customers,” JPMorgan said in a statement. It added that customers “still have access to their accounts and funds.”

The nation’s largest bank said other lenders may have been affected when JPMorgan was a party to a transaction with them, but that “this is not a systemic issue.”
In other words, reported outages at other banks appeared to be due to customers trying to do business with Chase accounts.
JPMorgan said it was working to resolve the problem by the end of the day.

Customers reported issues at Capital One, PNC, US Bank and the Zelle money transfer app.
Zelle indicated any problems customers faced sending payments were due to problems at partner banks.
“Zelle is not down. Zelle is operating as it should. Consumers use Zelle through participating banks and credit unions, which manage their accounts and hold their funds,” a Zelle spokesperson told The Post.
Advertisement
Capital One made a similar statement, suggesting it was indirectly affected by banking woes at Chase.
“Capital One has not been impacted and continues to operate normally at this time,” a Capital One spokesperson said.
Some customers said online they couldn’t use their banking apps.
“My Chase App has a warning that I’ve never seen before: Some services aren’t available. We’re working on them. Please try back later,” one user wrote on Reddit.
Advertisement
Earlier in the day, Chase said it was sorry to an X user complaining of banking woes.
“I do apologize, it appears that we are currently working to resolve an issue with online banking. Please try again at a later time. We’re sorry for any inconvenience, and appreciate your patience,” a Chase support account wrote on X.
The technical difficulties were the latest in a series of incidents to cause major inconvenience for Americans.
Advertisement
Air travel slammed to a halt on Friday, with authorities blaming a botched contractor job or a faulty router. After thousands of flights were cancelled through the weekend, some stranded travelers were still trying to make their way home on Monday.
Also last week, Microsoft had a widespread outage that prevented online communications for governments and companies for over 24 hours.
The software giant said Saturday services was fully operational for Teams, Outlook and other products.
Advertisement
Independent cybersecurity expert Katie Moussouris told the Post the extent of the bank problems suggested they were interconnected.
“It could be a really wild coincidence, but it seems like they share a component on the backend,” she said.
A former top Treasury Department official urged calm.
Advertisement
Ex-Assistant Treasury Secretary Graham Steele said things can get out of hand if people start to lose trust in the banking system.
“What really becomes a problem is when people don’t trust the banking system because they think it has become corrupted or compromised in some way,” he told The Post, adding that the Federal Reserve does worst-case-scenario drills to prepare for extended problems.
