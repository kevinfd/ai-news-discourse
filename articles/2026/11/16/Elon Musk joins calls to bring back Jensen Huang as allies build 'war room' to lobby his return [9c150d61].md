# Elon Musk joins calls to bring back Jensen Huang as allies build 'war room' to lobby his return

Euronews · Pascale Davies · 2026-11-16

Silicon Valley is supporting the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's CEO, while Elon Musk joins a petition demanding that the company "Bring Jensen Back".
Nvidia's co-founder and former CEO Jensen Huang, who resigned last week after multiple extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy, is eyeing a comeback, with tech executives calling for his reinstatement.
Huang, a long-admired figure in artificial intelligence who rose to international stardom, discussed with confidants the possibility of returning to his post, according to a report by the Financial Times.
A Huang ally also told the publication they were putting together a “war room” of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for his return.
It comes as tech executives, including Elon Musk, have called for Huang's reinstatement in an online petition, which has gained more than 160,000 signatures.
The petition also counts Epic Games boss Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan among its signatories. More than 7,500 signatories were also identified as Nvidia employees.
But in the United States, the US treasury secretary, Scott Bessent, said on Sunday that Nvidia should look “outside” the company for its next leader. “They need a dramatic shake-up,” Bessent said on Fox News.
The treasury secretary also urged Nvidia to explain whether it knowingly allowed its most advanced semiconductors to reach China.
The US bans the sale of the most advanced chips to China as part of its strategy to stay ahead of Beijing in the race to lead AI. This measure came under former President Joe Biden, who signed the CHIPS Act into law.
This was bad for Nvidia, as China was one of its largest markets for GPU chips. Then, under the Trump administration, Huang lobbied the US to relax its export controls on chips to China, with several meetings with President Donald Trump.
Trump eventually allowed the export of its H200 chips, which are more powerful, to China in exchange for 25% of the revenue.
