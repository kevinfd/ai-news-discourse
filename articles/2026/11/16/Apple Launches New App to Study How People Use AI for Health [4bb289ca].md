# Apple Launches New App to Study How People Use AI for Health

CNET · Anna Gragert · 2026-11-16

Since 2019, when Apple first launched its Research app, the company has partnered with medical universities and institutions to run a range of health studies on hearing, heart health and movement, women’s health and, most recently, the Apple Health Study, which covers a wide variety of health areas.
On Sunday, Apple launched its latest study, and it comes with its own app: the Apple Health AI Study.
During a press briefing, Apple said the study aims to understand how people interact with conversational health AI tools. To do so, it will examine comprehension, response quality, the drivers of user trust and everyday health actions.
More from CNET
The goal? To inform the responsible design of health AI experiences for both Apple and the industry and to explore how health AI might help people prepare for doctor’s visits. Key questions the study aims to address include: Do users understand the information provided by health AI? Do they trust it? Is it helpful?
This is particularly relevant considering that one in three US adults use AI chatbots for health information.
Study data will not be used to train AI models, but it may inform Apple’s feature development.
Apple’s study partner
To facilitate the study, Apple is partnering with OpenEvidence, an AI platform designed specifically for doctors. It generates answers using medical information from these partners: The New England Journal of Medicine, Journal of the American Medical Association, National Comprehensive Cancer Network, Nature and Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.
In the dedicated app, study participants can ask the AI their text-based health questions and then see answers with linked, peer-reviewed clinical citations. They can also choose to share their HealthKit data, including sleep, lab results, activity and resting heart rate, for more personalized answers.
HealthKit is Apple’s hub for health and fitness data collected on the iPhone and Apple Watch.
Who’s eligible?
The Apple Health AI Usage Study is open to 200,000 participants. You must be in the US and age 21 or older to participate. An Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone (iPhone 15 Pro or later) running iOS 26 or later is also required.
Why an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone? According to Apple, the study app requires the processing capabilities of those iPhones, and it uses on-device Apple Foundation Models that are part of Apple Intelligence. These models provide access to any large language model. For example, as part of the study, these models are used to classify question categories.
What’s required?
Each participant is asked to participate for up to 12 months. During this time, they can ask questions about general health, specific symptoms, recovery from a procedure and their own uploaded health data, as examples.
In addition to a baseline survey, participants will receive monthly surveys and additional surveys specific to preparing for doctor’s visits.
Participants will also be prompted to check in with the app. For instance, if you have a paired Apple Watch, you will receive notifications to check in, along with suggested topics to explore.
If there is a complete lack of participation, a participant may potentially be removed from the study.
Study privacy
After downloading the Apple Health AI Study app, participants must complete a mandatory onboarding process that includes informed consent. They will also be asked to share their demographic information, such as race, age and education level, which will be used to explore how these factors may affect their preferences and communication needs. Then, they can start asking the AI questions.
The iPhone first processes questions using tools designed to remove details that could identify participants. They then go to a secure cloud-based study environment managed by a third-party study support contractor called UST Global, where queries are delinked from individual participant IDs and screened using tools designed to further remove any remaining personal details. Lastly, this info is sent to OpenEvidence, where answers are generated using supporting reference materials.
OpenEvidence will delete both question and response data within 24 hours.
A small group of Apple researchers will have access to coded versions of survey responses, separated from personally identifiable information, summaries of conversations and categories of the most common conversation types.
Within the study app, there are also safety guardrails that detect concerning questions and respond by suggesting that a participant follow up with a provider or other resources if needed.
There is no live monitoring, and study staff won’t contact participants if any questions related to self-harm are asked.
It’s also up to the participant whether they share any HealthKit metrics. They will first have to choose to upload their clinical health records to Apple’s Health app, and then separately choose to share those records from the Health app to the new Apple Health AI Study app.
Availability
The Apple Health AI Study app is now available in the App Store. As of now, there is no set publication date for the study.
