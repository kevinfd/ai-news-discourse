# Indian Chip Startup Bharat Semi Raises $24M to Build AI Sensors That “See and Think”

TechJuice · Abdul Wasay · 2026-11-16

Indian semiconductor startup Bharat Semi raised $24 million in seed funding to develop AI-enabled image sensors. The New Delhi-based company combines image capture, processing, and AI capabilities on a single chip. Despite generating zero revenue so far, the startup already secured design wins with US defense contractor General Atomics and India’s Kaynes Semicon.
The company targets commercial production in 2027. Info Edge Group, South Park Commons, and Indus Bridge Ventures led the all-equity round. Valour Capital, Micromax, Gaingels, and individual investors also participated.
Co-founder Vrinda Kapoor, a South Park Commons fellow with a healthcare technology background, said that the funding will go toward expanding engineering, completing product development, and bringing sensors to market.
Bharat Semi’s core pitch centers on cost reduction. Conventional camera systems use three separate components: an image sensor, an image signal processor, and a system-on-chip. By combining all three functions into one programmable sensor, Bharat Semi claims it can cut system costs by at least 70%. Consequently, devices that need visual intelligence become cheaper to build. “Our aim here is to move from sensors to sense-making solutions,” Kapoor said.
The applications span smart homes, industrial automation, surveillance, and aerospace. For example, an air conditioner with Bharat Semi’s sensor could detect an empty room and adjust automatically. Similarly, a drone could process visual data on the sensor itself without requiring expensive computing hardware elsewhere in the system.
Kapoor co-founded the company in late 2021 with Vinayak Dalmia, who previously worked at McKinsey and in private equity. Their CTO Boyd Fowler held the same title at OmniVision, a major image sensor manufacturer. That hire gives the startup credibility in a field dominated by established players.
First-generation sensors have already reached the prototyping stage. Meanwhile, second-generation sensors with more advanced AI capabilities should reach prototype by mid-2027. Bharat Semi plans to get those newer sensors into customer hands by the end of 2027.
The startup now employs around 75 people across India and the US. Its American operations in Santa Clara cover engineering and business development with 10 to 12 staff. That headcount should reach 20 by mid-2027. Additionally, the company started hiring engineers in Europe.
Bharat Semi also announced plans for a semiconductor fabrication project in India involving a technology partnership with the US Space Force. However, that initiative remains separate from the sensor business. The company plans to stay fabless for its core product line, outsourcing manufacturing while focusing on design and software. The new $24 million will not fund the fabrication project.
“It’s a story of can we build a global technology company out of India,” Kapoor said. For South Asia’s semiconductor ambitions, the answer to that question carries weight far beyond one startup.
