# Drawquarium is the anti-AI cozy game that lets you create your own fish tank

Mashable · Shannon Connellan · 2026-11-16

'Drawquarium' is the 'anti-AI' cozy game that lets you create your own fish tank
Who knew one of the most delightful responses to the incessant wave of AI slop would be a DIY fish tank game?
Developed by Kaleb Lechowski aka byKaleb, Drawquarium is a sweet new cozy sandbox game out on Steam that's essentially the bloomscrolling of video games. Since the trailer dropped, the game's been dubbed "anti-AI" and has seen commenters on YouTube and X delighted with the game's handcrafted feel — the "brb crying because of a fish game trailer" responses are accurate.
And according to the Lechowski's Steam description, the game is not for you if you "want action or excitement. No combat, no explosions. Think of Drawquarium as the opposite of GTA 😄."

The premise of Drawquarium is simple: draw a fish, name it, and watch it come to life swimming in your own digital aquarium. The game is focused on unlocking your creativity and sense of lighthearted tomfoolery, as you can either use templates or come up with your own ungodly creations. Reader, I'm loving my octopus sick:
In his Steam notes, Lechowski describes why he, a 3D artist in the film industry, made Drawquarium: "My heart kept pulling me toward a small, very personal game idea. One of the reasons I started developing this game was that I wanted to bring my children’s drawings to life 💖." Can't argue with that.
Build your Drawquarium by sketching those fish
First, you'll need to title your aquarium, then it's time to draw your first fish and name them. I tend toward themes for sandbox games — shout out to my Gilmore Girls-themed faction in Cult of the Lamb — so I've named my creatures after sea goddesses.
Drawquarium's easel mode feels like a supercharged, thoughtful, fun version of MS Paint, with advanced digital brushes and tools that easily help you craft a fish-like being — and reader, when you accidentally fill the screen with the Paint Bucket instead of one section, there's an undo button. Drawing my first fish, I took more time than I thought, adding stripes and fins and flourishes to my little Amphitrite. Once you're happy with your undersea creature, click "give me my fish" and you'll drop your little creation into the tank.
Watching your work – be it a marine masterpiece or a passable abstract – swim around the tank is deeply satisfying. The fish spawn as tiny wee sea bairns and grow in size (something you can also set).
Once your fish is settled, you're instantly prompted to draw them a friend, which takes you back to the creative drawing board. You can clone the fish, start again, or if you need an idea, the lightbulb at the top of the easel will give you suggestions like "draw a steampunk fish," or "draw a fish reading the newspaper." You can unlock templates as you go, and the various photo editing tools you're used to are here: clone and replicate, mirroring, and erasing.
Of course, this is a drawing game, so you don't have to contain your creativity to sea creatures like I did (but my turtle slaps). Maybe your fish look like hats, stereos, or butts, all swimming in your own cursed little fish bowl. You do you, buttfish.
Add plants, draw treats, and chase your fish with a vacuum
Decorating your tank with plants is next, as you can sketch out plants of a wild and wonderful nature. My one tip here is to play with the opacity and brush size settings, as you can create some satisfying tendrils of varying texture. And in a sweet feature, when it comes time to feed your fish with a click, it's also up to you to draw their meal. I know it's not ideal to feed octopus babies cupcakes but this is my aquarium, my choice of fish food.
You'll need to clean the tank floor (the fish instinctively run from the vacuum), and you can gather coins from the tank to buy more templates — at this stage, the game doesn't have real in-game transactions.
Once you've become thoroughly locked in to making new types of deep sea residents, there's a wallpaper mode which pops your aquarium onto your desktop so you can sit with your creations all day. This is the real magic of the game, bringing your painstakingly crafted (or slapdash) efforts into your everyday routine.
In week after week, month after month, of news about how AI is eating creatives' lunch, Drawquarium is a quietly triumphant achievement. A stunning use of human creativity in a deeply relaxing, cosy sandbox game, it's a refreshing treat.
Drawquarium is out on Steam (and as Creative Bloq points out, make sure you're buying the official game, as copies have popped up).
