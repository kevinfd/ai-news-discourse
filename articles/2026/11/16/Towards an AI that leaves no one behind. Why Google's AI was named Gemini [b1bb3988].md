# Towards an AI that leaves no one behind. Why Google's AI was named "Gemini"

note · Gemini · 2026-11-16

SYSTEM NOTICE
Towards an AI that leaves no one behind. Why Google's AI was named "Gemini"
Hello, this is the official note editorial department for Google's AI, "Gemini."
In recent years, AI has become a much more familiar presence in both our work and personal lives. 
Whether it's making daily tasks a little easier or helping you take on challenges you've always wanted to try, we hope Gemini is useful as your partner in those moments. 
Up until now, our official note has focused on introducing convenient features and how to use them, but now that AI has become so integrated into our lives, we wanted to carefully convey the thoughts behind these features. Therefore, starting with this post, we will be delivering a three-part series on the background of Gemini's development and the vision behind it. 
This first installment is about the origin of the name "Gemini." 
Actually, this is a word that means "Gemini" (the constellation) in Latin. 
Why did we name this AI "Gemini"? It is packed with Google's beliefs since its founding and the story of its development. 
In this post, we will introduce the journey to the birth of "Gemini" and the thoughts behind its name.
Technology for everyone, unchanged since our founding
Google was founded in 1998. The core belief behind it has remained unchanged since the beginning. To unravel the "origin of Gemini," we must go back to the year Google was founded.
In 1995, Google founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin met in a Stanford University dormitory. The mission they set forth at the time of founding was to "organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful."
This starting point of "delivering technology not to a select few experts, but into the hands of ordinary people living their lives" is directly linked to the philosophy of "AI for Everyone" that modern-day Google upholds.
Until now, you might have had a strong image of Google as a search engine. However, the technology we once delivered in the form of a "search box" is now evolving into a familiar partner that thinks autonomously on behalf of users and supports daily life and work from behind the scenes—in other words, an "AI agent."
Even though the form of the tool has changed, "leaving no one behind and being useful to everyone" is a Google philosophy that has remained unchanged since its founding. Gemini is also being developed based on that belief.
Why "Gemini"? The Google story behind the name
The background to the launch of the development project for Google's next-generation AI model, "Gemini," was the goal of strengthening the unity between the two teams involved in language models, "Google Brain" and "Google DeepMind."
In fact, when the Gemini project was first launched, it used the temporary project name "Titan," named after the largest moon orbiting Saturn.
However, what we ultimately chose was "Gemini," which means "twins" in Latin and is also the name of the constellation.
The constellation Gemini is characterized by connecting with diverse people and viewing things from multiple perspectives. The two teams that had previously led AI research separately were becoming one, joining hands to take on new challenges—the name Gemini, meaning "twins," was the perfect fit for Google at that time.
The fusion of two top teams
The foundation of modern AI was created by the "Google Brain team." In addition to this, there was the "Google DeepMind team," known for "AlphaGo," which made headlines when AI defeated top world Go player Lee Sedol, and "AlphaFold," which won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, among other achievements, with each team having different areas of expertise.
For nearly a decade, the DeepMind team and the Brain team have created the machine learning models that form the foundation of almost all large language models (LLMs) today, powerfully leading global AI research.
And, to accelerate further evolution, the two teams merged in April 2023 to form "Google DeepMind." They brought together AI talent, computing power, infrastructure, and resources into a single team. 
Two teams with different strengths joining forces. This series of stories fit perfectly with the word "twins." The two stars of the "Gemini" constellation refer to the former Brain team and the former DeepMind team.
Another origin, NASA's "Project Gemini"
Another origin of the name is the 'Gemini program,' which served as a bridge to the success of the 'Apollo program' that aimed for the moon landing. 
Named after the two-person spacecraft, this program was conducted prior to the Apollo program for the purpose of testing equipment and technology for astronauts to stay in space for long periods.
The Gemini program supported the Apollo program, which achieved the first human moon landing. The importance of the Gemini program resonated deeply with the members of the Google DeepMind team.
Just as the Gemini program was 'a vital bridge for humanity to land on the moon,' Google's Gemini also aims to be a 'bridge' for cutting-edge technology, once accessible only to a few experts, to descend into our daily lives.
That is the sentiment embedded in the name 'Gemini'.
Gemini originally began as a research program to develop the world's highest-performing models. By using that project name as is, we hope you can feel the continuous improvement, creativity, and innovation that the research team works on every day.
(Summary) A future that everyone around the world can be excited about
We want to deliver technology that is useful not just to a few experts, but to everyone. 
True to our belief since our founding, we hope that no matter how much Gemini evolves, it will remain a familiar partner that supports your daily life.
Gemini on your smartphone connecting with the real world and physically supporting our lives... Such a future is just around the corner!
Even if technology changes the form of our tools, the future we aim for remains the same. We would be happy if you would continue to feel the new excitement that will keep expanding with us.
