# Could Jensen Huang return to Nvidia? What Trump, Altman, the board are saying.

Mashable · Olivia Tauber · 2026-11-16

Could Jensen Huang return to Nvidia? What Trump, Altman, the board are saying.
For 33 years, Nvidia had only one CEO. Now, just days after the chipmaker announced Jensen Huang's resignation, some of the country's biggest names are already want him back — from OpenAI CEO Sam Altman to President Donald Trump.
The decision ultimately belongs to Nvidia's board, which has appointed longtime finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO.
The calls for a comeback follow a week of mounting scrutiny over Huang's intimate extracurriculars. On Nov. 8, Huang had told employees he’d been unfaithful to his wife after reports of a relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing company that buys Nvidia chips. U.S. officials suspect she had ties to Chinese intelligence, an allegation Beijing has denied. The same day Huang emailed employees, Nvidia's board announced an independent investigation into reports that he had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. Reuters also reported that Huang had relationships with two Nvidia employees, including one who reported directly to him. Inhales.

It's a lot. But that doesn't mean everyone thinks its enough to get rid of the co-founder permanently. On Nov. 15, Huang was already talking to people close to him about a possible return, according to the Financial Times. One ally told the paper they were getting tech executives together to lobby for his reinstatement and would seek support from the White House.
On Monday, Trump gave that effort some encouragement. "I would certainly not mind seeing that," he told reporters before leaving Washington for the Philippines and China. He said he thought Huang had "learned a big lesson" and questioned whether Alice Huang had worked for Chinese intelligence.

A day earlier, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Larry Kudlow on Fox News that Nvidia should hire its next CEO from outside the company, saying it needed a "dramatic shake-up."
Huang's supporters are also making their case online. "Thirty-three years deserve an answer," reads the Stand With Jensen petition, which demands that Nvidia's board reinstate its co-founder while the independent investigation is underway. "We are concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process," it adds. If directors think Huang should stay out of the job, the letter asks them to explain what evidence led them to that decision and why it serves Nvidia's interests. It also calls on the board to disclose what the investigation will cover, when it expects to finish, and how Huang will get a chance to respond.

By Monday afternoon, the campaign's website showed more than 185,000 signatures, including more than 9,300 from people who identified themselves as current Nvidia employees. The site also lists Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and Replit CEO Amjad Masad among the signers.
The push to bring Huang back is familiar territory for OpenAI CEO Sam Altman. OpenAI's board fired him in Nov. 2023, and he returned days later after employees and investors rallied behind him. At Semafor's Silicon Valley & the World event on Monday, Altman said he sympathized with Huang and had long seen him as a hero. When asked whether Huang should get his job back, Altman said, "I don’t think I know enough to answer that question." He added: "it's hard to imagine the AI revolution without Jensen Huang."

Altman also sounded confident in Nvidia's ability to back the data centers that AI companies depend on. "Nvidia is, like, a behemoth of a company," he said. "I think people still trust their guarantees very much."
OpenAI has a stake in that confidence. At an Ohio data center campus where an OpenAI affiliate is leasing capacity, Nvidia has agreed to guarantee up to $105 billion in certain lease and power payments if the tenant defaults under conditions spelled out in the deal. The campus is set to run on Nvidia hardware, with limited exceptions.
For now, Kress is running Nvidia on an interim basis while keeping the CFO job she's held since 2013. The board, led by longtime director Harvey C. Jones, says it will consider internal and external candidates. It has already approached Arm CEO Rene Haas, the Financial Times reported.
The next chance to hear from Nvidia's new leadership comes Tuesday at 5 p.m. ET, when the company holds its first earnings call since Huang's departure.
