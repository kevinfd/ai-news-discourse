# Corma Raises $125M Series A at $1B Valuation to Scale Defensive AI

Defensive-cybersecurity company Corma announced on November 16, 2026 that it had raised a $125 million Series A at a $1 billion valuation. NEA led the round, with continued participation from Sequoia Capital, Khosla Ventures and Coatue, bringing Corma’s total funding to $185 million.
Corma said the new capital would accelerate scaling of its foundation models, expand compute capacity and support selective hiring. The Series A follows its emergence from stealth on August 10, 2026 with $60 million in seed funding.
The company characterized demand from the world’s largest organizations as overwhelming and identified securing enough compute for that demand and its waitlist as one of its central challenges. Corma’s ability to expand access therefore depends on obtaining additional infrastructure as well as advancing its models.
How the Corma Workforce Operates
The company deploys those models through the Corma Workforce, a system designed to work autonomously alongside human security teams. It operates within customers’ existing security and IT stacks, including identity, cloud, endpoint, security information and event management, and firewall or intrusion-detection tools. Its work spans investigations, connecting evidence and taking action across those systems.
Employees can assign tasks through Slack or Teams, ask questions, review findings and approve actions. The design adds AI-led execution to a customer’s current operating environment while keeping human review in the workflow.
Corma reports live deployments across healthcare, finance, energy, critical infrastructure and retail, with 99% of security tasks completed autonomously and successfully, 100 times faster end-to-end completion and 95% lower cost per completed task.
One anonymized Fortune 1000 services-company CISO quoted on the product page said end-to-end response time fell 94% in a comparison with the customer’s former service provider, which the customer subsequently replaced with Corma.
Why Corma Uses Defense-Specific Models
Corma’s launch statement argues that improvements in coding and software reasoning map more directly to offensive security than to the broader defensive workload. Vulnerability research and exploit development are code-reasoning problems against bounded targets; defense instead requires continuous analysis of audit logs, events, configurations and network flows. It also involves connecting weak signals across systems and long periods while making thousands of accurate, consistent decisions each day under changing conditions.
That distinction is the basis for Corma’s focus on domain-specific foundation models. Its stated development program includes rethinking model architecture, extending context to detect faint anomaly signals, adversarial self-play and continuous learning that can establish baselines over months and years.
CEO Alon Pluda and the Corma team said the next generation of its foundation models would be shared soon, without giving a release date. The financing supports that next phase of model development and selective hiring, but the immediate scaling constraint is compute: broader deployments require enough additional capacity to serve current demand and organizations on the waitlist.
