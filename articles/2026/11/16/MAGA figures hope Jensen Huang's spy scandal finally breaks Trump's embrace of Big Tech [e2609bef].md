# MAGA figures hope Jensen Huang's spy scandal finally breaks Trump's embrace of Big Tech

Axios · Maria Curi · 2026-11-16

Prominent conservative thinkers and MAGA figures are seizing on former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy to pull President Trump away from his alliance with the tech right. Why it matters: The Nvidia scandal highlights some of the political weaknesses for Trump in allowing himself to be defined by his connections to an increasingly unpopular group of companies. Right-wing players are hoping the bombshell revelations about Huang's affairs will chip away at the president's embrace of the tech industry and offer an opening for anti-tech voices in his administration. What they're saying : They view the midterm elections as proof that people across the political spectrum hate AI, data centers and view Silicon Valley as too powerful. President Trump's perception of China "has always been entirely commercial and transactional. His view was, 'we're getting screwed. I'm going to get a better deal,'" Oren Cass, founder of conservative think tank American Compass, said. Cass, who has warned about Nvidia risks for more than a year, said the allegations of espionage are the strongest evidence yet that China is not a commercial partner but an adversary that is going to "subvert the American political process in ways that end up humiliating the President." MAGA figure Steve Bannon, meanwhile, said in a text he has called Jensen Huang "a CCP 'Agent of Influence' a thousand times." and that it's an OPEN SECRET." "We need to actually cut OFF ALL THE CHIPS NOW," Bannon added as Congress warms up to legislation aimed at that. Reality check: Amid daily revelations about Huang's affairs and the negative consequences for the AI boom and U.S. economy, Trump has created some distance. He backtracked on a suggestion that Jensen could go on a trip to China later this month. Other than that, he has kept it friendly. The president earlier this week called him "honorable" and on Thursday, following Jensen's resignation, the president said he "has done a fantastic job" and called him a "Legend." On Thursday, he said in an interview with Stephen Smith that Jensen is "a tremendous talent" who's done "incredible" and "brilliant" work at Nvidia. What we're watching: Supporters of Jensen Huang are lobbying the White House to support his reinstatement as CEO, according to the Financial Times. What's next: Conservatives are hoping the scandal changes who gets a seat at the table for the remainder of Trump's presidency as 2028 and mounting AI unpopularity approaches.
