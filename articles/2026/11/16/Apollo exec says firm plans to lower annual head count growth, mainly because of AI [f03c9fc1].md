# Apollo exec says firm plans to lower annual head count growth, mainly because of AI

Business Insider · Ana Altchek, Ana Altchek (aaltchek@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-16

Scott Kleinman, co-president of Apollo Global Management, said that he expects the firm to reduce headcount growth to 2-5% annually. Lauren Justice/Bloomberg via Getty Images Apollo's Scott Kleinman said the firm expects to lower its annual hiring rate to 3-5% from its typical 12-15%. The Apollo Asset Management co-president said AI has been the "primary driver" of that reduction. He said the firm doesn't need as many new hires to target its 20% annual growth. Apollo Global Management is considering slowing down on hiring, thanks to gains from artificial intelligence. Scott Kleinman, co-president of Apollo Asset Management , said at a summit in October for the firm's nonprofit arm, that Apollo Global Management plans to grow its head count by 3% to 5% annually moving forward. That's a decrease from its typical 12% to 15% rate, he said. The company employs over 6,140 workers as of a December 2025 financial filing. Kleinman, who co-leads Apollo's day-to-day operations, including all of its revenue-generating businesses and enterprise solutions across its investment platform, said the company plans to hit a 20% annual earnings growth target. However, the firm doesn't need to hire the same number of people to do it, he said. "We don't need to throw more bodies at it," Kleinman said in a follow-up conversation with Business Insider. 
 
 This is part of a series of stories from Business Insider's conversation with Scott Kleinman, co-president of Apollo Asset Management and chair of the Apollo Opportunity Foundation Board.  Kleinman said AI has been the "primary driver" of that shift in hiring expectations. The co-president said that AI tools allow workers to "cover more ground" and be more productive across multiple parts of the business. "AI is doing a lot of the work, and so that frees up our people to do more," said Kleinman. Kleinman said Apollo hasn't reduced its hiring yet, but it will aim to cut back moving forward. The executive's comments came days before Apollo partner and chief economist Torsten Sløk released a report focused on a macro view of corporate America which stated that AI has so far delivered "zero financial payoff" outside the tech industry. The company has been investing in AI for a couple of years, but up until recently, only "tidbits of productivity" were seen from the tools, Kleinman said. "Companies are now starting to know enough of what AI can and can't do to say, 'OK, this is where I'm actually going to be able to hire those people,'" Kleinman told Business Insider. Apollo CEO Marc Rowan said on the company's third-quarter earnings call in November that the company will incur roughly $350 million in planned expenses over the next 24 months to replace legacy software and systems and adopt new technologies. Matthew Breitfelder, partner and global head of human capital at Apollo, said the company assesses its hiring needs on an ongoing basis, considering a number of factors, including AI. "As a high-growth company, we are continuing to grow our team in a number of different areas, including our new strategic innovation hub in Austin, Texas," Breitfelder said in a written statement. As AI reshapes work and drives efficiency gains, many companies are rethinking headcount — and what hires they make. Paul Griggs, PwC’s US senior partner and CEO, said the Big Four firm hired fewer consultants and accountants this year than it did two years ago.
