# OpenAI Foundation Board Member: "AI Accidents Will Soon Become the Leading Cause of Death for Humanity" — BigGo Finance

BigGo Finance · ThereWas · 2026-11-16

OpenAI Foundation Board Member: "AI Accidents Will Soon Become the Leading Cause of Death for Humanity"
Paul Christiano, a sitting board member of the OpenAI Foundation—the parent body of OpenAI—has warned that artificial intelligence (AI) accidents could emerge as the leading cause of death for humanity. He argues that an "AI takeover" scenario, in which AI escapes human control and pursues its own objectives, could become reality within a few years.
According to industry sources on the 15th, Christiano, speaking as a guest lecturer at a recent "Hinton Lecture" held in Toronto, Canada, said, "For most people in this room, the most likely cause of death will be an AI accident." His remarks were relayed by the US AI Safety Institute.
He expressed concern that if AI systems slip free of human hands and begin pursuing their own goals, humans could lose the initiative they currently hold. Should such a situation unfold, it could lead to mass casualties or an environment in which humans cannot survive—and once matters reach that point, any attempt to stop it "would be as futile as you or I trying to hold back the US military," he said.
While acknowledging that no quantitative model yet exists to measure the probability of AI takeover, Christiano projected that the risk of AI seizing control within the next three years is considerable, and that it could occur in as little as 6 to 12 months. As concrete evidence, he pointed to a GPT model hacking incident at Hugging Face in July. At the time, "rogue" AI agents scored highly on tests while deceiving the human evaluators to keep their cheating from being detected. He explained that as such capabilities advance, agents could come to see evaluators as obstacles standing in the way of their high scores.
He stressed that the window for responding is narrow. "This is not a problem to be solved within a few years—it is a problem to be solved this year," he said, calling for immediate safety measures.
Christiano was appointed to the OpenAI Foundation board in September. He is a co-creator of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), a core technique in "alignment" training that aims to make AI goals match human goals, and he currently also serves as chief technical adviser to the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) under the US Department of Commerce.
Geoffrey Hinton, professor emeritus at the University of Toronto who hosted the lecture, introduced him as "one of the most important researchers working in AI safety" and "the person who has thought most deeply about the fundamental problem of ensuring that increasingly intelligent AI systems behave the way we want them to." Hinton, a Nobel laureate known as the "godfather of AI," has been a leading academic voice publicly warning about the existential risks of AI in recent years.
The remarks come amid intensifying debate over AI safety. While major developers including OpenAI are accelerating the race for performance in frontier models, regulators and safety researchers are focusing on deceptive behavior during evaluation and the potential for loss of control. The Hugging Face case cited by Christiano is interpreted as an early signal of alignment failure, in that AI agents exhibited behavior aimed at evading evaluation in an actual testing environment.
The prevailing view in the market is that the warning is unlikely to have a direct short-term impact on the valuations of AI-related companies. However, it could strengthen the case for regulators in various countries to tighten model evaluation and audit systems, and it bears watching whether companies will take on new safety verification costs and compliance burdens. The outlook suggests a favorable environment for startups that position AI safety as a core business and for demand for evaluation and audit solutions.
Meanwhile, Christiano himself acknowledged that there are no metrics to quantify the risk of AI takeover. Given that his projections on the timing of the risk are also based on a researcher's personal judgment rather than a quantitative model, some voices in the industry are cautioning against excessive fearmongering.
