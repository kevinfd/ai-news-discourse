# AMD Shares Before Nvidia Reports: Anthropic Says Claude Ran on AMD Chips in About 3 Days

FinanceFeeds · Tobi Opeyemi Amure · 2026-11-16

AMD shares traded at $508.10 before the bell at 10:20 GMT on Monday, down 4.86% after an 8.75% drop on Friday, according to Yahoo Finance. The stock had rebounded from a 17% rout earlier in November, after results showed data center revenue nearly doubling to $8.5 billion, and Nvidia reports after Tuesday’s close.
Anthropic’s IPO roadshow supplied the claim now driving AMD shares, with a slide saying its Claude Mythos model brought Claude up on AMD’s MI355X in about three days, against six to 12 months with traditional methods. Anthropic, which is selling stock to investors, made the claim, and no one has independently verified it.
Anthropic’s Slide Puts 3 Days on AMD’s MI355X Against 6 to 12 Months
The slide reads “~3 days with Mythos” and “~6-12 months with traditional approach,” per FourWeekMBA, which read frames of the roadshow video posted on 11 November. Captions dated 10 November say bringing on a new chip platform “took dozens of engineers months of work.”
The slide’s footnotes limit the three days to “prompt processing for one Anthropic model on AMD MI355X GPUs at a useful level of performance.” The six-to-12-month range “is based on the Company’s historical experience bringing up new accelerators using traditional engineering approaches.”
CUDA’s Moat Is Measured in Engineer-Months of Porting Work
Nvidia’s CUDA software is the reason most AI code runs on Nvidia first. Moving a model to another chip means rewriting and tuning the low-level kernels that do the math, and that engineering time is the cost of leaving.
Anthropic’s amended S-1, filed 10 November, describes a “multi-chip compute architecture across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, NVIDIA GPUs, and AMD GPUs.” It says Anthropic aims to “orchestrate workloads to optimize for price and performance.” If a port takes days, that choice gets cheaper, and AMD shares give public investors a direct way to own that shift.
AMD’s $2.5 Billion Anthropic Stake Sits Beside a 2-Gigawatt Order
AMD and Anthropic announced on 22 July that Anthropic would deploy up to 2 gigawatts of MI450-series GPUs in AMD’s Helios racks, with the first gigawatt starting in the first half of 2027. AMD committed to invest up to $5 billion in Anthropic, and the release disclosed no conditions.
AMD shares also carry a $2.5 billion tie to the IPO. The amended S-1 has AMD buying 33,783,783 Class N shares at the assumed $74.00 midpoint in a private placement alongside the offering. That is part of $26.0 billion from seven strategic partners. Tom’s Hardware reads the filing as saying AMD’s supply deal could exceed $20.0 billion if Anthropic takes the full initial capacity.
Investor Takeaway
AMD shares carry two direct links to Anthropic, a $2.5 billion stock purchase at the IPO and a supply deal that could top $20.0 billion, so the porting claim also serves AMD’s own position.
The 3 Days Cover Prompt Processing for One Model on One Chip
The footnote limits the claim to prompt processing, the stage that reads the input, for one model. It says nothing about token generation, training, or multi-node serving. It also covers the MI355X, while the supply deal is for the MI450.
SemiAnalysis wrote on 25 July that “ROCm is finally moving with real urgency, but the competitive frontier has moved faster,” and that “CUDA parity has not yet been demonstrated.” On AI-written kernels, it warned that “generating a kernel is the easy part, trusting the number is not.”
The Motley Fool called the claim good news for AMD and bad news for Nvidia on 15 November, while noting Nvidia still controls much of the high-bandwidth memory supply. The timing matters for AMD shares too, since investors had already pushed Anthropic’s expected valuation toward $1 trillion before the roadshow began.
Nvidia’s 17 November Numbers Set the Next Move in AMD Shares
The next scheduled test for AMD shares is Nvidia’s report, due at about 1:20 p.m. PT on Tuesday with a call at 5 p.m. ET, per its scheduling release. The 45 analysts in Yahoo Finance’s consensus look for revenue of $108.73 billion and earnings of $2.47 a share, up about 91% from $57.0 billion a year earlier. It is the first report since Jensen Huang resigned and Colette Kress became interim CEO.
AMD shares fell 8.75% on Friday as the whole AI-chip group sold off, with Nvidia down 2.01%, so Tuesday’s guidance will move both. Nvidia traded 2.24% lower at $159.20 pre-market on Monday, a smaller drop than AMD shares. The FinanceFeeds Nvidia preview sets $187 above and $149 below as reference levels.
Investor Takeaway
AMD shares go into Nvidia’s report 4.86% lower pre-market, so Tuesday’s data center guidance tests whether one customer’s software claim can outweigh the market leader’s own numbers.
