# U.S. attorney targets another Democrat over congressional investigtions

Axios · Avery Lotz, Andrew Pantazi · 2026-11-16

A U.S. attorney put likely incoming Senate Banking Committee Chair Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) on notice about her intent to investigate corporations and expected probes into the president's inner circle. Why it matters: The letter demanding Warren preserve documents related to her congressional oversight is the latest in a string of threats levied against congressional Democrats ahead of the party assuming the congressional majority — and gavels — next year. Congress' authority to investigate, hold hearings and gather testimony is a core legislative power. Analysts have long forecasted a Democratic subpoena storm into President Trump's lucrative presidency. But Democrats' plan to exercise that power and hone their focus on the administration's "cost, chaos and corruption," as reported by Axios' Hans Nichols, is already facing Justice Department pushback. Driving the news: U.S. Attorney Aaron Reitz of the Southern District of Texas contended in his Sunday letter that Warren appeared "eager to wield" her "gavel like a tomahawk," instructing her to preserve oversight records. In the strongly worded letter, he warned Warren about targeting private citizens close to the president and reiterated that any probes must serve a legislative purpose or risk violating those individuals' First Amendment rights. Those arguments echo his prior letters to Sens. Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.) and Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.). Trump's new chief of staff, Stephen Miller , shared Reitz's letter and referenced two civil rights statutes. Warren's office did not immediately respond to Axios' request for comment. Zoom in: Reitz's letter referenced Warren, Blumenthal and incoming Oversight chair Rep. Robert Garcia's (D-Calif.) recent inquiry into Trump fundraiser Meredith O'Rourke's role in coordinating contributions for the president's ballroom and other projects. It also referenced letters Warren, with Slotkin and other lawmakers, sent to tech CEOs probing the extent to which their AI investments were subsidized by GOP-led tax breaks. What they're saying: The White House did not respond to detailed questions but referred Axios to White House counsel Will Scharf's letter to Garcia that argued Democratic threats of investigations "may cross the line into blatant criminality." Asked if Warren was the subject of a criminal probe or other proceedings, a Justice Department spokesperson told Axios that "the Department does not confirm or deny the existence of ongoing investigations." "The Department has both the statutory authority and the responsibility to examine potential violations of the law," the spokesperson added. Asked if DOJ leadership authorized or reviewed the letter, the DOJ referred Axios to Attorney General Todd Blanche's prior statement that "DOJ leadership is working with every U.S. attorney, including U.S. Attorney Reitz, on ongoing investigations." Friction point: Blanche refused to rule out prosecuting Democrats set on investigating administration officials in an interview earlier this month, claiming lawmakers' rhetoric had overstepped their authority. That came after Trump demanded the DOJ look into what he labeled as "Weaponization and Lawfare" from his political opponents.
