# Gartner: Debunking the top 10 myths about open generative AI models

Express Computer · Express Computer · 2026-11-16

By Arun Chandrasekaran, Distinguished VP Analyst, Gartner
Open generative AI models are rapidly reshaping the enterprise AI landscape. As organizations seek to scale AI initiatives, many are turning to open models to reduce costs, increase flexibility and avoid overreliance on a single vendor. At the same time, growing interest in data sovereignty, evolving regulations and the narrowing performance gap between open and proprietary models are fueling enterprise adoption.
However, as enthusiasm for open models grows, so do misconceptions. Many organizations continue to make decisions based on assumptions about model quality, cost, security, sovereignty and vendor dependence that do not reflect the realities of the current market. These myths can lead to poor architectural choices, unrealistic expectations and suboptimal AI investments.
CIOs should evaluate models based on business requirements, deployment approaches and long-term operational considerations rather than industry narratives. Understanding the myths surrounding open generative AI models can help organizations build resilient multimodel strategies that balance performance, cost, governance and flexibility.
Myth 1: Open-Source AI Is Equivalent to Open-Source Software
Open-source AI shares some principles with open-source software, but the ecosystems are fundamentally different. While open-source software benefits from mature standards and governance, open Gen AI model ecosystem remains fragmented, with custom-made licensing schemes and varying degrees of transparency. Many so-called open source models are actually open-weight models, where pretrained weights of the model are available but training data, code or model architecture may not be fully disclosed.
Myth 2: Open Models Are Lower Quality Than Proprietary Models
The performance gap between proprietary and open models has narrowed significantly and is increasingly dependent on the specific task being performed. While frontier proprietary models often maintain an advantage in complex reasoning, many open models can compete effectively in coding, summarization and instruction-following applications. Organizations should evaluate models against specific use cases rather than assume proprietary options are always superior.
Myth 3: Open Models Are Free and Inherently Lower Cost Than Proprietary Models
Although many open models carry little or no licensing cost, model acquisition represents only a small portion of the total cost of ownership. Infrastructure, inference, governance, security, integration, monitoring and operational management contribute significantly to total cost of ownership. In many cases, deployment architecture has a greater impact on costs than whether the model itself is open or proprietary.
Myth 4: Open Models Eliminate Vendor Lock-In
Open models can reduce dependence on a single model provider, but they do not eliminate lock-in across the broader AI stack. Organizations may still become dependent on inference infrastructure, development tools, guardrails, evaluation frameworks and platform services. Replacing a model often requires significant engineering effort to validate and adapt surrounding systems.
Myth 5: Sovereign AI Requires Open Models
Sovereignty is not determined by model openness alone. Rather, it depends on control over infrastructure, operations, governance and legal frameworks. Open models can contribute to sovereignty objectives, but they are only one component of a broader strategy designed to increase enterprise control over AI systems and data.
Myth 6: Open Models Are Lower Risk Because You Control Them
Open models are not inherently more secure. Their risk profile depends largely on deployment and governance practices. While self-hosting may offer greater control over data, it also increases responsibility for security, patching, operational resilience and life cycle management. Organizations must apply the same rigor to open models that is required for proprietary alternatives.
Myth 7: Model Customization Is a Huge Advantage of Open Models
Open models can be customized through fine-tuning, but customization is not always the most effective path to better outcomes. Gartner finds that approaches such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), context engineering and agent memory often deliver stronger returns on investment than full fine-tuning. As AI agents become more prevalent, the quality of context increasingly matters more than deep model customization.
Myth 8: Agentic AI Makes Proprietary Frontier Models Mandatory
Many enterprise AI agents can perform effectively using smaller open models, especially when combined with orchestration frameworks, tools, memory and retrieval capabilities. Agent performance increasingly depends on workflow design and orchestration rather than the exclusive use of frontier proprietary models. A balanced portfolio approach can help organizations align performance requirements with cost considerations.
Myth 9: China Is Certain to Win the Open Generative AI Model Race
China has accelerated innovation and commercial adoption of high-quality open models, but leadership in open AI remains geographically dispersed. Significant innovation continues to emerge from vendors and research organizations across multiple regions. The competitive landscape remains highly dynamic, influenced by factors such as talent, infrastructure, ecosystem maturity, economics and policy changes.
Myth 10: Open AI Models Will Replace Proprietary AI Models
Open models are becoming increasingly capable, but they are unlikely to replace proprietary models entirely. Proprietary vendors continue to differentiate through advanced models, enterprise platforms, commercial support and integrated AI capabilities. The future of enterprise AI is more likely to be multimodel than winner-takes-all, with organizations selecting the best model for each use case.
As open generative AI models mature, organisations should move beyond simplistic assumptions about openness, cost or performance. Competitive advantage will increasingly come from enterprise context, workflow integration, governance and orchestration rather than from model selection alone. The most successful organizations will be those that maintain flexibility, adopt a multimodel mindset and align AI decisions with business outcomes rather than industry hype.
