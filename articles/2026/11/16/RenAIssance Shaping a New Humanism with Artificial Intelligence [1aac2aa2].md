# RenAIssance: Shaping a New Humanism with Artificial Intelligence

Il Mattino · Redazione Web · 2026-11-16

RenAIssance: Shaping a New Humanism with Artificial Intelligence
                            lunedì 16 novembre 2026, 11:53                                                    
                            4 Minutes of Reading
                        
                            
                            Using artificial intelligence to expand people's skills, without replacing them; and avoiding generative tools becoming a vehicle for cultural and creative standardization. These are some of the key issues at the heart of the presentation of the European RenAIssance project and its Manifesto, hosted today in the Aula Magna of the University of Naples Federico II, in San Giovanni a Teduccio.
The RenAIssance project is coordinated by CeSMA (Advanced Metrological and Technological Services Center of Federico II) as the lead partner and includes two Italian companies among the participants: Iniziativa and Medaarch.
The meeting provided an opportunity to discuss the strategy and roadmap for the use of AI in cultural and creative industries, with the aim of supporting companies, professionals, and the education sector in transforming ideation, design, and production processes.
The Rector of Federico II, Andrea Mazzucchi, emphasized the cultural and educational value of this challenge: "The Manifesto is not simply about spreading skills in the use of new tools, but about building a culture of artificial intelligence: a culture able to combine technical competence and critical awareness, innovation and responsibility, creativity and protection of rights. This is a fully university mission, and Federico II can and must contribute to this discussion by connecting scientific, technological, humanistic, legal, and economic knowledge. Our goal should not simply be to adapt humans to the possibilities of machines. On the contrary, it must be to put the possibilities of machines at the service of human development, knowledge, freedom, and the quality of collective life. If the historical Renaissance gave us the image of a culture capable of bringing arts, sciences, and techniques into dialogue, perhaps the task of our time is precisely to build a new technological humanism."
"Students clearly want to activate a mature use of artificial intelligence," said Domenico Accardo, director of CeSMA, "a use that is not just having AI do what would be their own learning tasks, but rather they want to have the ability to use this new technology as an accelerator, that is, to be faster but equally efficient in performing certain tasks, while keeping the human value—the value of why they are in training—at the center."
New jobs and new skills
The outlined challenge is that of conscious use of AI, which can also generate new job opportunities. Even entirely new professions, from Creative AI Expert, to AI-Driven Idea Generation Designer, to Computational AI Designer.
"Artificial intelligence has already entered companies operating in the cultural, creative, and manufacturing sectors. The project's question, therefore, is not whether to use it, but how to use it well: a tool that expands people's skills, does not replace them, and above all avoids the risk of standardization. An AI that supports and extends the abilities of workers, not an AI that flattens content," emphasized Flora Amato, Coordinator of the RenAIssance Project. "This is why we must learn to direct and manage AI-assisted work, developing skills that allow us to use it not only in ideation but also in the stages of design, prototyping, and production. Today, in fact, while AI support is significant in the concept phase, there are still difficulties in its practical application."
The centrality of human skills was also reiterated by Amleto Picerno Ceraso, founder of Medaarch and professor at Federico II University, who identified judgment as the fundamental principle for facing the ongoing transformation.
"Judgment, taking responsibility for judgment, seems to me the best way to avoid that irresponsibility or lack of accountability that is one of the inherent risks in the use of artificial intelligence," he said, illustrating the work behind the RenAIssance strategy. "In the fields of fashion, architecture, and design, tacit knowledge, built through experience, allows one to recognize and evaluate the value of a result. Artificial intelligence is now also a relational tool, capable of influencing our thinking: for this reason we must strengthen critical capacity and not limit ourselves to teaching how to use software, but teach how to design."
"Today's event demonstrates the importance of technology transfer and exchange with the productive fabric that we are carrying out with CeSMA at Federico II," commented Ivo Allegro, CEO of Iniziativa. "Projects like RenAIssance, as well as others carried out in recent years, generate a network and enrich the productive fabric, the University, and our young people. This is the way to affirm and nurture human intelligence to enhance interaction with new technologies, including AI."
Final objectives
RenAIssance aims to train over 360 people and involve more than 80 participants, as well as to create or renew six training infrastructures dedicated to artificial intelligence in eight European Union countries. The acquired skills will be certified through micro-credentials aligned with the levels of European training programs.
A roadmap that ultimately aims to build a more conscious relationship between artificial intelligence and creativity: not a delegation to technology, but a new alliance between generative tools, specialized knowledge, experience, and human judgment.
