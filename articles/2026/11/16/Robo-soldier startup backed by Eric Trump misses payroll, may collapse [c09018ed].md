# Robo-soldier startup backed by Eric Trump misses payroll, may collapse

Ars Technica · Jeremy Hsu · 2026-11-16

A humanoid robotics company backed and advised by President Donald Trump’s son, Eric Trump, has reportedly missed payroll and may be on the brink of collapse after a funding shortfall. The Silicon Valley startup, Foundation Future Industries, claims it is developing and testing robots for military roles on battlefields from Ukraine to Gaza.
Foundation failed to pay employees for the payroll period ending on October 30, anonymous sources told CNBC. The startup’s staff is especially angry because Foundation’s co-founder and CEO, Sankaet Pathak, supposedly borrowed $4.6 million in company funds to buy a luxury home overlooking San Francisco Bay about a year ago.
This comes as Pathak and Foundation’s leadership are still looking to raise funding to support a $3 billion valuation, based on their promise to develop a humanoid robot ready for combat duty and capable of wielding various weapons.
The company has demonstrated a humanoid robot named the Phantom MK-1 and is touting an upcoming robot called the Phantom MK-2. Foundation has touted its humanoid robots for roles in warehouses and other workplaces—but has courted media coverage by claiming it is developing humanoid robots for battlefield use.
Now CNBC’s reporting based on “people with direct knowledge of the matter” indicates that insiders worry the startup could soon collapse without additional funding.
Foundation’s business claims
Eric Trump holds an equity stake in Foundation and was announced as its chief strategy advisor in March 2026, according to the Financial Times. Investment funds linked to both Eric Trump and Donald Trump Jr. are involved with multiple military technology companies that have received Pentagon loans and contracts worth more than $3.2 billion as of July 2026, according to The Washington Post.
