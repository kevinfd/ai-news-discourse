# The AI bubble that almost burst.

IGN · Rhiannon Bevan · 2026-11-16

Character.ai went live in 2022, alongside many other AI apps like ChatGPT, forever changing the online landscape (for the worse, many would argue).
While it never became a household name, like ChatGPT, Grok, or Gemini, Character.ai found its place in online fandom spaces. This is because the app allows users to make their own AI companions and share them with others, and this was quickly used to make copyrighted characters from film, television, video games, and more.
It is no surprise, then, that the service ran into many legal and ethical issues. Some copyright holders would do what they could to have their characters removed from the service, and moderators would struggle to remove bots based on real people. More seriously, questions were raised about the safety measures in place to protect users, as at least two minors were found to have used Character.ai extensively before they died by suicide. This resulted in two lawsuits, one of which has since been settled.
Throughout all of this, Character.ai has been struggling to turn its sizeable user base into profit. Despite several attempts to turn this around — rolling out new features, ads, and testing out subscription fees — Character.ai has been unsuccessful and has now filed for bankruptcy.
In October, Disney came to the company's aid, paying $60 million to grab 66 of Character.ai's employees (as per Business Insider). It has further proposed paying $20 million to acquire some of Character.ai's tech, leading to speculation that Disney is looking to enter the AI chatbot space, perhaps with its own copyrighted characters.
Now, the Character.ai app itself might be saved by another offer. As reported by PPC Land, a new company called "Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC" has offered $10 million for everything Disney didn't want, including user data, user-generated content (bots, chat logs), and the website and app for the service.
Curiously, it appears that Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC was formed a day before this offer was made, seemingly with the sole purpose of acquiring this user data — or at least starting the bidding. Judging from the response from fans, the name of the company is a referenence to the chatbots and words they would frequently use, although the company claims to have no links to Character.ai's owners in the bid.
All in all, users are hoping that this saves the service, as its future remain uncertain during these bankruptcy proceedings.
Others, however, will be hoping to see the back of Character.AI. Many have spoken out against its impact on fandom culture, worrying that it is being seen as a replacement to traditional online roleplaying or fanfiction writing, and that this is stiffling creativity in younger fandom members.
That is before touching on the critiques of generative AI itself, like energy usage and the risk of what is often referred to as "AI psychosis", characterised by an AI chatbot encouraging delusional thinking in its users. It has also been accused of encourging dangerous behaviours like self-harm to users unprompted, or encouraged them to distance themselves from friends and family.
With that in mind, it is no wonder that Disney wasn't interested in propping up the Character.ai name itself. Despite this, it seemingly sees value in Character.ai's tech, since it has poached those who worked on it. It remains to be seen what comes of Disney's own AI chatbot endevours.
