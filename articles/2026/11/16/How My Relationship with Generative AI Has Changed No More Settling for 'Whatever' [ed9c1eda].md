# How My Relationship with Generative AI Has Changed: No More Settling for 'Whatever'

note · しみっく · 2026-11-16

SYSTEM NOTICE
How My Relationship with Generative AI Has Changed: No More Settling for 'Whatever'
Hello, this is Shimic.
Last month, I signed up for the paid plan of ChatGPT, Plus. It costs 3,000 yen per month. It's not exactly a huge amount, but it took a fair amount of courage to pay for something that I could technically use for free.
However, I feel that since subscribing to this paid generative AI plan, the number of times I think "whatever" has decreased. Today, I'd like to talk about that.
I got curious about the tablets at Sushiro
Last weekend, I went to Sushiro. It used to be that sushi would go around on a belt, making it a true "conveyor belt sushi" place, but when I looked it up, it seems they stopped having the sushi rotate around 2023. Now, the system is to order from a tablet installed at your seat.
At this point, I became a bit curious about this tablet.
Looking closely, it wasn't an iPad, and it seemed like they had developed the hardware independently. I've heard stories about companies trying to develop tablet-like devices in-house, and developing and operating such equipment is actually quite difficult.
Both hardware and software development cost money.
Since they are operating on the scale of Sushiro, they are constantly opening new stores, and if a tablet breaks, they need to replace it unit by unit. Yet, the hardware components they are currently using will eventually go out of production.
Software also faces competition from other companies, so it needs to be significantly updated occasionally to keep it from looking outdated, and recently, security risks have increased, so you can't cut corners on daily maintenance.
I think this is quite a challenge.
That makes me wonder about things like who makes the hardware and whether the software is developed in-house.
That said, in the past, I wouldn't have thought to go out of my way to search for this on Google.
If I searched, maybe an article from ITmedia or similar covering an interview with Sushiro might come up, but I don't know how much information Sushiro makes public. I might be able to piece the information together if I repeated a few searches, but it would take time.
It's just too much effort to look into while eating sushi with my partner. Usually, it's the kind of thing I'd just brush off with a "whatever."
I asked ChatGPT while eating sushi
However, with ChatGPT, I can just ask, "How is the development of Sushiro's tablets handled?" and it summarizes various information for me.
Looking into it, it seems that external companies are involved in Sushiro's system development, but the operating company also has an information systems department, and they seem to be quite deeply involved, even if it's not fully in-house.
While I was at it, I got curious about what kind of people are involved, and when I asked follow-up questions, it even told me what kind of talent they are currently recruiting.
While chatting with ChatGPT about things like that, I remembered that conveyor belt sushi chains like Kura Sushi and Sushiro are businesses with high cost-of-goods ratios, so they have implemented various innovations to reduce labor costs and waste rates.
Thinking about it that way, since the ordering system is directly linked to a company's competitiveness, it made sense that they wouldn't just completely outsource it to an external company but would be deeply involved in the development themselves.
Of course, not everything in ChatGPT's response is necessarily correct. It likely includes speculation regarding parts that haven't been made public.
Even so, from a question that popped into my head while eating sushi, the conversation expanded to corporate IT strategy, and I felt like I had somehow "understood" it.
It's something I would have just brushed off with a "whatever" before, but my, how convenient things have become.
I tried studying how generative AI works
Another area where I felt a big change while using generative AI is when I'm studying.
Recently, I took a course on Udemy about the origins of generative AI. Udemy is an online learning platform that uses videos.
Whenever I heard someone say something like, "Generative AI is just spinning words that seem statistically correct," I had doubts like, "Is that so? Can that really be true? Can it really talk about things that sound so correct just by doing that?" so I decided I wanted to listen to the explanation properly.
However, it was a 4-hour introductory course, and when I listened to it, it was quite difficult.
Neural networks, Word2Vec, Transformer, Attention, Multi-Head Attention, and so on. It was a parade of words I didn't know, and the content was too advanced for me to really understand.
In the past, when I took courses like this, even if there were things I didn't understand, I would often just let them slide.
It's that "Uh-huh, I see. Ah, so that's what it means, I completely understand" (← I don't understand (C) Pop Team Epic) kind of thing.
But if I stopped there to start researching, I wouldn't make any progress at all, and I couldn't ask the instructor questions every time, right? In the end, I would finish the 4-hour lecture feeling like I had somehow understood the essence.
I spent the time, but many small questions remained, leaving me with indigestion. That happened quite often.
A 4-hour video course that I can't finish even after 8 hours
This time, however, every time a question came up, I stopped the video and asked the generative AI repeatedly.
"Wait, wait, wait. I don't understand at all. What is this person saying?"
"My understanding up until now has been this, but was that wrong?"
"In this case, wouldn't it be like this?"
"You talk about vector operations, but how many dimensions is a single token represented by in generative AI anyway? Give me some concrete numbers so I can visualize it."
Once one question is resolved, another one pops up from it.
If I don't feel satisfied after hearing an explanation, I ask again. I ask for explanations using different examples or confirm if my understanding is correct.
By repeating this process, I ended up in a situation where a four-hour course took me eight hours to complete.
However, I do feel that my understanding has deepened more than before by using generative AI.
This is because I was able to think through things until I was satisfied in my own way, rather than just letting them slide with a vague 'I guess that's how it is' like I used to.
Of course, that doesn't mean I've fully understood the intricate principles of generative AI. Even though it's an introductory level, my level of understanding might only be around 50%. There are likely errors in the explanations provided by generative AI as well.
Even so, I felt that there was a huge difference in satisfaction between just finishing a lecture and deepening my understanding within myself while repeatedly asking questions.
When I use AI as a partner I can ask questions to as many times as I want like this, it feels like having a personal tutor who explains things according to my own knowledge and level of understanding. And what's more, I never get a dirty look no matter how many times I ask the same thing.
Isn't that pretty amazing when it comes to studying something?
I think if I had had generative AI back in my student days, studying all sorts of things might have been a bit more fun.
If you rely on AI, will you stop thinking for yourself?
There is also a fear that as generative AI becomes more convenient, the ability to research and think for oneself might decline.
In fact, lately, when a question pops into my head, I've started asking ChatGPT first. The opportunities to think of search keywords myself, perform a Google search, and investigate which site has the information I'm looking for have decreased significantly compared to before.
Sometimes I wonder if this is okay. Am I relying too much on generative AI? Will I become unable to live without it?
However, looking back at my experiences with Sushiro and Udemy this time, it doesn't seem like I've stopped thinking at least compared to before.
I've started looking into things that I would have previously found troublesome and just let slide with a 'good enough,' and I've started thinking one or two steps further about things I used to just listen to without understanding.
Of course, there is a danger of acquiring incorrect knowledge if you take generative AI's answers at face value. The ability to judge the accuracy of information for oneself might be even more necessary than ever before.
Even so, being able to easily dig into small questions is a big change for me.
We live in a world of rapid change, and there are many things I don't know. That said, I can't research and study everything. But, when something suddenly catches my interest in daily life, I can ask about it on the spot. And from there, I might unexpectedly arrive at an interesting story.
I am honestly glad that such small pleasures have increased.
How are you all using generative AI?
I intend to continue using generative AI a bit more and find my own way of engaging with it.
