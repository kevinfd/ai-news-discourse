# Nvidia shares under pressure as calls grow for Huang to be reinstated as CEO

CNBC · Leonie Kidd · 2026-11-16

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.
Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.
There has been a lot of debate over the real versus the perceived value of the "Jensen premium."
But today, the bears are back, sending Nvidia shares lower premarket despite speculation that Huang could be pushing to make a return to the AI firm.
Happening now: What's Next for Nvidia?
The calls for Jensen Huang to be reinstated as Nvidia CEO have grown louder over the weekend.
A Financial Times report suggests Huang has discussed a potential return with close confidants.
Here are the latest developments:
- An online petition has drawn over 175,000 signatures; Elon Musk has backed that campaign, and major shareholders Altimeter Capital and Atriedes Management have written an open letter to the board asking them to consider Huang's return.
- Nvidia also told CNBC in a statement that any speculation about individual candidates, whether internal or external, was premature.
- The view from the White House is more hostile towards the embattled boss, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent calling for a "dramatic shakeup" and further scrutiny of export controls.
- Bessent has also pointed the finger towards Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, saying "Commerce had the pen," when it came to deciding blacklisted export partners.
CNBC's Luke Fountain has done an in-depth analysis of Cantor Fitzgerald — which Lutnick formerly chaired — and its position in Nvidia. He found the firm's share count fell nearly half between September 2025 and March 2026.
- Coming up: CNBC's Scott Wapner will speak to Brad Gerstner, the founder and CEO of Altimeter Capital, on "Halftime Report" at 12 p.m. ET as he makes that call for Huang to be reconsidered.
Big Movers: Clouds over chips
Nvidia shares are under pressure in premarket trading.
Last week, the stock suffered its longest weekly losing streak since 2012, with its market value sinking $1.7 trillion.
Neocloud stocks, chip shares and U.S. server groups are also trading lower in premarket moves.
- IMF warning: IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva says the global economy is "on the AI leg," warning that the overdependence risks the world's financial stability.
Politics: Trump, Xi and Putin confirm summit
The Chinese government has confirmed that President Xi Jinping will meet with Russia's President Vladimir Putin and U.S. President Donald Trump in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday.
The summit between these three is unprecedented, following multiple bilateral meetings between the Heads of State over the years.
- Why it matters: The meeting comes at a sensitive time diplomatically for all three nations, amid spying scandals, Iran tensions and the Ukraine war.
- Topic tensions: Evercore ISI China strategist Neo Wang told CNBC there is "a disagreement over the agenda", with the U.S. focused on nuclear non-proliferation, while Russia looks to address Ukraine.
- Coverage: CNBC will be live from Shenzhen throughout the week with Sri Jegarajah reporting from the summit.
Anthropic: IPO countdown
Anthropic's IPO this week is set to be a defining moment for the AI market.
But as CNBC's Jordan Novet reports, it comes at a troubled time for the tech industry. From the neocloud storm to the leadership crisis at Nvidia, the stakes are high ahead of the group's debut, expected on the Nasdaq on Thursday.
- Why it matters: Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company are collectively investing $26 billion at the IPO price, which is currently expected at $70 to $78 a share. Some of those companies are existing investors, as is early backer Amazon, whose stake could be in the hundreds of billions of dollars.
- Seven co-founders: Four researchers, a former journalist, a public policymaker, and an engineer founded Anthropic in 2021. Dario and Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and Chris Olah each stand to make $250 million in cash proceeds and to hold stock worth upwards of $18 billion after Anthropic's IPO.
And Finally...
How Rockstar's 'GTA VI' release could reshape gaming, streaming and even paid time off
It's been 13 years since the last installment of Grand Theft Auto, and fans are getting ready for what analysts say could be the biggest video game launch of all time.
The latest game in the franchise, "Grand Theft Auto VI," is set to launch on Nov. 19, after Take-Two Interactive's Rockstar Games delayed its launch twice. Roth analyst Eric Handler estimated the game maker spent more than $1 billion developing the game.
Sales figures could end up breaking records. Sensor Tower estimated over 12 million preorders through Nov. 13, and Morgan Stanley forecast the game could sell 40 million copies by March 2027.
