# Apple provides a safe way to ask an AI questions about your health

9to5Mac · Ben Lovejoy · 2026-11-16

A growing number of people are asking AI chatbots about their health issues, raising two major concerns: safety and privacy. Apple is now offering a safer way to do this through an Apple Health AI Usage Study.
This is actually one of Apple’s research studies, but those volunteering to take part in it will be able to ask health questions – with three key assurances not guaranteed by other AI chatbots …
Apple Health AI Usage Study
Apple launched its Research app back in 2019 with the goal of allowing customers to help universities and medical institutions with health studies. You typically agree to share certain anonymized information from the Apple Health app in order to provide data that can advance medical research. You may also be asked to complete questionnaires.
The Apple Health AI Usage Study is one of these, but it works in a somewhat different way. You’ll still be invited to share some of your health data, but will also be able to ask an AI system questions about your health.
Three key assurances
When you ask health questions of a typical AI chatbot, you don’t know how reliable those answers may be or what will happen to your data. Apple study offers three assurances:
- Authoritative sources
- Not used for AI training
- No retention of questions or answers
Authoritative sources
Apple says that answers will be generated by the same platform used by healthcare professionals.
The study is conducted in collaboration with OpenEvidence, a leading clinical AI platform widely used by healthcare professionals that generates answers using medical information from established sources such as The New England Journal of Medicine, Journal of the American Medical Association, and Nature.
The answers provided to you will include links to peer-reviewed clinical citations.
Your data won’t be used for AI training
As you’d expect from Apple, there are strong privacy protections, including the assurance that your data will not be used to train AI models.
The study is designed from the ground up with privacy protections, transparency, and clinical guardrails, and study data will not be used to train AI models. Participants retain control over whether to share any HealthKit metrics or survey responses, and Apple has implemented security measures to protect the study data it maintains, as well as the confidentiality of participants’ information.
No retention of questions or answers
CNET reports on Apple’s assurance that none of your chats will be retained.
OpenEvidence will delete both question and response data within 24 hours. A small group of Apple researchers will have access to coded versions of survey responses, separated from personally identifiable information, summaries of conversations and categories of the most common conversation types.
How to get access
Access to the study is currently limited to users in the US by downloading the app. If you try it, please do share your thoughts in the comments.
