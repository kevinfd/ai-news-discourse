# AI singer tops South Korea's Melon chart for first time with 'Did You Become the Adult You Dreamed Of?'

The Herald Business · ZephyrArctic · 2026-11-16

Meomureur's 'Did You Become the Adult You Dreamed Of?' reaches No. 1
Beats out RESCENE, A2O Light and other K-pop acts
Listeners in their 30s and 40s make up 66% of audience
Lyrics and concept by humans; composition and vocals by AI
A song performed not by a human but by artificial intelligence has topped the charts on Melon, South Korea's leading music streaming platform — a first for the service. The track used generative AI technology for both its composition and vocals, beat out popular K-pop acts to claim the No. 1 spot, and has prompted observers to say AI has moved beyond a supporting role in music production and is now competitive in the mainstream pop market.
According to music industry sources, "Did You Become the Adult You Dreamed Of?" by the music project Meomureur reached No. 1 on Melon's Top 100 chart as of 5 p.m. Friday, overtaking RESCENE's new single "With."
It is the first time an AI-assisted song has topped the Melon Top 100. The achievement came roughly five months after the track was released in June.
On the same chart, RESCENE ranked second, A2O Light third and ILLIT fifth. The fact that an AI-assisted track outperformed K-pop acts with established fandom underscores the milestone.
"Did You Become the Adult You Dreamed Of?" is a rock ballad defined by an emotive melody and a compelling vocal delivery. Its lyrics resonate with listeners by exploring the gap between childhood dreams of adulthood and the weight of real-life concerns.
The song found its strongest audience among listeners in their 30s and 40s. According to Melon, those in their 30s made up the largest share at 36 percent, followed by those in their 40s at 30 percent — together accounting for 66 percent of all listeners. Those in their 20s represented 19 percent, while listeners in their 50s made up 11 percent.
Another reason the song has drawn attention is that no human sang it. The vocals were generated using Suno, a generative AI music service, and AI technology was also used in the composition process.
However, the production was not left entirely to AI. Meomureur is a project by Glorywave, a producing team that has worked with established artists. Composers Lee Yong-seok and Choi Seong-hyeok, who lead the team, made all creative decisions — including the concept, lyrics and overall production direction — and used AI to bring the music to completion.
On its YouTube channel, Meomureur described its approach as writing lyrics from everyday feelings and using AI tools "to capture them in plain melodies and words that linger."
In the track description posted on music platforms, the project stated that "the vocals on this album were generated using Suno AI technology" and that "the concept, lyrics and overall production direction were handled directly by the artist Meomureur."
"For me, AI is a modern instrument — a partner that helps bring my imagination into reality," the description added.
The song did not shoot to the top immediately after its release but climbed the charts steadily through word of mouth. It reached No. 18 on the Melon Top 100 on Oct. 2, broke into second place earlier this month and continued its upward trajectory before ultimately reaching No. 1.
Industry observers see the achievement as evidence that AI-assisted music can compete for listeners in the same market as songs by established artists.
While AI music was previously used mainly to mimic the voices of famous singers or to produce background tracks for short videos, an increasing number of AI-generated songs are now being distributed as standalone releases and entering the mainstream pop market.
As AI-assisted music grows in popularity, debate continues over copyright protection and the rights of human creators. Questions about the scope of copyright recognition for AI-generated music, how revenues should be distributed, and the potential impact on traditional music creators are among the challenges the industry will need to address.
