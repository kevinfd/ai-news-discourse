# Small regional town fights back against proposed AI data centre

ABC News Australia · Charlotte Wilkes and Steve Martin · 2026-11-16

Small regional Victorian town fights back against proposed AI data centre
In short:
Thousands of Victorians protested against data centres on Saturday including dozens from the small town of Miners Rest.
Miners Rest, north of Ballarat, is home to just under 4,000 people and is the proposed location for a data centre to be constructed by Resi Ventures.
What's next?
Protesters are calling for a statewide moratorium on the construction of AI data centres.
The small regional town of Miners Rest, north of Ballarat, is home to just under 4,000 people.
Locals say their quiet country life might be about to change after developer Resi Ventures announced to the Australian Stock Exchange in June its intention to build an AI data centre at the town's old quarry.
Volunteer firefighter and First Nations man Jedd Willis said the proposal on a 67 hectare plot of land in his town had been overwhelming.
"There been a complete lack of care for country which disturbs me as a First Nations person," Mr Willis said.
"[The site] has a very valuable resource for water and for firefighting."
Mr Willis, who is a CFA volunteer, said firefighters would often use the water at the proposed site for fighting bushfires.
"Without that [water] source, we'd be forced to rely more heavily on the treatment plant,"
he said.
"Along that, the concerns around the lack of water availability across the state as a whole, let alone just this region and with the upcoming fire season being forecasted to be quite treacherous is concerning."
Thousands across Victoria protest
On Saturday, dozens of Miners Rest locals joined thousands from across the state at the steps of the state library in Melbourne to protest against the rise of AI data centres in Victoria.
As of September this year, there were 50 data centres operating across Victoria with another 18 in the pipeline.
Protesters called for a statewide moratorium on their construction.
Secretary for the No Data Centre Miners Rest group, Chris Phillips said the developer had not been open about its plans.
He said locals learned about this proposal through the media, rather than consultation with the community.
"It's been extremely difficult [to find information about it],"
Mr Phillips said.
"The benefit to the community would be very little."
Mr Phillips said one of his concerns was whether the company would be making use of an aquifer at the site.
"There's an aquifer [at the site] that goes many hundreds of kilometres underground and people draw water out of that to grow food like, potato farmers," he said.
"We don't want that touched.
"We're scared that water could get contaminated and flow through to the rest of our agricultural industry and be a blight on our area."
'Nervously watching' upcoming election
Mr Phillips said locals were also concerned about the noise pollution that could come from the centre.
"It's going to have a noise transmission of 63 to 65 decibels, which will be heard up to 1 kilometre away, over a constant 24 hours," he said.
He said he would be anxiously watching the upcoming election and hoped politicians were listening to the local community.
"I think if [politicians] don't listen to people they're going to get a very, very rude shock come this election," he said.
"We are very nervously watching and we've had commitments from various political parties but we are also very nervous as to what could happen once the party is elected we could be steam rolled."
In a statement Ripon MP, Martha Haylett said Miners Rest families deserved to be consulted before decisions were made that could fundamentally change their local area.
"There has been no planning approval, no permits issued, and no meaningful consultation with the community," Ms Haylett said.
"That is simply not good enough.
"A project of this scale and significance cannot be imposed on a community without proper scrutiny, transparency, and engagement."
The City of Ballarat said in a statement it had not received any planning applications for the proposal.
"While Council officers have had early conversations with the developer, no formal applications have been lodged," it said.
"All planning applications are subject to a thorough assessment process."
The ABC has contacted Resi Ventures for comment.
