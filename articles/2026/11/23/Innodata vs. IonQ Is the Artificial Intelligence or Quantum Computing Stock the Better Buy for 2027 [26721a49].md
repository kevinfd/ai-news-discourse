# Innodata vs. IonQ: Is the Artificial Intelligence or Quantum Computing Stock the Better Buy for 2027?

The Motley Fool · Robert Izquierdo · 2026-11-23

Innodata vs. IonQ: Is the Artificial Intelligence or Quantum Computing Stock the Better Buy for 2027?
Innodata's revenue grew nearly 48% last year, while IonQ's quantum hardware sales surged even faster, though its losses tell a very different story.
By Robert Izquierdo – Updated
Key Points
- Innodata provides critical data engineering and AI solutions to many of the world's largest technology enterprises.
- IonQ is a pioneer in quantum computing, delivering next-generation hardware through major cloud provider partnerships.
- Which innovative technology stock is the better choice for your long-term growth strategy?
Choosing between a data-focused AI provider and a quantum computing pioneer represents two very different paths for your portfolio. Innodata (INOD +14.54%) and IonQ (IONQ +12.65%) both operate at the cutting edge of innovation.
Innodata focuses on the data engineering required to fuel artificial intelligence, while IonQ builds the actual quantum hardware that could redefine processing power. Comparing these two helps highlight the trade-off between established AI software services and the high-stakes potential of quantum hardware in the tech stocks sector.
The case for Innodata
Innodata is a global data engineering enterprise that provides human expertise and evaluation frameworks to help businesses build and adopt generative AI solutions. The company serves five of the "Magnificent Seven" tech giants and various AI research labs. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as one specific customer accounted for approximately 58% of total revenue in 2025.
In its 2025 fiscal year (FY), revenue reached $251.7 million, representing growth of 47.6% over the previous year. The company also reported a net income of $32.2 million, resulting in a net margin of 12.8%.
As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio stood at 2.7x, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term debts with its short-term assets. The debt-to-equity ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, was zero, meaning it had no debt on its balance sheet.
Free cash flow, which is cash from operations minus capital expenditures, was $35.6 million. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented 23.8% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.
The case for IonQ
IonQ develops and delivers hardware solutions across quantum computing, networking, and sensing. It offers its technology through a quantum-computing-as-a-service model, partnering with major cloud platforms including Amazon (AMZN +3.47%), Microsoft (MSFT +1.65%), and Alphabet (GOOGL +6.34%) (GOOG +5.88%). These partnerships allow researchers and enterprises to access quantum processing power through familiar infrastructure.
In FY 2025, IonQ reported revenue of $130.0 million, which reflects a year-over-year growth rate of 201.9%. Despite this rapid expansion, the company is still in its early stages of financial development. It reported a net loss of $510.4 million for the year, resulting in a negative net margin of 392.6%.
As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio was 15.5x, indicating a strong position for meeting short-term obligations. Its debt-to-equity ratio was zero, showing no reliance on traditional debt for its operations. Free cash flow for the period was negative $299.6 million, as the company continues to invest heavily in the development of its quantum technology.

Risk profile comparison
Innodata faces risks from its heavy customer concentration, although it is working to expand its clients. The company also deals with legal and regulatory challenges, including an ongoing securities class action and potential liability from a 2008 Philippine labor judgment. Additionally, it must navigate intense competition from large tech companies and operational risks associated with its facilities in the Philippines, India, and Sri Lanka.
IonQ is an early-stage company with a history of significant operating losses and an unproven business model. It faces operational risks related to achieving scalable quantum capabilities in a nascent technology and the integration of numerous acquisitions. Furthermore, the company depends on government contracts and faces intense global competition from well-capitalized incumbents like Alphabet and Microsoft, which are also developing quantum computers.
Valuation comparison
Innodata appears significantly more affordable for investors based on its P/S ratio multiple while IonQ does not have a Forward P/E ratio because it is not expected to be profitable any time soon.

Which stock would I buy for 2027?
Innodata offers immediate, profitable exposure to the commercial artificial intelligence boom, while IonQ represents a higher-risk, long-term bet on pure-play quantum computing. Both are tackling innovative technologies, but between the two, I would invest in Innodata.
For starters, Innodata's share price valuation is far more attractive. Moreover, it's positioning its business for increased resilience in 2027 by targeting physical AI, such as humanoid robots. Training data for robotics models requires custom video and movement information that is hard to come by, so Innodata is using motion capture to help robots learn to move like humans.
Innodata is also a profitable business growing rapidly. In the third quarter of 2026, it delivered 53% year-over-year revenue growth to $95.6 million, and net income of $10.3 million.
Although IonQ's revenue is rising quickly, the company remains deeply unprofitable. Its technology has yet to achieve commercial adoption as its quantum computers have not reached quantum advantage, the stage where a quantum machine is more efficient and cost-effective than a classical computer. It could be years before that happens, while Innodata is already delivering strong results.
