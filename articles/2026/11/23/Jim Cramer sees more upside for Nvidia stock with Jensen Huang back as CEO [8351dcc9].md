# Jim Cramer sees more upside for Nvidia stock with Jensen Huang back as CEO

CNBC · Alexa LoMonaco · 2026-11-23

Nvidia's remarkable stock comeback isn't done yet now that Jensen Huang has returned as CEO, CNBC's Jim Cramer said Monday.
"It's terrific to have this admittedly tarnished national treasure back leading Nvidia," the "Mad Money" host said. "It's kind of a miracle given the board's insistence that he would never get the CEO job again. But it's a miracle that saved the Nasdaq which, in turn, saved the market, at least for now."
Nvidia shares soared 31.5% on the news, fueling a broader rally in AI infrastructure stocks. The S&P 500 jumped 4.7%, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite surged 6.5%, with both indexes posting their best days since April 2025. Monday's rebound erased nearly all of Nvidia's decline following Huang's Nov. 12 resignation amid reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one involving an alleged Chinese spy. Before the rally Monday, the stock had fallen in all trading sessions since his departure.
"Jensen's only one man, and Nvidia is only one company," he said. "But the truth is that Jensen is the linchpin that holds the AI solar system together, and Nvidia is the biggest beneficiary."
Cramer argued that Huang's reinstatement removes a major source of uncertainty for investors, strengthening the case for a higher valuation. The chipmaker delivered blockbuster quarterly results last week, raising its revenue growth outlook to 75%, but the results did little to stem the sell-off as investors remained concerned about its leadership.
"If it weren't for the Jensen overhang, and the insistence that Jensen could never come back, ... I think the stock would have been up significantly on that quarter," Cramer said.
Still, Cramer cautioned that Huang's return doesn't erase the broader headwinds facing Nvidia. Growing political opposition to data center construction following the midterm elections could slow AI infrastructure expansion, while uncertainty surrounding chip sales to China threatens a potential source of demand. Meanwhile, a more hawkish Federal Reserve has pressured technology stock valuations as investors brace for higher interest rates.
Those challenges, Cramer argued, make it difficult to justify a return to the $213 level Nvidia traded at before The New York Times first reported about Huang's relationship with a woman suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence on Oct. 29. But with Huang back at the helm and the company's growth outlook intact, he said the stock deserves a higher valuation. The stock currently trades at roughly 10 times earnings estimates for Nvidia's fiscal 2028, according to FactSet.
"There is just no way that, with the return of Jensen, this stock deserves the same 10 times price to earnings multiple," Cramer said.
"These things are subjective, of course, but to start, let's say this stock deserves to trade at twelve times next year's earnings estimate, which would make this a $199 stock," Cramer said. "That's my target."
