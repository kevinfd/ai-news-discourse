# Elon Musk says former baristas and shelf-stockers would make good SpaceX data center workers

Business Insider · Brent D. Griffiths, Brent D. Griffiths (bgriffiths@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-23

SpaceX CEO Elon Musk said some of the company's "best" data center construction workers come from other backgrounds. Finn Gomez/Getty Images SpaceX CEO Elon Musk said his team has found a way to help fuel its big data center bet. Musk said SpaceX has made it possible for people with no direct experience to join its construction army. He said some of the "best" people have never worked on data centers before. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk says the company's best data center construction workers could be former baristas or shelf-stockers. "Those are some of our best people, and they've never done data centers before," Musk said earlier this week during a remote appearance at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin. Musk said that SpaceX has assembled an internal team, which he called "ArmyX," that has broken down all the jobs needed to construct data centers into smaller tasks "that are easily trainable in a few days." "You don't need people that have, say, been doing networking cables for their whole life," he said. "You divide the tasks into things that can be where you can train someone new who may have been a Starbucks barista or stocking shelves at Target." The billionaire praised the skills and work ethic that retail and fast-food workers could bring to data center construction. Musk specifically called attention to the skills of a former In-N-Out cook. "Let me tell you, if you get some aces out of In-N-Out who've been making epic burgers all day long perfectly, they're going to do a damn good job of network cables," he said. Musk says this team is powering SpaceX's expansion of its massive Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 data centers in Memphis. During SpaceX's most recent earnings call, Musk said that he "would be surprised" if the company added less than 13 gigawatts of compute by the end of 2027. Like the rest of the industry, SpaceX has a lot riding on the buildout. According to its latest earnings report, the company is spending more than ever on AI — and that could soon double. Business Insider previously reported that SpaceX offered employees a 20% pay increase if they volunteered to work six weeks in Memphis or Southaven, Mississippi, where another large data center is located. "These days when I go to Memphis, which I go to Memphis pretty frequently, it's becoming a well-oiled machine where it's just getting done," Musk said. "And we run a twenty-four-seven operation. So I can walk the site at one or two in the morning and see more or less the same productivity as I would the next morning at 10 AM, seven days a week." Read the original article on Business Insider
