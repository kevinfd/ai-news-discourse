# Amazon signs $7.5 billion deal for Israeli AI startup Decart's technology and team

CTech · Sophie Shulman, Golan Hazani · 2026-11-23

Amazon signs $7.5 billion deal for Israeli AI startup Decart's technology and team
The agreement covers the proprietary technology behind Decart's Optimization Stack and brings its three co-founders and engineering team to Amazon. The deal follows the collapse of earlier acquisition negotiations with Anthropic and Nvidia, and is aimed at strengthening Amazon's AI chips against Nvidia's dominance.
Amazon has signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind the Israeli artificial intelligence startup Decart's Optimization Stack (DOS), in a transaction valued at $7.5 billion, Calcalist has learned. As part of the agreement, Amazon will also bring Decart's three co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, together with their team of AI researchers and engineers, into the company. The deal was signed last night and announced by Amazon today. The consideration will be paid through a combination of cash and stock, with payments structured in several installments.
The agreement brings to an end a months-long acquisition saga that began on August 10, when Calcalist first reported that Decart was in talks regarding a potential sale. The startup, considered one of the most prominent AI companies to emerge from Israel, attracted interest from some of the world's largest technology companies, including Nvidia and Anthropic, before Amazon emerged as its eventual buyer.
The first serious acquisition discussions were with Nvidia, which is also an investor in Decart and had sought to acquire the company. However, Decart's founders preferred a potential deal with Anthropic, the developer of the Claude chatbot, which subsequently entered advanced negotiations to purchase the startup for approximately $6 billion. The proposed transaction was seen as a possible entry point for Anthropic into Israel, ahead of its planned Wall Street IPO.
The Anthropic negotiations advanced significantly, with the two sides exchanging draft agreements and Anthropic effectively completing its due diligence. However, the company withdrew from the transaction in early September, bringing the proposed acquisition to an abrupt end. The reasons for its decision were not publicly established.
Decart then attempted to revive acquisition discussions with Nvidia, but the chipmaker declined to reopen negotiations. Amazon subsequently emerged as the next potential buyer and agreed to the transaction.
Calcalist reported last week that the Amazon deal was close to being signed, with consideration exceeding $7 billion and potentially reaching nearly $7.5 billion. The proposed structure included cash, stock and deferred payments, with part of the consideration tied to the company's performance. The founders also agreed to remain with Amazon for five years, with a significant portion of the payment scheduled for the fifth year.
Amazon said its agreement covers the proprietary technology behind Decart's Optimization Stack, as well as the recruitment of the company's founders and its team of AI researchers and engineers. The company said the technology will help it improve the performance of its custom chips and AI inference, allowing customers to train and run AI model faster and at lower cost.
The companies began working together in 2025, with Decart helping Amazon optimize the performance of its Trainium AI chips through an approach that combines software and hardware improvements. Amazon said the collaboration demonstrated the potential of Decart's full-stack optimization technology and helped establish the basis for the agreement.
"Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," said Peter DeSantis, Amazon's senior vice president of foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing. "More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost."
Dean Leitersdorf, Decart's co-founder and CEO, said joining Amazon would give the team access to greater scale, customers and infrastructure. "Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own. We're looking forward to exploring what we can build together," he said.
Founded in 2023, Decart initially gained attention for its real-time video-generation technology, including Oasis, an interactive model capable of generating virtual environments, and Lucy, which can edit video in real time. The company subsequently expanded its focus to AI infrastructure and software optimization, an increasingly important area as the cost and availability of computing power become major constraints on the industry's growth.
The company's technology is designed to improve the efficiency of AI computing by optimizing how software interacts with hardware. This is particularly important for Amazon, which is investing heavily in its own chip portfolio, including Trainium for AI training and inference. By bringing Decart's technology and engineering team in-house, Amazon aims to make its custom processors more competitive with Nvidia's products and more attractive to developers and businesses running AI workloads.
In May, Decart raised $300 million at a valuation of approximately $4 billion, in a round that included participation from Nvidia. The company has raised more than $450 million in total and employs approximately 100 people. A transaction valued at $7.5 billion would represent a substantial increase over its last reported valuation.
Completion of the transaction is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance.
