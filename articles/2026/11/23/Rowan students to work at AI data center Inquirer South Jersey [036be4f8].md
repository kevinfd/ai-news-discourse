Under a new agreement, Rowan University and DataOne, the massive 2.4 million-square-foot data center in Vineland, would put students to work at the complex as early as next year.
According to DataOne’s CEO, the program is intended to educate young people about data centers and to build an artificial intelligence workforce.
Last week’s announcement from Rowan comes after controversy, such as the data center’s $1 million fine from the state over clean-air violations and a now-dismissed lawsuit from neighbors who shared quality-of-life concerns, particularly about noise.
Students will need to apply and interview for the roles. Those who are hired would begin working in the summer before senior year and continue into the fall semester. The initial cohort of three or four workers are expected to be onboarded by April, the CEO told the Inquirer.
Reporter Sarah Nicell has more details about the partnership.
Before algorithms and doomscrolling, there was Myspace.
Myspace, debuting in 2003 and peaking in 2007, was once the go-to place to find new bands, comedians, and community. At its height, it had 300 million users.
Tommy Avallone’s latest documentary, Myspace, dives into the story of the old social media platform and highlights its pivotal role in pop culture. It debuted earlier this month.
“In our doc, we tried to show how Myspace was sort of the origin of everything we know,” Avallone recently told The Inquirer while sitting inside a coffee shop in Haddon Heights.
In Avallone’s personal life, Myspace was where he first began talking to his wife and had conversations with actors. He credits the social media platform for helping him become who he is.
The Inquirer’s Jason Nark has the story.
