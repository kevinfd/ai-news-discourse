# Generative AI Practical Training for Managers: A video training course covering how to delegate to AI, review deliverables, and develop subordinates

Generative AI Practical Training for Managers: A video training course covering how to delegate to AI, review deliverables, and develop subordinates
In the course of receiving inquiries about generative AI training, I often hear concerns like these.
“I want my subordinates to use AI, but I don't know which tasks to delegate.”
“I receive documents created by AI, but I don't know what to check.”
“A gap has emerged between subordinates who master AI and those who use it as-is.”
As more people use generative AI, the judgment of managers is increasingly being tested.
Which tasks to use it for, how to verify quality, and how to develop subordinates.
Therefore, following our previous “Generative AI training covering everything from analysis to document creation”, we have created 
video training for managers.
This time, we focused specifically on the following three points.
- Conduct training in-house at any time (individual/managerial team)
- Experience the full range of managerial tasks—selecting, delegating, verifying, developing, and testing—through exercises
- Learn using high-quality materials incorporating a strategic consulting perspective
1. Conduct training in-house at any time
For managers, it is not easy to coordinate everyone's schedule for group training. It is also a burden to hold the same training every time a new manager joins.
This training combines videos, handouts, and exercises, designed to be conducted in-house. You can pause the video to think, and go back to check parts you don't understand. You can proceed at your own pace.
The estimated time required is approximately 120 minutes, including exercises and breaks.
About half of that time is spent on exercises.
We aimed for content that can be used for individual study, as well as in section manager meetings or study groups for managers.

2. From “using it yourself” to “achieving results as a team”
General generative AI training focuses on mastering AI for personal use.
On the other hand, what is required of managers, in addition to using it themselves, is managing the team's AI utilization. In this training, you will work on the following five exercises as the manager of Sales Section 1 at the fictional company “ABC Solutions”.
- Exercise 1: Task Selection: Evaluate four candidate tasks based on risk conditions and three evaluation axes (expected effect, ease of verification, and ease of execution), and select one task to try AI on first.
- Exercise 2: Delegating to Subordinates: Instead of saying “Use AI to make something good,” create a request document covering five items: purpose, reader, conditions, deliverables, and completion criteria.
- Exercise 3: Reviewing Deliverables: Identifying numerical errors, baseless root cause analyses, and illogical policy proposals from AI-generated reports that sound plausible.
- Exercise 4: Providing Feedback to Subordinates: Thinking through what to say and how to say it during a meeting with a subordinate who has adopted AI analysis without modification.
- Exercise 5: Trial Plan for Your Team: Deciding on target tasks, role assignments, and evaluation criteria to create a plan to test over 2 to 4 weeks.
The goal is not for managers to become skilled at writing prompts. We aim to reach a state where you can grasp what your subordinates are delegating to AI and how they are verifying it, allowing you to take responsibility for the quality of the deliverables.
3. Learning the mindset for decision-making through high-quality content
AI output is natural in its phrasing and well-formatted in appearance.
Because of this, it becomes harder to notice errors.
For example, in Exercise 3, we handle an AI report that takes the correct figure of 'a 12.5% increase in sales compared to the previous month' and makes leaps in logic such as 'sales reform was a success,' 'the decline in unit price was caused by discounting,' and 'discounting should be completely banned.'
Even if the numbers are correct, the conclusion is not necessarily correct.
This training material incorporates perspectives cultivated through work in strategic consulting.
- Confirm in the order of conclusion, numbers, and evidence
- Think by separating facts, hypotheses, and judgments
- Create materials that allow the reader to understand what they should decide next
- Measure effectiveness not by the speed of drafting, but by the total work time including verification and correction, and by quality
The content is aimed at improving the quality of your judgment as a manager, rather than the operation of AI.
5 practical skills you can acquire in this training
- Selection Ability: Judging which tasks should use AI
- Instruction Ability: Communicating objectives, conditions, and completion criteria to subordinates
- Review Ability: Verifying the numbers, evidence, and logic of AI deliverables
- Development Ability: Enhancing the AI utilization and judgment skills of subordinates
- Execution Ability: Creating a trial plan for AI utilization within your own team
At the end of the training, we aim for you to have in hand the tasks to be tested by your team and a concrete trial plan.

Frequently Asked Questions
Q. Who is this for?
A. It is intended for those in positions such as managers, department heads, and team leaders who delegate work to subordinates and review their deliverables. It is especially recommended for those who say, “I am encouraging my subordinates to use AI, but I am not confident in how to delegate or how to check their work.” While we use sales-related cases, the concepts can be applied to any job function.
Q. Can I take this course if I am not knowledgeable about generative AI myself?
A. Yes. We focus more on management decisions—such as how to select tasks, how to delegate, and how to verify work—rather than prompt engineering techniques. You will actually use AI in Exercise 2, but if you do not have an environment available, you can proceed by comparing your work with the prompt examples provided in the materials.
Q. How is this different from the previous generative AI training?
A. The previous training was content for individual contributors to use AI themselves, from analysis to creating report slides. This course is about how to delegate, verify, and develop subordinates' AI usage as a manager. Using both within a team allows supervisors and subordinates to share the same perspective for verification.
Q. Which generative AI should I use?
A. We do not limit this to any specific service. Please use the generative AI approved by your company or organization.
Q. Do I need to prepare actual internal data?
A. No. We use fictional training data for the exercises. Please check your organization's rules if you intend to input actual customer information or internal data.
Q. How long are the handouts?
A. The PDF is 45 pages long. It includes evaluation sheets for task selection, examples of request messages and prompts, mock AI reports for review exercises, trial planning sheets, and comprehension checks.
Q. Can this be used by multiple people for internal management training?
A. Yes. You are welcome to watch and use it together as a team or in internal study groups. However, please refrain from reselling or making it publicly available to external parties.
Q. Can you also provide training or reviews tailored to our company's specific operations?
A. Yes. We accept individual consultations for creating trial plans for your company's operations, having instructors facilitate sessions, and reviewing trial results or deliverables. Please contact us using the inquiry information provided above.
