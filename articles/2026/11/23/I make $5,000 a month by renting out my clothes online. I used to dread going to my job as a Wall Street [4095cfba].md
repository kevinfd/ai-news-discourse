# I make $5,000 a month by renting out my clothes online. I used to dread going to my job as a Wall Street trader — now I have flexibility.

Business Insider · Jamie Phillis, Jamie Phillis (insider@insider.com) · 2026-11-23

Wen Song. Courtesy of Wen Song Former Wall Street trader Wen Song earns $5,000 a month by renting out her clothes on the Pickle app. She has 460 pieces listed and uses AI trend research and low prices to stay a Star Lender on Pickle. Song is also a private chef and does a lot of her clothing repairs and maintenance herself. This as-told-to essay is based on a conversation with Wen Song, a 35-year-old private chef who also rents out her clothing on Pickle in Boston. It's been edited for length and clarity. I've always had a passion for fashion, and I wanted to be a fashion designer growing up. After I graduated from college, I interviewed with a couple of fashion companies, but I was ultimately hired by a hedge fund to become a derivatives trader. After seven years in finance, I burned out and decided to try entrepreneurship. Now, in addition to working as a personal chef, I rent out my closet on the Pickle app . I started renting my closet on Pickle in May 2025 It started last May, when I read an article about people renting out their clothes to make money. I've always had a great collection of dresses, so I thought, why not give it a shot? I started by putting up a couple of pieces on the Pickle app, and I got a rental for one of the dresses right away. It was a unique, Gatsby-style fringe dress with sequins. The process felt so quick and easy, and the app was user-friendly. It took me a couple of months to really get comfortable, and a few days were a bit tough, dealing with things like shipping and customer service, but by spring 2026, my volume started to increase, and I became a Star Lender on Pickle. Pricing my items competitively against other lenders on Pickle has been key. Because customers outside Boston have to pay for shipping, I'll usually price my items an average of 20% to 30% lower than similar items in other major cities to generate volume. By April 2026, I was consistently making $5,000 a month Now, I have about 460 items listed. Once I started making money on the app, I decided to invest in more pieces. At first, all of my pieces on Pickle were items I wore myself, but I've recently started buying multiples of my most successful pieces, which rent out almost every week. I've also added some more diversity in the sizing I offer. As my business has grown, I've started building relationships with customers. I now have regulars who trust me to rent to them — the positive, grateful ones keep me going. Throughout the process, I've made shipping mistakes, dresses have been completely destroyed, and I've wasted time trying to help difficult customers, but I take accountability whenever I can, offer discounts if something goes wrong, and continue to try to create a positive experience. With that approach, I've been able to maintain a 5.0 rating. I keep up with the trends I try to stay ahead of trends and make sure I have key pieces and the most viral dresses. I have AI help me do the research, but the Pickle app is helpful as well. I browse the app to see which styles and listings seem to be popular. I spend about two to three hours a day researching and shopping — it feels like it did back when I was an options trader looking at 20 screens all day. While dresses are my most popular items, especially from Retrofete and Cult Gaia, designer shoe rentals are also popular. I'm also expanding to offer more winter coats and designer purses. I'd love for Pickle to offer more tech-forward features, like a chatbot that could help answer common renter questions about fit, sizing, and shipping. I'd also be excited to see an AI-powered virtual try-on feature so renters can get a sense of how a piece will look on them before they rent it. I used to sell my clothes before I started renting them out I used to use platforms like Poshmark , Depop, and eBay to sell old things I no longer wore, but I quickly realized that renting is much more profitable. I'm in a group chat with other top lenders, and they've found that to be true as well. I connected with them on Pickle's Looking For forum. There are now about 70 girls in the chat, and it's a really cool community. We share our experiences and talk about issues or questions that come up during the rental process. I have so many passions in life I wake up, and there are so many things I want to do. Renting through Pickle lets me pursue something connected to fashion while also traveling, investing, and staying fit. When I worked on Wall Street, I was always waiting for Friday to come. I'd wake up at 7 a.m. dreading going to work. I knew I needed to change my life and try entrepreneurship. The first thing I started was a private chef business after attending culinary school at Le Cordon Bleu Paris . I've also considered becoming a VIP concierge because of my travel experience. I'm open to seeing what happens in the future. Even with setting my own schedule, I'm very busy During a typical week, I cook maybe two days, usually on the weekend, and on those days I'm typically working anywhere from eight to twelve hours. Then I have weekdays to focus on Pickle, which works well because that's when everyone is renting clothes for the weekend. On weekdays, I ship items and spend at least a couple of hours packing, cleaning, and repairing them. I'm at the tailor almost once a week, but there are also things I've learned to do myself. I'm a good seamstress and taught myself how to dry-clean most pieces. A Pickle shipment. Courtesy of Weng Song If you don't have those skills, those maintenance expenses add up and fall on the owner of the clothes. Pickle can help cover eligible damage or loss, but lenders must document an item's condition before renting it out and promptly report any damage. I love the flexibility My schedule always changes, and there are times I'm cooking five days a week or want to travel, so I have the flexibility to pause my closet on Pickle. I've found that it doesn't necessarily have to be a full-time business as long as I'm responsive. For both of my businesses, I'm looking to bring on help and find ways to expand. I hope to become a mom one day, and I think even then I'll still be renting because the business allows me to multitask and work on my own time, which has been so valuable to me.
