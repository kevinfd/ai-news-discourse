# 📈 Monday data: Turbulence is getting weirder, and nobody knows why

Exponential View · Hannah Petrovic · 2026-11-23

Hello, Hannah Petrovic here. I was on a long-haul red-eye flight last week that hit turbulence so bad it was hard to sleep. Being a scientist, I started looking into turbulence data to understand what it is and how it’s changed over time.
What surprised me was where conditions favored clear-air turbulence, the kind of turbulence that often forms near jet streams and that ordinary onboard weather radar can’t detect. It accounted for 29% of turbulence-related accidents on US airlines between 2009 and 20181. From August through October this year, the pattern departed further from the long-term trend than in any other year I examined.
