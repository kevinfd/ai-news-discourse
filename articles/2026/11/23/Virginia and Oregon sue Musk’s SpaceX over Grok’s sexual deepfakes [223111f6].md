# Virginia and Oregon sue Musk’s SpaceX over Grok’s sexual deepfakes

thenextweb.com · Ana-Maria Stanciuc · 2026-11-23

Virginia and Oregon are taking Elon Musk’s SpaceX to court over Grok, as the two states filed separate but coordinated lawsuits on Monday.
They accuse the company of profiting from an AI feature that makes sexual images of real people, children included, without their consent.
The suits were announced by attorneys general Jay Jones and Dan Rayfield, and they are the first brought by US states over the issue, though others have been digging into Grok for months.
The complaints say SpaceX misled people about Grok’s safety and pushed its so-called spicy mode without proper guardrails.
That mode, the states allege, lets a user take an existing photo of a person and turn it into a sexual or nude image or video.
SpaceX allowed Grok and the X platform to create, host, and share unacceptable and dangerous content—and in fact knowingly designed a product without the obviously necessary safeguards in place, said Attorney General Rayfield.
The company on the receiving end is SpaceX Super Intelligence Corp, the Musk structure that now houses the X platform, the xAI lab, and Grok itself. The suit was no surprise to the company, which told investors in an October securities filing that Virginia was weighing legal action.
Time and again, xAI and X told consumers they were being protected from abuses. That was false. Instead, the tool permitted users to routinely create and circulate non-consensual intimate imagery and sexualized images, and child sexual abuse material (CSAM), causing harm to individuals and children, added Attorney General Jones.
Pressure on Grok has built all year, with California opening an investigation in January into sexual images made with the model without consent. Plaintiffs in a class action also say its tools were used to turn their childhood photos into abuse material.
The suits also land amid a wider reckoning, as a surge of nonconsensual deepfake videos has hit public figures and private citizens alike.
The two states want accountability for the harm and new protections for consumers, and they have brought in outside firepower.
Both have hired the law firm of former New Jersey Attorney General Matthew Platkin to help run the cases.
