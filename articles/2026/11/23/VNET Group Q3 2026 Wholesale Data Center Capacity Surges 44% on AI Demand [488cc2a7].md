# VNET Group Q3 2026: Wholesale Data Center Capacity Surges 44% on AI Demand

24/7 Wall St. · 2026-11-23

VNET Group Q3 2026: Wholesale Data Center Capacity Surges 44% on AI Demand
As seen on the 24/7 Wall St. homepage on November 23, 2026.
AI demand is showing up in the capacity numbers: wholesale data center capacity in service jumped 44% year over year to 1,127MW, plus a fresh 60MW order from a major cloud provider. The loss per ADS beat expectations, full-year revenue and adjusted EBITDA guidance was reaffirmed, and the board authorized a $50 million ADS buyback against a $611 million market cap.

VNET Group posted a GAAP loss per ADS of $0.04 for the third quarter of 2026, beating expectations. Part of that came from a fair-value gain on financial instruments that meaningfully narrowed the net loss attributable to VNET to $12.44 million, compared with $45.97 million in the same period a year earlier.
VNET reported $443.66 million in total net revenues, missing expectations, though the figure still represented 15.4% growth year over year. Wholesale IDC was the strongest segment, with revenues up 27.6% year over year and reaching 41.1% of total net revenues, while retail IDC grew a more modest 5.1%.
Wholesale data center capacity in service jumped 44% year over year to 1,127MW, and the quarter ended with a fresh 60MW order from a leading cloud service provider, a concrete signal that AI-driven demand is translating into contracted business. Another 548MW of wholesale capacity was under construction at quarter end.

Despite the revenue miss, management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance, targeting revenues of $1.72 billion to $1.77 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $531.47 million to $561.41 million. The board also authorized a $50 million ADS repurchase program, a notable capital return commitment against a $611 million market cap.
Risks worth watching include gross margin compression from heavier depreciation as the rapid capacity build comes online, a wholesale utilization rate that slipped to 70.7% from 74.3% a year earlier as new capacity ramps up, and a significant debt load. Regulatory scrutiny around Chinese firms accessing Nvidia computing power abroad adds an additional layer of uncertainty the guidance does not fully address.
