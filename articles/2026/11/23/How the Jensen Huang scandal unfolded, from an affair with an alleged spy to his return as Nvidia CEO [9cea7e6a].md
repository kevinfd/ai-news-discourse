# How the Jensen Huang scandal unfolded, from an affair with an alleged spy to his return as Nvidia CEO

Business Insider · Natalie Musumeci,Brent D. Griffiths,Ana Altchek, Natalie Musumeci,Brent D. Griffiths,Ana Altchek (nmusumeci@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-23

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is back. Soeren Stache/picture alliance via Getty Images Nvidia's Jensen Huang has returned to his CEO post less than two weeks after resigning. On Monday, the board chair told employees Huang was back "after careful consideration." Here's a timeline of the key moments before and after Huang's reinstatement. Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia CEO after a whirlwind series of events. His resignation followed reports of more than one extramarital affair and a board investigation into concerns he might have been the subject of a foreign-intelligence effort. If you're having trouble keeping up, you're not alone. Here is a timeline of everything that happened leading to Huang's bombshell disclosure of infidelity, resignation, and his surprising return to the company: Spring 2026: Rubio's classified warning memo Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the drafting of a "highly classified" memo in the spring warning about the Nvidia CEO's relationship, The New York Times reported . The Times reported that the memo was based on electronic surveillance of a woman called Alice Huang . The memo was later shared among top Trump administration officials, according to The Times. May: Jensen Huang boards Air Force One Jensen Huang was not among the initial list of top executives set to join President Donald Trump for his May visit to China. News organizations pointed out the curious absence given that the Nvidia CEO had joined Trump on similar occasions. In a seemingly last-minute decision, Huang joined the trip when Air Force One made a refueling stop in Alaska. Trump published a Truth Social post calling out CNBC for reporting Huang's absence. "Jensen is currently on Air Force One and, unless I ask him to leave, which is highly unlikely, CNBC's reporting is incorrect or, as they say in politics, FAKE NEWS!" Trump wrote in the May 12 post. Jensen Huang's name was not on a list of business executives that were part of a US delegation that a White House official sent Business Insider at the time. The Nvidia CEO later told reporters that Trump "asked me to come," CNBC reported. October 29: Chinese spy allegations emerge Questions about Huang's relationship publicly came to light in October. On October 29, the Times reported that US officials had concluded that Alice Huang, or Huang Le, was "a Chinese intelligence asset" who "had developed a close relationship" with the Nvidia CEO. The Times article included photos of the pair at a party in June 2024, which the publication said had been posted on LinkedIn. The article also recounted that in May 2025, Alice Huang had waited behind the Nvidia CEO as he spoke to reporters after leaving a restaurant in Taipei. October 29: Nvidia calls meetings 'not unusual' Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo was quoted in the Times report as saying, "Jensen's interactions with Ms. Huang are not unusual or unexpected. Meetings with customers are part of a C.E.O.'s job; Jensen talks with thousands of customer executives every year." October 29: Jensen Huang downplays interactions The Nvidia CEO echoed the company's statements, downplaying his interactions with Alice Huang as routine business. "Our relationship, our interactions, have been related to Megaspeed, and it was all completely normal and not unusual," he told Bloomberg News in a report published October 29. Jensen Huang also told the publication that the US government hadn't told him about its concerns about Alice Huang, confirming another aspect of the Times reporting. In its initial story, the Times reported that the Nvidia CEO had not received "defensive briefings," the term for such formal warnings sometimes communicated to business leaders. November 4: House GOP leaders seek briefing Three House Republican committee chairs sent a letter to Jensen Huang on November 4 asking him to appear for a joint closed-door meeting about his interactions with Alice Huang. "Recent reports raise significant national security concerns regarding the PRC's access to sensitive U.S. technology and information. The issue of PRC infiltration and access to cutting-edge U.S. technology is core to the investigative work of our three committees this Congress," the letter read. "It is vital that Congress determines the extent to which U.S. national security has been placed at risk." The letter, which described Alice Huang as a "publicly reported People's Republic of China (PRC) intelligence asset," asked for a November 18 meeting. November 8: Jensen Huang admits infidelity In a November 8 internal memo to employees, JensenHuang said he had been "unfaithful" to his wife of more than 40 years and said that his actions "hurt her and our children." "I owe my family and all of you an apology," he wrote, adding, "There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry." November 8: Nvidia opens probe The same day, Nvidia's board of directors announced it was opening an investigation into whether Jensen Huang was the "target of a foreign intelligence effort." The board said a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the probe. "While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, the Board's inquiry will examine all available information," the board said. Jensen Huang said he supports the investigation and "will cooperate fully." November 8: Trump defends Jensen Huang Later that day, Trump defended the Nvidia CEO , calling him an "honorable guy." "I think he probably has an excuse for it. You have to hear it," Trump told reporters. The president also said he thought Jensen Huang should remain Nvidia's CEO. "He's done an amazing job for our country," Trump said. November 9: Nvidia CEO off tech events lineups In the wake of the scandal, Huang didn't attend an Anthropic-hosted private CEO forum in Montecito, California. Huang was also removed from the lineup at the ElevenLabs Summit in NYC later that week, a spokesperson for the AI voice technology startup confirmed to Business Insider. He was scheduled for a fireside chat with ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski. November 9: China denies allegations The Chinese government has pushed back against claims that Alice Huang is a spy. "The 'Chinese spy' allegation is purely unsubstantiated and directed at smearing China," Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun told reporters during a news conference on November 9. November 11: Report emerges about a second affair The New York Times published a report saying that Huang was involved in a second extramarital affair with Nvidia vice president of healthcare, Kimberly Powell . The 48-year-old VP was a direct report of Huang, and the two worked together for over 18 years. The report cited five company sources who confirmed the affair. The two were said to have been "captured on video being affectionate" at an Nvidia conference hotel in San Jose in March. November 12: Huang is out; Colette Kress becomes interim CEO On November 12, Nvidia announced that Huang had resigned as CEO. In a memo announcing his resignation, Nvidia said that Colette Kress, the company's chief financial officer, would serve as interim CEO "until a permanent successor has been named." Kress joined Nvidia in 2013 and has served as executive vice president and CFO since. Prior to joining Nvidia, she spent nearly 25 years in finance roles at companies including Cisco and Microsoft. The board said in the memo that it would consider internal and external candidates as it initiated a search for a permanent chief. November 12: Nvidia stock plunges Nvidia's share price dropped 13% at market open following Huang's resignation, deepening the decline  it had seen since the scandal came to light. By late morning on November 12, the share price had partially recovered, trading about 5% below the market close the day before. November 14: A petition to reinstate Huang gains traction A petition to reinstate Huang as Nvidia's CEO received over 100,000 signatures as of November 14, including from employees, shareholders, and prominent tech leaders such as Replit CEO Amjad Masad. November 16: President Trump backs Huang's return Trump told reporters outside the White House that he would support Huang's return to Nvidia. He said Huang "learned a big lesson" and that he wouldn't mind if Huang were reinstated. Other tech executives, including Elon Musk, similarly called for his return. November 17: Huang says he would be 'honored' to return as CEO On November 17, Huang broke his silence with a post on X, thanking those who had shown kindness to him and his family, and saying he loves the US and Nvidia. He admitted to making "serious mistakes" in his personal life and said he was committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation. In the post, Huang also shared his interest in returning to lead the company. "If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO," Huang wrote . November 17: Nvidia chair says it doesn't intend to reinstate Huang Nvidia reported blockbuster third-quarter earnings, beating on both top and bottom lines. Nvidia chair Harvey Jones said during the company's call with investors that the board "unanimously accepted" Huang's resignation and "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." November 23: Huang is reinstated as CEO On Monday, Jensen Huang was reinstated as CEO and Nvidia's stock soared more than 30%. The chipmaker's board chair told employees in a brief message that Huang was returning "after careful consideration." Jones said he would remain board chair and Kress would return to her role as CFO. Hundreds of Nvidia employees reacted to the message with celebratory emojis. President Trump said he worked behind the scenes to help secure Huang's return, speaking to the board both directly and indirectly, and felt "very strongly about it." "I was talking to a lot of people" about Huang's return, Trump told CNBC. "The board decided to do what we wanted them to do." Huang returned to Nvidia's headquarters in Santa Clara on Monday and was greeted by cheers and chants from employees. "I'm gonna be a better CEO the second time around," he said , according to a person in attendance. Read the original article on Business Insider
