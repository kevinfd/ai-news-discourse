# Trump Says He 'Convinced' Nvidia to Reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-23

President Trump is taking credit for the shocking reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia’s CEO. In an interview with CNBC, Trump said he “convinced” Nvidia’s board of directors to make the sudden reversal. "I spoke to them, let me just put it that way, and I convinced them,” he said.
“And I must tell you, it was a lot easier to convince them yesterday than it was a week and a half ago,” he added, alluding to Nvidia’s stock value plummeting after Huang resigned due to reported extramarital affairs.
“When the stock starts dropping 40-50%, it was a lot easier for me to do it yesterday than it would have been,” he said. Trump also indicated that without his intervention, Huang wouldn’t have been able to return. “I believe my intervention was very important.”

The comments explain more about why Nvidia’s board abruptly reappointed Huang this morning, even though days earlier it said it “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” despite a mounting campaign calling for his return.
According to Semafor, Trump participated in a call with Nvidia’s board on Sunday night. Monday morning's announcement from Nvidia then said "senior levels" of the US government made the board “confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.” Trump also told CNBC that Huang exhibited “bad judgment,” but that “he did nothing wrong,” even though Huang had a romantic relationship with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.
“Investors should be very happy,” Trump said after news of Huang's return caused Nvidia’s stock to soar. Last year, Trump owned at least $12 million in Nvidia stock.
'Profoundly Wrong'
Trump’s role in Huang’s reinstatement is already facing bipartisan criticism given concerns that Nvidia was trying to bypass US export controls to sell sensitive AI chips to China. “There is something profoundly wrong about Jensen Huang having a relationship with a Chinese Communist Spy and helping China get a huge number of very advanced computer chips when that was specifically against the policy of the United States Government, and now he is being reinstated,” former Republican House Speaker Newt Gingrich tweeted.
US Sen. Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) also questioned Huang's return, tweeting: "Jensen Huang had multiple affairs, including with subordinates and an alleged Chinese spy. His own board described his behavior as lacking 'candor.' I’ve served on a public company board. His behavior does not support reinstatement."
Last week, Ricketts introduced the MEGA Act, which would require the Commerce Department to consider placing Megaspeed International, the company that employed Alice Huang, the alleged Chinese spy, on the US Entity List. "This is a matter of national security," he said.
Meanwhile, Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) also argued: “Jensen Huang’s reinstatement shows that under Trump, [Vice President] Vance, and [Secretary of State] Rubio, our national security is for sale. We will all pay the price for letting China plunder America's cutting-edge technology.”
But others, including SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, approve of Huang's return. Still, Nvidia's CEO is bound to face more scrutiny and potential investigation. The company itself is investigating China's alleged attempt to spy on him. Huang is also slated to testify before Congress starting on Dec. 1 over the company’s AI chip sales to China.
"Nvidia’s decision today doesn’t excuse Jensen Huang from testifying before Congress," Sen. Ricketts tweeted. "It also doesn’t absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips. I look forward to Mr. Huang coming to the Senate in December."
