# India’s AI funding nearly doubles to $1.6 billion, but a handful of deals drive the surge: Report

Fortune India · Tamanna Mohanty · 2026-11-23

India’s AI funding nearly doubles to $1.6 billion, but a handful of deals drive the surge: Report
India’s artificial intelligence (AI) funding has nearly doubled in 2026, but the sharp rise in investment has not necessarily translated into a wider pool of startups attracting capital. Instead, a handful of large funding rounds have accounted for most of the money flowing into the sector, even as the overall number of deals has declined.
Indian AI companies raised $1.6 billion in equity funding between January and September 2026, a 96% increase from the $822 million raised in the corresponding period last year, according to Tracxn’s latest report. The funding was also 162% higher than the $616 million raised in the first nine months of 2024, making it the highest total for any corresponding nine-month period since 2019.
The rise in funding has not translated into more deals for India's AI startups. In fact, the number of funding rounds fell 42% to 87 in the first nine months of 2026, from 149 a year ago. What changed was the size of the cheques. The average deal value climbed to $18.5 million from $5.5 million, indicating that investors are putting substantially more money into a smaller pool of companies.
A closer look at the deals shows just how concentrated the funding has been. The 10 largest rounds together brought in $1.5 billion, accounting for 90% of the total raised by Indian AI companies. Neysa, a Mumbai-based AI cloud provider, led the pack with its $600 million Series B in February. The round alone made up 37% of all AI funding during the period and contributed 76% of the increase in funding over the year-ago period. Sarvam, the Bengaluru-based generative AI startup, was another major recipient, raising $255 million in a Series B in June and a further $90.4 million in August. Yotta also secured $150 million in a Series D round in July.
The flip side of these large transactions is that fewer companies are getting funded. The number of startups receiving funding for the first time dropped 60% to 24, compared with 60 in the first nine months of 2025. Series A and later-stage deals also became fewer, falling 33% to 24 from 36.
Early-stage cheques get bigger, but seed funding loses ground
Early-stage investments were the biggest contributor to the funding increase. Startups in this category raised $1.3 billion during the nine-month period, compared with $434 million a year earlier. As a result, early-stage deals accounted for 78% of total AI funding, up from 53% in 9M 2025. But the increase in money did not mean more startups were receiving it. The number of early-stage rounds actually fell 38% to 21. The average deal size, meanwhile, shot up to $60.2 million from $12.8 million.
Late-stage funding saw a smaller increase, rising 4% to $280 million across three rounds. Seed-stage companies had a more difficult run, with funding falling 42% to $69.2 million. The number of seed rounds also dropped 44% to 63 from 113. The investor base also became narrower during the period. A total of 111 investors backed Indian AI companies, down 34% from 169 in the first nine months of 2025. First-time investors declined 39% to 43, while international investors fell 38% to 29.
Among early-stage investors, Peak XV Partners led with three investments, followed by Prosus and Khosla Ventures with two investments each. Antler, IvyCap Ventures and Finvolve made two seed investments each. SoftBank Vision Fund and Creaegis each backed one late-stage round. The geographical distribution of funding also saw a significant change. Mumbai overtook Bengaluru to become the largest destination for AI investments, raising $765 million and accounting for 48% of total funding. This marks a substantial jump from the $15.7 million raised by companies in the city during the corresponding period last year, when Mumbai accounted for just 2% of the total.
The increase was largely driven by Neysa and Yotta's large funding rounds. Bengaluru followed with $711 million, representing 44% of total funding, compared with a 54% share in the year-ago period. Sarvam and Emergent were among the major contributors to the city's funding activity. Chennai ranked third with $75.5 million, accounting for 5% of the total, largely on the back of Freehand's $75 million Series B round. Together, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Chennai accounted for 96% of all AI funding in the country.
In terms of business segments, enterprise applications companies raised $1.6 billion, compared with $780 million in the first nine months of 2025. Enterprise infrastructure funding increased to $362 million from $166 million, while semiconductor companies raised $32.8 million, against $16.8 million in the year-ago period.
On the exits front, Indian AI companies recorded six acquisitions, marginally higher than the five reported in the year-ago period. These included Thermax's $3.4 million acquisition of ExactSpace in February, while C2I Semiconductors, WeCP and Rampp.ai were among the other companies acquired during the period. No public listings were recorded, unchanged from the corresponding periods in 2025 and 2024.
Despite the rise in domestic funding, India continues to account for a small share of global AI investments. Worldwide, AI companies raised $383.5 billion during the first nine months of 2026, with India contributing just 0.4% and ranking ninth among countries. Globally, the period also saw 175 acquisitions and 26 public listings.
