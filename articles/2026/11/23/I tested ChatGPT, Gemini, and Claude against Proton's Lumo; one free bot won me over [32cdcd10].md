# I tested ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude against Proton's Lumo; one free bot won me over

Android Police · Marvin Velasco · 2026-11-23

I tend to jump from one AI bot to another. When I'm not happy with one bot's answer, I switch to the next for a second or third opinion. This is the life of a free tier user.
Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude have become three of my most commonly used apps, and not just in the AI category. However, their free tiers are plagued with limited daily usage and unclear lockout timers.
While looking for an alternative, I came across Lumo. It's a refreshingly basic AI bot built by the team behind Proton Mail and Proton VPN.
After running the same prompts across all four free AI bots, I realized Lumo is surprisingly potent. On top of that, it won me over with its clean user interface, transparent usage meter, and its straightforward answers.
Lumo's free tier holds up well
There's no urgency to subscribe
Lumo feels a lot like the initial release of ChatGPT. Lumo prioritizes a distraction-free, minimalist UI that strips down most feature bloat. At the same time, it's comprehensive for a free app.
Lumo gives users access to Proton Drive within the app, similar to how Gemini has Google Drive integration. If you log in using a Proton account, you get additional features, such as project creation and custom skills.
Unlike Gemini, Lumo gives free users access to all of its AI models: Apertus, Lumo Lite, and Lumo Max. The latter two have a choice of Fast mode for quick answers or Thinking mode for extended responses.
Apertus is an open source Swiss model for light tasks.
And unlike Claude, Lumo's free tier provides a usage bar presented in percentage. It's a great quality-of-life touch that clearly indicates how close I am to the usage limit.
Although Proton advertises Lumo as open source, it's not end-to-end. While the web and mobile client applications are open source, Lumo's backend and server infrastructure are proprietary.
Lumo routes queries across a mix of open-weight models, including Alibaba's Qwen and other China-based models. Proton hosts these models on its European servers, meaning no user data leaves its jurisdiction.
Proton claims it doesn't log or share user data for model training. This option is set by default, without the need to turn off model training in the settings or subscribe to a paid subscription.
Lumo Plus officially costs $13 per month, but I always see it discounted to $10 a month. It unlocks more bot usage, additional image generation, and unlimited projects and custom skills.
I compared Lumo against the Big Three
Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude had their work cut out for them
In the first comparison, I used each AI bot's free model to create a four-day itinerary for a family trip to Tokyo. The goal was to see how realistic each bot's plan would be.
Lumo matched Gemini and ChatGPT on pacing and practical logistics. All three bots considered how my four-year-old child would enjoy each attraction. They also suggested helpful tips between days or at the end of the response.
Claude's answer was overly basic. It didn't offer additional suggestions or go beyond what I requested. I couldn't use Claude's itinerary on its own.
The second prompt revolved around copy editing. I provided each free bot with a paragraph to improve grammatically.
All four bots gave pretty much the same corrected paragraph. However, Lumo and Claude gave the most comprehensive explanations. I appreciated how Lumo clarified each point.
It's impressive how Lumo can satisfy my needs while knowing less about me than the other three apps.
For the third prompt, I had the four AI bots do deep research into a complex topic. To make things fair, I didn't use extended thinking on any of the four apps.
ChatGPT gave the most comprehensive response. It went above and beyond what I asked for. Gemini was close behind with easily understandable explanations and helpful tables.
Claude's answer was overly simple once again, similar to the first prompt. Lumo's reply was satisfactory and left no holes in its explanation. This category also gave Lumo a chance to show off its neat text formatting.
I fed the four bots a 68-page PDF document to summarize. Each chatbot had to limit its response to only five bullet points with a total word count of 150.
Lumo delivered the most number-heavy answer. It prioritized formulas and the specific page numbers they referenced in each bullet point, which I appreciated.
Claude did a good job of maintaining the formulas while adding some context. Gemini and ChatGPT surprisingly took the simple route for their answers.
Lastly, I tasked each free bot with a basic image manipulation task. It was simple: remove the Firefox logo from the photo.
Gemini failed the test miserably, removing the entire graphic instead of only the logo. The other three bots understood the assignment and sent me back the image I requested.
Of the three correct responders, Lumo was the fastest, followed by ChatGPT. Claude took a while to process the request and didn't show the modified image directly inside the chat.
Lumo sets the standard for free tiers
Gemini and ChatGPT are tough to dethrone. Gemini has tight integration within my Google-centric ecosystem, and ChatGPT gives me the most insightful answers out of the four bots.
Lumo is similar to Claude: Its answers are fast, well-researched, and straight to the point. Lumo's advantage is its more generous free tier. It doesn't suddenly cut me off like Claude sometimes does, and its daily usage gauge is clearly presented.
When comparing their free tiers, Lumo is the most consistent performer. As shown in the five comparisons, Lumo either performed the best or was a close second.
Since installing Lumo, I've often defaulted to it for a first opinion, then used the rest to verify facts.
