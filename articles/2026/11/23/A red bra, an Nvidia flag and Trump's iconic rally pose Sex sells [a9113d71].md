# A red bra, an Nvidia flag and Trump's iconic rally pose: "Sex sells"

Axios · Josephine Walker · 2026-11-23

President Trump posted a doctored image of newly reinstated Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang holding a red bra in front of a Nvidia flag, posing as Trump after his viral assassination attempt at a rally in Pennsylvania in 2024. The post came shortly after the embattled Huang was reinstated Monday. Why it matters: The post highlights just how much the White House views Huang as an ally and how the president appears to see Huang's fall from grace and comeback as similar to his own history-making return, despite warnings about national security risks. Driving the news: Nvidia's board reinstated Huang early Monday, declaring him the right person to lead the company. After the announcement, Nvidia shares surged more than 30% by midday, lifting the broader stock market. The reinstatement followed reported pressure from the Trump administration and prompted accountability experts to question the board's judgment . What Nvidia's saying: "The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO," Trump wrote in a separate Truth Social post Monday. "The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen's return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security. The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct." "On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure. Separately, NVIDIA should continue its thorough internal investigation of the Company's compliance with U.S. export controls in order to ensure that it is fulfilling its legal obligations and Patriotic Duty to protect U.S. National Security," Trump wrote. Catch up quick: Huang was ousted after he admitted an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, who allegedly used her relationship with Huang to help funnel Nvidia chips to China. Reports also alleged he had affairs with at least two subordinates. What Trump's saying: Trump suggested in a CNBC interview Monday that Huang's sex scandal could ultimately boost his reputation. "Sex sells, fellas," Trump said, predicting that the controversy could be a "net positive" for the Nvidia CEO. Trump said he didn't think Huang would be diminished by the scandal and argued that people respected what he had gone through. Zoom out: Lawmakers on the Hill were immediately taken aback by the decision, as Nvidia's board had previously said it would not bring Huang back. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) warned that Democrats would investigate Huang and characterized his reinstatement as the president "putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security." Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) who will likely soon chair the House Oversight committee, questioned why Trump was congratulating Huang after the affairs, and asked why the White House was allegedly "putting China's interests first." "Is it just the tens and hundreds of millions of dollars they're making out in the open or does Huang have even more damning information on them? A real Congress will find out," he wrote. Nvidia did not immediately respond to Axios' request for comment. The White House referred Axios to the president's comments on Truth Social and CNBC .
