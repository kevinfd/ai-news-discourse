# Is Spotify still tracking your listening for Wrapped 2026? Heres what we know.

Mashable · Crystal Bell · 2026-11-23

Is Spotify still tracking your listening for Wrapped 2026? Here's what we know.
Every year, as Spotify Wrapped season approaches, a familiar kind of panic sets in. You start mentally calculating how many times you've streamed your favorite artist, wondering whether that one song you played obsessively in Feb. will come back to haunt you, and perhaps making a last-minute attempt to influence your results.
Recently, someone asked me who I thought my top artist would be this year, and for the first time in a long time, I genuinely had no idea. Stray Kids have held that spot for the past four years, but with fewer new releases from the group in 2026, my listening habits have been a little more unpredictable. I've spent the year bouncing between artists and genres, which means my top five could be anyone's guess. (And yes, I'm a little nervous about that.)
After all, Spotify Wrapped isn't just a recap of what you listened to. It's become a public declaration of your musical identity, one that inevitably ends up on Instagram Stories for everyone to judge.

But with Nov. nearly over and Spotify Wrapped 2026 expected to arrive in early Dec., there's one important question: Is Spotify still tracking your listening habits?
The answer is a little complicated.
When does Spotify Wrapped 2026 stop tracking your music?
Spotify has not confirmed the exact cutoff date for Wrapped 2026. However, based on how the company has handled previous editions, your listening data likely stopped counting sometime in mid-Nov.
For years, a persistent rumor suggested that Spotify stopped collecting data for Wrapped on Oct. 31, leaving the final two months of the year out of your annual recap. But that's not quite how it works.
In 2025, Spotify confirmed that Wrapped included listening activity from Jan. 1 through mid-Nov., just a few weeks before its Dec. 3 release. The company explained that it needs time to process listening data and prepare millions of personalized recaps ahead of launch.
So while Spotify continues recording your listening activity for its regular features and recommendations, that doesn't necessarily mean your late-Nov. streams will count toward Wrapped 2026.
Until Spotify confirms this year's tracking window, there's no way to know for certain.
Can you still change your Spotify Wrapped 2026 results?
If you're hoping to push a particular artist into your top five before Wrapped arrives, you may already be out of luck.
Even if Spotify is still counting streams, a few days of obsessive listening probably won't dramatically change the habits you've accumulated over the past 11 months.
But there's an important distinction between how Spotify calculates your top songs and top artists. According to Spotify's explanation of its Wrapped methodology, your top songs are ranked by the number of times you've listened to them, with each play counting after more than 30 seconds. Your top artists, meanwhile, are determined using a weighted stream count that gives primary artists more credit than featured performers.
That means an artist whose entire discography you've been listening to all year could rank higher than someone responsible for your single most-played song.
In other words, your Wrapped results aren't necessarily as straightforward as counting how many times you've played your favorite track.
Can you hide songs from Spotify Wrapped?
Maybe you're less concerned about getting your favorite artist to No. 1 and more worried about the songs you don't want showing up in your results. Fortunately, Spotify gives users some control over what influences their Wrapped.
In a Nov. 5 announcement, Spotify reminded listeners that they can exclude individual tracks and playlists from their Taste Profile, preventing those streams from influencing certain Wrapped results and everyday recommendations.
To exclude a song or playlist:
- Open Spotify and find the track or playlist.
- Tap the three-dot menu.
- Select "Exclude from your taste profile." (You can reverse the decision by selecting "Include in your taste profile.")
Spotify says this setting applies to past and future listening, meaning you can remove a particularly embarrassing musical obsession from your taste-based results even after you've spent hours streaming it.
There's also a dedicated setting for kids' and family music, which is especially useful for parents who don't want their children's favorite songs dominating their year-end recap.
One caveat: Excluded listening can still contribute to your total minutes listened. It just won't shape your taste-based rankings.
When will Spotify Wrapped 2026 come out?
Spotify hasn't announced an official release date, but Wrapped typically arrives in late Nov. or early Dec.
Last year's edition launched on Dec. 3, while the 2024 and 2023 editions arrived on Dec. 4 and Nov. 29, respectively. If Spotify follows its recent pattern of Wednesday releases, Dec. 2, 2026, is a possibility, though the company has yet to confirm that date.
Until then, you can keep listening to whatever you want. Whether those streams make it into your Wrapped is another question entirely.
And perhaps that's for the best. The fun of Spotify Wrapped has always been discovering what your listening habits reveal about you, even when the results aren't quite what you expected.
