# Anthropic IPO: Tracing an ‘unusual’ AI company’s race to the top

The Hindu · Pon Vasanth B A, Sambavi Parthasarathy · 2026-11-23

Minutes before Anthropic, the maker of Claude, began trading on Nasdaq on November 19, its co-founder and president Daniela Amodei opened her short speech by calling it an “unusual” company, one many even call “weird”.
Founded in May 2021 by seven former OpenAI employees who left over what they saw as a culture that did not put AI safety and alignment first, Anthropic is registered as a Public Benefit Corporation (PBC), a structure that legally binds the company to weigh public benefit alongside profit.
Its CEO Dario Amodei has consistently warned about the existential risks of misaligned AI models and pushed for regulation and a slowdown in the race to build frontier models. Rivals and some critics, however, have called this posturing. The provocatively titled “MisAnthropic Statement”, released on the eve of the IPO and signed by a few prominent figures from different fields, said Anthropic was not a safe AI company and urged rejection of its “reckless attempt to amass power in the name of the greater good”.
The charts and timeline below trace the unconventional journey of a company seen as a harbinger of everything unusual unfolding in the AI zeitgeist.
Anthropic raised $81.3 billion despite listing during a market slump — with many beginning to wonder whether the ballooning investments in AI were a bubble — and against the backdrop of the Republican rout in the U.S. midterms and the Nvidia scandal. It broke the record for the largest-ever IPO, set by Elon Musk’s SpaceX just five months earlier in a more bullish market.
