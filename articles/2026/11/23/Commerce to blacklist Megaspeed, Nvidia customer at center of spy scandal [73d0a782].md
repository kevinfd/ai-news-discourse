# Commerce to blacklist Megaspeed, Nvidia customer at center of spy scandal

Business Insider · Insider Inc., Insider Inc. (insider@insider.com) · 2026-11-23

US Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick attends a welcome banquet for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation on November 18, 2026 China Pool/Getty Images The Commerce Department is preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Nvidia customer at the center of a chip-smuggling controversy. The update is expected to be published in the Federal Register on Monday, POLITICO reports. The news comes the same day Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive at Trump's urging. The Commerce Department is preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia customer at the center of a chip-smuggling controversy, after coming under withering bipartisan criticism for not taking the step months ago. The update is expected to be published in the Federal Register on Monday, according to two people with knowledge of the update, who were granted anonymity to discuss policy changes that have not been formally announced. The move was first reported by Semafor. Blacklisting Megaspeed would bar US companies from selling chips and other technology to the data center and cloud computing company without a government license. In addition to lawmakers of both parties, some in President Donald Trump's own Cabinet have publicly slammed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick for not previously adding the company to the department's "Entity List," despite U.S. intelligence warnings that it was a possible front for smuggling sensitive AI chip technology to China. "Treasury, War, USTR and State were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the Entity List for months," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a CNBC interview earlier this month. "But Commerce has the pen," he said. The news comes the same day Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive at Trump's urging. Huang resigned less than two weeks ago amid reports of an affair with a former Megaspeed executive whom US officials suspect of being a Chinese intelligence asset.
