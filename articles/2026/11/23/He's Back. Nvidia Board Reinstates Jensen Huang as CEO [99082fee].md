# He's Back. Nvidia Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-23

UPDATE: President Trump is taking credit for convincing Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang.
Original Story:
In a shocker, Nvidia’s board of directors is reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO, president, and a member of the board, less than two weeks after he resigned amid reports of his extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy.
Nvidia’s board announced this morning that it “voted unanimously” to reinstate Huang, despite saying it had no intention to do so in an earnings call last week.
“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” board chair Harvey Jones said in a statement.
The statement likely alludes to Nvidia’s stock, and perhaps to AI stocks in general, which have plummeted in recent weeks amid the scandal. In addition, some Nvidia employees, along with outside CEOs such as Elon Musk, were calling for Huang’s reinstatement, sparking questions about whether Nvidia might suffer some brain drain without his leadership.
Bringing back Huang will no doubt trigger a flood of scrutiny and concerns, but Nvidia says it's “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead Nvidia,” after holding a series of meetings and discussions with him. Allegedly, Huang lied to Nvidia’s board and kept them in the dark about his extramarital affairs, according to Wired.
The announcement added: “In the process of reaching its decision, the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.” On Truth Social, President Trump said having Huang return is the "correct and proper decision."
This comes even though Huang had an affair with a Chinese woman named Alice Huang, whom US intelligence suspects had contacts with China’s top spy agency, according to a Sunday report from The New York Times.
In the announcement, Huang himself said he was honored to return as CEO. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” he said. “I am also deeply thankful to our employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support. Above all, I am grateful for the love and support of my family. I can’t wait to get back to work.”
However, Nvidia’s board said its investigation into the alleged Chinese spying effort targeting Huang remains ongoing. “The Board reiterated that the committee’s investigation will proceed to conclusion. Mr. Huang fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it,” the announcement says.
Trump claims the US government "has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to National Security. The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the US Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct."
Huang is also slated to testify before Congress on Dec. 1 about Nvidia’s chip sales to China and his relationship with Alice Huang. A key concern is that Nvidia’s CEO may have been influenced, or may have deliberately sought to bypass US export controls, to sell sensitive AI chips to China.
