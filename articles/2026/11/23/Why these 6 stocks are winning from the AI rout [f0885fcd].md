# Why these 6 stocks are winning from the AI rout

Business Insider · Theron Mohamed, Theron Mohamed (tmohamed@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-23

Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway has been one of the winners from the tech rout. Johannes EISELE / AFP via Getty Images Investors have been swapping their high-flying AI stocks for more stable and predictable names. Walmart, Berkshire Hathaway, Chipotle, Clorox, Charter, and Tyson Foods have all risen strongly. There's been a shift toward companies with strong finances and reliable demand, one analyst said. Investors have traded excitement for security over the past month as AI stocks have become too hot to handle . The Nasdaq 100 has tumbled 14% over the past month, touching its lowest level since April at Friday's close, as Nvidia and Intel have plunged by around 40% and other tech stocks have stumbled. In contrast, stocks including Walmart, Berkshire Hathaway, Chipotle, Clorox, Charter, and Tyson Foods have all risen strongly in recent weeks. There's been a shift away from tech toward "stodgier areas" such as consumer staples, where "demand is less sensitive to the economic cycle and vagaries of consumer sentiment," Russ Mould, AJ Bell's investment director, told Business Insider by email. "Companies with fortress, net-cash balance sheets are also back in vogue," Mould continued, "as investors worry about whether some firms are overextending themselves " as they issue more and more debt and equity to fund investments. The AI sell-off has been fueled not only by rising concerns about overinvestment in AI infrastructure but also by worries that Democratic lawmakers will curb the data-center buildout and fears that stubborn inflation will drive interest rates higher . Those headwinds have spurred some investors to sell fast-growing but aggressively valued and volatile AI stocks, and buy cheaper, stable, and more predictable stocks instead. Investors back Berkshire and Chipotle Berkshire shares have jumped 14% since the end of September, hitting all-time highs last week. The conglomerate is widely considered a haven because of its vast cash reserves, prudent risk management, and highly diversified business. Led by legendary investor Warren Buffett from 1965 through 2025, Berkshire had $357 billion in cash and other liquid assets at the end of September — about 30% of its $1.2 trillion market capitalization. Walmart stock has rallied 23% over the same period, as investors wager the retail giant will do well whatever happens . It sells essentials such as groceries at low prices, which has historically made it resilient to economic downturns, and even a beneficiary if cash-strapped consumers trade down from pricier rivals. Clorox has surged 21% in the past month, as the seller of consumer staples is seen as a strong defensive pick . Investors worried about a sustained AI sell-off that hammers much of the market may be betting that people will keep buying cleaning products and trash bags even if their finances take a hit. Shares of Tyson Foods, the nation's largest meat supplier, have climbed 15% in the past month too. Investors likely feel confident that consumers will keep buying chicken, pork, and beef from supermarkets even if the economy worsens and they cut back on dining out. Similarly, Chipotle stock has surged 29% over the past month on hopes that the fast-casual restaurant chain will still draw customers even if the AI boom loses steam and consumers feel the pinch from rising prices. Shares of telecoms giant Charter have also risen 19% in a month, reflecting the market view that people will cut back their discretionary spending on vacations and luxury dining before they cancel their home broadband. It's worth noting the rotation out of tech into consumer staples could prove short-lived.
