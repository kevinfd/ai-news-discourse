# Jensen Huang is set to testify before the Senate, and he has some explaining to do

Mashable · Olivia Tauber · 2026-11-23

Jensen Huang is set to testify before the Senate, and he has some explaining to do
If you thought the Jensen Huang saga was over when he stepped down as Nvidia's CEO, think again. Less than two weeks later, Nvidia gave him his job back.
And within hours of Monday's surprise announcement, senators set a date to question him in public — and under oath.
Huang, who co-founded chipmaker Nvidia, is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Thursday, Dec. 3, at 10 a.m. ET, just two days after he's scheduled to speak with various committees of the U.S. House of Representatives. He’s the only witness listed for the hearing, which will focus on U.S. rules limiting where companies can sell their most advanced AI chips. If you want to watch, the committee will be livestreaming the hearing on its website.

The questions about Nvidia's chips go back well before Huang initially stepped down. In 2024, federal investigators began looking into whether Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer based in Singapore, had sent advanced AI chips to China in violation of U.S. export rules. That investigation took on new significance when reports surfaced that Huang had an affair with Alice Huang, who used to run Megaspeed. Jensen Huang acknowledged the affair, and U.S. authorities suspect Alice Huang of working for Chinese intelligence, according to the New York Times (though China has since denied that she was a spy).
The newly empowered House Democrats also have questions for Huang, including whether Nvidia interfered with that investigation. In a letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, they asked whether his department had allowed Nvidia to intervene in the Megaspeed probe.

Lawmakers are also looking into exactly how Huang got his job back and what role the Trump administration played.
Just days before Nvidia brought him back, the board had made clear it had no plans to reinstate him, despite Huang saying he would be "honored" to return. On the company's earnings call on Nov. 17, board chair Harvey Jones even said, "The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO."
Yet, on Nov. 23, the board voted unanimously to do just that. In a statement, the board wrote that it had heard from senior U.S. officials, and "based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk."
"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Harvey Jones said in the statement.
Trump had already signaled he was open to Huang getting his job back. On Nov. 16, he told reporters he "would not mind" if Huang returned as CEO and said Huang had "learned a big lesson."
By Monday, Trump was taking credit for the decision. He told CNBC he'd pushed Nvidia's board to bring Huang back. Asked whether the board would have done so without him, Trump said, "The answer is no. I believe my intervention was very important." He also posted on Truth Social, writing, "The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO… Congratulations to Jensen, the NVIDIA Board, to the People of the United States — and, indeed, the World!"

That leaves lawmakers in both parties asking what changed. Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) called the decision "reckless" and asked the administration to name the officials who spoke to Nvidia and explain what they told the board. Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) asked, "No national security risk? Based on what?" he wrote on X.

Huang had already agreed to answer questions from House lawmakers before he got his job back. That interview is still set for Tuesday, Dec. 1, two days before the public Senate hearing. He’ll meet with three House committees behind closed doors, and the interview will be transcribed.
But now, Huang could also be answering a new set of questions in public about the affair that helped cost him his job and the president who says he got it back for him. So far, Nvidia and Trump have told their sides of the story. The hearing is a chance to hear Huang's.
