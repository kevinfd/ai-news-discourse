# Google says the first autonomous AI cyberattack has happened. Expect a lot more like it.

Mashable · Matt Binder · 2026-11-23

Google says the first autonomous AI cyberattack has happened. Expect a lot more like it.
Fully autonomous cyberattacks are here, according to Google and its cybersecurity firm Mandiant.
The Google Threat Intelligence Group recently released a report revealing that cyberattackers are now using multi-agent AI frameworks capable of searching for vulnerabilities and exploiting them independently. Furthermore, these tactics are already being used in cyberattack campaigns.
Google cybersecurity researchers uncovered "an exposed staging server hosting a multi-agent vulnerability research and weaponization framework dubbed exploit_agent," which "integrates with multiple frontier and local LLMs."

Google's investigation revealed that the entity behind the server hosting this framework appeared to be specifically targeting French websites in the media and entertainment sector. Some education sites were targeted as well.
Google's report cites even more examples of fully autonomous cyberattacks.

In one example, the cybersecurity firm Greynoise found that a bad actor used LLMs to identify vulnerabilities and automatically build exploits. The hacker was able to put together a list of targets and then execute the exploits against hundreds of organizations. In one particular hack, the threat actor was able to gain administrator access "within minutes."
The adversary went from an empty workspace to first achieving RCE against a real victim in just under four hours, first domain admin in an additional two hours, and once the full campaign launched, compromised at least 11 organizations in 26 seconds. In one instance, the adversary went from initial access to full domain administrator in seven minutes against a high school in the United States.
The Google report also cites the PHP vulnerabilities that forced some websites (and a secure NSA network) to go offline recently — the P2PSHELL incident. Those PHP bugs were originally discovered by a new OpenAI model, and when they leaked online, Google said hackers were able to use AI to try to build exploits before websites could be patched.
The Google report concluded that this new reality "represents a paradigm shift towards intrusions occurring at machine speed."
Cybersecurity experts have long warned about AI tools helping hackers carry out their attacks. With LLMs and AI agents now scanning for vulnerabilities and creating exploits, it appears that the threat is here.
