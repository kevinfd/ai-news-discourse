# Apple vs. Broadcom: Comparing Revenue Trends Amid the Artificial Intelligence Boom

The Motley Fool · Robert Izquierdo · 2026-11-23

Apple vs. Broadcom: Comparing Revenue Trends Amid the Artificial Intelligence Boom
Broadcom's quarterly revenue has climbed for seven straight periods, while Apple's has swung up and down, and the next report could change the picture.
By Robert Izquierdo –
Key Points
- Apple currently looks stronger on absolute revenue, with sums significantly higher than Broadcom's over the past two years.
- Apple's sales experience seasonality, while Broadcom's revenue rose every quarter in recent periods.
- Investors should watch if Broadcom's revenue continues to increase in upcoming quarterly reports.
Apple: Seasonal Revenue Spikes
Apple (AAPL +1.90%) makes mobile phones, computers, and tablets for consumers, businesses, and schools. It released the Apple HomeView smart display on Nov. 13.
The consumer electronics giant's top line exhibits sharp quarterly seasonality tied to consumer hardware cycles. Apple tends to release new products in the fall, timed to capture holiday shopping demand. That's why its revenue soars in the fourth quarter.
Broadcom: Consistent Quarterly Revenue Growth
Broadcom (AVGO +3.00%) makes semiconductor components and infrastructure software for enterprise networking. It announced an agreement with NTT-owned DOCOMO on Oct. 20, and launched Arcot Scam Defense on Oct. 21.
Broadcom posted positive quarter-over-quarter revenue growth in every quarter over the past two years. That increase is accelerating in fiscal 2026, reflecting secular enterprise and hyperscaler AI infrastructure demand.
Why Revenue Matters for Investors
Revenue represents the total money brought in by sales before any expenses are deducted. Closely tracking this top-line financial figure helps investors understand the total scale and growth trajectory of a business.

Foolish Take
Apple and Broadcom's revenue trends over the past two years reveal a sharp contrast between the former's consumer-driven seasonal cycles and the latter's rapid, uninterrupted sequential compounding fueled by the rise of artificial intelligence.
In fact, AI has become a key factor in both companies, with Apple signing a multiyear commitment with Broadcom exceeding $30 billion through 2031 to secure custom AI server silicon, wireless and radio frequency components, and semiconductor chips manufactured domestically. Apple intends to use Broadcom's server chip to power Apple Intelligence cloud servers, the retailer's proprietary AI offering.
Apple's revenue trend shows a pronounced increase in 2026 over 2025, thanks to the release of popular new products such as its foldable iPhone Duo. This was further helped by the company's hardware-plus-services flywheel, where customers buy a product, then sign up for additional subscription services such as Apple's cloud storage.
Broadcom is displaying unbroken quarterly sales growth, illustrating the strong demand for its AI solutions. Its compounding top line is driven by custom AI accelerators, data center networking chips, and high-margin infrastructure software. However, investors face higher customer concentration risk. The company's top five customers account for about 40% of revenue, with Apple alone representing roughly 20% of Broadcom's annual sales.
