# Thanksgiving holiday travel could be disrupted by floods, winds and rain

Scientific American · Jackie Flynn Mogensen · 2026-11-23

A series of poorly timed weather events from extreme floods to strong winds are slated to disrupt Thanksgiving travel this week across the U.S. East and West Coasts, National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration forecasters say.
To the East, a storm is expected to dump at least 1.5 to two inches of rain on parts of Massachusetts, Connecticut, New York State, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and Virginia between Wednesday and Friday, with up to four inches expected in areas of the Carolinas, according to the National Weather Service (NWS).
Winds along the East Coast could exceed more than 50 miles per hour on the waterfront and 40 miles per hour inland over the same time, NWS says. If the prediction holds, there’s “likely to be some travel disruptions just based on those winds,” says Dave Radell, a meteorologist at the NWS’s New York City office.

Making matters worse, strong tides thanks to the sun’s and the moon’s pull could lead to “significant flooding and beach erosion” along the East Coast later this week, according to the NWS. The Saint Louis metro area might get a “sneaky, quick hit” of snow on Wednesday night, as the storm starts to “wind up,” says Joe Wegman, a meteorologist at NOAA’s Weather Prediction Center. But for the Northeast, “this is almost entirely a rainfall event.”
On the West Coast, strong tides, as well as a storm “well out” in the Pacific Ocean could help push water toward California, bringing significant flooding along parts of the coast, Wegman says. Parts of the San Francisco Bay Area could see as much as 2.7 feet of flooding above ground level this week, according to the NWS office in San Francisco.
That flooding could affect homes, roadways and airports along the coast, says Dial Hoang, a meteorologist at the NWS’s Monterey, Calif., office. Two major airports in the region, San Francisco International Airport and Oakland San Francisco Bay Airport, are in coastal flood warning areas.
“We’ve been in close coordination with our government partners at both of the airports,” Hoang says. “They aware of the forecast implications, and they are taking mitigation steps to prevent disruptions to their operations.”
For travelers, Wegman recommends keeping an eye on weather forecasts. In the New York region, “Thursday really looks like the brunt of things in terms of travel and in terms of impacts,” Radell says, so take that into consideration for your vacation plans, he says.
In areas where flooding is expected, residents should take precautions to protect flood-prone properties, Hoang advises. And if you come across a flooded road while driving—turn around.
