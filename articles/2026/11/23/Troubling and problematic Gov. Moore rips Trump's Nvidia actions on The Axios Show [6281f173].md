# "Troubling and problematic": Gov. Moore rips Trump's Nvidia actions on "The Axios Show"

Axios · Herb Scribner · 2026-11-23

Maryland Gov. Wes Moore criticized President Trump's involvement in Nvidia's decision to reinstate CEO Jensen Huang , calling it "troubling and problematic" in an interview for "The Axios Show." Why it matters: Moore, chair of the National Governors Association and often seen as a 2028 presidential contender, is one of the most outspoken state leaders pushing for stronger federal oversight of AI companies — but not like this. Driving the news: Trump told CNBC Monday that he spoke directly to Nvidia's board about Huang's return. Trump had previously pushed for former Apple CEO Tim Cook to become Nvidia's leader, but changed tune over the last few days to favor Huang. What they're saying: "The president is not a board member of Nvidia. So, why he is involved in board dealings, I have no idea. ... It seems like it's rules for me, but not for thee," Moore told Axios' Mike Allen in an interview just minutes after the news broke. "You have the wealthy and the powerful and the well-connected that are now making decisions to reinstate, where, frankly, if that was any of us, we would be held to a different type of standard." Zoom in: Moore said the "market is being inflated essentially by seven companies," including Nvidia. Huang's return — which led to a stock surge — "highlights how problematic this entire thing is," Moore said. "We are talking about not just millionaires, but billionaires, who are sitting in a quiet room by themselves, making this decision because they know this company is that important."
