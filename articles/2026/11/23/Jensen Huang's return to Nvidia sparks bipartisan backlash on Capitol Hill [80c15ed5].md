# Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia sparks bipartisan backlash on Capitol Hill

Axios · Stef W. Kight, Andrew Solender, Kate Santaliz · 2026-11-23

Nvidia's decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO is drawing criticism and scrutiny — including about national security risks — from members of Congress in both parties. Why it matters: President Trump's push to reinstate Huang creates a headache for Republicans and new fodder for Democrats eager to deploy their investigative powers when they take control in January. "No national security risk? Based on what," Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) asked in a social media post following the news. "Last week, the board said it didn't intend to reinstate him. I'd like to know what changed," Sen. John Curtis (R-Utah) said in a statement to Axios. "The risk of extortion by a Chinese asset who's been banned by Singapore is risk enough to not rehire Huang. The worst part of this is what we may never know," Sen. Kevin Cramer (R-N.D.) posted on X . Driving the news: The House Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and China committees have announced that Huang would testify next week. His reinstatement "changes nothing," an Intelligence Committee spokesperson told Axios on Monday. "When Jensen was CEO, our three committees were demanding answers. When he was removed and no longer CEO, we were still pursing the facts." The spokesperson added: "There are serious questions on national security implications that still need answers. Seems like the only thing that is consistent these days on this issue is our three Committee's commitment to oversight." Huang is scheduled to appear before the Senate banking panel on Dec. 3. What we're watching: Top Democrats say they will open their own investigations into Huang's reinstatement when they take power next year. "Congress will not be deterred from conducting a swift, thorough and wide-ranging investigation into business malfeasance by NVIDIA and corrupt abuse of power by Howard Lutnick and the Department of Commerce," House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) said. "Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain,"Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) said in a post on X . Said Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.), the top Democrat on the Finance Committee: "In January, Democrats will hold this corrupt administration accountable." Rep. Greg Meeks (D-N.Y.), the ranking member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, told Axios: "We need to know why Trump put his thumb on the scale to have Jensen reinstated as CEO and why his administration failed to restrict the PRC's access to our most sensitive technology." What Democrats are saying: "Trump is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security," Schumer said. Jeffries called for the annual defense authorization bill to include "ironclad export control legislation to prevent powerful AI technology from being exploited by the Chinese Communist Party and other adversaries." "The President is selling out our national security to keep the stock market high and probably for some fucking donation to his ballroom," Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-Ariz.) said . "Jensen Huang's reinstatement shows that under Trump, Vance, and Rubio, our national security is for sale," Wyden said . Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.), the top Banking committee Democrat, called the decision "reckless" and said the board still owes Congress "answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," adding that the Trump administration "needs to explain to Congress what 'input' was given and by which senior officials in the United States government to support his reinstatement." "Yet another example where, in Trump's America, the wealthy and well connected are held to a different standard," Meeks told Axios. Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-Ill.), the top Democrat on the China Select Committee, called the decision "outrageous." Rep. Debbie Dingell (D-Mich.), a member of House Democratic leadership, blasted it as "irresponsible." What Republicans are saying: " Nvidia's board says it relied on input from senior U.S. officials to conclude that bringing Jensen Huang back posed no national security risk. I have some questions," Curtis said in his statement to Axios. "Who in our government weighed in? What did they tell the board? And on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?" "Jensen Huang had multiple affairs including with subordinates and an alleged Chinese spy. His own board described his behavior as lacking 'candor.' ... His behavior does not support reinstatement," said Sen. Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.), adding that he looked forward to Huang's testimony next week. Catch up quick: Nvidia announced Monday morning that it would reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, president a board member after ousting him earlier this month. The board voted unanimously for Huang's return. The reinstatement comes after Trump repeatedly urged the board to bring Huang back. Stocks saw an early, massive rally following the news. Huang's departure had sent the stock market into correction territory. The bottom line: Both sides have a lot of questions. Democrats will soon have the power to get answers.
