# Nvidias board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO following Trumps endorsement

Mashable · Timothy Beck Werth · 2026-11-23

Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO following Trump's endorsement
Updated on Monday, Nov. 23 at 12:45 p.m. ET — This story has been updated with additional information on how Wall Street is responding to Jensen Huang's return and additional details on why the board reversed course.
The Nvidia board of directors has voted unanimously to reinstate the embattled Jensen Huang as the company's CEO, the board announced in a surprise statement Monday morning. The move follows President Donald Trump publicly calling for Huang's return as CEO.
Huang famously resigned on Nov. 12 following reports from the New York Times and the Financial Times that he had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy. In the days that followed, revelations of two more alleged extramarital affairs — with Nvidia employees, no less — emerged, seemingly sealing the CEO's fate.
You May Also Like
On Nov. 12, Huang announced he was resigning his position as CEO, a role he'd held since he co-founded the company in 1993. Under his leadership, Nvidia became one of the most valuable companies in the world, and in recent years, Nvidia chips have powered the fast-growing AI industry.
In reinstating Huang, the board cited "input from senior levels of the United States government...that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk."
President Trump called the board's surprise decision "correct and proper" in a post on his Truth Social website.

"The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO," the president posted. "The country needs Jensen’s leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy."
Behind the scenes, President Trump intervened directly with the Nvidia board, according to CNBC. "I made the deal," Trump told CNBC.
However, not all U.S. officials are convinced that Huang is the right person to lead Nvidia, or that his behavior doesn't pose a national security risk. The newly empowered Democratic Party has pledged to investigate Huang, as well as senior members of the Trump administration involved in AI chip export controls.
Huang's return had an immediate market impact, and as of this writing, Nvidia's stock has climbed almost 40 percent.
