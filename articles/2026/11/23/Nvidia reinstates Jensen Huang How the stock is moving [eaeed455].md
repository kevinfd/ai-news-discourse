# Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang: How the stock is moving

Mashable · Alex Perry · 2026-11-23

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang: How the stock is moving
Update at 2:47 p.m. ET — Updated with information about how the stock has moved throughout the day.
Somehow, Jensen Huang returned.
A mere 12 days after the controversial, embattled Nvidia CEO was ousted from his position, the big tech firm announced that he would be reinstated. Coincidentally or not, this decision followed Donald Trump's public endorsement of Huang's return. Regardless of how or why it happened, investors now have a big question: What does Huang's return mean for Nvidia's stock?

If you're an Nvidia shareholder, Monday morning probably brought some good news. As of around 11 a.m. ET, Nvidia shares (NVDA) were trading at approximately $170, up about 35 percent from Friday's closing price of $125.
That might sound like an enormous jump, and it is. But there's some important context: Nvidia's stock had already been trading at around $162 early last week, before a broader tech sell-off sent its price tumbling. In other words, Monday's surge largely made up for last week's losses rather than pushing the stock to dramatically higher levels.

The stock did give up some of those gains as the day went on. Shortly before 3 p.m. ET today, shares were trading at around $164. That's down from the day's earlier high of $170, but still approximately 31 percent above Friday's closing price. So, while Monday was a big day for Nvidia shareholders, the bigger picture is a little less dramatic. At $164 per share, Nvidia was only slightly above where it had been trading before last week's sell-off.
The timeline of Huang's resignation is messy and convoluted. It started with the revelation that he was having an extramarital affair with a suspected Chinese spy, though there is reason to believe she may not have been an intelligence asset. Regardless, a second affair with a subordinate Nvidia executive came to light, which led to his resignation almost two weeks ago.
After his resignation, reports came out detailing an abusive work culture that allegedly existed at Nvidia under Huang's direction. Despite that, Nvidia's board decided to reinstate Huang.
