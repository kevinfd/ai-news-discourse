# ‘Great Patriot and National Treasure’: Trump Applauds NVIDIA’s Decision to Reinstate Jensen Huang

TIME · Chantelle Lee · 2026-11-23

President Donald Trump on Monday applauded NVIDIA’s board of directors for reinstating Jensen Huang as the company’s president and CEO, calling it “the correct and proper decision.”
“The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO,” Trump said in a Truth Social post. “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.”
Huang’s return to the technology company comes 11 days after he resigned in scandal, amid news reports that he had an extramarital affair with a woman who U.S. officials believed was a Chinese spy.
The President said in his post on Monday, though, that the U.S. government “has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security” and that the U.S. Attorney General “has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that [Huang] has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”
Trump went on to say that he considers Huang to be “a Great Patriot and National Treasure.”
NVIDIA announced the news of Huang’s return on Monday morning, saying in a statement that the board “voted unanimously” on the move after having several conversations with Huang that left the board “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead NVIDIA.” The company added that the board “received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”
The company said that Huang’s reinstatement as president and CEO is effective immediately.
“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” Harvey C. Jones, chair of NVIDIA’s board of directors, said in a statement. “We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company.”
Huang said that he was “honored” to resume leading the company.
“I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” he said in a statement. “I want to thank the NVIDIA Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process.”
The night before, the President had expressed his desire for NVIDIA to reinstate Huang.
“I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang,” Trump said in a Truth Social post on Sunday evening. “It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A.”
Later on Monday morning, Trump posted an edited image on his Truth Social account: the image depicted Huang with a cut on his face and holding a red bra in the air, seemingly photoshopped over an image of Trump with his fist in the air that was captured after he was shot at during a rally in July 2024.
Bipartisan backlash
In Congress, meanwhile, NVIDIA’s decision to reinstate Huang was met with backlash from both sides of the political aisle.
Senator Elizabeth Warren, a Democrat from Massachusetts, called the move “reckless,” and insisted that Huang and the company “still owe Congress answers.”
“The Trump Administration needs to explain what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials to support Huang’s reinstatement,” she said in a social media post. “Huang’s return only increases the urgency to pass bipartisan export control bills and make sure NVIDIA does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk.”
New York Democrat Senator Chuck Schumer accused the President of “putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security.” Schumer added that “Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain.”
Republicans also expressed concerns about Huang resuming his leadership role at the technology company.
Texas Republican Senator John Cornyn responded to NVIDIA’s statement that its board doesn’t believe that Huang’s reinstatement poses a national security risk by saying on social media, “No national security risk? Based on what?”
Another Republican lawmaker, Senator John Curtis of Utah, questioned which U.S. officials provided such input to NVIDIA and “on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?”
“Mr. Huang was right to step down when the affair came to light,” Curtis said in a social media post. “Just 11 days after Nvidia announced his departure, the board brought him back while its own independent investigation was still underway. Last week, the board said it didn’t intend to reinstate him. I’d like to know what changed.”
Senator Pete Ricketts, a Republican from Nebraska, said in a post on X that Huang’s “behavior does not support reinstatement.” He went on to say that Huang’s reinstatement “doesn’t excuse Jensen Huang from testifying before Congress.”
“Jensen Huang was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy. Nvidia should have moved on without Huang and ignored the Administration’s inexplicable desire to save Huang,” Representative Don Bacon, a Republican from Nebraska, said in a social media post. “We cannot say we want to win the AI race and then give China our best technology. We need sunlight on who is responsible.”
