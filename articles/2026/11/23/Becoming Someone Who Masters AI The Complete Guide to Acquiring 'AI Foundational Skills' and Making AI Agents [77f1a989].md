Why the work you can entrust to AI changes, even with the same AI
What I call 'AI Foundational Skills' is the ability to master AI in your own work, regardless of which AI you use.
When an AI's answer is lackluster, the cause is almost always what you provided, rather than the AI's performance.
That is why I only do three things.
1. Communicate
Properly provide the AI with the context of the work that only you know, and the criteria for what you value when making decisions.
2. Assess and Correct
Look at what is produced and return feedback to the AI, saying exactly what is wrong.
3. Store and Delegate
Save what you communicated and the reasons for your corrections so you can use them for the next task.
For example, when having it create a social media post.
Who do you want to reach? What do you want to convey? What have you tried so far? 
Which posts got a reaction, and what did you consider good about them?
If an AI understands that much, you can move from just generating generic text to co-creating plans and drafts that you can actually use in your work.
If what comes out is slightly off, tell it what is wrong and have it correct it. 
Keep the reasons for the corrections and the successful procedures to use in your next task.
If you keep doing this, you will have an AI that understands your judgment and works accordingly, without you having to explain everything from scratch every time.
