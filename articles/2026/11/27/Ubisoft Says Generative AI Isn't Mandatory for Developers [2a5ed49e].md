# Ubisoft Says Generative AI Isn't Mandatory for Developers

80 Level · Emma Collins · 2026-11-27

Ubisoft Says Generative AI Isn't Mandatory for Developers
Ubisoft's statement follows a report that internal pitches must explain how generative AI would be used in production.
Ubisoft has responded to reports claiming it has been forcing its developers to use generative AI in game development.
Recently, Vertical Slice reported that the company required internal teams to explain how they would leverage generative AI in production or why it wasn't suitable. In response to the report, the Assassin's Creed developer issued a statement saying it encourages teams to explore where the technology can be used but does not require it:
"We encourage our teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work. This doesn't mean that projects are required to use generative AI. Teams need to determine whether, and how, these technologies make sense for the game they want to make and the experience they want to create for players."
Insider Gaming's sources at Ubisoft say that using AI may not be formally mandatory. However, developers are still being pushed to adopt it, causing tension within the studios. Reportedly, one of these tools, Imaginarium, is a fork of an existing generative AI tool. It has mainly been used for concept art, but lately, it has been pushed into the wider game development process.
Earlier this week, Ubisoft established a new subsidiary focused on AI-driven game experiences, called Motherbrain. With the initiative, Ubisoft hopes "to empower creators and open up new possibilities for richer, more immersive and engaging player experiences," said Yves Guillemot, Co-Founder and Chief Executive Officer. In January, Ubisoft released a document describing its new operating model, explicitly stating that there would be "accelerated investments" in generative AI.
