# What Can Microsoft Copilot Do Now? | A Visual Guide to 5 Autumn Announcements for Office Workers

note · Taka｜Pythonで業務を「自動化」するエンジニア · 2026-11-27

What Can Microsoft Copilot Do Now? | 5 Autumn Announcements Illustrated for Office Workers
Copilot has arrived on your work computer. But your only use for it so far is typing questions into the search bar.
On top of that, new names like "Cowork," "Code," and "Autopilot" keep multiplying, and frankly, it is getting hard to keep up.
For anyone feeling that way, here is an illustrated summary of the new Copilot features announced by Microsoft this autumn. I have not tested everything myself; this overview is organized based on official announcements and news reports.
What you'll learn in this article: What the 5 new names do / When they will be available / What you can do today
1. Home: Chat and Cowork in One Place
This is your "starting point" when opening Copilot. You can pick up where you left off with recent work, and Copilot will also suggest ways it might be able to help.
Inside, two ways of working sit side by side: Chat for quick questions, and Cowork for handing off larger tasks.
Cowork takes multi-step projects from start to finish through iterative refinements, such as putting together a complete set of presentation materials or drafting a response to a proposal. Soon, you supposedly will not even need to choose which one to use yourself—it will route your request automatically based on the content.
2. Office in Copilot: Generating Real Files
This feature eliminates the hassle of copying answers from a chat window and pasting them into Word or Excel.
When you ask Copilot, it generates editable documents, spreadsheets, and slides directly. In Excel, it makes it clear who changed what and why, and it can also recommend appropriate charts.
3. Code: Build Small Tools Just by Describing Them
Simply describe what you need in plain words—like "I want a spreadsheet like this" or "I need a screen like that"—and this feature builds tracking sheets, dashboards, or small internal apps for you.
Official announcements shared the perspective that creating these small tools will eventually become a basic everyday task, just like taking notes or drafting a budget. Rollout is slated to begin with early-access programs for enterprise users, followed by a preview for personal users (Microsoft 365 Premium / Pro) later this year.
4. Autopilot: An AI That Doesn't Wait for Instructions
This might be the biggest shift of all.
Once you define a name, role, and goal, it monitors chats and threads, proactively sending follow-up messages and carrying out routine tasks on its own. At the Microsoft Ignite event on November 17 (US time), it was introduced as a feature that keeps projects moving forward without requiring step-by-step instructions each time.
However, it is currently in a limited preview. It is not something you will be able to start using at work next week.
5. Today: Proactively Organizing What to Do Today
This feature looks across your emails, calendar, Teams, meetings, and tasks, sorting them into "things you missed," "things to do right now," and "things that can wait." It will even prepare draft replies and proposed schedule adjustments for you. This feature is also currently in the limited preview stage.
When Can You Use It?
This is the most important point: what has been announced and what you can actually use are two different things.
Whether you can use it at your company depends on your corporate contract and IT department settings. Additionally, it has been announced that features like Cowork, Code, and Autopilot will be offered under a pay-as-you-go pricing model.
What to Do Today with the Current Copilot
Even without waiting for the new features, there are things you can try right now.

In Conclusion
There are many names, but the overall trend is simple: moving from an "AI you ask" to an "AI you delegate to."
