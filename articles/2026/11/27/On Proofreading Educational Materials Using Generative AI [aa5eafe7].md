# On Proofreading Educational Materials Using Generative AI

note · 教材編集という仕事 · 2026-11-27

On Proofreading Educational Materials Using Generative AI
Entering 2026, I have had more opportunities to hear from clients about proofreading (and copyediting) using generative AI. Today, I'd like to talk about proofreading educational materials with generative AI.
Some clients have already introduced proofreading with generative AI
Among the clients our company receives work from, some run proofreading via generative AI in parallel with human proofreading. For now, proofreading by generative AI has lower accuracy than professional proofreaders and still misses things.
Going forward, by continuously training generative AI on examples of errors found during the proofreading stages of educational materials, its proofreading accuracy may eventually surpass that of humans.
Some clients have established guidelines for using generative AI
With the spread of generative AI, some clients have established guidelines for when it is used. These guidelines covered content regarding manuscript writing as well as proofreading.
If you feed a manuscript into generative AI to have it proofread before educational materials are released, there is a risk that the manuscript will be used as training data for other generative AIs and leaked externally. For that reason, the guidelines stipulated rules such as not feeding unreleased manuscripts into generative AI, or configuring opt-out settings (ensuring it is not used as training data for other generative AIs) before feeding them in.
Attending an online seminar on proofreading with generative AI
To learn about proofreading and copyediting with generative AI, I recently attended an online seminar held by members of a publishing company's copyediting department. However, because they were from a general trade book publisher, the content was not specifically focused on proofreading educational materials.
At the seminar, they compared the results of a veteran copyeditor and multiple generative AIs proofreading the same text, explaining the areas generative AI excels at and struggles with. They also explained that proofreading accuracy varies depending on how the prompt (input text) is written, and that because generative AI gives different responses every time even with the same prompt, it remains unstable as a proofreading tool.
How to improve the accuracy of proofreading with generative AI
If you train generative AI on low-quality manuscripts or proofreading results, there is a risk of it presenting low-quality correction suggestions or producing hallucinations (generative AI providing responses that diverge from facts). Moreover, with the spread of opt-out settings in the future, generative AI might become unable to learn enough information necessary for proofreading. To improve the accuracy of proofreading with generative AI, it seems essential to acquire numerous resources needed for proofreading educational materials—such as examples of errors—and train the generative AI on them.
Also, since proofreading accuracy appears to vary depending on the prompt's content, it is necessary to create prompts that describe checking criteria in as much detail as a proofreading checklist. It might even be a good idea to have a generative AI create the prompt itself and then have another generative AI review it.
In the end, the skills of educational material editors are what matter
Because generative AI responses carry risks such as hallucinations, completely offloading proofreading to generative AI could result in releasing educational materials with low-quality manuscripts or erroneous content still remaining. Furthermore, generative AI merely flags potential issues, and its answers vary each time even with the exact same prompt, so deciding how to evaluate these flags is the job of the educational material editor.
Additionally, even if generative AI catches every single error, if the educational material editor overlooks the AI's feedback or enters incorrect revision instructions on the proofs, flawed educational materials will still be released. Just as before, countermeasures against human error remain critical.
In the near future, it seems humans will no longer need to handle every single step of proofreading educational materials from start to finish, but it does not appear that we will be able to completely hand everything over to generative AI either. In the end, the skills of educational material editors seem to be what truly matter.
