# Trump is destroying the rule of law in the US – to protect himself and the tech titans he worships as new gods | Jonathan Freedland

The Guardian · Jonathan Freedland · 2026-11-27

To get Jensen Huang back at Nvidia, the president trampled on the justice system, national security and the trust America needs to do business in the global economy Don’t let the sex distract you. Or the lies or the boardroom intrigue or even the AI-generated bra, all of which could have come from an especially wild episode of Succession. Don’t let that lure you into thinking that the Jensen Huang affair is, as Donald Trump would have it, little more than a sex scandal involving a corporate maestro. It is much more serious than that. The events of the past week tell instead a story of legal immunity and indeed impunity – revealing how Trump is putting himself, the tech bros and even artificial intelligence above the law. It can be hard to see that through the thicket of questions that surround this episode, each one of which, don’t get me wrong, merits attention. Consider the personal profit Trump made thanks to his re-installation of Huang as the CEO of Nvidia, the world’s leading maker of advanced AI chips. Breaking the norm observed by previous presidents who, keen to avoid even the appearance of a conflict of interest, either divested themselves of stock holdings or placed them in a blind trust, Trump cheerfully buys and sells stocks in companies whose fates he can influence. He saw the value of his shares in Nvidia plunge when Huang was forced out, following revelations that the CEO had had an affair with Alice Huang, reportedly an asset of Chinese intelligence. Last weekend Trump urged the board to reconsider; Huang was reinstated on Monday and Nvidia’s value promptly soared – putting $15m in Trump’s pocket .
