# AI impact on jobs: Campus placements decline by up to 20% as IT companies cut entry-level hiring; skilled freshers still command higher salaries

Moneycontrol · MC Education Desk · 2026-11-27

15
Campus placements in Tamil Nadu engineering colleges have declined by up to 20% as AI reshapes IT hiring. Companies are prioritising specialised skills, while skilled freshers secure higher salaries.
  November 27, 2026 / 16:55 IST  
The growing adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) is beginning to reshape the campus placement landscape in India, with engineering colleges across Tamil Nadu reporting a decline of up to 20% in IT job offers in 2026. As companies increasingly use AI to automate routine programming, software testing and other entry-level tasks, traditional recruitment patterns are changing, forcing fresh engineering graduates to develop skills beyond conventional coding.
Employers are shifting their focus towards candidates who can work with AI tools, evaluate machine-generated code and solve complex business problems. While the reduction in campus hiring has raised concerns among engineering students, graduates with specialised technical skills are continuing to attract competitive salary packages.
The changing trend also signals a shift in what IT companies expect from freshers. Rather than recruiting large numbers of graduates for routine technical work, employers are increasingly looking for candidates who can contribute to specialised projects involving artificial intelligence, cloud computing, cybersecurity and data engineering.
Engineering colleges report decline in campus placements
Several engineering institutions in Tamil Nadu have reported a fall in IT recruitment during the 2026 placement season. At Anna University, placements in software and product development declined from 498 students in November 2025 to 416 during the corresponding period in 2026. This represents a drop of approximately 16.5%. The university also recorded 607 placements across its four campuses until November 18, compared with 657 during the same period last year.
Hindustan Institute of Technology and Science reported a 10% to 15% reduction in job offers from IT companies. Vellore Institute of Technology (VIT), meanwhile, witnessed a 5% decline in job offers, while recruitment by Global Capability Centres (GCCs) reportedly fell by 20%.
These figures reflect placement trends at individual institutions and should not be treated as a nationwide assessment of campus recruitment.
Why are IT companies reducing entry-level hiring?
The growing use of artificial intelligence and automation is changing the way technology companies organise their workforce. Tasks that traditionally required large teams of junior programmers and software testers can now be handled, at least partly, through AI-powered tools.
As a result, companies are becoming more selective about entry-level recruitment. Routine coding skills alone may no longer be enough to secure a job, with employers increasingly expecting graduates to understand AI-assisted development, review machine-generated code and contribute to more complex technical assignments.
Demand is also rising for specialised skills in artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, cloud computing and data engineering. Companies are looking for candidates who can build AI-powered workflows, evaluate machine learning models and develop AI agents. According to recruitment industry estimates, specialised roles could account for 45% to 55% of campus hiring in FY27, indicating a growing preference for skill-based recruitment over conventional mass hiring.
Skilled freshers continue to receive higher salary packages
Despite the decline in overall IT job offers, graduates with advanced technical skills are finding opportunities with better compensation. Sastra University has reported that some skilled graduates are receiving annual salary packages of Rs 6 lakh, compared with the earlier figure of Rs 4 lakh. This marks an increase of 50%. Certain specialised roles are also attracting packages of up to Rs 15 lakh to Rs 16 lakh annually.
Companies are increasingly differentiating between candidates based on their technical expertise and ability to work with emerging technologies. Infosys, for instance, is placing greater emphasis on recruiting specialist programmers and digital specialist engineers. This suggests that while the number of conventional entry-level opportunities may be shrinking, candidates with the right technical capabilities can still secure attractive offers.
Engineering colleges revise curriculum to address AI disruption
The changing demands of the IT industry are also prompting engineering institutions to reconsider how students are trained. Tamil Nadu's higher education department has asked colleges to establish industry advisory boards and update their curricula every two years. The objective is to ensure that academic programmes remain aligned with changing industry requirements.
Institutions are also strengthening industry partnerships, introducing specialised training programmes and creating more opportunities for students to gain practical experience before graduation.
Industry experts believe that internships, hands-on projects, domain knowledge and familiarity with AI tools will become increasingly important for engineering graduates entering the job market. Students may therefore need to move beyond textbook knowledge and conventional programming skills. Understanding how to use AI tools effectively, identify errors in AI-generated output and apply technology to real-world business problems could give candidates an advantage during recruitment.
Does AI mean fewer jobs for fresh engineering graduates?
The decline in campus placements does not necessarily mean that entry-level IT jobs are disappearing altogether. Instead, the nature of these roles is changing as companies adopt automation and restructure their hiring requirements. Routine tasks are increasingly being handled through AI-assisted systems, while demand is growing for employees who can manage, evaluate and improve these technologies.
For fresh graduates, this means that a traditional engineering degree and basic coding knowledge may no longer be sufficient to stand out in an increasingly competitive job market. Practical experience, specialised technical skills and the ability to work alongside AI systems are becoming important factors in recruitment.
The shift presents challenges for students preparing to enter the IT sector, but it also creates opportunities for those willing to adapt to the industry's changing expectations.
