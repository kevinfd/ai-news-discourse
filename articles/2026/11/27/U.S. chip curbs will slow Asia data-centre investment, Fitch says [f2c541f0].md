# U.S. chip curbs will slow Asia data-centre investment, Fitch says

The Hindu · Reuters · 2026-11-27

Asia-Pacific data-centre investment will slow and become more selective as tighter U.S. chip controls pressure operators reliant on Chinese customers and advanced American semiconductors, Fitch Ratings said on Thursday.
Fitch’s statement comes a day after S&P placed Nvidia’s rating on downgrade watch over governance concerns and Moody’s said CEO Jensen Huang’s reinstatement reduced near-term execution uncertainty but governance and regulatory risks persisted.
The new rules, effective November 19, require licenses for covered chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia and Thailand, and overseas data centres supplying significant computing power to China-based companies.
Fitch said this could reshape Asia’s AI boom, redirecting capital toward projects with secure chip access and diversified customers. It would also widen the divide between physical data-centre owners and highly leveraged computing providers, it said.
Neocloud operators — specialised cloud providers that rent out AI computing power — face the greatest pressure because they must keep costly graphics processors highly utilized, Fitch said.
Additionally, China-focused operators may have to lower prices, offer less-advanced computing or keep using existing Nvidia chips without access to upgrades, which could hurt sales, profits and their ability to compete, Fitch added.
Also at risk would be nearly completed projects, facing delays if operators cannot secure chips or confirm that customers are eligible to use them, pushing back when the facilities can begin generating revenue.
Projects with uncertain chip supplies or customer eligibility will face tighter financing as lenders conduct more checks and delay funding or new loans, Fitch added.
Earlier on Thursday, Reuters exclusively reported that the restrictions could delay flagship models from China’s Z.AI and Moonshot AI, with Moonshot expecting development cycles to double to 12 months or longer if overseas computing access is cut off entirely.
The Financial Times reported earlier in the day that Nvidia told manufacturing partners to stop server shipments to the four Southeast Asian countries while assessing compliance with the new controls.
