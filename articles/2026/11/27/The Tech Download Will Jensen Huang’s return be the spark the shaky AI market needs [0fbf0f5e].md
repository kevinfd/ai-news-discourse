# The Tech Download: Will Jensen Huang’s return be the spark the shaky AI market needs?

CNBC · Kai Nicol-Schwarz · 2026-11-27

This report is from this week's The Tech Download newsletter. Like what you see? You can subscribe here.
The recent Nvidia saga has had more twists and turns than an Agatha Christie novel.
It all started with Nvidia co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's admission that he had been unfaithful to his wife. Days later, amid an independent board investigation and broader scrutiny over national-security concerns, he resigned. Less than two weeks later, Huang was back at the helm.
During all of that, Nvidia stock lost over $1 trillion and then regained just under $1 trillion.
The leadership drama has played out at a time when the artificial intelligence market is looking shakier than it has in some time.
Neoclouds have seen stocks plummet in recent months as investors question the sustainability of the AI boom. Chip stocks like Arm and AMD have both fallen in the past month.
Huang's surprise return saw Nvidia stock surge 31%, with other AI stocks also gaining. But the sheen was off by Tuesday's close, with Nvidia and a host of chip stocks falling.
The sharp reversal raises questions for investors: did Huang's return restore confidence in Nvidia, or did it spark a short-lived relief rally as the market faces broader questions about returns on AI investment?
Judging by the first few days of his return, it's a hard one to call.
Volatility
Volatility in the tech sector looked set to continue on Friday, as tech stocks rebounded again.
"Wall Street pared its Monday advance as sobriety returned to the market and a focus returned to fundamentals," wrote Kyle Rodda, analyst at Capital.com, in a note at market close on Tuesday.
"While Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO is indubitably a boon for the markets and the overall AI trade, it does not assuage every risk and concern about an industry confronting a potential peak in its investment boom and moderating earnings growth."
The early week rally was "largely driven" by Huang's return, said Violeta Todorova, senior research analyst at Leverage Shares.
"Fresh tariff threats from the Trump administration have added to market uncertainty," she added. "The short-term rally has reversed course, and Nvidia's share price is likely to trade sideways between $125 and $170 in the near term."
Nvidia stock closed at $149.30 on Wednesday, giving it a $3.6 trillion market cap — a figure around $2 trillion lighter than it was at its peak in October.
"We expect a selective recovery across the AI ecosystem, and NVDA to trade sideways and stay volatile below its October peak at least until the Dec 1–3 Congressional appearances," Egor Tolmachev, senior analyst at Freedom Capital Markets, said in a note on Wednesday.
Other key AI stocks have also been volatile. Anthropic's first week as a public company has been mixed. AMD has fallen 25% in the past five days, and memory chip maker Micron has dropped 18%. Both spiked on Monday before paring gains.
The Huang evangelists might've been hoping for a speedier return to AI boom times.
But while the Nvidia CEO's return has removed one source of uncertainty around the chip giant, the volatility among AI stocks suggests investors are still trading on questions much bigger than who occupies the chief executive's office.
