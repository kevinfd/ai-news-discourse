Conclusion Absolutely. All the grunt work that used to require an actual human — screening for new ideas, writing initiation reports, building financial models, writing morning notes — can now be replaced by Claude and tools like its new Equity Research plugin. We’ll see how many analysts lose their jobs. I think it’s more likely that fewer analysts will be hired into the profession. And the analysts who are employed will spend less time on grunt work and more time scanning for new ideas. They’ll then present these ideas to their portfolio managers, who are trying to manage an overflow of information from all sources. I also believe Claude and other generative AI tools are empowering retail investors in a way never seen before. There are now hordes of retail investors who are just as informed – if not better-informed – than the fund managers they compete with. So there will now be thousands of analysts covering even the smallest stocks. Competition will certainly heat up. Of Claude’s Equity Research skills, I was particularly impressed by the sector report, the thesis tracker, the catalyst calendar, and the screener. You’ll still need to question what you read and exercise judgment. But Claude can certainly replace much of what we humans have focused on over all these years. Addendum note on Agents in Banking This is not banking advice but many to most Banks have blocked personal assistants, and for good reason. Like Meta’s Muse. I do not personally recommend giving any AI agent access to your bank account, e.g. using them for shopping activities yet. I know this is confusing for the shopping season, but the technology isn’t there yet. Citibank, the third largest commercial bank in the United States, changed its terms of service this week, telling customers who use third-party AI tools for personal finance that anything that goes wrong is their problem — even if they get hacked. Be aware of the risks. Know the terms of your banks. This requires almost a warning statement. AI agents, ranging from frontier lab models to purpose-built personal finance tools, can be given the credentials to your bank account and other online financial portals, with the idea that they can analyze spending and investment decisions, offer advice, and even handle transactions and bill payments. At the current time when cybersecurity is not able to keep up with AI, this is not recommended. If financial institutions aren’t thrilled with the unreliable AI actors in their systems, and are telling customers that they won’t be held responsible for reversing an intentional or unintentional transaction made by automated assistants. Anthropic’s approach is telling: The personal finance tool it launched earlier this month is explicitly billed as “read-only” and “can’t move, manage, or invest your money.” Beyond cybersecurity concerns, banks also view efforts to enter their franchise as a competitive threat so proceed with extreme caution of how you use AI with anything to do with your money, or investing decisions. These tools are extremely nascent, and you should experiment with them only on an ad-hoc and project basis as one tool in your human judgement and those of the professionals who serve your best interests. For retail investors, analysts and DIY stock pickers, I still think AI and personal finance tools can be extremely valuable. While agentic AI in investing apps are in Beta, you should not rely upon them. For example, Robinhood launched native Agentic Trading and an official Model Context Protocol (MCP) server in mid-to-late 2026. However, there is a wide gap between an AI mechanically executing trades and an AI reliably generating alpha without blowing up an account. I’m not an expert but never allow AI to shop or invest for you, without human-in-the-loop safeguards to the max. Until this technology is fully fleshed out, which may take a while.
