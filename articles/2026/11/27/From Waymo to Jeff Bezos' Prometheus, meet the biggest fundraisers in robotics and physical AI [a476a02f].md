# From Waymo to Jeff Bezos' Prometheus, meet the biggest fundraisers in robotics and physical AI

Business Insider · Rya Jetha, Rya Jetha (rjetha@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-27

Jeff Bezos Kimberly White/Getty Images for Semafor Robotics and physical AI funding hit $97.3 billion so far in 2026, more than double 2025’s total. Waymo and Jeff Bezos’ Prometheus landed funding rounds of $16 billion and $12 billion, respectively. The boom spans established businesses, defense companies, and early bets on humanoids. From robotaxis and military drones to humanoids that might someday fold your laundry , Silicon Valley is betting billions on a future filled with robots. 2026 was a record year for funding. So far, robotics and physical AI companies have raised $97.3 billion dollars across more than 1,200 deals globally — more than double the $47.4 billion raised in all of 2025, according to Crunchbase. The funding boom spans established businesses like Waymo and early bets on humanoids from companies like Neura Robotics . Defense companies, particularly drone makers such as Anduril, Shield AI, Helsing, and Saronic, account for some of the biggest rounds. Gené Teare, a research lead at Crunchbase, told Business Insider that drones attract more capital because the technology is more mature than other experimental areas of robotics. Sanjay Aggarwal, a partner at VC firm F-Prime, pointed to four drivers of the boom: growth in autonomous vehicles and defense, a push to revive domestic manufacturing, bets on "once-in-a-generation" founders like Jeff Bezos and Travis Kalanick, and futuristic technologies like humanoids. Aggarwal expects the first three categories to keep attracting big checks in 2027, while investors scrutinize the fourth more closely. "You can only raise money on the promise of future revenue for so long," he said. Here are the 10 robotics and physical AI companies that have raised the most funding this year, according to data from Crunchbase. 1. Waymo: $16 billion Waymos in Santa Monica Genaro Molina/Los Angeles Times via Getty Images Waymo landed the sector's biggest round of the year so far, and its robots are hard to miss. Its Jaguar robotaxis and new Ojai vehicles trundle along practically every block in San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Phoenix. It operates in 15 US cities , with more planned for 2027. Its $16 billion Series D, announced in February, valued Waymo at $126 billion. Dragoneer Investment Group, DST Global, and Sequoia Capital led the round, with backing from Alphabet, its majority investor. 2. Prometheus: $12 billion Jeff Bezos is back in the CEO seat. The Amazon founder co-leads Prometheus with former Google X executive Vik Bajaj, marking his first CEO role since leaving Amazon in 2021. The company is building an "artificial general engineer" — AI to help engineers develop and test products from smartphones to jet engines in computer simulations — before manufacturing them in the real world. Its $12 billion Series B, announced in June, valued the company at roughly $41 billion. Investors included Bezos himself, JPMorgan, BlackRock, and Goldman Sachs. 3. Anduril Industries: $5 billion Palmer Luckey Adam Gray/Getty Images for The New York Times Palmer Luckey cofounded his defense tech company nearly a decade ago to build machines for the battlefield. Today, its surveillance towers are deployed along the US-Mexico border, and the company says it has delivered hundreds of Altius drones to Ukrainian forces. The company said it brought in $2.2 billion in revenue last year, more than double its 2024 revenue. Its $5 billion Series H, announced in May, doubled its valuation to $61 billion. Andreessen Horowitz and Thrive Capital led the round. 4. Shield AI: $4 billion Co-founded by former Navy SEAL Brandon Tseng, Shield AI builds military drones and Hivemind, an AI pilot for aircraft. Its V-BAT surveillance drone takes off vertically and can fly even in the presence of signal jamming. It helped the US Coast Guard seize $362 million worth of cocaine last year and flew more than 200 missions in Ukraine in 2025. This year, Shield AI raised two $2 billion rounds of financing. In March, the company raised a Series G round led by Advent International and JPMorgan Chase. In November, it announced plans to raise another $2 billion from investors to help fund the acquisition of radar maker Echodyne. The package includes a $1 billion Series H valuing Shield AI at $28.8 billion, plus a separate $1 billion equity investment from Blackstone-managed funds. 5. Zipline International: $2.3 billion Zipline drone Jay Janner/The Austin American-Statesman via Getty Images Zipline began delivering blood in Rwanda in 2016. Today, its drones carry everything from medical supplies to takeout. The company says it has made more than a million deliveries in Dallas-Fort Worth alone this year and is working with Walmart to add 300 delivery sites across the US. Its $1.5 billion Series I, announced this month, valued Zipline at $21.5 billion. Paradigm, WCM Investment Management, and Layer Global led the round. That followed an $800 million Series H that closed in March. 6. Helsing: $1.8 billion Munich-based defense tech company Helsing builds AI software and hardware for military and national security use, including drones and autonomous underwater vehicles. Its HX-2 drone uses AI to navigate and identify targets even when GPS signals are jammed. The company said thousands had been deployed to Ukrainian forces in the first half of 2026 . Its $1.8 billion Series E, announced in July , valued the company at $18 billion. Investors included Dragoneer Investment Group, Lightspeed Venture Partners, and ICONIQ Capital. 7. Saronic: $1.75 billion Austin-based Saronic builds boats designed to operate without crews for the US military and its allies. In June, one of its drone boats, operated by the US Navy, helped rescue two American soldiers after Iran shot down their helicopter off the coast of Oman. The company's $1.75 billion Series D, announced in March , valued it at $9.25 billion. Kleiner Perkins led the round. 8. Atoms: $1.7 billion Travis Kalanick Jordan Vonderhaar/Bloomberg via Getty Images Uber cofounder Travis Kalanick publicly unveiled Atoms earlier this year after years of operating in stealth. The Los Angeles-based company has revealed few details but says it is building robots for food, mining, and transportation. It also absorbed Kalanick's ghost-kitchen business, CloudKitchens , as a subsidiary. Atoms announced a $1.7 billion funding round on July 22, led by Andreessen Horowitz, to pursue what Kalanick called "a journey to digitize the physical world." 9. Skild AI: $1.4 billion Founded by Carnegie Mellon researchers Deepak Pathak and Abhinav Gupta, Pittsburgh-based Skild AI is building an AI model capable of controlling machines ranging from robotic arms to humanoids. Its model is designed to help different robots learn new jobs without being programmed for each task. Skild said in September that it had reached $100 million in annual recurring revenue from more than 60 customers. Projects include robotic assembly with Nvidia and Foxconn and kitchen pilots with Mitsui. Its $1.4 billion Series C, announced on January 14, valued Skild at more than $14 billion. SoftBank Group led the round. 10. Neura Robotics: $1.4 billion Neura Robotics Sven Hoppe/picture alliance via Getty Images Germany's Neura Robotics builds robotic arms and mobile carts and is developing humanoids. Customers can rent space at its "robot gyms" — training centers equipped with simulators and tracking technology — to teach machines specific jobs, then transfer those skills to other robots. Its $1.4 billion Series C, announced in June, was led by Tether Investments, with backing from Amazon, Nvidia, and Bosch. Have a tip?
