# Jensen Huang's return did little to ease AI jitters. We used the weakness to buy

CNBC · Alexa LoMonaco · 2026-11-27

Stocks advanced over the holiday-shortened trading week, but it was far from smooth sailing. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite advanced 6.6% for the week, while the S & P 500 gained 5.6% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 5.5%. The gains were driven by Monday's surge, when the Nasdaq and S & P 500 posted their best sessions since April 2025 — up 6.35% and 4.73% , respectively — after Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO. But the relief rally quickly lost momentum. Selling intensified Wednesday, with the Nasdaq plunging as much as 4.6% and the Dow shedding more than 1,100 points at their session lows. Stocks staged a dramatic comeback as the session progressed, with the Dow turning positive and the Nasdaq and S & P 500 paring much of their losses, though the two remained in negative territory as AI stocks continued to struggle. A worsening macroeconomic backdrop also weighed on investor sentiment. Wednesday's personal consumption expenditures price index report showed annual inflation accelerating to 3.8% in October, nearly twice the Federal Reserve's 2% target, while core inflation came in hotter than expected at 3.3%. The data, coupled with the Trump administration's announcement of new tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, heightened concerns about further Federal Reserve rate hikes. The CME FedWatch tool indicates a 98% chance of a quarter-point hike at the December meeting, up from 93.5% a week ago. Stocks bounced back on Friday, with technology stocks once again taking the lead. Here's a closer look at the three things that shaped our portfolio. Huang's back, but AI jitters remain Jensen Huang's stunning reinstatement as Nvidia CEO on Monday lifted a major overhang on the chipmaker and temporarily reignited the AI trade. President Donald Trump told CNBC he intervened directly with Nvidia's board to bring Huang back, underscoring the CEO's importance to U.S. national interests. Nvidia shares soared more than 31% Monday, erasing nearly all their losses following Huang's Nov. 12 resignation amid an affair scandal involving an alleged Chinese spy. The news fueled the Nasdaq and S & P 500's best sessions since April 2025. "Jensen's only one man, and Nvidia is only one company," Jim said Monday on " Mad Money ." "But the truth is that Jensen is the linchpin that holds the AI solar system together, and Nvidia is the biggest beneficiary." Jim argued that Huang's return warrants a higher valuation for Nvidia. He said the stock deserves to trade at roughly 12 times earnings, putting his price target at $199. Still, headwinds remain, including political opposition to data center construction, uncertainty over chip sales to China and a more hawkish Fed. The relief rally proved short-lived , with chip stocks coming under renewed pressure Tuesday and Wednesday and Anthropic even falling below its $83 IPO price. Shares of Nvidia slipped roughly 5.9% on Tuesday and another 4% on Wednesday . Huang's return removed a major source of uncertainty, but the broader AI trade is hardly out of the woods. CrowdStrike's $44 billion bet CrowdStrike announced Monday that it would acquire Zscaler for roughly $44 billion in an all-stock deal, marking its largest acquisition to date and the latest major consolidation in the cybersecurity industry. CrowdStrike shares fell roughly 9% on Monday, ending the week as the worst performer in our portfolio amid concerns about shareholder dilution that overshadowed the company's better-than-expected quarterly results. Shares of Zscaler rose more than 17% on Monday. Strategically, the acquisition fills a significant gap in CrowdStrike's cybersecurity offerings. While its Falcon platform protects devices and cloud systems, Zscaler brings network security capabilities, allowing the combined company to offer customers more comprehensive protection against increasingly sophisticated AI-powered attacks. In an interview with Jim on "Mad Money," CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz said the acquisition expands the company's addressable market by roughly $185 billion and creates significant cross-selling opportunities, given the limited overlap between the companies' customer bases. Kurtz called the deal "a winning combination." Putting our cash to work Despite Monday's enormous rally, the S & P Oscillator, our trusted momentum indicator , moved deeper into oversold territory, reaching minus 8.75% from minus 8.61%. Any reading below minus 4% indicates technically oversold conditions. According to MarketEdge, roughly half the time the Oscillator has reached minus 8%, it has subsequently hit minus 10% — historically an excellent buying opportunity. In keeping with our investment discipline, we bought 50 shares of Alphabet on Tuesday's weakness and upgraded the stock to our buy-equivalent 1 rating. While concerns about Google Search's ability to monetize AI-powered queries and fend off competition persist, Alphabet's valuation of just below 19 times estimated 2027 earnings presents an opportunity. Mounting political opposition to data center construction could also make Alphabet's existing AI infrastructure more valuable while easing capital spending pressures. We put more cash to work Wednesday, buying 10 shares of Goldman Sachs . With shares down about 30% from their July highs, much of the concern about an AI-driven slowdown in capital markets activity is priced in. Meanwhile, M & A activity has been heating up, with CrowdStrike's acquisition of Zscaler and Sanofi's $41 billion deal for Insmed announced this week. Goldman is advising Zscaler.
