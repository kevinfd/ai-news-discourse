# Health visitors join calls for ban on AI ‘chatbot’ toys for children

Nursing in Practice · Gee Harland · 2026-11-27

Health visitors join calls for ban on AI ‘chatbot’ toys for children
Listen
3 min
The government has been urged to prohibit the sale of toys with built-in AI chatbots to children until they are ‘independently proven safe’.
The Institute of Health Visiting (iHV) is among a list of signatories to an open letter to the secretary of state for business, innovation, science and trade, Jonathan Reynolds, outlining a series of concerns about AI-enabled toys.
They warn that such products are being marketed to children despite concerns about safeguarding, privacy, psychological and developmental risks.
In the letter, also signed by the National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children (NSPCC), the signatories argue that ‘current toy safety regulations do not adequately assess these potential harms before products reach the market’.

According to the organisations, 28 AI chatbot toys are currently on sale with more than half under £50 and ‘many labelled’ for children aged three and up.
They cited one example of a plush toy, listed for ages three and up, which was tested by an adult speaking to it as a young child.
‘Its responses suggested that the child could give consent to being touched for pleasure,’ the letter said.
‘This is not a fault in one product. It is a new category of toy that no UK regulator tests for psychological, developmental or safeguarding harm before it reaches children.
‘They are marketed as educational, with no independent evidence that they are.’
The organisations want to see the government introduce an immediate temporary ban on AI chatbot toys for under-16s and to issue product safety alerts ahead of Christmas.

The letter also calls for an independent testing framework to ensure that any AI-enabled toy is demonstrated to be safe before being sold to children.
iHV’s policy manager Sam Lawes said: ‘Children should not be used as the testing ground for rapidly evolving AI technologies.
‘When serious questions have been raised about the potential impact of AI chatbot toys on children’s safety, wellbeing and development, the precautionary principle is clear: these products should be proven safe before they are sold, rather than waiting for evidence of harm to emerge after they are already in use.
‘Children’s safety must always come first, and we support urgent action to ensure that children’s protection keeps pace with technological innovation.’

The Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade spokesperson said: ‘We are very concerned by these reports and are already investigating these products, as well as the potential risks posed by AI in toys.
‘There is no excuse for anyone to sell products that could put children at risk and companies need to do all they can to act quickly and put things right.’
