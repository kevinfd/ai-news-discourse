# Meta's Personal AI Agent Muse Is Amazing

note · いあ｜自分の裁量で日々を紡ぐ · 2026-11-27

Meta's Personal AI Agent Muse Is Amazing
An incredible era has arrived. I have gone from being someone who "uses" apps to someone who "builds" them.
The other day, I successfully automated the process of compiling our entire family's library loan statuses into a single list using Manus (an AI agent).
Thanks to that, I now only need to check the weekly notifications, which has made things so much easier.
For items eligible for an extension, it handles everything just by being instructed to do so.
It's genuinely convenient.
However, I struggled to take things any further from there.
Meanwhile, ads for an app from Meta kept popping up on my Instagram feed over and over again.
Muse is a personal AI agent that Meta began offering in Japan on October 23, 2026.
To think there were already people exploring this world more than a month before I started using it...!
Honestly, I didn't pay much attention to it at first, but today I decided to install it on a whim.
It turned out to be far more amazing than I had imagined.
You can operate it much more intuitively.
Making revisions is simple.
What I decided to try this time was something I'd been wanting to find a solution for over a very long time.
Managing it in a planner, managing it in smartphone calendar apps—none of it ever felt quite right.
That was managing the recurring schedules for a family of five.
Take hair salon visits, for example.
Timing when to book each family member's next hair appointment is tough. If you don't reach out the moment booking slots open up, your preferred dates and times get booked solid in no time, making tracking them a real hassle.
Routine dental checkups are the same story.
Who visited when, roughly when the next appointment should be, whether a booking has already been made or not...
It's far too detailed to keep in your head, yet merely plugging it into a calendar isn't enough to manage it properly.
I was truly at a loss.
I had long wished for something that could manage all of these things in one place.
Whenever I tried to organize things in a way that worked well for me, previous methods always felt like something was missing.
So this time, I decided to give it a prompt as an experiment:
"Build an app that lets me manage hair salon and dental appointments for each of my family members."
Just like that, the app was ready in the blink of an eye.
What surprised me even more was what came after.
"I want to be able to select multiple people when choosing family members."
"For upcoming bookings, please group them together if they fall on the same date."
"Line up three monthly calendars at the very top covering up to three months ahead, so it's easy to grasp visually."
Just by putting what came to mind into words like that, the screen and features would update within seconds.
No programming knowledge is needed.
You don't need to know any technical jargon.
You just describe what you want in your own words.
Until now, you had to search through apps someone else built to find one that fit your lifestyle.
Now, you can build the app itself tailored to your own life.
Fine-tuning and making improvements through actual use are still necessary.
Even so, I'm astonished by how low the barrier to taking that first step has become.
There are so many things in daily life that aren't quite worth building an entire app for, yet have been a constant inconvenience.
We may have entered an era where we can solve those minor inconveniences ourselves.
An incredible era has arrived.
And above all, from now on, whenever you think "I wish an app like this existed," you won't have to wait around for someone else to make it.
Thinking about that makes me genuinely excited.
