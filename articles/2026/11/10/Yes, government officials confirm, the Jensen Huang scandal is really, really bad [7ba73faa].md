# Yes, government officials confirm, the Jensen Huang scandal is really, really bad

Fast Company · Rebecca Heilweil · 2026-11-10

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has found himself embroiled in a scandal involving an alleged affair, a suspected Chinese spy, and some of America’s most closely guarded technology. Not exactly your garden-variety corporate indiscretion.
There’s a lot to worry about. Experts are concerned that Huang’s repeated calls to loosen restrictions on advanced chip exports to China may have been influenced by the relationship. The affair also raises questions about Nvidia’s dealings with Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer led by Alice Huang. The Commerce Department began investigating Megaspeed last year, The New York Times reported, but transferred the probe to the Justice Department after Nvidia reached out. (Though they share a surname, Jensen Huang and Alice Huang are not related.)
One of the stranger twists in the still-unfolding scandal is that the Trump administration appears to have known about the relationship—and Alice Huang’s suspected ties to the Chinese government—for some time. Yet intelligence officials apparently never warned Jensen Huang, and Trump seems to be brushing off the scandal. Despite an FBI investigation, the president has called Nvidia’s CEO an “honorable guy” and says he believes Huang didn’t know Alice was a spy. Nvidia’s board, meanwhile, says it has “no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised” and has “confidence in Jensen and his leadership team.”
But current and former government tech officials say the assurances from Trump and Nvidia’s board do little to dispel their concerns. “A sophisticated individual should not have fallen prey to these advances,” a senior government official who focuses on AI issues tells Fast Company.
‘Obvious targets for espionage’
The concern extends beyond Jensen Huang’s call for U.S. regulators to clear advanced chips to China. It’s possible Alice Huang may have picked up classified information—anything Trump might have mentioned—from the Nvidia CEO, given his close relationship with the president, suggests David Nesting, a former White House AI and cybersecurity official. Alice Huang may have had access to Nvidia information, too. (CHIPS for America, the Commerce Department initiative overseeing federal investments in semiconductor manufacturing and research, did not respond to Fast Company’s request for comment.)
“His home, office, electronic devices, and private and professional communications would all be attractive intelligence targets,” Nesting tells Fast Company. “China would be enormously interested in Nvidia’s intellectual property, technology, road maps, supply chain, and how they might circumvent their export controls.”
People who have sensitive and classified information are supposed to protect that information, but also work with security officials and alert them to anyone who might be asking about it, the senior government official says. “This is not just personal foibles,” the official adds. “Nvidia’s power and integral role in AI means that all decisions made by Mr. Huang inure [accrue] not only to Nvidia but to the AI industry.”
