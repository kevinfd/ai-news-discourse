# Forget Meta’s Tamagotchi-Like Charm: Your Smartwatch Does the Same Thing

PCMag · James Peckham · 2026-11-10

Meta’s Muse has been many people’s first experience with agentic AI, and it’s now even easier to interact with if you have a compatible smartwatch. Meta first announced the smartwatch rollout on Threads with a simple post that read, “Found a new place to hang out,” in the voice of the tool's mascot, Jolly.
It's available now on Apple Watch or Wear OS devices, if you have Meta Muse set up on your phone. You need watchOS 9 or later on Apple Watch, while compatibility restrictions for Wear OS are less clear.
It works as a companion app, letting you interact with the assistant without opening your phone. The default version shows Jolly as part of a clock face, and you can then choose to interact with a back-and-forth voice conversation, text message, or voice recording.
Resulting actions from Muse then appear in a chat-like interface so you can task it with new projects, hear updates on what it has done so far, or discuss wider topics like you were messaging a friend from your wrist.
Agentic AI tools are becoming more commonplace, but few are available on smartwatches. Interestingly, this looks similar to the gadget teased at Meta Connect in September, when CEO Mark Zuckerberg said it would offer a dedicated Tamagotchi-like device for accessing its Muse agent.
The device is expected to be available before the end of the year, and it’s called the Muse Charm. The user interface looks similar to the smartwatch app, but it instead works as a Muse-exclusive device without access to other software.
Muse has proven a success for Meta, with Sensor Tower data suggesting it hit over 10 million downloads in its first 40 days. PCMag's own testing has found ChatGPT's Dots agent works better than Muse for everyday tasks, but access costs more, with OpenAI charging a minimum of $100 per month.
