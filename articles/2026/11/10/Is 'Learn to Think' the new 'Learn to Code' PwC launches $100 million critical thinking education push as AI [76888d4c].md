# Is 'Learn to Think' the new 'Learn to Code'? PwC launches $100 million critical thinking education push as AI threatens consulting revenue and quality

news.bloombergtax.com · SpiritRealistic8174 · 2026-11-10

PwC’s US arm is launching a critical thinking certification for its more than 85,000 partners and staff as artificial intelligence tools transform the professional services firm’s everyday tasks.
The program includes personalized simulations to help people practice what they learn. An employee, for instance, could be tasked with reviewing an AI-generated assessment of a company’s internal controls for assurance, identifying any deficiencies, and challenging the tool’s conclusions, PwC US Chief People and Inclusion Officer Yolanda Seals-Coffield told Bloomberg Tax.
PwC and its Big Four accounting and advisory industry competitors EY, Deloitte, and KPMG are baking AI — including next-generation autonomous tools — into workflows. They also are overhauling their talent strategies to focus on AI and traditional business skills alike.
“As our people start to use AI in their daily work and are embedding it in the way that they deliver for our clients, we want to make sure that they are keeping critical thinking and judgment at the forefront of those deliveries,” PwC’s Seals-Coffield said.
The firm’s new certification program aims to help workers navigate complex decisions by providing a repeatable approach to evaluating information and exercising judgment, especially when AI is involved.
The firm is rolling out the program beginning next month across its US and Mexico offices, as well as its offices in countries like India that house the firm’s offshore services. The launch is slated to be complete by June 2027.
Beginning early next year, PwC plans to make its framework available to universities that want to integrate it into their lesson plans. It also plans to fund selected universities to support professors in bringing the framework into introductory courses.
The firm estimates more than $100 million in total investment, including program development costs and funding for universities.
The new certification program begins with a diagnostic exercise to help participants understand their thinking style, strengths, and opportunities for growth. The goal is to help people discover what type of critical thinker they are naturally, Seals-Coffield said.
“If your thinking style is an analyst, for example, you may check all the inputs for reliability, but you may dive into the details so quickly before deciding how much checking the work actually needs,” Seals-Coffield said.
The certification fits within PwC’s broader learning and development opportunities related to the responsible use of AI tools, she said.
“Our goal for our people is to understand that all of this works together,” Seals-Coffield said. “Things are not isolated and disconnected activities.”
