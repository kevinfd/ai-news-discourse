# From FinOps To TokenOps: The New Economics Of Enterprise AI Infrastructure

Forbes · Gouri Sankar Dash, Forbes Councils Member · 2026-11-10

Gouri Sankar Dash, a technology leader at TCS with 20+ years driving enterprise data, AI platforms and multimillion-dollar transformations.
For years, enterprise infrastructure leaders have focused on optimizing compute, storage, networks and cloud consumption. FinOps introduced financial accountability to cloud infrastructure, helping organizations manage spending and improve efficiency.
AI is introducing another economic dimension. As enterprises adopt GenAI and autonomous agents, infrastructure costs increasingly depend on model selection, inference workloads, token consumption and agent interactions.
The next frontier of infrastructure optimization isn’t simply managing the cost of computing but understanding the cost of intelligence.
This article begins a series on enterprise AI infrastructure economics, examining how AI consumption is reshaping enterprise infrastructure economics, architecture and operations. I believe this evolution calls for an expanded operating model: TokenOps.
From Infrastructure Consumption To Intelligence Consumption
Traditional infrastructure optimization measures CPU utilization, memory, storage and cloud spending. AI introduces additional variables: input and output tokens, context windows, model selection, retrieval operations and inference infrastructure.
Consider an AI agent investigating an infrastructure incident. It may analyze monitoring alerts, retrieve historical tickets, examine configurations, consult runbooks and coordinate with other agents before recommending remediation. Every interaction consumes resources. Without proper orchestration, organizations risk creating another form of infrastructure sprawl through unnecessary AI interactions and repeated reasoning.
FinOps remains essential, but enterprises must also understand how AI consumption translates into operational value. A million tokens consumed is a cost metric. An incident resolved accurately and efficiently is a business outcome. Understanding that relationship is where TokenOps begins.
What One Regulated Enterprise Got Right
A recent walkthrough with a large regulated enterprise reinforced an important lesson: Don’t start with the AI agent. Start with the operational foundation, knowledge and controls required for reliable decisions.
The enterprise prioritized integrating telemetry, service management data, access controls, automation, runbooks, incident history and engineering knowledge. Its technology ecosystem included ServiceNow for workflows, Amazon Bedrock for AI capabilities, Dynatrace, Datadog and Amazon CloudWatch for operational signals and Ansible for controlled automation.
Specialized agents supported event correlation, incident diagnosis, change-risk assessment, remediation planning and engineering assistance. Each agent had a defined responsibility. Low-risk activities followed approved automation paths, while high-impact changes required engineering authorization.
The architectural lesson wasn’t the number of agents deployed but how effectively operational knowledge, intelligence and controlled execution worked together. Well-structured knowledge and reusable workflows can reduce redundant investigations, repeated retrieval and unnecessary AI interactions.
This is where intelligent operations and token economics converge.
The Five Dimensions Of TokenOps
I view TokenOps as an operating discipline that extends financial accountability into AI consumption, infrastructure efficiency and measurable business outcomes. Five dimensions form its foundation:
1. Consumption Visibility: Track token usage, model costs, agent interactions and infrastructure consumption across enterprise workflows.
2. Intelligent Workload Optimization: Match model capabilities to task complexity. Routine classification may require smaller models, while complex incident analysis may justify advanced reasoning capabilities.
3. Knowledge And Context Efficiency: Optimize retrieval, caching and context management to avoid repeatedly processing unnecessary information.
4. Governance And Controlled Autonomy: Establish access boundaries, approval thresholds, auditability and rollback mechanisms. Expand autonomous execution only when operational evidence supports it.
5. Outcome-Based Economics: Measure cost per resolved incident, resolution time, repeat incidents, engineering effort and operational reliability.
The objective is maximizing the value that AI-enabled workflows generate.
Five Practical Steps For Infrastructure Leaders
Enterprises can begin applying TokenOps principles without redesigning their entire infrastructure.
• Start with operational friction. Target recurring incidents, alert noise, slow diagnosis and excessive handoffs.
• Build a reliable knowledge foundation. Integrate telemetry, configuration information, service models, historical incidents and runbooks.
• Earn autonomy incrementally. Begin with AI-assisted investigation and recommendations before introducing approved remediation.
• Redesign workflows, not tools. Embed AI capabilities into existing engineering and service management processes.
• Measure outcomes alongside consumption. Evaluate AI costs against reliability, resolution efficiency and engineering productivity.
These practices help prevent AI investments from becoming another unmanaged operational expense.
Why TokenOps Changes Infrastructure Economics
Enterprise infrastructure economics is evolving through three interconnected stages:
• Traditional Infrastructure Economics: Optimizing physical assets, capacity and technology investments
• FinOps Economics: Managing consumption-based cloud spending and financial accountability
• TokenOps Economics: Optimizing AI consumption, intelligent workloads and measurable business outcomes
TokenOps complements FinOps rather than replacing it. Its implications extend beyond token pricing. Model placement, GPU utilization, caching, network latency, data gravity and hybrid-cloud architecture can significantly influence AI operating costs.
A workload that appears economical under traditional infrastructure metrics may become expensive when repeated inference, extensive retrieval and multi-agent orchestration are considered. Infrastructure leaders must evaluate not only where applications run but also where intelligence should be generated.
What’s Next: Building The Token-Aware Enterprise
The future of enterprise AI will depend less on how many agents organizations deploy and more on how efficiently those agents operate.
Should every AI request use the same model? When should inference run on-premises rather than in the public cloud? Can intelligent caching eliminate unnecessary processing? How will token economics influence data center migration decisions?
These are becoming infrastructure strategy questions.
How can organizations connect AI agents, observability, model routing, knowledge systems and financial controls into a practical operating architecture? I plan on exploring that in the next part of this series. The ultimate goal is building infrastructure that delivers the right intelligence, in the right place, at the right time and at a justifiable cost.
