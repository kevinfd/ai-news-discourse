# Firm embroiled in Nvidia scandal linked to Australian newsagent worker

ABC News Australia · Jasper Wells · 2026-11-10

Megaspeed Australia is registered as a subsidiary of Megaspeed, the Singapore-based firm linked to the scandal engulfing Nvidia's CEO.
A 29-year-old man living in regional Victoria is listed as the subsidiary's director, but his father told ABC News he had never heard of Megaspeed.
ASIC filings suggest mail to Megaspeed Australia's business address in Martin Place was unable to be delivered in January.
The Australian subsidiary of the firm at the centre of the alleged Nvidia spy scandal lists a newsagent worker as its sole director.
Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company whose former executive Alice Huang has been accused of being a Chinese spy, has a subsidiary, Megaspeed Australia, according to ASIC filings.
Kai Kang, a Shanghai-born 29-year-old man, is listed as the director of Megaspeed Australia, which was registered in 2024.
On Monday, Jensen Huang, the chief executive of the world's most valuable company, Nvidia, admitted to having an affair with Ms Huang (no familial relation).
Mr Kang's father told ABC News his son worked at his store in a small rural town in Northern Victoria.
He said he had never heard of Megaspeed and was unaware that his store was being used as his son's listed address as director.
Megaspeed Australia's business address is registered more than 700 kilometres away, in Sydney's Martin Place.
It shares a floor at 20 Martin Place with more than 40 other businesses, including migration lawyers, accountants and a private investigator.
An employee at a migration law firm that shares the address with Megaspeed said they had never heard of the company or Mr Kang.
ASIC filings indicated mail sent by the regulator to Megaspeed Australia in January this year were unable to be delivered to the 20 Martin Place address.
Space in the building, which is located near the ASX, can be rented out from $17 a day per person.
The company does not appear to have any listed financial assets or to have completed any work in Australia since opening in 2024, and it is unclear if there are any other employees or staff.
Mr Kang was unable to be reached for comment. He is understood to be out of the country and is not accused of any wrongdoing.
FBI investigating potentially compromised state secrets
US President Donald Trump said the FBI was investigating the affair, and whether any state secrets had been compromised.
Megaspeed International, a low-profile company until now, is a buyer of Nvidia's microchips, which are used to build data centres that then rent computing power to buyers.
According to short-seller Culper Research, China is one of those buyers.
