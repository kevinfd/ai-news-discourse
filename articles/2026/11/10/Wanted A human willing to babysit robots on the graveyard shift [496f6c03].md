# Wanted: A human willing to babysit robots on the graveyard shift

Business Insider · Sarah E. Needleman, Sarah E. Needleman (sneedleman@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-10

A worker performs maintenance on a humanoid robot at a Figure AI facility in 2023. Jae C. Hong/AP Have you ever wanted to boss around robots? This hourly job may be for you. Figure AI is hiring a night-shift coordinator to oversee its robots and human operators. Figure envisions robots eventually replacing human labor, but for now, it still needs people in charge. A new kind of babysitter is wanted in Silicon Valley . The robotics startup Figure AI is looking to hire a night-shift coordinator in Santa Clara to oversee its humanoid robots alongside the humans who operate them. The contract-to-hire job entails scheduling and coaching the team, troubleshooting robot failures and safety issues, and sometimes operating the machines themselves, according to a recently posted ad for the position . It pays $40 to $45 an hour, runs from 10:30 p.m. to 7 a.m., and includes a weekend shift. The role is also physically demanding. The coordinator will be on their feet most of the time and must be able to safely lift, carry, and maneuver equipment or humanoid hardware weighing up to 50 pounds, the posting says. The unusual career opportunity comes as a growing number of startups race to bring humanoid robots into homes and businesses. Last week, one of Figure's rivals, Agility Robotics , went public through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company. Investors poured $33.4 billion into robotics and physical AI companies worldwide in the first half of this year, data from PitchBook show. That is well above the $28.7 billion that the sector raised in all of 2025, and a huge jump from 2020, when such companies raised just under $5 billion. Figure's opening for a night-shift coordinator isn't the only human role that it's advertising to keep its robots humming . The company is also seeking humanoid robot operators to work directly with its machines to test and validate new behaviors and skills. Founded in 2022, Figure said a little over a year ago that it was valued at $39 billion after securing $1 billion through a Series C fundraising round led by Parkway Venture Capital. Its website says Figure is focused on providing resources for jobs that humans don't want to perform. It's starting with roles in industries such as manufacturing, shipping and logistics, warehousing, and retail, where it says labor shortages are especially acute. The company expects its machines initially to handle structured, repetitive work before eventually taking on more complex tasks. "Robots that can think, learn, reason, and interact with their environments will eventually be capable of performing tasks better than humans," Figure's website says. "Over time, humans could leave the loop altogether as robots become capable of building other robots — driving prices down even more." For now, though, Figure still wants a night-shift coordinator with a pulse. "You'll set priorities, keep robots running for real commercial use cases, solve problems as they come up, and hand the day team a clear, accurate picture of what happened overnight," the job posting says.
