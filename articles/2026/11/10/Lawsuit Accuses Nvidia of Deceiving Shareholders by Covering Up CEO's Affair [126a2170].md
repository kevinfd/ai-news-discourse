# Lawsuit Accuses Nvidia of Deceiving Shareholders by Covering Up CEO's Affair

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-10

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with a suspected Chinese spy has sparked a class-action lawsuit that claims the company misled investors by failing to disclose the relationship.
Rosen Law Firm, which specializes in securities class actions, announced the lawsuit a day after Nvidia’s CEO publicly admitted to an affair with Alice Huang, an executive with Singapore-based cloud computing provider Megaspeed who was allegedly recruited by China as a spy.
The lawsuit accuses Nvidia of making false and misleading statements about Jensen Huang’s relationship with Alice. For months, Nvidia downplayed the relationship, only for its CEO to then admit to the affair this past weekend following more press coverage.
In the 19-page complaint, Rosen Law Firm cites a New York Times article from October 2025 that questioned Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang and whether Megaspeed was helping China obtain sensitive AI chips.
At the time, an Nvidia spokesperson said nothing out of the ordinary was occurring. Nvidia’s PR said the same last month, despite The New York Times reporting that US intelligence had concluded Alice Huang was a “Chinese intelligence asset."
Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly warned President Trump about the situation in a classified memo this spring. However, in a press briefing on Monday, Trump declined to say whether he'd read that memo, adding, "Certainly didn't make much of an impact on me."
The class-action lawsuit requests a jury trial and damages for anyone who bought Nvidia stock between Oct. 9, 2025, and Nov. 8, 2026. The complaint also argues Nvidia’s stock became inflated because executives, or at least Jensen Huang, "acted with reckless disregard for the truth when they failed to ascertain and disclose the true facts."
You can request to join the class-action on Rosen's website.
The case might be the first of several class-action lawsuits targeting Nvidia, which continues to face questions over its CEO’s relationship with Alice Huang. US lawmakers are already discussing launching probes amid concerns that Nvidia's CEO may have been pressured to push Washington to allow AI chip sales to China. Currently, China is restricted from obtaining Nvidia’s most powerful enterprise GPUs, which Jensen Huang has argued against.
Nvidia didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment. But the company’s board has opened its own investigation.
