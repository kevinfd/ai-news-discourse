# Apple Now Charges for High AI Use In Logic Pro, Debuts New Credit System

PCMag · James Peckham · 2026-11-10

Apple has openly shared its plans to charge customers for intensive AI use, and its first restriction has now arrived. It’s first coming to Apple Creator Studio tools, launching in Logic Pro but working differently from the subscriptions of many rival AI tools.
Apple Intelligence access in Logic Pro will remain free for limited use, and then switch you over to paid access when you run out of capacity. Most services offer a monthly subscription, but Apple instead uses a refill-like system that rolls over unused credits to the next month.
Logic Pro now shows a new tracker to help you follow your monthly restrictions, located under Intelligence Features > Show Usage. It also comes with a button called Buy More Usage, which costs $49.99 for what Apple calls its “intelligence usage pack.”
This will increase your AI access, although it’s unclear how long a $49.99 payment will last you, as it will be dependent on what you use it for. If you have a remaining balance at the end of a month, your free usage will be replenished and used before switching back to the paid credits system.
Any usage packs will continue to roll over month to month until 12 months after initial purchase, when they’ll disappear altogether. Interestingly, Apple won’t be supporting Family Sharing with these tools, restricting access to the sole account that made the original purchase.
Apple has yet to confirm which apps will get this system next, but if it expands to other Creator Studio apps, it may come to Final Cut Pro, Pixelmator Pro, and the iWork suite in the near future. From there, Apple may then choose to bring it to its other consumer-friendly tools, such as Photos editing or Image Playground.
Many AI tools are beginning to crack down on free use to profit from what was previously offered without limitations. Samsung recently began testing a monthly charge for access to select Galaxy AI features, while AI chatbots have also begun retooling usage, such as Google Gemini restricting which models free users can use.
