# The Jensen Huang spy scandal is becoming a White House problem

Fast Company · Mark Sullivan · 2026-11-09

The scandal engulfing Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is no longer simply a question of whether one of Silicon Valley’s most powerful men had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy. It’s also a question of what the White House knew—and what it might have chosen to overlook.
Huang and Nvidia confirmed the relationship Sunday, following a bombshell New York Times report. Alice Huang (no relation to Jensen)—the woman at the center of the revelations—is an executive at Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed, a major customer of Nvidia chips. U.S. intelligence surveillance reportedly showed that she had been funneling information directly to the Chinese government.
What remains unclear is how much sensitive information may have been compromised, what Huang knew about the woman he was seeing, and why U.S. intelligence officials apparently failed to warn him about her suspected ties to Beijing. The answers could prove deeply damaging to both Huang and the White House.
“It was pretty clear to a lot of people that [Jensen Huang] would be a target of Chinese intelligence collection,” given his proximity to President Donald Trump and his leadership of a company that is “central to the U.S.-China AI competition,” says Sam Bresnick, a research fellow at Georgetown University’s Center for Security and Emerging Technology.
Nvidia’s changing story
The admission of the affair also contradicts how Nvidia and Huang himself had previously characterized his ties to Alice Huang. As recently as October 29, company spokesperson John Rizzo described their interactions as routine business dealings, while Huang insisted they were “completely normal and not unusual.”
Huang now faces an investigation by Nvidia’s board, although directors said in a statement Sunday that they had “no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised” and expressed “confidence in Jensen and his leadership team.”
The board’s investigation may be the least of Huang’s worries. The powerful CEO faces the prospect of bipartisan congressional scrutiny, along with mounting questions about his influence in Washington and his future at Nvidia.
