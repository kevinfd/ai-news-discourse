# Elon Musk's empire braces for Democratic crackdown

Axios · Zachary Basu · 2026-11-09

Elon Musk's sprawling empire — from rockets to robots, electric cars to AI — is facing a political onslaught with few parallels in modern corporate history. Why it matters: The world's richest man confronts a constellation of risks spanning the halls of Congress, his Texas industrial empire, Wall Street and his vision for AI supremacy. 💰 Political: To Democrats, Musk embodies corrosive forces of the MAGA era — extreme wealth, billionaire influence over government, the gutting of federal institutions, the poisoning of social media and the unchecked power of AI. Musk spent at least $475 million on the midterms, becoming the election's largest individual donor — only to see more than 99% of his traceable spending go to losing candidates, according to WIRED . Musk's most humiliating defeat came in Texas, where a $160 million bet on Ken Paxton ended in a Democratic sweep of the Senate seat, governorship and attorney general's office. In a hint of a possible détente, Sen.-elect James Talarico praised the billionaire as a "great business leader and entrepreneur," and offered to work with him on technology and innovation. Musk, who said last month that Talarico "hates Texas," responded : "Nice of him to say that." ⚖️ Congressional: Armed with subpoena power, Democrats are preparing to investigate Musk's DOGE operation, its access to sensitive government data and whether his companies benefited from his extraordinary influence over the Trump administration. Musk is already downplaying his dependence on Washington, claiming government contracts will account for less than 5% of SpaceX revenue a year from now. "Happy to cancel any that the government doesn't want," he posted Friday . The scrutiny also extends to national security: Lawmakers are demanding answers about Musk's reported communications with Vladimir Putin and his role in the October slowdown of Starshield that disrupted Ukraine's drone operations. Elon Musk responds to reporting about his vulnerabilities in Texas. Screenshot via X 🏭 Regulatory: Musk moved his home and the headquarters of Tesla, SpaceX and X to Texas, betting on a safe haven for his expanding empire. Now, the Democratic takeover threatens to turn his political sanctuary into a regulatory minefield. Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa is suing to uncover 1,374 pages of largely redacted communications with Greg Abbott, while incoming Attorney General Nathan Johnson has pledged to scrutinize state broadband grants awarded to Starlink. Tesla's robotaxi ambitions also face new obstacles as Democrats threaten to derail federal legislation that eases state safety restrictions. Hinojosa herself urged Tesla last year to delay its Austin launch until stronger regulations took effect. 📉 Financial: Tesla and SpaceX shed roughly $460 billion in combined market value in the two days after the election, then rebounded Friday as investors raced to reassess the risks to Musk's business empire. Wall Street is especially concerned that Democratic scrutiny could complicate a potential Tesla-SpaceX merger , while new restrictions on AI data centers threaten a cornerstone of Musk's growth strategy. In a fitting coda to a brutal week, Musk announced Friday that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production by mid-2027, pulling the plug on a pet project plagued by weak sales, recalls and quality problems. 🤖 Technological: The midterms have given the backlash against AI new political muscle, threatening both the massive data centers powering Musk's ambitions and the technology behind Grok itself. Democrats are preparing legislation that would require independent safety testing of advanced AI models and hold developers liable for harmful outputs. Lawmakers have already demanded SpaceX's AI safety records. Voters sided against data-center development in 44 of 45 local referendums , while incoming Democratic governors in Georgia and Ohio pledged tougher restrictions. Musk joked on X: "Data centers in space looking pretty appealing right now." The bottom line: Democrats must find a way to separate Musk the political combatant from Musk the industrialist, whose companies are central to America's technological and military power.
