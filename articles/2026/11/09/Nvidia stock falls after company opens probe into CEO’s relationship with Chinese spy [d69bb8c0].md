# Nvidia stock falls after company opens probe into CEO’s relationship with Chinese spy

NBC News · nbcnews · 2026-11-09

It was a great day for stocks. Just not Nvidia’s.
The company’s shares slid 4.2% on Monday after it announced that it had opened an investigation into CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with a woman who U.S. intelligence agencies say is a Chinese spy.
Despite Nvidia’s importance to the AI trade, the news affected almost no other stocks. The Nasdaq 100, which tracks the top tech stocks at the Nasdaq exchange, soared 3.4%. The broader S&P 500 rose 2.3%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose more than 1,100 points. Stocks were also propelled higher by a more than 4% drop in oil prices Monday on news that U.S.--Iran talks were continuing.
Nvidia’s peers, such as AMD, Qualcomm, Taiwan Semi and Intel, all rose along with the broader market. Nvidia was the biggest loser on the Dow and the S&P 500.
The investigation has the potential to unsettle the entire AI boom, fueled by trillions of dollars of spending and investments, in part to purchase Nvidia’s cutting-edge AI chips. Nvidia’s drop wiped out almost $200 billion in value.
Huang, who founded the company more than three decades ago, admitted in a note to employees that he had been unfaithful to his wife. “To my colleagues at NVIDIA, including those I have worked alongside for decades, and to our board, partners, and shareholders around the world: you deserve better from me,” Huang’s email also said.
In recent days, the Financial Times and The New York Times reported that Huang had a relationship with a Chinese businesswoman named Alice Huang. A person with knowledge of the matter told NBC News that U.S. intelligence agencies had determined that Alice Huang was a Chinese spy. The two Huangs are unrelated.

China has dismissed the espionage allegations as “smears.” The company where Alice Huang is alleged to work, Megaspeed, did not reply to requests for comment.
Nvidia said in a statement Sunday that independent directors and counsel will oversee the probe. “The Board prioritizes U.S. national and economic security, which is paramount to the interests of NVIDIA and its shareholders,” it said.
An Nvidia spokesperson told NBC News that Huang was “committed to continuing to lead NVIDIA as CEO without interruption.”
Before the Huang revelations, the tech industry was on edge, with every headline about AI-linked companies sending stocks either soaring or tumbling. Just last week, tech stocks surged after it became clear on election night that the Democrats would win majorities in Congress. But stocks plunged the next day. On Thursday, stocks suffered their worst one-day drop since President Donald Trump rocked the global economy with his initial wave of tariffs in April 2025.

Given that there are few more important companies to the tech sector overall, investors and even market watchers not invested in Nvidia shares will be closely following the investigation.
There are still many unanswered questions. On Sunday, Nvidia’s board said it had “no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised.” But it did not provide a timeline for how fast the probe would be conducted or any other details about Huang’s contact with Alice Huang.
Jensen Huang’s influence
Jensen Huang has become one of the main forces driving the global AI push while steering Nvidia to a market value of more than $4 trillion. He has led Nvidia since he founded it in 1993, so it would be a seismic change if he were to leave the role.
Huang is the largest individual holder of shares, outside of investment funds, and he is not protected from removal if the board decides on it. His stake in shares of Nvidia is worth about 3.5% of its shares outstanding, but unlike other major tech firms, like Meta, Nvidia has just one class of shares.
Trump on Sunday gave Huang his support, which likely reduces the chance that he is removed as CEO in the near term. “I just know he’s a fine person, but you never know what is going on,” he said.
Trump was asked whether Huang should remain CEO, to which he replied: “I think so. Oh yeah, sure.”
With so many outstanding questions about the scandal, few major analysts who cover Nvidia had weighed in Monday morning about what it could mean for the company’s future.
Those who did said it was a blemish on an otherwise sterling track record, which could spark even more investigations.
“This is a black eye moment for Jensen and will spark multiple congressional inquiries at a minimum and will create an overhang on the stock for a myriad of reasons,” wrote Dan Ives, of Yorkville Ives. “There is clearly risk that Jensen could be removed as CEO depending on the Board investigation and this will add risk to the stock as he is so important to the Nvidia story and strategy globally.”
Chip analyst Stacy Rasgon of Bernstein Research summed up the gravity of the revelations: “NVIDIA is not an ordinary company and Jensen is not an ordinary CEO; he IS NVIDIA.”
