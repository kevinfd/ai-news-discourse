# Exclusive: Donald Trump Jr. signals investors underestimated AI pushback

Axios · Avery Lotz · 2026-11-17

Donald Trump Jr. signaled that investors underestimated the political pushback to the AI ecosystem in an interview at Axios' annual BFD summit in New York. The big picture: The 2026 midterms reflected deep distrust of the AI race, with voters approving dozens of moratoriums on data centers — which President Trump rallied around. Driving the news: Trump Jr. asserted that "a lot of" the pushback "is manufactured." But he added "that doesn't mean it's not real" in an interview with Axios Pro Rata author Dan Primack Tuesday. "I think some of the big founders of the biggest [AI] companies out there probably have not done themselves the best service as [a] marketing department," he said, adding that they're "probably not the best messengers when they basically say, 'AI is gonna rule the world, and you should be thrilled about it.'" Asked by Primack if investors, peers or the pair themselves underestimated the populist pushback to AI, Trump Jr. said, "I think we saw that very clearly." He likened AI to other "any great innovation," but said, "This is gonna be one of those things, but it is going to take some time for people to learn how to adapt that into their regular lives." State of play: Trump Jr. spoke with Primack alongside 1789 Capital co-founder Omeed Malik, whose venture Trump Jr. joined in 2024. The investment firm has since exploded from overseeing a few hundred million dollars to billions. Zoom in: Trump Jr. agreed that the rage toward data centers represents a broader concern over growing AI influence. That public anger is becoming key to candidates: Democrats in six early 2028 primary states expect their presidential nominee to prioritize AI regulations, according to new polling data first reported by Axios' Maria Curi. Ahead of the midterms, Republicans took note of the sagging views of the data centers needed to power the AI boom. While Trump has prioritized self-regulation for the industry, the November results raise questions about how Republicans will tackle the tech moving forward. Zoom out: Asked if there was anything Democrats and the White House could agree on after a November shellacking that could be good for business, he said "we've got to focus" on costs and inflation. But he argued that "the second they win ... it's about investigations" — which Trump Jr. could find himself targeted by. Editor's note: This story was updated with additional context throughout.
