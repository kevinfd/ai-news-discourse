# Nvidia's Kress faces earnings test after stock drags Wall Street into the red

CNBC · Leonie Kidd · 2026-11-17

This is CNBC's Morning Squawk newsletter. Subscribe here to receive future editions in your inbox.
Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.
Futures are struggling to hold the flatline after a negative start to the week for Wall Street.
It could be a wait-and-see session as investors await Nvidia earnings after the bell today, while Disney results hit the wire first thing.
Happening now: Earnings anticipation
Another day, another Nvidia moment.
Nvidia reports earnings after the bell in a key milestone for new interim CEO Colette Kress. Analysts expect the group to report a 90% jump in revenue to around $109 billion. Investors will be focused on how Nvidia addresses its exposure to China. Short seller Culper Research claims as much as 20% of Nvidia's fiscal 2026 compute revenue was "driven by China."
- Scoop: CNBC's Kai Nicol-Schwarz reports that Nvidia has warned its customers that new legislation on Chinese companies remotely accessing its artificial-intelligence chips could expose it and them to U.S. "legal action." That's according to sources. You can read more here.
- Exclusive: CNBC's Luke Fountain has done a deep dive on how Trump's investment accounts have traded Nvidia, having bought and sold the shares at least 103 times in transactions worth up to $97.5 million.
Monday marked another rough day for Nvidia shares, with a 4.1% fall dragging the Nasdaq sharply lower.
CNBC has analysis across the AI trade:
- Crunch time: CNBC's Sean Conlon has analysed the latest 13F filing for Situational Awareness. He found Leopold Aschenbrenner's artificial intelligence-themed hedge fund loaded back up on AI stocks despite its near-collapse in July. All those holdings are under pressure since that fresh positioning. For a detailed breakdown, read more here.
- Coming up: Steve Eisman of "The Big Short" fame will join Squawk Box this morning. Last month, he told CNBC that he is getting more conservative with his portfolio, saying the AI trade is nearing a "tipping point."
Big Movers: Deutsche Bank leak
Deutsche Bank shares are trading sharply lower, down as much as 6%, after a raft of leaked documents appear to show the German lender accidentally leaked Donald Trump's tax statements.
According to an exposé led by the Financial Times, CEO Christian Sewing described the 2017 incident as an "awful mistake."
The report also claims that documents show Deutsche Bank processed almost $600 million of suspicious payments linked to Jeffrey Epstein, far higher than previously disclosed.
- Deutsche Bank's statement: "The matters which are in focus of the media reports date back many years, in some cases as much as two decades. Where relevant, we reviewed these matters at the time and worked closely with regulators... Deutsche Bank is a different bank today..."
In early premarket trading, Nvidia shares are slightly lower alongside the other chipmakers.
The outlier is Intel, with shares dipping in early trade. It extends a 3% drop after hours following the news that Nvidia has cut its stake in the company by 25%.
Earnings Watch: Disney
Disney is due to report earnings before the bell, with some suggesting the company may be turning the corner.
Wall Street expects earnings per share of $1.67 and revenue of $25.2 billion, according to LSEG. Investors will be focused on how CEO John D'Amaro is reviewing the group's strategy after a series of widespread job cuts.
- Analysis: Portfolio Manager and CNBC Contributor Mike Khouw says, "Driven by a renewed focus on the bottom line, management's cost discipline now supports a runway for double-digit earnings-per-share (EPS) gains through fiscal 2027, even as it continues to trade at about its 5-year average forward multiple of 14.3x, well below the broad market multiple of more than 18x."
IPO countdown: Anthropic
Anthropic is expected to price its IPO on Wednesday, with shares scheduled to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday.
Institutional demand is expected to eclipse SpaceX, which went public in June in a record $86 billion IPO.
- IPO guide: CNBC has compiled everything you need to know about the IPO here.
- "Cornerstone Compounder": Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner is planning to invest $5 billion in Anthropic's IPO, which is five times more than he has ever placed in a listing.
- Profile: Anthropic's CFO Krishna Rao has helped the AI group raise billions of dollars and is being described as its Wall Street whisperer; for more details, you can read on here.
Shaken not stirred: Amazon's Bond bet
You may have thought the debate over who would lead Nvidia would be the most-watched race this week.
But there is another "Who's Next?" that has dominated the headlines and social media.
Now, the wait is over, after Robert Pattinson was named as the new James Bond. He will star in the 26th film for the franchise, and the first since Amazon MGM took control.
- Why it matters: In 2022, Amazon bought MGM Studios for around $8.4 billion. Together, it then acquired the creative control of the James Bond rights in 2025 from longtime producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson.
- Goldfinger: The total worldwide box office revenue generated by the franchise over its history is estimated to total around $7 billion.
- Coming up: Catch Halftime Report's exclusive interview with Amazon CEO Andy Jassy, later today.
