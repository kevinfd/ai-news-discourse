# Jensen Huang says he would be ‘honored’ to return to Nvidia as CEO

The Guardian · Blake Montgomery and Johana Bhuiyan · 2026-11-17

Jensen Huang said he would be willing to return to Nvidia as CEO in his first remarks since resigning from the company on Thursday.
“If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” he wrote in a statement published in the Wall Street Journal and CNBC.
Nvidia’s board of directors is not considering reinstating Huang, according to reports from the Financial Times and Wall Street Journal.
The CEO founded Nvidia in 1993 and ran the company until last week, when he resigned over affairs with an alleged Chinese spy and employees who reported to him.
Huang and Nvidia initially said the executive’s dealings with Alice Huang, a former executive at the Singaporean datacenter firm Megaspeed and a suspected spy, were a routine customer relationship. However, Huang disclosed the affair in an email to employees, and the board announced an investigation into its national security implications .
Nvidia’s board has also created a committee to oversee exports and national security issues.
The US government confirmed on Tuesday morning that the commerce and justice departments had been investigating Megaspeed since 2024. Though the government has never officially restricted sales and exports to Megaspeed, Nvidia put Megaspeed on an internal “no sell” list in June 2025 after the commerce department requested its records of any sales to Megaspeed, according to a statement from the department. Megaspeed is suspected of diverting US advanced AI chips to Chinese firms in potential violation of export restrictions.
Huang’s full statement is published below.
 Over the past week, I’ve been overwhelmed by the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I’ve never met. Thank you to everyone who has shown kindness to my family and me. It means the world to me.
I came to the United States 53 years ago, and becoming an American citizen is one of the greatest gifts and proudest moments of my life. NVIDIA has been my life’s work. Building it into the company it is today alongside my colleagues has been a privilege and an honor. Nowhere but in America could a company like NVIDIA be possible. I love our country, and I love NVIDIA.
I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life. I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation.
Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.
Thank you again to everyone who has shared their good wishes. I am profoundly humbled by your support. To my family: I love you, and I will spend the rest of my life being worthy of yours.
