# Nvidia sets up committee to oversee national security and export controls

Euronews · Pascale Davies · 2026-11-17

“Nvidia recognizes that advanced silicon technologies and the emergence of artificial and superintelligence present unprecedented challenges for national security,” Nvidia chairperson Harvey Jones said.
Nvidia's board has established a national security and export control oversight committee, which comes amid growing scrutiny from the United States over how the artificial intelligence giant handled exports of its chips to companies linked to China.
Nvidia's cofounder and CEO Jensen Huang resigned last week after multiple extramarital affairs were revealed, including with an alleged Chinese spy.
Huang and Trump had formed a close relationship, with Huang becoming a key voice for the US on artificial intelligence while he also lobbied for allowing chip exports to China.
“NVIDIA recognises that advanced silicon technologies and the emergence of artificial and superintelligence present unprecedented challenges for national security," said Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia's board of directors.
He added the company "has both a responsibility and an opportunity to lead the industry in addressing these challenges," and that it was "committed to working constructively with government and industry partners to help establish and advance a robust framework that uses every tool at our disposal to safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation”.
The new committee will oversee the company's national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management, Nvidia said in a statement
The panel will meet quarterly, including with management and in executive session, and retain independent outside experts in law, national security and trade compliance, the company added.
Nvidia launched an investigation into Huang's relationship with the alleged Chinese spy. The board said last week that it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets had been compromised.
Meanwhile, on Monday, the US Commerce Department unveiled an interim final rule targeting remote access to advanced computing chips and servers through overseas data centres.
