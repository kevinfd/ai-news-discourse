# Cognition’s new revenue chief says he came out of retirement because golf and advising was ‘not fulfilling’

Business Insider · Shubhangi Goel, Shubhangi Goel (sgoel@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-17

Cognition's new revenue chief shares how he landed at the AI coding startup after retiring. Illustration by Thomas Fuller/SOPA Images/LightRocket via Getty Images Chris Degnan ended his early retirement to join Cognition as CRO. He said that he was not as happy as he expected to be advising startups and playing golf. His move to Cognition sparked a clash with Factory, a rival AI coding startup. Chris Degnan thought he was done with operating jobs after leaving Snowflake. But golf and startup advisory work left him wanting more. “I was done,” Degnan, Cognition’s new chief revenue officer, said on an episode of Kleiner Perkins’ podcast released on Monday. “No intent to take an operating job — none.” But after 18 months away from full time work, he said he was not as happy as he expected to be. “It’s all well and good that I could go and play golf every single day of the week at 10 in the morning,” he said. “It’s not fulfilling.” Degnan joined Cognition , the maker of the AI coding agent Devin, in September after initially declining outreach about the job. The company’s president, Russell Kaplan, eventually persuaded him to take a meeting. After learning more about its growth, Degnan said he woke up the next day with a realization: “Either I’m never going to do another operating job, which was in the cards, or I’m going to do this.” Degnan’s move to Cognition also sparked a public clash with Factory, a rival AI coding startup where he had been an advisor and board observer. In September, Factory CEO Matan Grinberg said the company had terminated Degnan, accusing him of holding undisclosed discussions with Cognition while advising Factory on confidential matters. Degnan denied sharing Factory information, saying Cognition had never asked for it and that he would never provide it. He announced his new role as Cognition’s chief revenue officer shortly afterward. Before he announced his retirement, Degnan was at Snowflake for over a decade, and rose from a sales leader to CRO at the software-as-a-Service company. '10 times more fulfilling' The decision to come to Cognition put him back at the center of the AI frenzy. “You’re in the middle of this insane thing,” he said, referring to the market’s AI momentum. “In a month on the job, it’s 10 times more fulfilling.” Degnan added that the draw to return to work is less financial than personal. “I have the luxury of not having to do these things,” he said. “I’m having a ton of fun doing it.” Degnan is among a growing list of chief executives who have boomeranged back to work after stepping down. Google cofounder Sergey Brin stepped down from a management role at Alphabet in 2019, but later said he was working at Google “pretty much every day,” drawn back by the pace of AI.
