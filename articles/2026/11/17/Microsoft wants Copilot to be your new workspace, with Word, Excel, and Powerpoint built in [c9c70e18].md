# Microsoft wants Copilot to be your new workspace, with Word, Excel, and Powerpoint built in

Digital Trends · Shimul Sood · 2026-11-17

For decades, working with Microsoft’s productivity software has meant opening Word to write a document, Excel to crunch some numbers, or PowerPoint to put together a presentation. Microsoft now wants to change that routine, and unsurprisingly, Copilot is at the heart of its plans.
At its Ignite 2026 event, Microsoft announced that Word, Excel, and PowerPoint are now generally available within Copilot. CEO Satya Nadella described Copilot as the new operating system for work, signaling a considerable shift in how the company sees its most products fitting into an AI-powered workplace. Instead of jumping between different applications, you’ll be able to create documents, spreadsheets, and presentations directly from Copilot. And if you prefer making adjustments yourself, the generated files remain editable in their corresponding Office apps.
Microsoft wants AI to take more work off your plate
The announcements build on the Copilot overhaul Microsoft unveiled in September, but creating files through a chatbot is only a small part of what the company has in mind. With Copilot Autopilot, Microsoft is introducing an autonomous, cloud-based AI agent designed to handle ongoing responsibilities rather than respond to individual requests. For instance, instead of repeatedly asking Copilot to catch you up on emails or organize your calendar, you could delegate those responsibilities to Autopilot, provided you’ve granted it the necessary permissions.
Work IQ helps these agents understand your workplace by drawing information from company emails, conversations, meetings, files, and professional relationships. The idea is to give AI enough background information to handle assignments without requiring employees to explain everything from scratch. Microsoft also wants to make working with AI more collaborative. Its Copilot Workspaces feature, currently available through the Frontier early-access program, will eventually allow employees to bring their personal Autopilot agents into shared projects. Autopilot will operate on a usage-based billing system through Copilot Credits, although Microsoft hasn’t revealed final pricing yet.
Even Microsoft thinks AI needs to be kept on a leash
Perhaps the most interesting part of Nadella’s presentation was his acknowledgment that AI systems aren’t inherently trustworthy. That’s a noteworthy admission from a company encouraging businesses to give AI access to sensitive workplace information. Microsoft’s approach is to limit what agents can do. Each Autopilot agent will have its own identity, administrators can manage or revoke access, and activities will be recorded for oversight. Certain actions can also require human approval.
The company is expanding Agent 365 to help businesses monitor their AI agents, track spending, and assess whether their investments are delivering meaningful results. However, estimates of employee time saved are calculated using a model rather than measured directly. Microsoft’s ambitions for Copilot are certainly becoming clearer. It no longer wants AI to be something you occasionally turn to for help within an application; it wants AI to become the starting point for your workday. Whether businesses are comfortable handing over that much responsibility, however, will largely depend on how reliably Microsoft’s safeguards hold up outside a keynote demonstration.
