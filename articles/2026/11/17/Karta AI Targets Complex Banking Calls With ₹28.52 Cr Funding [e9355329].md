# Karta AI Targets Complex Banking Calls With ₹28.52 Cr Funding

CIOL · CIOL Bureau · 2026-11-17

Bengaluru-based voice AI startup Karta AI is expanding its focus on complex customer service operations in banking, lending, fintech, and insurance, backed by ₹28.52 crore ($3 million) in seed funding led by Peer Capital and Cedar Hill Capital.
The investment, first announced in October 2026, also saw participation from Wyser VC, CDM Capital, and Seedstars. According to a November 17 statement from legal advisory firm VERTICES PARTNERS, which advised Karta AI on the transaction, the company will use the capital to strengthen its team, expand distribution, and develop its predictive context engine.
Unlike conventional voice bots designed to answer routine questions or direct callers to human representatives, Karta AI is developing agents that can handle more complicated customer requests involving multiple steps and enterprise systems.
The approach addresses a persistent challenge for financial institutions: automating customer interactions without compromising accuracy, security, or regulatory compliance.
Moving Beyond Routine Customer Calls
Founded in 2024 by Vijaypal Singh and Abhishek Kushwaha, Karta AI operates in a market where banks, lenders, and insurers are increasingly exploring AI to manage customer service volumes.
Traditional interactive voice response systems and scripted chatbots can handle straightforward requests such as checking account information or directing customers to the appropriate department.
However, more complex problems often require human intervention.
A customer reporting an incorrect loan deduction, a failed disbursement, or a mismatch in know-your-customer (KYC) records may need information retrieved from several systems before the issue can be resolved.
Karta AI is attempting to address these situations through AI agents that can understand the customer's request, retrieve relevant information, and execute workflows across connected business systems.
According to investor Cedar Hill Capital, the company's platform integrates with customer relationship management systems, application programming interfaces, and ticketing tools to support such operations.
The aim is to move beyond answering questions toward completing the task behind a customer request.
How Karta AI's Context Engine Works
A key part of the company's technology is its predictive context engine, which is designed to interpret customer interactions using information drawn from business policies, operating procedures, and previous conversations.
Rather than generating responses based solely on the immediate conversation, the system is intended to apply relevant business rules and customer context before taking action.
Cedar Hill Capital describes the architecture as converting standard operating procedures and company policies into executable workflows.
This is particularly relevant to financial services, where an AI system must follow defined processes rather than independently decide how a sensitive request should be handled.
For example, resolving a disputed transaction may involve verifying the customer's identity, checking transaction records, examining applicable policies, and determining whether a case can be resolved automatically or requires human review.
The reliability of these steps becomes critical when an AI agent is allowed to make changes to customer records or initiate operational actions.
Karta AI's investors say the platform incorporates workflow controls and human oversight for regulated use cases.
However, the company has not publicly disclosed sufficient independent technical testing to establish how consistently these controls perform across different financial institutions.
Voice AI Adoption Gains Momentum
The company's existing operations provide an indication of the demand for AI-powered customer service automation.
According to information disclosed during its October funding announcement, Karta AI serves more than 10 financial institutions and processes approximately two million customer conversations every month across more than 25 languages.
Cedar Hill Capital has also cited customer-reported automation rates of 60% to 80% for inbound calls.
These figures suggest that enterprises are experimenting with automating a substantial share of customer interactions.
However, automation rates do not necessarily indicate that every customer issue has been resolved correctly.
For banks and insurers, the more meaningful measures include successful case resolution, the frequency of human intervention, compliance with internal procedures, and the number of errors requiring correction.
The ability to handle regional languages, accents, and mixed-language conversations is another important consideration in the Indian market.
Karta AI's technology is designed to support Indian-language customer interactions, including multilingual conversations that conventional voice systems can struggle to interpret.
Funding to Support Distribution and Product Development
The seed round gives Karta AI additional resources to expand its business and develop its technology.
Peer Capital and Cedar Hill Capital led the investment, with participation from Wyser VC, CDM Capital, and Seedstars.
The company plans to direct much of the funding toward expanding distribution and strengthening its team, while continuing work on its predictive context engine.
The development priorities include improving the reliability of AI agents handling more complex customer workflows.
Vinayak Burman, Founder and Managing Partner of VERTICES PARTNERS, said the company's focus on reliability is particularly relevant to customer-facing AI applications.
“Karta AI is working on a practical application of AI in customer interactions, where reliability and the ability to handle complex workflows matter,” he said.
VERTICES PARTNERS advised Karta AI on the transaction, with Partner Manu Varghese leading the legal team.
Reliability Will Determine Enterprise Adoption
As financial institutions move from experimenting with AI assistants to allowing them to complete customer service tasks, reliability is becoming a central consideration.
An automated response that provides incorrect information can inconvenience a customer. An AI agent that incorrectly modifies a financial record, processes a request, or overlooks an authentication requirement can create more serious operational and compliance risks.
This makes financial services a demanding environment for autonomous AI systems.
Karta AI's approach of connecting voice agents to enterprise workflows reflects growing interest in moving beyond basic conversational automation.
The commercial opportunity is significant, particularly for institutions managing large customer service operations across multiple languages.
Yet the technology's longer-term value will depend on whether it can consistently complete complex requests, reduce servicing costs, and maintain appropriate human oversight.
With its seed funding secured, Karta AI now faces the challenge of demonstrating that its agents can deliver those outcomes as deployments expand.
