# Google Launches "Gemini 4 Carbon". How Does It Differ from Anthropic's "Claude Fable 5.2" and "Claude Mythos 5.2"?

"The tasks I want to ask AI to do are gradually becoming larger."
It's not just about paraphrasing text or asking short questions; people are increasingly thinking about using AI to read and compare many documents or to carry out tasks that require multiple steps.
In November 2026, Google and Anthropic successively announced new AI models for tackling difficult tasks.
Google is expanding the availability of "Gemini 4 Carbon." Anthropic has announced "Claude Fable 5.2" and "Claude Mythos 5.2."
While the list of names might look a bit complicated, what matters is "what kind of work is it good at, and can I use it?"
In this article, I will organize the features and availability conditions of each in a way that is easy to understand for those who use AI on a daily basis.

What kind of model is Gemini 4 Carbon?
On November 10, 2026, Google announced that it would provide "Gemini 4 Carbon" to developers, businesses, researchers, and general users.
The uses for Carbon cited by Google include complex coding and debugging, scientific research, and intellectual tasks involving multiple steps.
For example, it might be easier to understand if you imagine a task where you compare multiple documents, find necessary information, and compile it into a report in a fixed format, rather than just reading and summarizing a single document.
Google explains that Carbon is designed to use more tokens and perform repeated verification while thinking deeply. Tokens are units used when AI processes text and the like. As the processing volume increases, it also affects API usage costs.
Meanwhile, Google introduces "Gemini 4 Argon" as a suitable choice for many software development and intellectual tasks. When considering which model to use, you should look not only at the difficulty but also at the processing time and cost.
[Google Official Announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/)
Differences between Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2
On November 9, Anthropic announced "Claude Fable 5.2" and "Claude Mythos 5.2".
According to the company, these two use a common base model. One of the differences lies in safety measures regarding cybersecurity and biology, as well as the scope of who can use them.
Fable has strong safety measures in place to prevent misuse in these areas, and Anthropic explains that it is available to all users.
Mythos is intended for use in certain specialized fields, and access is granted to organizations and others that meet the criteria through cybersecurity and life sciences certification programs.
Even if they both carry the "5.2" name, the terms of use are not the same. You need to check which models are available for the service or subscription plan you wish to use.
[Anthropic Official Announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)
What kind of work is each intended for?
Both companies' announcements frequently mention tasks involving long procedures and addressing specialized problems.
Regarding Carbon, Google cites examples such as creating and debugging complex programs, analysis based on large amounts of documentation, and scientific reasoning.
Anthropic explains that Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 have improved capabilities in coding, reasoning, and image understanding. The announcement also includes examples such as software migration, electronic device design, and mathematical verification.
These are examples provided by each company. Rather than assuming the same results can be obtained for any task, it is best to judge by testing them with your own work.
For familiar tasks, one could, for example, find necessary parts from multiple documents, summarize them in a table, and check for omissions. Instead of leaving everything to the AI at once, it is easier to proceed by conveying the purpose of the task, the materials to be used, and the desired format, while checking the results along the way.
How do the users and pricing differ?
Regarding Gemini 4 Carbon, Google states that it will be rolled out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, Google Antigravity, and the AI mode of Search.
For developers and businesses, it is also available via API through Google AI Studio and other channels. The introductory pricing shown by Google at the time of the announcement is $2 per 1 million input tokens and $10 per 1 million output tokens.
However, this is the API price. It must be considered separately from the subscription fee for the Gemini app. Also, the actual billing amount depends on usage volume and applicable terms.
Claude Fable 5.2 will be provided on the Claude Platform, as well as AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and others. You should check the scope and limits of availability based on the service and subscription plan you use.
Claude Mythos 5.2 has access conditions through certification programs and the like. Just because you can use Fable does not mean you can necessarily choose Mythos in the same way.
Since pricing and terms of service are subject to change, it is safer to check the latest information from each company before using them.
How to think about choosing for your daily work
When a new model is released, you might feel like you have to use the most high-performance one available.
However, for everyday tasks like drafting emails, organizing text, or asking short questions, the model you are already using is often sufficient.
On the other hand, when you have a lot of documents and the work process is long, or when you want to proceed while checking things multiple times, it makes sense to try models like these.
For example, when extracting common points from multiple documents: first, have the AI organize the key points for each document, then create a table of similarities and differences, and finally check the supporting evidence. By breaking it down into stages like this, it becomes easier to notice mistakes along the way.
The results produced by a model may include misreadings of documents or calculation errors. When using them for particularly important decisions, it is best to combine them with checks against original sources or expert verification.
