# Jensen Huang agrees to face Congress over Nvidia scandal

Axios · Herb Scribner · 2026-11-17

Jensen Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter will officially testify before Congress in early December, lawmakers announced Tuesday. Why it matters: Lawmakers want answers on whether Nvidia's powerful AI chips were compromised by Chinese intelligence and whether the company did enough to protect sensitive American technology. Huang and Teter will participate in transcribed, closed-door interviews with the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China select committees. Context: House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast (R-Fla.), House China committee Chairman John Moolenaar (R-Mich.) and House Intelligence Chairman Rick Crawford (R-Ark.) sent letters to Huang, Teter and Commerce undersecretary Jeffrey Kessler on Nov. 12 , requesting they appear. The chairmen told Teter they were seeking information on alleged chip smuggling and the use of Nvidia products by U.S. adversaries, as well as the company's alleged export-control investigations. Kessler was expected to join a classified briefing with Commerce Chair Ted Cruz, but the meeting was postponed. What they're saying: "We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security," the three committee chairmen said in a release . A representative for Huang did not immediately respond to Axios' request for comment Tuesday. What's next: Huang will appear before the committees on Dec. 1, and Teter will appear on Dec. 2. Catch up quick: Nvidia announced last week that Huang had stepped down as CEO over an alleged affair with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy and former executive of Nvidia customer Megaspeed International. Huang's resignation ignited requests from Congress to investigate his relationship with Megaspeed , a Singapore-based data center company that gained access to Nvidia's AI chips. Megaspeed faces its own investigations for allegedly smuggling chips into China, which the company denies. The big picture: Huang ties to President Trump and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick have raised concerns from lawmakers about whether the government did enough to crack down on Nvidia's chips finding their way to China.
