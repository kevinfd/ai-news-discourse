# Poem: ‘LET x BE’

Scientific American · Carol Hart · 2026-11-17

I scavenge in the junkyards of science,
sifting through discarded jargon until
I find some vintage notion and consider
how the rust seems to give it character.
Shame to let it languish in the trash.
Maybe something can be done with it.
Phlogiston, for instance, a principle
once thought to be present in all things
combustible. An inner, secret spark.

Ether, the medium that permeates all
space, by virtue of which light shines,
electricity sparks, magnets attract.
It was junked in favor of the idea
of a vacuum, a void, a nonexistence
that does not exist. Such a waste.
Here’s another, mysterium, a beauty
of a word. Just thrown away, barely
used, still packaged in its scare quotes.
It signified an odd set of spectral lines
emitted from the void that is not a void,
subsequently determined to be ... but
you don’t need to know what it meant
back in the sixties, the era of tie-dye and
bell-bottoms—which keep coming
back from oblivion. Why shouldn’t
mysterium make a comeback too?
Repurposed to mean an anomaly or
fugitive trace, nothing tremendous—
just disquieting, to be briefly noted
then ignored or reasoned away.
Should humans persist and succeed
in building machines that obediently
answer every question we can ask,
let mysterium be what’s left behind—
the blind spot, the irritating doubt,
the x that will forever be unknown.
