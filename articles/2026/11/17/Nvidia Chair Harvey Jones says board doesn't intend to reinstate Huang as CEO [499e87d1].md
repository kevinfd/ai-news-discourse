# Nvidia Chair Harvey Jones says board doesn't intend to reinstate Huang as CEO

CNBC · mfiguiere · 2026-11-17

Nvidia beat on profit and revenue after the bell on Tuesday and issued strong guidance, in the chipmaker's first earnings report since co-founder Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation last week as CEO. The stock rose more than 3% in extended trading.
Here's how the company did compared to analysts' estimates, according to LSEG:
- EPS: $2.68 adjusted vs. $2.48 expected
- Revenue: $115.84 billion vs. $109.10 billion expected
Revenue more than doubled from $57 billion a year earlier, Nvidia said, a sign of continued strength in its core data center business. The company said revenue this quarter will expand by roughly 90% to about $130 billion, topping the $125.1 billion average analyst estimate, according to LSEG.
Finance chief Colette Kress is used to addressing Wall Street, but never like this. She's now functioning as interim CEO, meaning her responsibilities go well beyond the key numbers. At the start of the earnings call, Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones said the board doesn't intend to bring Huang back as CEO.
The biggest cloud for Nvidia is around its China business after Huang's reported affair with a businesswoman who's allegedly a Chinese spy. The Commerce Department on Monday announced a change that would prevent Nvidia's chips from being rented to Chinese companies entirely, putting revenue at risk.
Nvidia kept with its recent language on China, telling investors in its commentary, "We are not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook."
One of the central questions from investors heading into the call was whether Nvidia would scrap a previous forecast for 70% growth in the fiscal year ending in January 2028. However, Kress raised guidance, saying that Nvidia now expects 75% growth.
Kress said during the Q&A portion of the call that it's "business as usual at Nvidia."
"Our leadership has stood up so strongly over the last week to support both what we are doing here at earnings, but also supporting all of our different employees around the world," she said. "Remember, we have the best technologists on the planet with a world-class multi-year roadmap. And I think that's important from a business perspective."
Nvidia's stock is down 24% this month and on Friday wrapped up its sixth straight weekly drop, the longest losing streak since 2012. The stock has dropped the first two days of this week and is trading at its lowest since July 2025. In addition to the Huang saga, the company is dealing with broader concerns about an AI slowdown and a political shift in Washington, D.C., following this month's midterm elections.
