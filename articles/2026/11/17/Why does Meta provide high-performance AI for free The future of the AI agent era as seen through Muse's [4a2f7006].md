# Why does Meta provide high-performance AI for free? The future of the AI agent era as seen through Muse's strategy

note · 井上じゅん · 2026-11-17

SYSTEM NOTICE
Why does Meta provide high-performance AI for free? The future of the AI agent era as seen through Muse's strategy
*Picked up by note money!
Recently, what has been attracting attention in the AI world is the personal AI agent "Meta", provided by "Muse".
Until now, AI has had the image of a convenient assistant that returns answers when you input questions.
However, AI agents are a little different.
They carry out actual tasks on our behalf, such as organizing emails, planning trips, adjusting schedules, and helping with shopping.
Moreover, Meta provides the basic functions of Muse for free.
A question arises here.
Why can Meta provide such advanced AI for free?
Of course, there is a reason for companies to provide services for free.
In fact, hidden in the background is a new business strategy for giant IT companies that goes beyond just the AI performance race.
This time, using Meta's Muse as an entry point, I will explain the mechanism of AI agents, the reasons for their free provision, and what kind of changes will occur in our lives in a way that is easy for beginners to understand.
1. How are AI agents different from previous AI?
First, let's clarify the term AI agent.
General AI until now, such as ChatGPT and Gemini, is basically a mechanism that returns answers to questions or instructions from humans.
For example,
- Tell me tomorrow's weather
- Tell me travel recommendations
- Draft an email
- Find me a delicious restaurant
It answers questions like this.
On the other hand, AI agents aim not only to answer, but to carry out the tasks that follow.
For example, if you ask it to plan a trip.
In the case of conventional AI
- Research travel destination candidates.
- Look for hotels.
- Suggest sightseeing spots.
- Make the reservations yourself.
In the case of an AI agent
- Ask about travel preferences.
- Organize budget and schedule.
- Research hotels and transportation.
- Compare candidates that meet the criteria.
- Proceed with booking procedures while getting necessary confirmations.
In other words, AI will take over multiple tasks that humans used to do.
This is not just about AI becoming smarter.
It means AI is changing from a "consultant" to a "partner that helps with work."
2. Can't work with just AI intelligence?
Here are some terms you should know to understand AI agents.
They are "models" and "harnesses."
These might sound a bit difficult, but they can be explained simply.
A model is the AI's brain
A model is the part that acts as the AI's brain.
It has the ability to understand text, analyze information, and think of answers.
For example, the ability to think of accurate answers to difficult questions and the ability to organize complex conditions are related to the model's performance.
A harness is a mechanism that manages the AI's limbs and actions
On the other hand, a harness is a mechanism for the AI to actually take action.
For example,
- searching for information on the internet
- checking emails in Gmail
- registering appointments in a calendar
- operating a hotel reservation page
- entering necessary information into another app
The mechanism to realize these tasks is related to the harness.
To put it simply, the model is the brain, and the harness is the limbs.
No matter how smart it is, it cannot perform actual tasks if it cannot use its limbs.
Conversely, even if you have free movement of your limbs, if your brain's judgment is poor, you will perform tasks incorrectly.
The true capability of an AI agent is determined by both the intelligence of its brain and the mechanism that allows it to actually take action.
3. Why does Meta provide Muse for free?
This is the most interesting part of this topic.
Developing and running AI requires vast amounts of computer equipment and electricity.
As the number of AI users increases, the operational costs increase accordingly.
Even so, why does Meta provide the basic functions for free?
One conceivable reason is a strategy to capture a massive future market early.
Reason 1: First, they want to increase the number of users
To make a new service successful, you first need to have many people use it.
For example, a social media platform that no one uses cannot be used to interact with friends or acquaintances.
However, the more users it has, the higher the value of that service becomes.
Meta has many users around the world, such as on Facebook and Instagram.
It is thought that Meta is trying to build a user base for AI agents by having as many people as possible use Muse in the same way.
In the future, when it becomes common for AI to help with shopping and travel bookings, a service that many people are already using will be a major strength.
Reason 2: Earning revenue from shopping and booking mechanisms
Another point to pay attention to is the relationship with shopping and bookings.
For example, suppose you ask Muse the following:
"I want to find and book a hotel that fits my budget for next week's trip."
If Muse can search for hotels, compare candidates that meet your criteria, and even help with the booking process, it would be extremely convenient for users.
As such systems become more widespread, there is a possibility that product purchases and service bookings through AI will increase.
For Meta, supporting these transactions opens up opportunities to generate future revenue related to payments or through partnerships with businesses.
In other words, receiving a monthly fee from users is not the only way to generate revenue in the AI business.
Increase the number of users for free, and generate revenue in the shopping and service markets that lie beyond.
This is an important point for understanding Meta's strategy.
However, a system where Meta receives a commission from every transaction has not yet been established. This is an area to watch as a future revenue model.
4. Do Google and Meta have different approaches to AI?
When considering the future of AI agents, the differences from Google are also interesting.
Google has services that are deeply integrated into our daily lives, such as its search engine, Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Calendar.
On the other hand, Meta develops its business primarily around social media platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and Threads.
While both companies are focusing on AI, the business foundations they have built so far are different.
Google's strength lies in its integration with existing services
Google already has services that many people use.
For example, checking emails in Gmail, managing schedules in Google Calendar, and saving documents in Google Drive.
Being able to incorporate AI into these daily tasks is a major strength.
However, careful design is required for important operations such as sending emails or deleting files.
If you accidentally delete an important email or modify work documents, it could cause a major problem.
Therefore, determining how much control to entrust to AI is a very important challenge.
Meta aims to have AI handle everyday tasks.
Meanwhile, through Muse, Meta aims to popularize AI agents that are closely integrated into personal life.
For example,
- organizing emails
- planning trips
- assisting with shopping
- scheduling appointments
- gathering daily information
If users can delegate these tasks to AI, they can reduce the burden of tedious procedures.
While Google can leverage its existing service infrastructure, it can also be thought that Meta is trying to establish new AI agents within daily life.
It is not yet clear who will win.
However, it is worth noting that the AI competition is shifting from mere accuracy of answers to how much work can actually be delegated.
5. The more convenient it gets, the more worried we become? The risks of delegating to AI
Reading this far, AI agents seem like very convenient entities.
However, is it really okay to leave everything to AI?
In fact, the more convenient things become, the more issues we must be careful about.
1. Management of personal information and passwords
When AI agents integrate with email, calendars, shopping sites, and the like, they will be handling personal information.
In some cases, login information and payment-related details will also be required.
Meta explains that for Muse, it has prepared a dedicated virtual environment called "Muse Secure VM" and established an independent security monitor called "Sentinel."
It also introduces mechanisms to manage passwords and payment information separately from the AI itself, as well as a system that requires user confirmation before performing important operations.
However, just because there are safety measures in place does not mean that all risks are eliminated.
It is important to keep the services you integrate and the permissions you grant to the absolute minimum necessary.
(2) The possibility of AI making incorrect judgments
AI has become extremely capable, but it can still make mistakes.
For example, there is a possibility that it might suggest a hotel based on outdated information, get business hours wrong, or select a product that is different from what you wanted.
Caution is especially necessary for operations involving money, such as reservations and purchases.
It is important not to trust AI suggestions blindly, but for humans to perform the final verification.
(3) Will we end up overspending because of AI recommendations?
Personally, a shopping issue is something I feel we need to be careful about in the future.
Once AI comes to understand your preferences and past purchase history, it may start suggesting products you are likely to want one after another.
For example,
"We found something similar to a product you purchased previously."
"It is on sale right now."
"There is a new product that matches your preferences."
If you continue to receive such suggestions, you might end up purchasing things you do not need.
On social media, the issue of people continuing to watch videos and posts endlessly has already been pointed out.
From now on, we will also need to consider the problem of overspending due to convenient suggestions from AI.
To use convenient AI, you will also need the ability to make your own judgments.
6. From now on, you won't be able to choose just based on 'which AI is smarter'
Until now, when comparing AIs, the focus has been on points like which one is smarter or which one can write better text.
Of course, the performance of the model, which serves as the AI's brain, is important.
However, in the era of AI agents, that alone will not be enough.
For example, the following points will also become important.
- Can it integrate with the apps I use?
- To what extent can it perform tasks on my behalf?
- Does it have mechanisms to prevent incorrect operations?
- Can it properly manage personal information?
- Can I confirm important operations myself?
In other words, the criteria for choosing an AI itself will change.
From now on, won't we enter an era where we value not just an AI with excellent answers, but an AI that can be trusted to handle work?
Summary: Is the free provision of AI a strategy to seize the initiative in the future?
Behind Meta's decision to provide the basic functions of Muse for free, it is thought that there is not only a desire to simply deliver convenient services to users, but also an aim to seize the initiative in the future AI agent market.
As the number of users increases, shopping, reservations, and integration with various services through AI will also expand.
As a result, there is a possibility that new businesses will be born.
Meanwhile, Google is also strengthening its AI agent capabilities by leveraging its existing service infrastructure.
The AI competition from here on out will not just be about competing for raw intelligence.
How many services it can connect to and how safely it can perform tasks on behalf of humans will become important.
And for us as users, what is important is not to choose AI based solely on convenience.
We must be cautious about the mindset of using something just because it is free or entrusting everything to it just because it is convenient.
While delegating tasks to AI, we should still verify decisions involving money and personal information ourselves.
Isn't that the kind of relationship we will need in the coming AI era?
Will AI take away human jobs, or will it enrich human lives?
I believe the answer depends not only on the evolution of AI, but also on how we choose to engage with it.
