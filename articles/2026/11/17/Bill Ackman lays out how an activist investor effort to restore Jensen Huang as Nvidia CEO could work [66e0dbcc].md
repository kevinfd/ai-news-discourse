# Bill Ackman lays out how an activist investor effort to restore Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO could work

Business Insider · Lloyd Lee, Lloyd Lee (lloydlee@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-17

Bill Ackman said he'd want to clarify the legal uncertainties around Jensen Huang's affairs before launching an activist campaign to restore him as Nvidia CEO. Godofredo A. Vásquez/AFP via Getty Images Investor Bill Ackman has defended Jensen Huang amid the scandals surrounding the former Nvidia CEO. Ackman said he believes there would be ample investor support for reinstating Huang. The case could be made if the legal uncertainties around Huang's affairs are cleared, he said. Bill Ackman said there could be a case for reinstating Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO — that is, only if Huang is cleared of any wrongdoing related to putting his company's national security obligations at risk. At a conference hosted by Semafor on Monday, the billionaire activist investor was asked what strategy he would use to pressure the board into reinstating Huang. Huang resigned from his role on Thursday following the disclosure of extramarital affairs and concerns that he may have been a target of a "foreign intelligence effort." Ackman said that if Huang's relationships were determined to be a "personal foible as opposed to a corporate defense conspiracy," there could be a "powerful case" for putting Huang back in the CEO seat, though the investor said it would be "unlikely" for him to be involved in such an effort. "You just present the facts, and you call a special meeting," he said, adding that an activist investor could also partner with Huang, who owns more than 3% of the company. Said special meeting would be a shareholder vote on reinstating Huang. The now-former CEO's involvement would not only help the company meet the threshold for calling one — which requires at least 15% of the voting power — but also send a public message that the founder wants to return. "You call a special meeting, the writing will be on the wall very quickly, I would expect, at which point you don't even have to go the meeting," Ackman said. "Just put him back in, make some changes to the board, and push forward." A spokesperson for Nvidia did not respond to a request for comment. Ackman, who has defended Huang since the Nvidia founder's affairs were publicized, said the situation is an "interesting activist setup," given that Huang is a heralded founder who would have plenty of shareholder support . "I think the only cloud is there is some legal uncertainty about what actually went down," Ackman said. "It would be helpful to know that answer before you pursue that."
