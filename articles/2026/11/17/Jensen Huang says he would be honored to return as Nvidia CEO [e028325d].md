# Jensen Huang says he "would be honored" to return as Nvidia CEO

CBS News · Megan Cerullo · 2026-11-17

Jensen Huang breaks silence after resigning from Nvidia, says he "would be honored" to return as CEO
Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang broke his silence after resigning from the company following revelations of multiple affairs, saying he'd be willing to return as its CEO.
"I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life," Huang said in a statement Tuesday morning. "I created problems for the board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the board's independent investigation."
He added, "If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."
Late Tuesday, however, Nvidia Chair Harvey Jones said the company's board doesn't intend to rehire Huang as chief executive. The company's search for a new chief is underway, he added, while noting that the board has "every confidence" in Kress' ability to lead the company. Jones said the company is considering both internal and external candidates to replace Huang.
Support for Huang's return
Huang has gained support from more than 190,000 people, including several thousand Nvidia employees, who signed a petition to reinstate him. On Monday, President Trump suggested to reporters that he would support Huang returning to Nvidia.
"I would not mind seeing that," Mr. Trump said. Later, the president said he recommended former Apple CEO Tim Cook as Nvidia's next leader.
In his Tuesday statement, Huang thanked his allies for their support since his departure last week, saying that in addition to the public calls for him to return to the company, many "continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive and geopolitical dynamics."
Nvidia's board said on Tuesday that it has formed a committee to oversee its national security policies and ensure compliance with U.S. export controls. The former CEO has also agreed to appear before House lawmakers on Dec. 1 to address questions about a potential breach of national security amid an investigation into his affair with an alleged Chinese spy.
The former CEO's return to Nvidia is unlikely, said Yorkville Ives & Co. technology analyst Dan Ives.
"In our view the sequencing is the tell: Jensen has conditioned his return on a board decision, and the board's first public act this week was to build a compliance structure around the issue that forced him out, which is not what a board preparing to reverse itself does," he said in a Tuesday research note.
Shares of Nvidia declined $2.63, or 1.7%, to $153.51. The stock has lost 16% of its value since Huang's extramarital relationships became public.
