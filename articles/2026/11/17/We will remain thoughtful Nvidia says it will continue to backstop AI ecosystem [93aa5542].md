# "We will remain thoughtful": Nvidia says it will continue to backstop AI ecosystem

Axios · Ina Fried · 2026-11-17

Nvidia's interim CEO said Tuesday that the company will be "disciplined" as it decides how much to invest in neoclouds and other AI customers going forward. Why it matters: Nvidia isn't just the biggest chip partner for AI startups — it's a major source of capital . What they're saying: "Our momentum is real," interim CEO Colette Kress said on at the end of conference call with investors and analysts. "Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing. Our customers and our partners are with us, and employees are committed. We've got this." She said the company would continue to use its balance sheet and support startups and the broader AI ecosystem, but she made clear it won't be a blank check. "As the market matures and additional sources of capital become available, we are rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees," she added. In particular, Kress said the company would continue to be "disciplined and selective in our support" for so-called neoclouds . Those companies provide additional computing capacity to big AI players as well as smaller and regional operators. "Our neoclouds have been so instrumental in terms of both supporting enterprises and supporting many of the different countries around the world." CoreWeave and Nebius, two such neoclouds, each ose by more than 10% in after-hours trading. Zoom in: Kress didn't name-check any other specific customers but said the company would offer capital to suppliers to help them move quickly. Nvidia will provide financing assistance or guarantees to some "very strong customers," she said. The chip-maker will "look very deeply" into whether guarantees are needed and conduct "a lot of work to understand if those are the right types of investments we need to make." Catch-up quick: Huang resigned last week after revelations that he had a relationship with a woman who allegedly had ties to Chinese intelligence. She was also a major Nvidia customer and helped Chinese AI companies gain access to cutting-edge Nvidia technology while the U.S. had banned some chip sales to the country. On the call, Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones through cold water on the notion of a quick return for Huang . "The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," he said. The company said it wouldn't be taking questions on Huang's departure or its ongoing investigation into his actions.
