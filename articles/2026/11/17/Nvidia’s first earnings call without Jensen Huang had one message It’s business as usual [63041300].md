# Nvidia’s first earnings call without Jensen Huang had one message: It’s business as usual

Fast Company · Mark Sullivan · 2026-11-17

Nvidia on Tuesday held its first earnings call in its 33-year history without cofounder Jensen Huang, who resigned five days ago after a scandal involving an extramarital affair with a suspected Chinese spy. After reporting strong third-quarter numbers, interim CEO Colette Kress (who has been Nvidia’s CFO since 2013) kept the call focused on Nvidia’s immediate business concerns.
Analysts wanted to know what the company would say about the succession plan after Huang’s departure, and about the future of Nvidia’s pivotal role as the company powering the AI race.
Most of the salient information about Huang was given at the front of the call by Harvey Jones, Nvidia’s chair of the board. Jones said Nvidia doesn’t intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO, providing a clapback to the growing contingent on social media lobbying for the ousted CEO’s return. Jones said the executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles is handling the search for a new CEO, but that Nvidia is looking at both internal and external candidates for the job.
“The board is acting deliberately and in the best interest of the company’s employees and investors,” Jones said. “With that we will take no further questions about the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation.”
The numbers
Kress then took over the call and kept things focused on Nvidia’s numbers. Kress has run Nvidia’s earnings calls for years, but always alongside Huang, who set the company’s vision and did much of its dealmaking with customers and partners. Today her main job was to report the quarterly numbers, and convince investors that the world’s most important AI chip company can run without its founder.
The numbers, at least, are good. Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue for its fiscal third quarter, well above Wall Street’s roughly $109 billion forecast, and projected $130 billion for the current quarter. Investors expected a beat.
Kress said Nvidia has seen “explosive” adoption of its latest Vera Rubin computing platform, which includes a Rubin GPU and a Vera CPU. “Our momentum is real,” she said. “Our ambition will not change, our passion for innovation will not change.”
