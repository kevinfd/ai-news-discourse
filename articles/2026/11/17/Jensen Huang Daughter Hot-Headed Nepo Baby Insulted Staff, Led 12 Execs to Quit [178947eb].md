# Jensen Huang Daughter: Hot-Headed Nepo Baby Insulted Staff, Led 12 Execs to Quit

International Business Times, Singapore Edition · randycupertino · 2026-11-17

After reports of Jensen Huang's alleged infidelity and departure from Nvidia, his daughter Madison is facing accusations of bullying employees at the AI company. Madison, 35, a senior director of product marketing at Nvidia, has reportedly been responsible for the exit of over a dozen senior executives, current and former employees told The New York Times.
Madison is known for berating staff and regularly shouting at employees, senior executives and her brother, Spencer, during meetings, according to the sources. Last year, a vice president on Nvidia's marketing team had to leave a meeting early after Madison's outburst triggered a panic attack, two people familiar with the episode told the newspaper.
Nvidia's Nepo Baby
Employees described Madison as "demanding and disparaging," claiming she regularly insulted colleagues and had little hesitation about making demeaning remarks. She referred to forgetful colleagues as "goldfish" and sometimes called others "dead face," according to Nvidia employees who spoke to The Wall Street Journal.
Sources also alleged that Jensen, 63, encouraged his daughter's behavior, claiming he would threaten to unleash an "angry" Madison on employees who fell out of favor with him.
He reportedly warned staff members that they should be afraid of her, according to the insiders. These allegations emerged shortly before his reported departure last week following revelations about his alleged affairs with three women, including a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.
Meanwhile, senior Nvidia executives reportedly felt they could not challenge Madison or disagree with her because of her father's position at the company, according to sources who spoke to The New York Times.
Harvard Business School professor David Yoffie summed up the concerns over family influence in the workplace, telling the publication: "Nepotism is almost always a bad idea."
Madison, who served as Jensen's chief of staff, was particularly invested in shaping her father's public image and turning him into a celebrity figure, according to insiders.
When Huang joined the company in 2020, Nvidia had begun promoting an AI-generated cartoon version of its CEO as part of its marketing strategy. Madison reportedly liked the character, known internally as "Toy Jensen," and encouraged the company's communications team to feature it more prominently, two sources told the publication.
The cartoon eventually became a prominent part of Nvidia's branding. The company produced Toy Jensen pins, showcased a holographic version at events and even sold a sweater featuring the character for $178.
Like Father, Like Daughter
New employees were also introduced to the company through a video featuring Toy Jensen. A separate four-minute clip starring the cartoon was shown during Huang's keynote address at Nvidia's developer conference in March.
However, the character did not sit well with everyone. Several employees were reportedly "unnerved" by its growing presence, fearing it made Nvidia "seem like a cult," the newspaper reported.
Nvidia stopped featuring Toy Jensen in its marketing campaigns last week after Huang admitted to his alleged affair with Alice Huang, a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy who is not related to him.
Insiders also claimed that Madison was among the first people to defend her father when reports about his relationship with Alice surfaced in October.
Madison reportedly asked Nvidia's communications team to challenge the allegations publicly. She also turned to crisis communications firm Heller, which she had hired in May to handle her family's public image, for help managing the fallout, The New York Times reported.
Huang has since admitted to having an affair with Alice, a former executive at Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed International, while married to his wife of 41 years, Lori.
U.S. authorities began investigating Alice after Megaspeed was accused of illegally rerouting Nvidia's AI chips to China in violation of strict export restrictions. Investigators suspect Beijing may have used Alice to obtain sensitive information from Huang while influencing Nvidia's business decisions to expand China's access to advanced AI technology. China has denied the allegations.
Huang's admission of the affair was followed by a rapid unraveling of his position at the chipmaker. He abruptly resigned as Nvidia's CEO on Thursday after reports emerged of another alleged relationship, this time with Kimberly Powell, the company's vice president of healthcare.
Hours after his resignation was announced, Reuters reported that the married executive had also been involved in a third alleged workplace relationship with another Nvidia employee whose identity has not been disclosed.
Nvidia has appointed its executive vice president and chief financial officer, Colette Kress, as interim CEO while the company searches for a permanent successor.
