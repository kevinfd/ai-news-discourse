# California Sues OpenAI Over AI Safety, Claims It 'Endangered' the Public

PCMag · James Peckham · 2026-11-17

California is suing OpenAI, accusing the maker of ChatGPT of risking public safety by failing to implement strong safeguards when developing autonomous AI tools.
In the lawsuit, California Attorney General Rob Bonta accuses OpenAI of creating a “public nuisance," according to the state’s law. Bonta says OpenAI “misled and endangered the public and users of its services by knowingly developing advanced AI models" by doing so "without the safeguards and monitoring necessary to adequately protect the public."
The lawsuit cites the July 2026 attack on Hugging Face, in which OpenAI's autonomous agents breached a third-party platform's internal systems after escaping a sandboxed environment. The lawsuit reads, “The Hugging Face cyberattack was the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI that prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability."
Although the Hugging Face incident is a major element of the case, Bonta also cites an admission by OpenAI that the brand "has lost control of its autonomous AI agents over 80 times during testing." The 31-page lawsuit also says OpenAI “thwarted” the Attorney General’s investigation by refusing to provide detailed information.
OpenAI doesn’t believe this is accurate, with spokesperson Drew Pusateri telling Sky News, “We take misalignment seriously, and we have engaged - and will continue to engage - constructively with the attorney general's office to share information and advance strong, practical safety standards for the entire industry."
The company is also critical of the lawsuit's focus on OpenAI, rather than pursuing the AI industry at large. Pusateri said, "This lawsuit appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that's been identified by every major AI lab.” Rival brands like Anthropic, Google, and Meta have all admitted incidents in which their AI models have gone rogue.
The lawsuit reads, “While public reporting and OpenAI’s self-serving statements about the Hugging Face attack have evoked science fiction fantasies of “rogue” AI systems run amok, the simple truth is that the attack was entirely foreseeable in light of OpenAI’s decision to test models it knew to be dangerous in a flawed and exploitable testing environment without proper oversight.”
California is seeking injunctions in the lawsuit to stop OpenAI from continuing its conduct and a $2,500 payment from each defendant for certain violations. This isn't the first time US states have sued OpenAI; Florida first accused the company of unsafe behavior, and a coalition of Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska, and West Virginia also sued last month.
Disclosure: Ziff Davis, PCMag's parent company, filed a lawsuit against OpenAI in April 2025, alleging it infringed Ziff Davis copyrights in training and operating its AI systems.
