# Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang can nominate two directors to company board, filings show

Channel NewsAsia · CNA · 2026-11-17

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang can nominate two directors to company board, filings show
SAN FRANCISCO, Nov 17 : Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has the ability to nominate two directors to the Nvidia board ahead of the company's annual shareholder meeting and vote, according to company proxy and corporate filings, giving the co-founder a route to future influence on the company.
Chip industry executives, investors and customers are scrutinizing Nvidia closely to understand what role Huang will play in the company after he resigned as CEO last week. The ability to nominate directors, who choose leadership and provide oversight, could be a powerful tool in influencing the future of the AI chip leader.
Any Nvidia shareholder or group of shareholders that owns more than 3 per cent of the voting power for at least three years is entitled to nominate directors who will be included in the company's proxy materials and vote at its annual meeting, which typically takes place in June.
Huang and entities tied to him have owned more than 3 per cent of Nvidia's shares for more than three years, according to data from LSEG. He currently owns roughly 3.5 per cent of the company.
Huang would be free to nominate anyone who would qualify as an independent member of the board. The candidates would go on Nvidia's proxy ballot.
Nominating board members would not guarantee Huang's allies seats on the company board; shareholders would need to vote to approve potential candidates. Nvidia may also leave a nominee out if the person has violated the company's bylaws and charter.
FOREIGN INTELLIGENCE INVESTIGATION
Nvidia's board said on November 8 that it initiated an investigation in light of recent reporting that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Reuters could not determine if Huang's conduct broke the company's bylaws or charter.
Nvidia and a representative for Huang did not respond to a request for comment.
Since Huang resigned from Nvidia last week in the wake of his extramarital affairs, executives such as Elon Musk, employees, investors and US President Donald Trump have advocated for his return to the top job at the chip designer. The board was not considering reinstating Huang, three people with knowledge of the matter told Reuters on Monday.
Huang released a statement on Tuesday and said he "would be honored" to return as CEO if the board decided such a move would be in the best interest of shareholders and employees.
On Tuesday after the market close, Nvidia forecast quarterly revenue above Wall Street estimates, giving investors some relief after the uncertainty caused by Huang's exit.
