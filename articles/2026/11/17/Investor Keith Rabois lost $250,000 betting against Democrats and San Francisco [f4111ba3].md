# Investor Keith Rabois lost $250,000 betting against Democrats and San Francisco

Business Insider · Henry Chandonnet, Henry Chandonnet (hchandonnet@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-17

Khosla Ventures' Keith Rabois paid $250,000 after losing three bets tied to San Francisco and the midterms. Al Drago/Bloomberg via Getty Images Khosla Ventures' Keith Rabois is making good on some bets he lost against X users. He paid $200,000 to Sheel Mohnot and $50,000 to Geiger Capital after betting on politics and startups. "The beauty about venture is you can be wrong a lot of the time and still make a lot of money," Mohnot told Business Insider. Keith Rabois rolled the dice. Now he's paying the price. The managing director of Khosla Ventures made a handful of sizable bets on X. In 2023, Rabois bet against fellow investor Sheel Mohnot that no venture-capital-backed startup based in San Francisco would exit for more than $10 billion. They put $100,000 on the line. In April, Rabois added another $100,000 to the pot with a new bet against Mohnot: that Republicans would win the House of Representatives in the midterms. Both turned out to be bad bets. SpaceX acquired Cursor for $60 billion, and Anthropic is heading toward a blockbuster IPO . Then there's the "blunami" that swept the House. On Saturday, Mohnot posted on X that Rabois had paid him the full $200,000. "It's easy to criticize Keith, the bets look lopsided in hindsight but at the time many people were on his side and thought SF was never going to be the same," Mohnot texted Business Insider. Mohnot said he planned to donate $100,000 of the prize pot to the nonprofit Inclusive Abundance. The other half will go to people working to eradicate a disease that runs in Mohnot's family, he said. Rabois also bet Geiger Capital , a popular investing account on X and Substack. In April, Rabois bet $50,000 that Republicans would keep control of the House in the midterms. Earlier in November, Geiger Capital posted a screenshot of the transaction on X. "Wager has been settled," they wrote. "Man of honor." Rabois replied in the thread with a stock-buying suggestion. Rabois and Geiger Capital did not respond to requests for comment from Business Insider. Venture capital is full of (mostly friendly) betting. Poker is big , when investors and founders alike gather around the table to place wagers. In 2023, Andreessen Horowitz alum Balaji Srinivasan said he paid out $1.5 million after betting that bitcoin would reach $1 million in 90 days. Mohnot told Business Insider that some bad bets don't define Rabois, whom he called an "amazing investor." "The beauty about venture is you can be wrong a lot of the time and still make a lot of money," he wrote. "Keith has done that." Read the original article on Business Insider
