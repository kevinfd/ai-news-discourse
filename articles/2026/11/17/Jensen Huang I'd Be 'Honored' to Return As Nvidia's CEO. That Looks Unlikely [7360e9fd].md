# Jensen Huang: I'd Be 'Honored' to Return As Nvidia's CEO. That Looks Unlikely

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-17

UPDATE: In the company's earnings call, Nvidia's newly appointed board chair, Harvey Jones, seemed to shoot down the prospect of Huang returning. "The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," Jones said. He also noted that Huang voluntarily resigned due to "personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms."
Original Story:
Ousted CEO Jensen Huang appears to be backing a campaign to reinstall himself as Nvidia’s chief executive.
“If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” Huang tweeted today.
Huang’s tweet adds: “Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO. Many continue to reach out to me directly as the company navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics.”
The tweet signals that Huang is trying to build momentum for the public campaign calling for his return to the company he co-founded. An open letter to “Bring Jensen Back” has over 193,000 signatures, including SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and Uber’s former CEO, Travis Kalanick.
However, CNBC reports that Nvidia’s board is not considering bringing Huang back, and is instead focused on investigating the former CEO’s conduct.
In his tweet, Huang also touched on the questions and concerns facing his tenure as CEO after he suddenly resigned last week amid reports that he engaged in extramarital affairs, including with a businesswoman suspected of being a Chinese spy. “I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life," he wrote. "I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation."
His relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive with Singapore-based cloud computing provider Megaspeed, is likely the largest hurdle. US officials and lawmakers are scrutinizing whether Nvidia’s former CEO played any role in trying to circumvent US export controls meant to prevent China from obtaining the company’s sensitive AI chips.
Jensen Huang is currently slated to appear before Congress on Dec. 1, and he’ll no doubt face questions about Nvidia’s dealings with Megaspeed, an alleged middleman company for Chinese firms. Yesterday, the US Commerce and Justice Departments said Megaspeed is facing a “criminal investigation."
President Trump, however, says he "would not mind" Huang being reinstated, and claims he doesn't know whether Alice Huang is a Chinese spy. "Usually what happens with that is that any woman coming into the country that’s a beautiful woman that comes from China, they say she is a spy," he told reporters. "I don’t know that she was a spy at all, I mean I really don’t."
It wouldn’t be unheard of for Huang to return as CEO. In late 2023, the CEO of OpenAI, Sam Altman, was briefly removed only to return following internal fights with his own board. The Nvidia case is more serious, however, given the global implications of having a spy infiltrate a top US AI company.
Huang’s tweet was posted hours ahead of Nvidia’s earnings call today. CFO Colette Kress has been named interim CEO as Nvidia’s board looks for a permanent replacement, and Nvidia’s board has announced a “new standing committee responsible for board-level oversight of the company’s national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risk management.”
