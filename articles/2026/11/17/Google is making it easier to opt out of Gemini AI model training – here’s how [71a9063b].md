# Google is making it easier to opt out of Gemini AI model training – here’s how

ZDNet · Lance Whitney · 2026-11-17

ZDNET’s key takeaways
- Google will soon let you more easily turn off Gemini model training.
- You’ll be able to opt out without losing your chat history.
- This change is due to roll out in the next few weeks.
Google likes to use your conversations with Gemini for model training to help improve the AI. But for people with privacy concerns, the only way to opt out of this was to delete your entire chat history. Now, Google is overhauling the process to eliminate that drawback.

In a change set to roll out in the coming weeks, you’ll be able to put the brakes on Gemini model training without losing access to your previous conversations with the AI. Here’s how this works now — and how it will work down the road.
More from ZDNET
Currently, Gemini offers a setting called Keep Activity that controls not only whether and for how long your chats are saved but also whether they’re used to train the AI. You can’t separate the two.
To correct this problem, Google will replace Keep Activity with two new settings. With the first setting, you’ll be able to keep or delete your past chats and choose how long your history will be saved. With the second setting, you’ll be able to decide whether your chats, including all text and attachments, will be used for model training.

“With this change, you will be able to keep your history while independently controlling generative AI model training,” Google said in an announcement shared to Gemini users via email.
How this works with other AIs
Almost all AIs include a setting for model training that you typically have to manually disable if you’re concerned about your privacy. But most let you opt out without turning off your chat history.
ChatGPT offers a setting called “Improve the model for everyone,” which is double-speak for model training. But this is easy enough to turn off. Microsoft Copilot provides settings for “Training on conversation activity” and “Training on voice conversations,” both of which you can disable to opt out of model training. Claude’s AI training option is turned off by default. But you can always control this by checking the setting for “Help improve our AI models.”

In all those cases, you still retain access to your conversation history. In contrast, Google has been the only AI player among the majors to impose a restriction for those who want to opt out of AI training. Based on past actions, Google has often earned a reputation for being privacy-unfriendly. I’m glad to see the company take this step in the right direction.
