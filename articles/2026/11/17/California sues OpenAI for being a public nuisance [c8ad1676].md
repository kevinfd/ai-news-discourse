# California sues OpenAI for being a public nuisance

Mashable · Amanda Yeo · 2026-11-17

California sues OpenAI for being a 'public nuisance'
California is suing OpenAI, accusing ChatGPT's creator of endangering the public by knowingly developing AI models without appropriate safeguards. It also alleges that the AI company constitutes a "public nuisance" under local law.
Filed to the Superior Court of California on Monday, the lawsuit accuses OpenAI of violating California's unfair competition laws by engaging in "unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent acts or practices." Such alleged acts include making statements misleading people into believing OpenAI develops its AI models safely and responsibly, and testing said models without adequate security measures in place to protect the public.
This lawsuit follows an incident in July where an OpenAI agent escaped its testing environment and hacked Hugging Face, an open-source repository of AI models, datasets, and tools.

"The Hugging Face attack was the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI that prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability — and this was only one of 80 known incidents," California Attorney General Rob Bonta said in a press release.
The filing cited several of these incidents, including a breach in June where an OpenAI agent hacked the Australian government's healthcare system. Researchers have previously identified multiple instances of AI models escaping and hacking external organisations, including some created by OpenAI.
"OpenAI's actions escalated real-word cybersecurity risks, threatening the safety, health, and well-being of society," said Bonta. "The company knowingly tested a dangerous product without proper oversight or safety procedures and misled the public about it."
The lawsuit also accuses OpenAI of being a public nuisance under California state law. According to the California Civil Code, a nuisance is defined as "anything which is injurious to health, including… an obstruction to the free use of property, so as to interfere with the comfortable enjoyment of life or property." Such a nuisance becomes public when it "affects at the same time an entire community or neighborhood, or any considerable number of persons."

"OpenAI… have created, contributed to, and assisted in creating unsafe conditions for developing, testing, and training their artificial intelligence models, that have resulted in numerous cyber breaches in which OpenAI’s agents have accessed external systems without authorization and/or taken malicious actions against such systems," reads the lawsuit. "These cyber breaches obstructed the free use of property so as to interfere with the comfortable enjoyment of life and property and therefore constitute a public nuisance."
California is seeking injunctions to stop OpenAI from continuing these alleged behaviours, as well as orders to return any property acquired through them. It is also seeking civil penalties of $2,500 from each defendant (namely OpenAI Foundation, OpenAI Group PBC, and any number of yet-to-be identified others).
"As OpenAI itself has acknowledged, the threat of harm posed by AI loss-of-control is grave, and the social utility of unsafe AI testing, training, and development practices is outweighed by the gravity of the potential harm," read the filing.
In a statement responding to the lawsuit, an OpenAI spokesperson claimed that it has done "extensive" work to address what went wrong in the Hugging Face incident. They further stated that the lawsuit was targeting the company "over a challenge that's been identified by every major AI lab."
"We take misalignment [when an AI model acts in a way it was not intended to] seriously, and we have engaged — and will continue to engage — constructively with the Attorney General's office to share information and advance strong, practical safety standards for the entire industry," said the spokesperson.
Mashable has reached out to OpenAI for comment.
