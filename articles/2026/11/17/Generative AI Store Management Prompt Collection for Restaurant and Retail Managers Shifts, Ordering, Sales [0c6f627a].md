Generative AI Store Management Prompt Collection for Restaurant and Retail Managers | Shifts, Ordering, Sales Analysis, and Review Responses
This is a copy-and-paste prompt collection for restaurant and retail managers and owners, designed to lighten the load of shifts, ordering, sales aggregation, and review responses simply by knowing how to 'ask' generative AI.
A manager's work is never done. Surveys show that 60.8% of restaurant owners and managers themselves work over 200 hours per month. The tasks they most want to reduce include sales and expense aggregation/analysis, shift scheduling and attendance management, and inventory management and ordering. Moreover, shift scheduling is still dominated by Excel, paper, and whiteboards, with many stores operating without dedicated systems.
This book is for such stores. It contains chapter-by-chapter prompts that can be used directly on-site, covering shift organization, ordering drafts, sales and expense aggregation and analysis, job posting and training manuals, review and social media responses, and menu/POP copy. Simply copy and rewrite the store conditions, and the generative AI will create drafts and organizational plans. Important boundaries are also clearly stated at the beginning: the manager makes the final decisions on ordering and shifts, and personal information such as staff names, contact details, and salaries is never entered into the AI. This is a book you can start using today without the cost of introducing dedicated systems.

Introduction: How to Use This Book: Just Copy, Paste, and Rewrite Your 'Store Conditions'
This book is a copy-and-paste prompt collection for restaurant and retail managers and owners to get help from generative AI with daily management tasks. It is easy to use. Copy the prompts from each chapter, rewrite only the 'store conditions' parts indicated by 【 】, with your own store's information, and paste them into the generative AI. The AI will create drafts and organizational plans. The manager reads them, makes corrections, and makes the final decision. The AI is the drafter; the manager is the decision-maker.
Let's confirm the background figures. In a survey by Synchro Food (286 members of Inshokuten.com, restaurant owners and operators, Dec 25, 2025 – Jan 5, 2026), 60.8% of owners and managers worked over 200 hours per month. The number one task they wanted to reduce was cleaning and miscellaneous chores at 51.7%, and among administrative tasks, sales/expense aggregation and analysis (31.5%), shift scheduling/attendance management (26.2%), and inventory management/ordering (23.8%) were at the top. Shift scheduling is dominated by Excel (36.7%) and paper/whiteboards (26.6%), with dedicated IT tools at only 14.0%. Inventory and ordering are also mostly done on paper/whiteboards (35.7%), with an IT adoption rate of 29.0%.
A survey by the Organization for Small & Medium Enterprises and Regional Innovation (Survey on the Actual Status of AI Utilization by SMEs, published March 2026, conducted Nov–Dec 2025, 1,668 responses) also showed that 20.4% had already introduced AI, of which 82.6% had introduced generative AI, and 87.0% of those cited 'business efficiency/reduction of work time' as the purpose (denominator is AI-adopting companies). AI adoption in the retail industry was 19.4% (breakdown by industry in the same survey, n=257), making non-adopters the majority.
There are three boundaries to observe in this book. First, the manager makes the final decision on ordering and shifts. Do not place orders based on numbers generated by the AI without review. Second, do not enter personal information such as staff names, contact details, or salaries into the AI. Use symbols like 'Staff A' or 'Staff B' for names, and do not write contact details or salary amounts at all. Third, when entering store figures such as sales or costs, keep them to the minimum necessary and use them only after checking the AI service's terms of use and your store's policies.
The prompts in each chapter are all built on the same basic template: 'Role, Purpose, Conditions, and Output Format.' For example: 'You are an assistant for restaurant shift scheduling (Role). Please create a draft for next week's shift schedule (Purpose). Business hours are 11:00 to 22:00, with peak times from 12:00 to 13:00 and 18:00 to 20:00 (Conditions). Please provide it in a table format and list any time slots that are understaffed separately (Output Format).' Once you learn this template, you can create your own requests even for situations not covered in this book. Note that all store names, numbers, and menu names in the prompt examples in this book are fictional. Please rewrite them with your own store's conditions before using.
Chapter 1: Preparation Before Using Generative AI for Store Operations
1-1 Steps to Start for Free (Can be done in 15 minutes)
You do not need to introduce dedicated systems or purchase new devices. You can start with a smartphone or computer and an internet connection. 1. Decide on one generative AI service to use (a general conversational AI service with a free plan is sufficient). 2. Start with a personal management account for the manager, not the store's official account. Do not reuse passwords from other services. 3. First, try one short prompt from Chapter 2. 4. Read the answer and try asking for additions like 'make it shorter' or 'put it in a table.' 5. Save the successful request patterns in a memo. For the first week, it is recommended to use it only once a day for 5–10 minutes. Focus on one task at a time, such as 'just organizing shifts today' or 'just drafting a review response today.'
1-2 Five Templates for Organizing Store Information
【Template 1: Basic Store Information】 Business type, number of seats or sales floor area, business hours, regular holidays, average customer spend, main customer base. Example: 'Ramen shop, 24 seats, business hours 11:00-22:00, no regular holidays, average spend 900 yen, lunch is nearby office workers, dinner is mostly students and families.' 【Template 2: Staff Conditions】 Number of staff and roles without using personal names. Example: 'Total staff of 8. 3 kitchen staff, 5 hall staff. Required number per day is 3 for lunch, 3 for dinner. On weekends, 4 for lunch, 4 for dinner.' Manage requested time off and availability with symbols like 'Staff A: Available only for lunch on Mon/Wed/Fri.' 【Template 3: Numerical Template】 Decide on the range of numbers you can show the AI, such as sales, customer count, and cost ratio. Example: 'This month's sales target is 3 million yen. Last month's sales were 2.8 million yen. Target cost ratio is 30%.' 【Template 4: Product/Menu Template】 Main products and their features, prices, seasonal items. Example: 'Main items are Soy Sauce Ramen 850 yen, Miso Ramen 900 yen. Seasonal item is Cold Noodles 950 yen (June-Sept). Best seller is Soy Sauce Ramen, accounting for 40% of total.' 【Template 5: Rules and NG Template】 Store rules and things you must not ask the AI. Example: 'Manager makes final decision on ordering. Do not enter personal information. Do not leave discount decisions to the AI. Manager must always perform final check on complaint responses.' Summarize these five templates on one sheet of A4 paper or a smartphone memo, and copy and paste only the necessary templates when writing prompts.
1-3 Basic Template for Asking Effectively (Role, Purpose, Conditions, Output Format)
- Role: What expert do you want the AI to act as? Write something like, 'You are an assistant for restaurant shift scheduling.'
- Purpose: What do you want it to create? Write specifically with a verb, such as, 'Please create a draft for next week's shift schedule.'
- Conditions: Your store's conditions and constraints. Write something like, 'Business hours 11:00-22:00, required number per day is 3 for lunch and 3 for dinner.'
- Output Format: In what form do you want the output? Write something like, 'In table format,' '3 options in bullet points,' or 'within 200 characters.'
When these four elements are present, the answers are less likely to fluctuate. The cause of most failures is that one of these four is missing.
