# About 60% of AI-linked job postings in 2025 were for non-tech roles: SWDA report

AsiaOne · 2026-11-17

More than half of all job postings in 2025 that mention artificial intelligence (AI) were for roles that did not involve technology, the Skills and Workforce Development Agency (SWDA) shared in a statement on Wednesday (Nov 18).
SWDA's Human Capabilities in the Age of AI report examined over 300,000 job postings per year, representing the hiring of more than 1,600 job roles in Singapore.
Among these, 4 per cent of job postings mention AI - an increase of 0.4 per cent since 2022. Additionally, 5.3 per cent of job postings in the first quarter of 2026 mentioned AI skills.
In 2025, however, of all the job postings that mentioned AI skills, 60 per cent were from non-tech roles, the report stated.
These jobs span various fields, of which the most were seen in finance and accounting, architecture and construction, as well as maintenance and facilities.
"This demonstrates that AI skills are increasingly relevant beyond tech roles, highlighting the need for individuals across job functions to develop the capabilities to work effectively with AI," the report stressed.
Among the 1,600 job roles in 2025, AI skills also appeared in job postings for three in five roles - most of these were related to applications and tools.
Middle and senior roles experienced higher demand for AI skills, with an increase of job postings mentioning AI skills from 3.8 per cent in 2022 to 4.4 per cent in 2025.
The share for junior roles remained stable at about 3.4 per cent, according to the report. Education, infocomm technology and electronics sectors saw higher demand for AI skills.
"Overall, the findings show that AI skills and the ability to use AI-related apps and tools are becoming a requirement for various types of jobs in Singapore, underscoring the importance for individuals to equip themselves with the appropriate AI skills," SWDA said.
Learning opportunities, judgement and decision-making skills key for employees to work with AI
Separately, the report also highlighted that where AI is used to perform tasks needed to build skills, employers would need to provide opportunities for employees to learn.
As AI usage increases, employees must also learn to make decisions that they would not have needed to before. SWDA said they must know what to ask of AI, what to rely on it for, when to question its outputs and how to integrate AI into their work.
To address this, SWDA seeded the idea human-AI agency as a critical core skill - skills that are essential and transferable, applicable across sectors and job roles to support employability and career mobility.
The existing 16 core skills can be broadly categorised into three clusters of critical thinking, interacting with others and staying relevant.
The report added that although AI skills allow individuals to utilise AI tools, human-AI agency will enable them to stay in control when working with AI.
Dilys Boey, Chief Executive of SWDA said, "Individuals must continue to build their technical expertise, human judgement and other critical core skills to work effectively alongside AI, and deliver greater value with AI."
