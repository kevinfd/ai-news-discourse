Probes may delay Huang’s return
Haworth said the next 48 hours will be critical in determining whether Huang succeeds in returning to Nvidia.
“I think it’s going to be incumbent on the incoming Democratic leadership to insist that whether or not he returns, investigations will follow,” Haworth said.
But Yoon thinks Nvidia is unlikely to bring Huang back any time soon, despite current evidence suggesting that “Nvidia’s actions here have been perfectly legal” and that “influencing a political system for your financial interests is very much legal in the United States.”
It seems “untenable for Nvidia to continue doing what they’re doing,” Yoon said. Zooming out even more, he suggested the scandal showed that the entire AI industry can no longer afford to operate under a profit motive alone, so long as governments continue positioning the technology as a strategic asset.
If US investigations expose corruption, bribery, treason, or other crimes, Nvidia risks losing some of its dominant stake in the GPU market. On Tuesday, The Information reported that some Nvidia customers had already lost trust and were looking to Google and AMD for more stability.
Nvidia has also been accused of intervening in a Commerce investigation, which Yoon said could also be “pretty consequential” if any evidence bears that out.
“I think it’s pretty unlikely that Huang makes it back to Nvidia any time soon,” Yoon said. “Maybe he goes on a redemption tour and comes back in five years or something, but I’m not really seeing a path forward for him right now.”
