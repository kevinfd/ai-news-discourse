# Waymo halts freeway rides after California DMV orders ban, cites safety incidents

Mashable · Chase DiBenedetto · 2026-11-17

Waymo halts freeway rides after California DMV orders ban, cites safety incidents
Autonomous cab giant Waymo has suspended freeway access for its fleet of vehicles following a safety notice from the California Department of Motor Vehicles.
Frist reported by the San Francisco Business Times, the California DMV issued a notice on Nov. 11 that a small number of Waymo vehicles in the San Francisco Bay Area had entered restricted construction zones on U.S. Route 101 despite notice of closures. Another vehicle reportedly impeded California Highway Patrol on Highway 280.
Because of these "recurring incursions," the state announced it would ban self-driving taxis from state freeways until the company implemented "effective corrective actions," the DMV said in a statement to TechCrunch. The department called the incidents an imminent hazard to public safety.

Waymo, which is owned by Google's parent holding company, Alphabet, responded by pausing freeway access for its entire nationwide fleet. Freeways had been accessible to Waymo cabs in select cities since 2025, with a brief, unexplained pause from May to July of this year.
"In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance," said Waymo spokesperson Dana Gallagher. "Our ride-hailing service continues to operate safely on surface streets, serving over half a million trips each week. We are working with the California DMV and other state officials diligently and responsibly to resume freeway operations."
Several viral incidents of Waymo cabs driving erratically, ignoring obstacles, and being easily impeded by human obstructions have stoked concern about the nationwide rollout of autonomous vehicles. Video footage of the California Highway Patrol incident shows a Waymo driving toward two CHP vehicles leading a traffic break. The Waymo attempts to go around the vehicles despite the flashing lights and officers' weaving.
The company recently announced an expansion into Denver, San Diego, and Tampa markets, as well as a new collaboration with San Francisco Bay Area transit agencies.
