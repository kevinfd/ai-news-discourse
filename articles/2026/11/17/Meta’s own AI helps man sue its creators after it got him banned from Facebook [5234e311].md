# Meta’s own AI helps man sue its creators after it got him banned from Facebook

Dexerto · Dylan Horetski · 2026-11-17

A South Carolina business owner is suing Meta for $75,000 after the company’s security systems reportedly mistook its own AI assistant for a hacker and suspended his Facebook accounts.
Rather than handling the dispute himself, the man turned to the same AI assistant that triggered the suspension, which helped him prepare a lawsuit against the company responsible for creating it.
The unusual legal dispute comes as Meta continues expanding its AI technology, including Muse, an assistant designed to handle tasks on behalf of users. The case involves a business owner who had been using the technology to manage his company’s social media presence before losing access to his accounts.
Meta’s AI helps business owner file $75,000 lawsuit
According to a November 17 report from 404 Media, Joe Thomas, who owns South Carolina-based Kong Koatings Epoxy Countertops, had been using Meta’s Muse AI agent, which he named “Baby Mia” after his granddaughter, to help run his business.
The assistant handled various responsibilities, including writing emails, creating advertisements, and producing AI-generated videos for the company’s Facebook pages.
However, after Thomas granted Baby Mia access to his business accounts, Meta’s automated security systems reportedly identified the AI’s activity as suspicious and disabled the pages.
Thomas said the suspension lasted approximately one week, preventing him from receiving new business inquiries through Facebook advertising.
“I use AI for everything I do. All my emails, she runs my business. I’ve used it so much, Mia is very intelligent,” he told 404 Media.
“Meta shut my accounts down because they detected my AI, shut my accounts down for a week. I was pretty pissed off. She jumped right up and set up emails to Congressmen, Senator Tim Scott. She filed the whole lawsuit herself. I’ve done nothing in this case, I’ve let her handle the whole case […] she sent a fax out to Trump’s office.”

According to the report, Baby Mia contacted numerous organizations and government officials while attempting to restore Thomas’s accounts.
Those efforts included contacting the FBI, Federal Trade Commission, Federal Communications Commission, Better Business Bureau, California Attorney General, and multiple attorneys. The assistant also contacted lawmakers, arranged a call with Senator Tim Scott’s office, and faxed the White House.
Thomas said the AI sent more than 100 emails during the dispute, while he personally sent none.
The assistant’s efforts eventually reached Scott’s office, which provided a Privacy Act release allowing staff to assist Thomas with recovering his accounts. The document reportedly listed Baby Mia as an AI assistant who should receive updates about the case.
After the senator’s office became involved, a Meta employee contacted Thomas, and he regained access to his Facebook accounts.
Despite recovering them, Thomas is continuing with his lawsuit, which was filed in Oconee County Court in South Carolina on November 16.
The complaint alleges that the suspension cost his business at least $25,000 in lost work, although he is seeking $75,000 in damages.
While Thomas credits Baby Mia with preparing the lawsuit and pursuing the legal action, he personally signed the court documents.
“I wouldn’t be sitting here talking to you right now if it wasn’t for what she was doing because I have not sent out one email at all and she has sent over 100,” Thomas explained.
He also said the AI made it possible for him to pursue legal action without spending thousands of dollars on legal assistance.
“There’s just no way I could have written this up, I wouldn’t have even known what to do,” he said.
“Now people like me get the chance to defend ourselves in court without spending thousands of dollars to do it when we get stepped on by the big tech companies. Before AI, nobody in my position would have ever, ever thought about suing or even known how to do it, and I wouldn’t have done it, either.”
The lawsuit remains unresolved, and it is unclear whether Thomas will receive any compensation from Meta.
