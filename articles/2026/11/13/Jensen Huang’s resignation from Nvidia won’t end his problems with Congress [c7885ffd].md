# Jensen Huang’s resignation from Nvidia won’t end his problems with Congress

Fast Company · Mark Sullivan · 2026-11-13

Jensen Huang may be out at Nvidia, but the questions surrounding his relationship with a suspected Chinese spy—and what it might have meant for American national security—are only growing more serious.
The billionaire’s resignation Thursday has done little to quiet a scandal that threatens to reach well beyond Nvidia’s boardroom, raising questions about the Trump administration’s controversial decisions to loosen restrictions on AI chip sales to China. Huang played an influential role in shaping those policies. Now, Congress wants to know whether his personal relationship with a Chinese businesswoman exposed sensitive American technology or national security information to Beijing—and what government officials knew about the potential risks.
Despite his departure as Nvidia’s chairman and CEO, Huang has reportedly agreed to testify before congressional investigators. The former CEO and lawmakers have been discussing the timing of the closed-door hearing, which could provide answers about his relationship with an executive at a company suspected of funneling restricted Nvidia chips to Chinese AI labs.
Three Republican committee chairs in the House of Representatives—Reps. Brian Mast, John Moolenaar, and Rick Crawford—requested Huang’s testimony in a November 4 letter, more than a week before his resignation. Mast told reporters Thursday that his committee still wanted to speak to Huang, adding that his staff had been in contact with Huang’s team as recently as Wednesday night. Later that day, Bloomberg and NBC News reported that Huang had agreed to appear without a subpoena.
The request followed an October 29 New York Times investigation into Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a company U.S. intelligence had suspected for more than a year of collaborating with the Chinese government and helping funnel trade-restricted AI chips to Chinese labs. The lawmakers are concerned that Alice Huang may also have provided Beijing with access to trade secrets and sensitive national security information.
“Recent reports raise significant national security concerns regarding the PRC’s (People’s Republic of China) access to sensitive U.S. technology and information,” the chairmen wrote. “The issue of PRC infiltration and access to cutting-edge U.S. technology is core to the investigative work of our three committees this Congress. It is vital that Congress determines the extent to which U.S. national security has been placed at risk.”
Trump could be implicated, too
But the congressional scrutiny is no longer limited to Huang or Nvidia. Lawmakers are increasingly asking whether the Trump administration overlooked warnings about potential Chinese espionage while allowing the company to expand sales of advanced AI chips to China.
