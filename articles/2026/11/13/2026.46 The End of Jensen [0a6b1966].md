Agentic commerce purchases with Link, Stripe’s digital wallet that works surprisingly well for agents, grew 38x in September. The catalyst was consumer agents like Muse and Instinct.
Filling out a web form is not a value-add activity for anyone. No one chooses to fill out web forms in their spare time. That’s why these agents have been particularly useful.
I’ve written about how these platforms are giving ordinary people an AI agent with a computer, enabling agents to do genuinely useful things on our behalf. When one of those things is spending money, it needs a wallet that knows what it is, and isn’t, allowed to do.
Stripe has learned that’s really about deciding how much freedom to give it. You might approve one purchase, or give it $500 for flights this week, and let it figure out the rest. The wallet’s job is to keep it within those lines while handling everything between “go buy this” and the purchase actually happening.
That’s what Stripe is building with Link: You approve what it spends, it can come back to you if the final price changes, and eligible purchases can even come with protection if the agent makes a mistake.
You can use Link on Muse, Instinct, as well as many other of the agents you might use already.
