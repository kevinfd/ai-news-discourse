# Is it AI? Is it not AI?

The Hindu · Indulekha S · 2026-11-13

After school, phones at home wait for a sweet 14-year-old to pick them up. It’s more of a humdrum routine, courtesy of modernity, than an impulse or an addiction. Everyday the phone has different uses — sometimes it’s watching funny YouTube videos, other times it’s studying, sometimes calling friends, and some other times, it’s keeping up with social media; watching and sharing the most up-to-date, informative, and trending reels to close kin.
The 14-year-old, Sree Lekshmi, a 9th standard ‘soon-going-to-battle-boards’ student at the Crescent Higher Secondary School in Adaykkakkundu, Malappuram, Keralam, was once fervently excited about the prospect of AI. It was a novel addition which creeped into her amma’s and older sister’s phones and naturally, as someone having a temporary yet symbiotic relationship with the said phones, Sree Lekshmi came to know about it. “It is said to make things easy”, so of course there was no hesitation about trying it. First came ChatGPT which settled in nicely. Then came Google’s AI assistant Gemini, all thanks to that one Studio Ghibli trend which invaded society.
Welcome anyway
Nobody was ready for AI. Not me, not you, and certainly not Sree Lekshmi. “It came to us rather unexpectedly, at an unexpected time so I won’t be really surprised if it suddenly vanishes at an unexpected time either”. The personal assistant phone somehow had another quite intelligent assistant inside it.
12th standard student Jenny from Sarvodaya Vidyalaya ISC, Thiruvananthapuram, Keralam, rants “I better remember the simple things that happened in my childhood, like watching TV after coming from school, than remembering when exactly I first encountered AI in my life. At one point, I never knew what AI was. Then next I know, it’s constantly there in life. I have absolutely no memory of how that transition happened. It must have all taken place so quickly”.
What a visual confusion
The year is 2025. As a lover of the arts — dancing, singing, acting, you name it — and a witness to the viral Studio Ghibli AI trend, Sree Lekshmi’s curiosity took over. “I joined the trend because everyone was participating and generated a Studio Ghibli style animated picture of myself.” This new “AI-generated” image looks back at Sree lekshmi with a slight dissonance. “The dressing style is the same but the facial features showed changes.”
“I was feeling pleasantly surprised about the skills of this technology at the time and never thought about authenticity. Even amongst my friends, we have used AI to kind of have fun.”
The year is 2026. Sree lekshmi’s personal “AI-generated images” count is more than 1 and now she can sense the doubt: is it me or is it not me? AI or not AI? In a span of one year, AI has developed so severely that its generating accuracy has improved. “It’s not easy to spot differences between what is AI and what is real. I get tricked by Instagram reels every now and then. Once a video as simple as a room getting cleaned tricked me into thinking it was real. So now, my first reaction is to check the comment box.”
Meanwhile, Jenny with her “No AI” stance on art is also confused. “What is the point of all this? My classmates have come and whispered ‘ballerina cappuccina’ in my ear when I’m doing differentiation in class. What is the point of ballerina cappuccina? There are AI videos, stories, and even animated series with fruit and vegetable characters which even the adults watch. Some others are just so unnatural. The thing is, very old people believe that some of these unnatural videos are real, even in my family. At this point, basically, my job inside my house is to keep explaining how many of these are actually AI and how many are not.”

Accusations against hardwork
Jenny’s first emotional tango with people having suspicion and doubt over something entirely made by a human is actually on social media itself. “I have some favourite animators and editors that I follow ardently on social media. They do amazing animations and are literally among my favourite creators. A while back I started noticing people throwing hate or having an immense disbelief, and going so far as to accuse and comment on videos saying ‘AI’, ‘this is AI’, etc. This is not just one video but countless ones. When I saw it, I felt conflicted, bothered, and deeply sad. How can people genuinely believe their hard work is a product of AI, a mere AI prompt? It makes me wonder if humanity has really stooped so low. How can everything good be just AI? How can people think that humans aren’t capable of making this stuff?”
Jenny is quite fortunate to have not been the direct subject of such an accusation but her grief in this case of her favourite creators feels almost her own and she doesn’t really know how exactly it would be if she were to handle it. “In my case, my works, whether they are assignments or art, do not really have a huge audience. Everyone knows me and they know I don’t use AI. So there’s trust. On the other hand, I have also seen situations where students use AI and nobody really seems to care. The attitude is that the work should get done. That’s very annoying and sad.”
Teachers seem to say AI shouldn’t be used but they often miss explaining what the technology is all about.
Control and dilemma
Jenny controls her social media algorithm so much that she gets less AI content compared to some of her friends. “I don’t allow much AI to creep in and settle. I quickly just swipe it away. Then again, I myself have used AI for writing projects. I wonder how many skills I would lose if I were dependent on AI all the time, like, would I be able to even write a project on my own?”
With everything Sree Lekshmi has observed, it’s harder for her to trust things, believe what’s authentic. “I doubt almost every reel and I am constantly questioning whether it’s AI or not. I also don’t think removing social media has any point because of how AI is literally everywhere and everyone’s using it. I think the only way is to speak up against it when things are going wrong. I also feel that many people are losing opportunities. I personally wish to post dance videos in the future and I will not stop just because AI is taking over social media. I am just confused about AI’s usage especially when I realise how great and pristinely well it can fake things today.”
