# New Anthropic job listing offers $405,000 to land natural gas for its post-IPO data center buildout

Business Insider · Stephen Council, Stephen Council (scouncil@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-13

Tom Brown, cofounder and chief compute officer at Anthropic. Cornell Watson/Bloomberg via Getty Images Anthropic is approaching its initial public offering and planning its data center buildout. A brand-new job listing seeks someone who can get gas pipelined to its data centers. Natural gas, despite its emissions, has fast become a bedrock of the AI infrastructure boom. Anthropic is getting deeper into gas-guzzling. The company posted a new job listing earlier this month for its first "Gas Supply and Transport Lead," tasked with getting natural gas to Anthropic data centers. The listing — with a salary range of $320,000 to $405,000 — comes as Anthropic approaches a gigantic initial public offering that will provide cash to buy more computing power . Natural gas has become a core part of AI's infrastructure buildout, despite emissions that clash with some tech giants' previous green-energy goals. Companies are increasingly using the fuel "behind-the-meter," or separate from the larger energy grid. Anthropic wrote in its IPO prospectus that it expects to need to compete for natural gas in some areas where it's helping to build new data centers. "Some of our campuses will run on gas-fired, behind-the-meter generation," the new job posting says. "At those sites fuel is the power supply: if gas does not arrive, in the volume and at the pressure the plant needs, the compute does not run." Data center developers turn to natural gas because it's relatively quick to access, and because their customers ask for 24/7 power . The new posting puts this succinctly: the hire will need to "Design for fuel reliability at sites where an interruption means lost compute." The hire will negotiate gas supply agreements, test new sites for potential, and use interstate, intrastate, and Canadian pipelines, the job post says. It asks for someone with at least eight years of experience in the natural gas industry. Anthropic typically provides equity along with its cash salaries. The company did not respond to a request for comment from Business Insider. Anthropic's data center ambitions rely on natural gas Anthropic's push to access more computing power is a core challenge for its business — the company grew so fast in 2026 that CEO Dario Amodei quipped at one point that it was "too hard to handle." Computing power lets Anthropic train and run its AI models, making it a crucial resource to plan around. In May, Anthropic announced a deal to get hundreds of megawatts of computing power from SpaceX's Colossus One data center, which had provoked local pushback for its use of gas turbines. Amodei's company had already been using compute from the cloud giants Google, Amazon, and Microsoft. The company, in its IPO prospectus, also disclosed that it has committed around $900 billion in infrastructure investments, with around half due by 2030. In addition to buying compute from cloud providers, Anthropic is now developing its own data centers with partners. That's where the natural gas job listing fits in, and where Anthropic will grapple with the complex and competitive process of securing electrical power. It is already spending $3.4 billion to buy a 1.7-gigawatt batch of gas-fired engines from a Texan equipment supplier, the Financial Times reported this week. In its IPO filing's risks section, Anthropic wrote that its dependence on natural gas exposes it to potential cost swings and delays. It also said environmental standards for power generation could evolve amid "shifting public and political views regarding climate change." "The physical effects of climate change may increase our operating costs over time, as higher average temperatures can increase the power consumption and water usage required to cool equipment at the facilities on which we depend," the company wrote.
