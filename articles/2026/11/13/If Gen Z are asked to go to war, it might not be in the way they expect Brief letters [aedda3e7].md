# If Gen Z are asked to go to war, it might not be in the way they expect | Brief letters

The Guardian · Guardian Staff · 2026-11-13

Jason Okundaye addresses the issue of whether Britain’s youth would be willing to lay down their lives in defence of our country (Gen Z doesn‘t relish going to war – so what! You should worry more about the reckless generations that do, 10 November). A more likely scenario is that they will be asked to fight and die for the sake of a Nato ally on Russia’s border, such as Poland or Lithuania.
 
Matthew J Smith
 
New Malden, London
 Starbucks’ plans (Starbucks plans to hire 7,500 staff in major UK expansion plan, 9 November) coupled with the inexorable advance of AI would suggest a career as a barista might now be more secure than that of a barrister.
 
Philip Clarke
 
East Bridgford, Nottinghamshire
 Mr Netanyahu wants Israel to become a “super Sparta” (Editorial, 12 November). Has he checked what happened to Sparta after its defeat at Leuctra in 371BC – a generation after the Peloponnesian war?
 
Peter Brooker
 
West Wickham, London
 For itching powder (Letters, 12 November) we used to collect the seeds inside rosehips.
 
Stephen Walkley, aged 77¾
 
Lutterworth, Leicestershire
 I’m only 82, but I recall as an 11-year-old reading about “itchy” powder in the Beano comic. My schoolmates and I tried to make it with fibres from rosehips. We weren’t very successful.
 
Christopher Osborne
 
West Bridgford, Nottinghamshire
