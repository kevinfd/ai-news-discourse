# Nvidia's neocloud empire lost its biggest champion. Washington may have dealt a bigger blow.

Business Insider · Geoff Weiss, Geoff Weiss (gweiss@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-21

Nvidia helped fuel the rise of neoclouds, which now face mounting pressure. Chesnot/Getty Images Nvidia's neocloud empire faces new threats following Jensen Huang's departure. New US restrictions could cut neoclouds off from Chinese customers. Interim CEO Colette Kress signaled that Nvidia's financial backing has its limits. It's been a rough two weeks for neoclouds, which buy Nvidia's chips and rent compute out to businesses. They were already on shaky ground — then Jensen Huang got ousted , and Washington cut off a major customer base. Before his resignation, Huang essentially led the bank of AI by investing in neoclouds and providing financial guarantees that helped them raise money — and now he's no longer in charge. Then, on Monday, the US government closed a loophole  that had enabled it to do business in China. Finally, interim CEO Colette Kress clarified on  Nvidia's earnings call  on Tuesday that the company's financial backing for neoclouds has limits. "There is real fear that Nvidia might pull back on their investments and backstops," Gavin Baker, managing partner of Atreides Management, wrote on X. The timing couldn't be worse. Shares of major neoclouds, including CoreWeave, IREN, and Nebius, plummeted in the double digits this week. Nvidia's China loophole While China has long been barred from buying Nvidia's most advanced chips, a loophole has allowed Chinese companies to rent compute capacity from neoclouds operating overseas, including in countries such as Malaysia and Thailand. Now, those providers will need government licenses to serve Chinese customers, effectively barring them from doing so. This could abruptly shut Western access to the world's second-largest AI market. There were already signs that a crackdown was looming. Earlier this month, before the new restrictions took effect, Oracle canceled a $7 billion deal to provide AI computing power to Tencent following concerns from the Trump administration, The Financial Times reported. This week, Japanese infrastructure company Datasection — which SemiAnalysis linked to an Nvidia-powered project intended to serve Chinese demand — slashed its full-year revenue forecast by 89%. The company cited a canceled order and construction delays. "It won't be the last," SemiAnalysis wrote on X. Nvidia was the bank of AI Data centers are increasingly unpopular , power is hard to come by, and lenders are wary of projects that might not get finished on time. All of that has made borrowing the billions that neoclouds need increasingly difficult. For years, Huang has helped keep the money flowing. Neoclouds became a critical customer base to foster, as cloud giants like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft developed their own chips in a bid to become less reliant on Nvidia over time. Now, Huang's departure raises questions about how Nvidia will support neoclouds going forward. On Tuesday, Kress clarified the limits of its financial backing. She said certain Nvidia guarantees depend on neoclouds securing financing and completing construction. If projects run into trouble, she said, "our guarantee doesn't exist anymore." This signals to neoclouds that Nvidia's support is conditional. And a lack of backing from Nvidia could make borrowing harder. Gil Luria, an analyst at DA Davidson, said lenders are already pulling back from neoclouds and predicted Nvidia's financial support would diminish. "The only option they have is to borrow from their customers in the form of prepayments," Luria said. "That means they have to provide huge discounts to their customers, which will impair margins for the foreseeable future. " Have a tip?
