# I'm a Gen Zer who can't afford to doomsday prep the way I'd like to, but there's one item I'll buy — just in case

Business Insider · Agnes Applegate, Agnes Applegate (aapplegate@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-21

Gen Z seems more reluctant to prep for a potential blackout than older generations. Agnes Applegate As high-profile outages continue, the older generations in my life are panicking about a blackout. I, along with many Gen Zers, am far from prioritizing a blackout prep kit right now. The one thing I will buy? A hand-crank radio. For the past week or so, a large chunk of my personal, work, and social conversations have had a sprinkle of doomsday prepping in them. As a wave of high-profile outages , including banks and airlines, has unfolded, many people on social media have started prepping for a potential 90-day blackout. Some say the lingering feeling of being unprepared during COVID has led them to panic-buy supplies now. But I, as a 23-year-old, less than two years postgrad, read about the thousands of dollars people are panic-spending and it makes me cringe. With rising inflation, an unstable economy, and AI ( and Robert Pattinson ) seemingly taking all of our jobs, I've decided to keep the money I've managed to set aside tucked away for now. I'm not trying to make light of the disastrous effects of a potential blackout. My Gen X mother, who has never been one to buy into conspiracy theories, has worked herself into a tizzy, begging my older brother and me to look into how we could prepare ourselves. And I'll admit I feel more anxiety than I used to when my phone loses charge. As a reporter who covers technology, I'm aware of how dire this all could be. But I'm from a generation that, despite saving and investing earlier than previous ones, feels much broker than we are. Adding some variation of doomsday prepping into my budget feels like a sacrifice I'm not willing to make. However, for the sake of my mom and all of you, I decided to sit down and think about the easiest and most affordable preparations I could make for a potential 90-day blackout. The prep advice I'm not taking From my mom, my friends' parents, and other older adults in my life, I've heard a lot about prepping. I've been sent countless articles and a few very helpful Reddit threads on how others are approaching this. Beyond my Gen Z nihilism and economic anxiety stopping me, my tiny Brooklyn apartment is not equipped to handle most of the items I'm about to mention. So I'm not planning on following this specific advice. I also don't think I'll be heading home for Thanksgiving to find a bunker full of these items just yet, but those who are prepping in a very serious manner seem to be purchasing: Backup generators Gallons of water Batteries and portable chargers Nonperishable food and extra toiletries Fuel Flashlights and candles Camping supplies and portable stoves The one prep kit purchase I'm making I won't be pulling cash out of the bank, buying a $900 backup generator , or stocking my small pantry with pounds of non-perishable food. But the one real blackout-prep purchase I've decided I'll make is a hand-crank radio with a USB charging port. These radios can generate electricity when the user cranks the handle, and I found both new and secondhand options online for around $40 to $100. When Hurricane Sandy struck the Northeast in 2012, my family and I lost power for a few days. Right before the storm hit, my parents had gotten four crank radios with flashlights and charging ports from L.L. Bean for me, my brother, and themselves. These radios were not only life-savers for charging our devices, but also gave me a sense of control and power in a very uncontrollable environment. I remember being nine years old and terrified, but sitting there cranking my radio to life helped me feel like I was actually doing something to help. It was kind of a three-birds-one-stone situation — entertainment, lighting, and charging all taken care of in one. Gen Z doesn't seem that invested in doomsday prepping — but not for lack of concern It's been funny to be on social media this week. My TikTok feed has been littered with fellow Gen Zers joking about the potential blackout and advice they've seen. A personal favorite of mine was a TikToker who joked that she was one step ahead of people withdrawing cash from their bank accounts because her balance was $40. My friends and I have joked in group chats about the types of prep kits we would make, from the absolutely necessary camera film and vapes my one friend suggested would go in his kit to the not-so-necessary toiletries and food pantry supplies. We all still believe in the possibility of a blackout , and feel anxiety from it, but given the events of the last few decades that we've grown up through, there's a "so what if it gets worse, it already has?" attitude we can't help but possess. One of the most interesting things I've noticed, though, is this generational divide in reactions. More than economic differences, it's the boomers and Gen X people in my life who are texting me one news article about an outage after another, asking, "Is this all related?" Maybe trying to prepare for the unknown hasn't been lost on them, as it feels like it has for us. Regardless, mother, if you've read this whole story, you'll be happy to know I'm purchasing my crank radio tonight. Do you have a story to share about how you're prepping for a potential blackout?
