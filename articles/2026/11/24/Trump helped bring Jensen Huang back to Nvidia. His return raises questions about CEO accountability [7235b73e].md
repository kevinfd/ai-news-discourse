# Trump helped bring Jensen Huang back to Nvidia. His return raises questions about CEO accountability.

Business Insider · Hallam Bullock, Hallam Bullock (hbullock@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-24

David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images This post originally appeared in the Business Insider Today newsletter. You can sign up for Business Insider's daily newsletter here . Eleven days. That's how long Nvidia took to announce Jensen Huang's resignation, name an interim CEO, begin its search for a replacement — and end up back where it started . The board's explanation to employees ran just five sentences . So, what changed? "I spoke to them, let me put it that way," President Donald Trump told CNBC. "The board decided to do what we wanted them to do." Trump followed up with a visual aid. He posted a digitally altered version of the photograph taken after his 2024 assassination attempt. It showed Huang, not Trump, being lifted by Secret Service agents. Fist raised. Red bra in hand . Just like that, a boardroom crisis became a comeback poster, and a lesson for corporate America about who is in charge . Markets cheered Huang's return. Nvidia's rally added roughly $1 trillion in market value . The Nasdaq surged 7% , while Asian chip stocks roared back . But the decision raises an important question. When do the rules apply, and to whom? "When you're a star, they let you do it." Trump's words from the Access Hollywood tape now feel prescient, BI's Sarah E. Needleman writes . Huang's resignation followed reports that he had relationships with two employees, including a direct report, and had not been candid with the board. The board also launched an investigation to see if Huang had been the subject of a foreign-intelligence investigation after he reportedly had an affair with a Chinese executive . Much of the case made by Huang's supporters was not that the reports were unfounded. It was that Nvidia could not afford to lose him. The New York Times later reported that Huang displayed an alleged pattern of humiliating his employees . Huang reportedly told one executive his mother should have aborted him. He allegedly threatened to rip off another employee's arms and beat the person to death with them. On another occasion, he reportedly said he would rip the top off an employee's head and pee on their brain . The reports did not appear to deter Nvidia's board. It remains to be seen whether they will deter parents from dressing their children as Huang for Halloween . At Nvidia, it seems the more indispensable you are, the fewer rules you have to follow. Sarah spoke to workplace experts and company leaders about what Huang's return could mean for the rest of white-collar America — and why it could be another nail in the coffin of the #MeToo movement.
