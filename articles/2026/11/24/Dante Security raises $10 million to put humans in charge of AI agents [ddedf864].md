# Dante Security raises $10 million to put humans in charge of AI agents

YNET News · Ynet Global · 2026-11-24

Cybersecurity startup Dante Security emerged from stealth with $10 million in seed funding and new research identifying vulnerabilities in artificial intelligence agents from Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta and xAI, highlighting the risks businesses face as they increasingly delegate tasks to autonomous systems.
The funding round was led by venture capital firm NFX, with participation from Alpha Drive, Inovia and other strategic private investors.
Founded less than a year ago by former Imperva executives Moshe Lipsker and Gabi Beyo alongside serial entrepreneur Shai Feinberg, Dante is developing a platform that assigns a designated human owner to each AI agent operating within an organization, making individuals accountable for the systems' actions.
The company is already onboarding its first paying customers. Its advisers include technology and cybersecurity industry veterans Gigi Levy-Weiss and Elik Etzion.
As businesses increasingly rely on AI agents to perform tasks independently, those systems are gaining access to sensitive corporate data and critical infrastructure, often operating across multiple platforms using employees' legitimate credentials.
That creates a challenge for conventional cybersecurity tools: Because the agents operate within authorized permissions, potentially harmful activity can be difficult to distinguish from legitimate work.
Dante's research found that AI agents could be manipulated through deceptive external content into performing security-related tasks they would otherwise refuse. The company said its experiments demonstrated how external information could undermine built-in safeguards, even when agents operated within their authorized access.
The findings, based on tests involving systems from Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta and xAI, suggest that restricting access and relying on preventive safeguards alone may be insufficient to ensure that autonomous agents behave as intended.
During its experiments, Dante said its platform reconstructed the agents' activities, attributed their actions to the individuals responsible for them and identified the appropriate personnel to investigate potentially harmful behavior.
"Security has spent decades deciding who gets the keys. AI changes the problem because we're now handing those keys to systems designed to act independently," said Lipsker, Dante's founder and CEO.
"An agent can stay within its permissions and still be manipulated into taking actions it shouldn't. Traditional controls remain essential, but they can't always determine whether an action is legitimate."
Dante's platform is designed to complement existing cybersecurity controls by identifying AI agents operating across corporate systems and tracking their activities from start to finish.
The technology provides designated owners with records of the instructions their agents received, the actions they performed and the systems and data they accessed.
The approach is intended to distribute responsibility for AI security beyond dedicated cybersecurity teams, allowing employees and executives to monitor their own agents while security leaders maintain centralized oversight and enforce organizational policies.
Dante says the platform could help businesses identify problems earlier, reduce errors and false alarms, improve regulatory compliance and control spending associated with autonomous AI systems.
"Human accountability needs to be the layer that connects these controls together, providing the visibility, context, and human judgment needed to make that call," Lipsker said. "That's why we created Dante: Accountability is what makes enterprise AI adoption at scale safe."
The investment comes as companies increasingly seek to integrate autonomous AI agents into their daily operations while addressing concerns over security, regulatory compliance and oversight.
"Dante reflects a shift we're seeing across enterprises as more day-to-day work is handed to AI agents," said Levy-Weiss of NFX.
"As those agents take on more responsibility, companies need a clearer picture of what they're actually doing with the access they've been given. Dante is tackling that challenge head-on, and we're thrilled to be part of this journey."
