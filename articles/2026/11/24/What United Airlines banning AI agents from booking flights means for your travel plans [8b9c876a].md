# What United Airlines banning AI agents from booking flights means for your travel plans

Fox News · Kurt Knutsson, CyberGuy Report · 2026-11-24

Wouldn't it be nice to tell an AI assistant to find the cheapest flight to your destination and book your favorite seat while you do something else? That kind of convenience is getting closer. But United Airlines wants to make sure an unauthorized AI assistant never gets that far.
The airline is restricting third-party AI agents from accessing its booking systems without permission. United says these tools could expose sensitive customer information and make purchases travelers never intended to authorize. The concern also extends to MileagePlus accounts, where an automated mistake could put valuable airline miles at risk.
The decision comes as AI assistants become increasingly capable of taking action on our behalf. While that sounds convenient, United is raising questions about how much control we should give software over our travel plans. Here's where United draws the line and what you need to know before letting AI handle your next flight booking.

Why United Airlines is blocking AI agents
On Nov. 11, United Airlines published an official news release titled United Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents. The airline explained that protecting customer information and account security prompted the restrictions. According to United, sharing account credentials with personal AI assistants can expose sensitive information. The airline also warned that automated actions might lead to unwanted purchases or changes to existing reservations. Then there's the issue of frequent flyer miles. United specifically mentioned the possibility that an AI assistant could use MileagePlus miles without the traveler intending that redemption.
To address those concerns, United is restricting unauthorized personal AI agents from accessing its digital platforms. The rules also reach travel agencies and booking services that might use automated AI tools to access United's systems. United says it is updating its policies to support these restrictions. The airline also plans to continue evaluating how personal AI assistants interact with its services.
What exactly is an AI travel agent?
You may already use AI to research vacation destinations or figure out which airport is closest to your hotel. An AI travel agent takes that technology further by performing tasks for you. For example, you could ask an AI assistant to find a flight under $400 that arrives before dinner. Instead of simply recommending flights, an AI agent might search booking systems and attempt to complete the reservation. Some agents can also interact with websites using a browser. That means they may be able to enter personal information and complete steps that normally require you to click through a booking process.
The convenience is easy to understand. However, granting an AI assistant access to your airline account can also give it the ability to make changes you never intended. For more on what these assistants can do, see our article on eight seriously annoying tasks you can hand off to an AI agent.
United says AI agents could create airline safety problems
United's concerns go beyond account security. The airline also warns that automated AI activity could potentially violate Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and Department of Transportation (DOT) regulations. Consider what happens when you check in for a flight. Airlines require travelers to acknowledge important safety information, including whether their bags contain prohibited hazardous materials.
United specifically points to the requirement for a person to confirm that baggage contains no banned substances, such as explosives. An AI assistant handling check-in could potentially bypass that acknowledgment. After all, how would an AI assistant know what you actually packed in your suitcase? United has identified this as a potential regulatory concern. The airline has not established that every AI-assisted check-in violates federal law.
Could United cancel a flight booked by an AI agent?
This may be the biggest concern for travelers experimenting with automated booking tools. United revised its Contract of Carriage on Nov. 11. The updated rules prohibit AI agents from making or modifying tickets and purchasing airline products or services.
The contract also identifies reservations made or changed through AI agents as subject to cancellation without advance notice. The definition of an AI agent includes automated tools even when a traveler supervises or approves their actions. However, there is an important exception for existing reservations.
United says bookings made by AI agents before Nov. 11, will not be canceled under the new restriction. The airline recommends reviewing those reservations to confirm that they were properly booked and ticketed. If you previously used an AI assistant to arrange United travel, open the United app or website and verify your itinerary. Check that your ticket was actually issued rather than assuming a booking confirmation means everything is complete. You can review the airline's published rules in its Contract of Carriage at united.com/en/us/fly/contract-of-carriage.html.

Can you still use AI to plan a United Airlines trip?
Yes, although United has established limits on how outside AI tools can interact with its systems. You can still ask an AI chatbot for general travel advice or help creating an itinerary. United's guidance allows AI-assisted research and trip planning as long as the tool does not access United's website or app. However, an unauthorized AI agent cannot search United's website or app on your behalf. The restriction also covers automated purchasing and reservation management. That means an AI-powered browser feature that automatically searches United's website could fall under the restriction, even if it never buys a ticket.
United says assistive technology for travelers with disabilities remains unaffected. Traditional travel booking services can also continue operating through permitted channels. The restriction specifically addresses unauthorized AI agents, including those used by travel agencies. According to United's published guidance, the airline does not currently work with any third-party AI agents under this policy.
Southwest Airlines is taking a different approach to AI
While United is putting restrictions in place, Southwest Airlines is exploring a different approach. On Oct. 6, Southwest announced a ChatGPT integration. The feature allows you to discover destinations and shop for Southwest flights directly through ChatGPT. However, Southwest's integration still directs you to the airline's website to complete purchases. The company describes the arrangement as an early step toward broader AI-powered booking capabilities.
That approach gives Southwest a way to use AI for flight discovery while maintaining control over the final transaction. United is taking a more restrictive position on outside AI assistants. The airline says it will continue evaluating the technology as it develops.
How to protect your United Airlines bookings and miles
If you normally book flights through United's website or app, you can continue doing so. You can also use permitted travel booking services. However, if you've started experimenting with AI assistants that make purchases or manage accounts on your behalf, United's restrictions deserve your attention. An AI agent with access to your airline account could potentially reach sensitive travel information. Depending on its permissions, it might also make purchases you never intended to authorize. Here are a few ways to protect yourself.
1) Keep your airline account credentials private
Avoid giving third-party AI assistants your MileagePlus password or other sensitive login information. If you've already shared your credentials with an AI agent, United recommends changing your password immediately. You should also remove any saved login details from the AI service. Consider using a password manager to create and securely store a strong, unique password for your airline account. This can help protect your account if a password you use elsewhere gets exposed in a data breach.
2) Use strong antivirus software
Strong antivirus software can help protect your devices from malware and malicious links designed to steal login credentials. This is especially important if you use travel booking websites or receive airline confirmation emails. While antivirus protection won't stop an AI assistant from making an unauthorized reservation, it can help prevent cybercriminals from gaining access to your MileagePlus account. 3) Book United flights through permitted channels
Use United's official website or app, or a booking provider operating under United's rules. United currently does not work with third-party AI agents under this policy. Even if an AI assistant offers to complete a reservation with your approval, that does not necessarily make the transaction permissible.

4) Check your airline miles regularly
Review your MileagePlus balance for unexpected activity. If you notice a redemption or purchase you don't recognize, secure your account immediately. Contact United through its official website or app to report the activity and request assistance.
5) Verify reservations before heading to the airport
Check your booking confirmation and make sure United has issued your ticket. If an AI assistant arranged your travel before Nov. 11, verify the reservation directly with the airline. United says those earlier bookings will not be canceled under the new restriction.
6) Keep control of purchases and account changes
AI can still help you plan a trip using general information without accessing United's systems. However, complete your flight purchases and reservation changes yourself through permitted channels. That way, you can review what you're buying without risking a prohibited AI-agent transaction.
Kurt's key takeaways
I can see why United is concerned. I've spent enough time searching for flights to appreciate the idea of handing that job over to AI. Let the assistant find me a great fare and save me the hassle of comparing a dozen options. I'm all for that. But the moment it starts making purchases or accessing my airline miles, I want to know exactly what it's doing.
I recently spoke with travel expert Katy Nastro, who discovered that someone had stolen 50,000 miles from her American Airlines account. Her experience had nothing to do with AI agents, but it reminded me how much value we keep in these loyalty programs. I certainly wouldn't want an automated mistake putting those rewards at risk.
At the same time, I do wonder whether security is the only concern driving United's decision. Airlines have spent years building direct relationships with travelers. AI agents could change how we compare fares and choose which airline gets our business. I would hate to see useful technology held back simply because it gives you more control over the shopping process. United says it will keep evaluating these tools, and I hope that eventually leads to a secure way to book flights using AI. Until then, I'm happy to let AI help find my next trip. I'll take care of the booking myself.
