# OpenAI published its AI progress meter to encourage a slowdown. It won’t work

Fast Company · Mark Sullivan · 2026-11-24

Though it’s been lost amid the Jensen Huang drama at Nvidia, OpenAI made a startling revelation on Sunday. In an essay, the firm’s chief scientist, Jakub Pachocki, made an overture to rival AI labs to agree on ways to slow down the development of new models. But with billions at stake and companies racing for enterprise customers, there’s little reason to believe anyone will hit the brakes.
Pachocki’s essay, titled “Perplexity and Intelligence,” includes some progress numbers that may have been meant to jolt the industry into a slowdown. He discloses for the first time an internal metric that OpenAI uses to track how much its models improve during pretraining. Pachocki invites other frontier AI labs to publish comparable numbers, creating a common basis for monitoring—and potentially coordinating a slowdown in—the development of artificial intelligence until adequate safety and monitoring tools can be developed.
OpenAI’s CEO, Sam Altman, praised the essay, going on X to call it “the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while.” Microsoft AI’s CEO, Mustafa Suleyman, voiced his company’s support for the effort and reported a progress score for one of its own models. Pachocki’s proposal, however, is unlikely to matter if the other labs don’t publish their own progress numbers and agree to speed limits. And so far, other labs, including Anthropic and Google DeepMind, have not signaled a willingness to sign on.
An alarming acceleration
Pachocki has good reason to propose a pause. OpenAI’s numbers show AI advancing at an alarmingly fast rate. OpenAI’s own progress score improved more in 2026 than in the previous three years combined, Pachocki says, and an unreleased internal model shows another large stride in intelligence.
The numbers OpenAI published are “perplexity” scores, which measure how confidently a language model predicts the next word in unfamiliar text. Researchers test models on withheld data, with lower scores indicating more confident predictions and a log-perplexity score of zero meaning the model assigns 100% probability to every correct token. (Yes, the AI search company Perplexity was, sadly, named after this metric.)
Since 2022, OpenAI’s base-model log-perplexity has fallen from roughly 0.99 (capable of “fluent conversation”) to 0.76 (capable of solving “a Millennium Prize math problem”)—a striking improvement in how well models predict tokens, which can represent words, numbers, code, etc.
The chart’s biggest story may concern what comes after current frontier models. OpenAI’s most recent model, GPT-6.2 Astra, reaches a score of 0.76, while an unreleased internal model has already dropped to a score of 0.73, OpenAI says—a conspicuous break from the earlier pace of progress.
