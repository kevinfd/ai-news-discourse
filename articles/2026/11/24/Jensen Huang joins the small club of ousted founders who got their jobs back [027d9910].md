# Jensen Huang joins the small club of ousted founders who got their jobs back

Business Insider · Katherine Li,Thibault Spirlet,Natalie Musumeci, Katherine Li,Thibault Spirlet,Natalie Musumeci (katherineli@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-24

Former Apple CEO Steve Jobs, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman. Justin Sullivan/Getty Images; Soeren Stache/picture alliance via Getty Images; Spencer Platt/Getty Images Jensen Huang has become the latest founder to get his job back after being pushed out. Nvidia reinstated Huang less than two weeks after he was forced to resign amid a scandal. Here are four other founders who made a comeback after losing their jobs. It used to be called "pulling a Steve Jobs." Then it became "pulling a Sam Altman." Moving forward, don't be surprised if it's referred to as "pulling a Jensen." Jensen Huang just became the latest founder to return to lead their company after being pushed aside. On Monday, Nvidia reinstated Huang as CEO, 11 days after his November 12 resignation amid reports of extramarital affairs. Here are other founders who, like Huang, made their comeback after being pushed out. Sam Altman Open AI CEO Sam Altman speaks at the OpenAI DevDay 2026 conference, Tuesday, Sept. 29, 2026, in San Francisco. (AP Photo/Jeff Chiu) Jeff Chiu/AP Sam Altman 's ouster as OpenAI CEO didn't last long. The ChatGPT maker's board abruptly fired Altman on November 17, 2023, saying he had not been consistently candid in his communications with directors, hampering their ability to oversee him. The decision set off a corporate crisis. OpenAI cofounder Greg Brockman, whom the board had removed as chairman, resigned from the company before also returning. Microsoft, OpenAI's largest investor and commercial partner, offered Altman a job leading a new AI research team. OpenAI chief scientist Ilya Sutskever , who had voted to fire Altman, publicly reversed course three days later. On November 20, Sutskever posted that he deeply regretted his participation in the board's decision and would do everything he could to reunite the company. On the same day, more than 700 OpenAI employees signed an open letter demanding that the board resign and reinstate Altman, threatening to join him at Microsoft if it didn't. On November 21, four days after firing Altman, OpenAI announced an agreement in principle for him to return as CEO under a reconstituted board. Altman officially resumed the job on November 29, with Brockman also returning as president. Steve Jobs Steve Jobs. Justin Sullivan/Getty Images Steve Jobs , Apple's cofounder and chairman, was first pushed out by the person he had recruited to run the company. In 1985, then-CEO John Sculley and Apple's board stripped Jobs of his operational responsibilities after a power struggle over the company's strategy, amid slowing Macintosh sales. Jobs remained chairman but soon resigned, leaving to start the computer company NeXT. More than a decade later, Apple came calling. The company announced a roughly $400 million deal to buy NeXT in December 1996 and completed the acquisition in February 1997, bringing Jobs back as an advisor. Jobs became Apple's de facto leader after Gil Amelio's departure in July 1997. Apple announced his appointment as interim CEO that September, setting the stage for a monumental turnaround . The colorful iMac, launched in 1998, helped sustain the company's profitability. He then took the company beyond computers, introducing the iPod in 2001 and the iTunes Music Store in 2003, before unveiling the iPhone in 2007 and the iPad in 2010. Jobs resigned as CEO in 2011 amid long-standing health problems, including a rare form of pancreatic cancer. By then, Apple had grown from a roughly $2 billion company when Jobs returned to one worth about $350 billion. Jack Dorsey Jack Dorsey's Block is laying off employees again. Eva Marie Uzcategui/Bloomberg via Getty Images Jack Dorsey 's stint as Twitter's first CEO lasted less than two years. Dorsey, who co-founded the company in 2006, was pushed out of the CEO role in October 2008 as the company grew and directors questioned his management approach. Dorsey stayed on as chairman of the board, while cofounder Evan Williams took over as CEO. Dorsey went on to cofound payments company Square, now known as Block, and remained a major Twitter shareholder and board member. He regained the position roughly seven years after being pushed out, returning as interim CEO in July 2015 before being confirmed permanently that autumn. Dorsey then led Twitter and Square simultaneously until he stepped down from Twitter in 2021; the board appointed then-CTO Parag Agrawal as his successor. Less than a year later, Elon Musk completed his $44 billion acquisition of Twitter and fired Agrawal. Matt Mullenweg David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images Matt Mullenweg 's brief ouster as CEO of Automattic, the company behind WordPress.com, lasted 33 hours and 20 minutes. In September 2026, Automattic's board voted to put Mullenweg on a paid leave of absence, naming CFO Mark Davies as interim CEO. Mullenweg said board members conspired against him in messages to employees. The board's attempt to sideline the founder quickly unraveled after Mullenweg challenged the move and leveraged his controlling voting stake to regain authority. Mullenweg told employees he was back in control, and the company confirmed his reinstatement on September 12. Mullenweg had said at TechCrunch Disrupt in 2024 that he controlled 84% of the company's voting power.
