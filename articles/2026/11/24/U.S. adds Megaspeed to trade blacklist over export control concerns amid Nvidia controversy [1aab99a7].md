# U.S. adds Megaspeed to trade blacklist over export control concerns amid Nvidia controversy

CNBC · Jenny Lee · 2026-11-24

The U.S. has added Singapore-based Megaspeed and six other Southeast Asian companies to its trade blacklist, citing national security and foreign policy concerns.
The Department of Commerce in a Federal Register filing cited a "significant risk" of companies being or becoming involved in diverting advanced computing products to unauthorized end users or end uses.
The blacklisting comes as Jensen Huang returns to the helm of Nvidia following his departure amid revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, who is under investigation by U.S. authorities.
Megaspeed International is a Singapore-based cloud computing firm that operates data centers across Southeast Asia.
The restrictions apply to all items subject to U.S. Export Administration Regulations, with license applications to be reviewed under a "presumption of denial."
The blacklist also includes PT Megaspeed International and MSFusion Sdn. Bhd. in Malaysia, as well as Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. The filing identified 7Road International as an alias used by both Megaspeed's Singapore and Thailand entities.
The Commerce Department initiated an investigation into Megaspeed in 2024 for potential export control violations after identifying suspicious activity through its monitoring of potential semiconductor diversion, according to a join statement issued with the Justice Department.
In May 2025, Commerce Department investigators requested records from Nvidia regarding direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed. Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list the following month, according to the statement.
U.S. authorities, working with Singaporean law enforcement officials, also conducted an on-site end-use check at Megaspeed in June 2025 and interviewed its then-CEO Alice Huang. The Justice Department subsequently took the lead in the criminal investigation into the company.
Commerce also added an Alibaba subsidiary in Singapore, Apex Enterprise Solutions, to a trade blacklist, Reuters reported.
Alibaba said it would pursue all legal avenues to protect its rights and reputation, adding that Apex had never diverted advanced computing chips subject to U.S. export restrictions, according to Reuters.
Meanwhile, Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said at a news conference in Canberra, Australia, on Tuesday that investigations into Megaspeed were ongoing and that discussions with American authorities were continuing.
"We do not want Singapore to be used and abused as a conduit by companies to circumvent America's export controls," Wong said.
