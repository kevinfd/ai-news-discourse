# I gave Gemini access to my Wallet, and it immediately started saving me money

ZDNet · Artie Beaty · 2026-11-24

ZDNET’s key takeaways
- Personal Intelligence can now analyze your Wallet transactions.
- Once you connect Wallet, you can use Search for spending advice.
- The feature can’t move or spend any money.
Google has added Wallet integration to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode, letting you learn about your spending habits, plan a budget, maximize credit card rewards, and more with a simple Search.
Starting today, Google announced, Personal Intelligence for Search is expanding to more apps, including Wallet and Drive. Google can’t move or manage any money, but it can give you tailored, personalized search results about your finances, like showing where you spend the most, identifying recurring subscriptions you might not be aware of, and suggesting which credit card might be best to use for travel. All you have to do is ask in a search.

More from ZDNET
This feature comes just over a week after Google introduced Personal Intelligence to Wallet.
How to use Personal Intelligence to analyze your spending
To turn on the feature, go to https://gemini.google.com. Once you’re there, click on the Settings gear icon, and then “Personal Intelligence.” Choose “Connected Apps,” and you’ll see all available apps. When you choose Wallet, you might need to log in to your bank account to access it fully.
Once your Wallet is connected, it takes a few minutes to fully sync. Once it’s finished, you can ask questions through AI mode in Search.
Personal Intelligence tells me what I don’t want to hear
I decided to give it a try for myself, starting off with a simple query: “Based on my spending, what’s a quick way I can save money?” I could have told you one of the primary culprits, but Google happily responded with what I already knew: far too much of my money goes to fast food and coffee.
“You frequently grab quick-service meals,” the response said, pointing out several fast food places that I do indeed frequent, “and specialty coffees throughout the week.” If I just cut that down to two meals and two coffees a week, Gemini explained, I’d save about $30 a week, or $1,500 a year.

When I asked it to find recurring subscriptions, Gemini noted that I had several monthly charges. What I didn’t ask for, but it did, was to break down those subscriptions into an easy-to-see chart, complete with the name, price, category, the card it’s being charged to, and the timeframe it usually comes out. Gemini even offered a few free alternatives to services I was paying for.
Since Gemini also knows what cards are linked to your Wallet, it’s able to offer advice there, too. I’m planning a trip to New York City, so I asked which cards might be best to use for the hotels and airfare. Gemini was able to analyze my card benefits, considering things like rewards and built-in travel insurance, and make a recommendation.

While Gemini Personal Intelligence can access your Gmail to learn more about your spending, it can’t access your bank account. The only information it knows about your finances and spending comes from receipts in your inbox or from your Wallet transaction history. I do use Wallet often (mostly when ordering through online apps), but it’s not quite my go-to option when I’m in a store. Given how useful this feature can be, though, I might just start using Wallet more to give it more information to work with.
