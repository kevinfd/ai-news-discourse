# Uber's CEO says this is one of the biggest career mistakes you can make

Business Insider · Ana Altchek, Ana Altchek (aaltchek@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-24

Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said overplanning is one of the most common mistakes young people make. Riccardo Savi/Getty Images; Ana Altchek; Alyssa Powell/BI Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said overplanning is one of the most common mistakes young people make. When considering a career change, you should ask yourself three key questions. In the AI era, it's worth detaching from your career plan and considering an option you overlooked. Get better at: Making a career change Today's move In the AI era, change is the new normal, and no role or path is completely certain. That doesn't mean it's easy to change the trajectory you saw for yourself. Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said in a 2023 "Acquired" podcast episode that young people shouldn't overplan because they should stay open for opportunities that come up. That may be even more relevant now as entry-level jobs transform. Why it works In the podcast, Khosrowshahi said "the most common mistake" he sees among young people is overplanning their careers. "There's this human bias which is to look for a signal that agrees with the plan that you have and ignore everything else that doesn't agree with it," he said in the podcast. Khosrowshahi's started off his career in the early 1990s at the investment bank Allen & Company. However, an opportunity to work with Barry Diller arose, and he took it. That eventually led him to become CEO at Expedia and later Uber. He said being open to different options allowed him to take that leap and change paths when the opportunity presented itself. Try it today Netflix's first HR head Patty McCord, and former Google executive Lexi Reese have shared similar advice on making career changes. They recommend asking yourself three questions. First, do you love the type of work you do? Second, does it use your strengths? Third, is there a real need for the work you can contribute? Before you dismiss a job opportunity or alternative path, consider it in the context of those three questions. Similarly, if you're eager to quit your job or change companies, it may be worth posing those questions. The bottom line The World Economic Forum has estimated that by 2030, 170 million new jobs will be created and 92 million will be displaced. That means many employees will have to shift the career plans they originally saw for themselves. While you shouldn't impulsively leave your current career behind today, it's worth thinking about other options you previously overlooked or considering new opportunities you haven't explored yet.
