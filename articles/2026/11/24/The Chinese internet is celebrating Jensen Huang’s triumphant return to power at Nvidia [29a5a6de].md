# The Chinese internet is celebrating Jensen Huang’s triumphant return to power at Nvidia

Business Insider · Aditi Bharade,Huileng Tan, Aditi Bharade,Huileng Tan (abharade@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-24

Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia's CEO, and the Chinese internet has thoughts. Soeren Stache/picture alliance via Getty Images Jensen Huang is back at Nvidia, and the Chinese internet wasted no time in welcoming him back. From commenting on his makeover to memeing about "unpaid leave," the celebration was interlaced with humor. Huang was reinstated as Nvidia's CEO on Monday, less than two weeks after his ouster. It's a Jensen Huang party on the Chinese internet, and people are celebrating — and memeing — his return to Nvidia . Nvidia's board said Monday morning that Huang would return as the company's CEO, less than two weeks after he resigned. He made a jubilant speech at the Nvidia HQ on Monday, at which he apologized to his employees and promised to be a better CEO after his string of scandals, while surrounded by employees cheering his name. The same enthusiasm carried over to Weibo — a major Chinese social media platform similar to X — where people celebrated his return with teasing. As of press time, Huang-related topics accounted for three of Weibo's top five trending technology topics. "Jensen Huang makes a triumphant return after two weeks of unpaid leave," one Weibo user posting from the southern province of Hunan wrote. Another user from Jiangsu province in east China, calling out the fact that Huang's resignation period coincided with "GTA 6's" release, wrote, "Mystery solved: Jensen Huang quit his job to go home and play GTA 6. Now that he's had his fun, he's back at work." Others were quick to comment on his outfit. On his first day back at work, Huang had ditched his signature leather jacket, instead opting for a navy cotton one. Some Weibo users compared it to the jackets commonly worn by Chinese government officials, a style sometimes known as the "cadre look." But other users adopted a more serious, analytical tone. One user, posting from China's eastern Zhejiang province, wrote that Huang's return would benefit the AI industry but could lead to tighter restrictions on China's access to AI technology. Another user, posting from Shanxi province in northern China, wrote that Nvidia "cannot function without Huang," speculating that the company's manufacturing partners had pressured its board to bring him back because they feared a replacement CEO could disrupt their operations. It's not just the Chinese internet; Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia's CEO was a cause of celebration for many. Nvidia's stock rose more than 30% on Monday. Neocloud stocks rallied alongside it, with CoreWeave up 22% and Nebius up 20%. Asia's AI trade got a boost, too. Huang's allies in tech and politics rejoiced at the announcement. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, who last week offered Huang a job at the company after his ouster, wrote a triumphant "It is done" in an X post. President Donald Trump, who explicitly called for Huang's reinstatement in a Truth Social post on Sunday, wrote after the announcement that Nvidia's board had made the "correct and proper decision."
