# Jensen Huang cheered by Nvidia employees after return, pledges to be a better CEO

CNBC · Katie Tarasov, Spriha Srivastava, Seema Mody · 2026-11-24

The mood inside Nvidia was jubilant as CEO Jensen Huang walked in on Monday to chants of his first name and three floors of cheering employees, according to a video shared with CNBC.
"Thank you all for the second chances," Huang said to the crowd. "I'm going to be a better CEO the second time around."
Nvidia announced on Monday that Huang had been reinstated as CEO, effective immediately.
It was a dramatic turn in the chaos that's enveloped the chip giant and the entire tech trade since reports surfaced that Huang had an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. The reports sparked renewed investigations into the smuggling of restricted Nvidia chips into China.
Huang admitted to an affair in a note to employees on Nov. 8 and resigned as CEO on Nov. 12. The New York Times and Reuters subsequently reported that Huang had relationships with two employees, one of whom was a direct report.
On Monday, cupcakes were circulating at the office with "Jensen is back" written on them, according to a person inside the company who asked not to be named because they were not authorized to speak publicly.
There was a sense of excitement among employees, as well as frustration over how the events of the last two weeks unfolded, the person said, adding that last Wednesday's all-hands meeting was a turning point.
A side-by-side photo was circulated internally of an all-hands meeting hosted by Huang, contrasted with the most recent meeting with a group of executives and board members sitting on stage, according to the person.
Now, all eyes are on the board, which said just last week that Huang would not be coming back.
"It's embarrassing for the board," said a former Nvidia employee who was at the company for more than 15 years, who asked not to be named in order to share insight into the company.
"It's likely that the board will change and some people may step down, some people may be replaced," the former employee said. They added that many employees "felt that the board acted too hastily."
President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he played a large role in Huang's return, and that he spoke to the board "directly and indirectly."
Trump called into the board meeting that was held on Sunday, CNBC confirmed.
