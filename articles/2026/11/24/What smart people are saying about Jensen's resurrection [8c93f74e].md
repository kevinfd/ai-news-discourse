# What smart people are saying about Jensen's resurrection

Business Insider · Henry Chandonnet,Thibault Spirlet,Katherine Li,Shubhangi Goel, Henry Chandonnet,Thibault Spirlet,Katherine Li,Shubhangi Goel (hchandonnet@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-24

Sam Altman and Elon Musk weighed in on Jensen Huang's return as CEO of Nvidia. Manuel Orbegozo/Reuters; Thomas Koehler/Photothek via Getty Images; Annabelle Gordon/CNP/Bloomberg via Getty Images/Reuters The business world is buzzing about Jensen Huang's return as CEO. President Donald Trump called him a "Great Patriot and National Treasure," commending the board for the right choice. Huang's allies, like Brad Gerstner and Bill Ackman, celebrated the move. Christmas came early for the Jensen Huang defenders. Huang's allies are sounding off online after the Nvidia founder was reinstated as CEO — while his critics are throwing up their hands. Here's what smart people are saying about his return, from business leaders to political titans. Elon Musk, SpaceX and Tesla CEO "The mistake has been corrected," Elon Musk wrote. Annabelle Gordon/CNP/Bloomberg via Getty Images/Reuters "It is done," Musk wrote on his social media platform, X. The Tesla CEO supported Huang proudly and publicly during the scandal. Soon after his resignation from Nvidia, Musk said that he'd hire Huang . He also signed a petition calling for Huang's reinstatement and compared losing him as ousting Michael Jordan . "The mistake has been corrected," Musk responded to Nvidia's announcement. Michael Burry, "Big Short" investor Michael Burry said that Huang's reinstatement showed Nvidia was weak and craven. Jim Spellman/WireImage Burry is a longtime Nvidia bear , having proclaimed that he wouldn't buy the stock at any price, anytime soon. He wasn't thrilled by Huang's reinstatement. It proved that Nvidia was "weaker, more craven than even I thought," Burry wrote. He urged the Justice Department and the SEC to keep digging. "Godspeed," he wrote. Brad Gerstner, CEO of Altimeter Capital Brad Gerstner wrote one word on X: "Unanimous." Kimberly White/Getty Images for Semafor Gerstner led much of the public movement to reinstate Huang. Earlier this month, he co-wrote a letter with Gavin Baker, managing partner of Atreides Management, asking the board to bring him back. On Monday, Gerstner posted an image of the CNBC update on X. "Unanimous," he wrote with an American flag emoji. Elizabeth Warren, US senator Senator Elizabeth Warren called Huang's reinstatement "reckless." Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images Nvidia and Huang will still face scrutiny from Congress. Sen. Elizabeth Warren pointed that out — and called the decision to reinstate Huang "reckless." "Huang's return only increases the urgency to pass bipartisan export control bills and make sure NVIDIA does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk," Warren wrote on X. She also called out the Trump administration's involvement in the reinstatement, writing that it would need to explain what "input" it gave. Travis Kalanick, cofounder of Uber Travis Kalanick wrote that he'd have to save his wafer-encrusted megaphone for another day. Justin Sullivan/Getty Images For Kalanick, Huang's reinstatement aligned perfectly with the week's holiday. "Thanksgiving," he wrote on X. Kalanick also resigned from the company he founded after a reputational crisis, though Uber never reinstated him. He went on to build CloudKitchens, and now the robotics startup Atoms , instead. Days before Huang's reinstatement, Kalanick said that he would show up to Nvidia's headquarters with a "wafer-encrusted megaphone" to encourage a walkout. On Monday, he posted an image showing the megaphone and signed "Jensen Huang." "Will have to save this for another day," he wrote. Hakeem Jeffries, US House minority leader Hakeem Jeffries said the decision to reinstate Huang was "irresponsible." SAUL LOEB / AFP via Getty Images Jeffries called the decision from Nvidia's board "indefensible, irresponsible, and incomprehensible." Congress will investigate both Nvidia, Howard Lutnick , and the Department of Commerce, he said. He also called for "ironclad export control legislation" to prevent China's exploitation of American AI technology. Herb Greenberg, writer of Herb on the Street In a Substack post, Greenberg reminded his audience of the reports that Huang slept with a subordinate: Kimberly Powell , Nvidia's VP of healthcare. "Any other CEO would've been fired and never hired back," Greenberg wrote. "It makes a total farce of corporate governance, assuming there really is anything left of corporate governance." Huang's reinstatement was about Wall Street and the power of the Silicon Valley elite, he said. He expects it to backfire. Greenberg also included one particularly provocative claim from a friend: "Too bad Epstein isn't alive - he could make a comeback." Jason Calacanis, investor and "All-In" co-host Jason Calacanis called Huang ''GPU Jesus." Bloomberg/Getty Images Calacanis' reaction to Huang's reinstatement wasn't just celebratory. It was biblical. On X, he posted an edited photo of Huang looking like Jesus Christ. "Christós anésti!" he wrote. "GPU Jesus has risen!" Zohran Mamdani, mayor of New York City Zohran Mamdani said that the pensions should not be impacted by Huang's fidelity to his marriage. Michael M. Santiago/Getty Images When asked about Huang's reinstatement at a press conference Monday morning, Mamdani gave out a long sigh and a chuckle. "I mean…" he started. "I do not think that working people across the country signed up for their pensions being tied to the question of Jensen Huang's fidelity to his marriage," Mamdani said. "And yet, for many days, that had a massive impact." Mamdani expanded his argument to the entire AI sector and its power over the market. "It cannot help but give you a sense of concern about just how much this technology is now dominant," he said. Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI Sam Altman kept his response to Huang's rehiring to one word: "Jensen!" Manuel Orbegozo/Reuters Altman knows a thing or two about board ousters. His happens to be the subject of an upcoming movie . The OpenAI CEO previously declined to publicly throw his weight behind Huang, though he called the Nvidia leader a "hero" that he had "strong empathy" for. On Monday, he kept his response to one word: "Jensen!" Newt Gingrich, former Speaker of the House Newt Gingrich said that Huang's reinstatement was a "significant failure" of federal bureaucracy. Aaron Schwartz/Bloomberg via Getty Images For Newt Gingrich, something is "profoundly wrong" with Huang's reinstatement. On X, Gingrich listed off some of the national security concerns that have flanked Nvidia's exports and Huang's reported relationship with Alice Huang. "Congress should really dig into this as a national security issue of enormous proportions and also a significant failure in the federal bureaucracy," Gingrich wrote. Bill Ackman, founder of Pershing Square Bill Ackman wrote on X that "sanity prevails." Kimberly White/Getty Images for Semafor Ackman was another champion for Huang's return. Minutes after the announcement, he quoted an earlier post that criticized Nvidia's board for "not acting in the best interest of shareholders." "Better governance prevails," Ackman wrote. Then he amended his thought: "Actually, sanity prevails." Lloyd Blankfein, former CEO of Goldman Sachs For Lloyd Blankfein, Huang's return was a test of who can "come back from the graveyard." Jose Sarmento Matos/Bloomberg via Getty Images How important was Huang to Nvidia's business? Very, according to Blankfein. "Guess you can come back from the graveyard if you're really, really, really indispensable," he wrote on X. "The NVDA board will not have that opportunity." Analyst Dan Ives, tech bull The Nvidia board had "no choice" but to bring Huang back, Dan Ives said. Evan Agostini/AP Dan Ives predicted the Nvidia stock decline that came with Jensen Huang's exit. Following his reinstatement, Ives wrote that "the pressure for Board to bring Jensen back was too much." Despite Huang's scandal, Ives wrote that Nvidia's board still knew it had "no choice" but to bring him back. "For the US technology sector and global economy this was the move it had to make," he wrote. Ron Conway, investor and 'Godfather of Silicon Valley' Ron Conway said that he was "glad to see" Huang back as CEO. Steve Jennings/Getty Images for TechCrunch Last week, Conway slammed the Nvidia board and questioned whether it had "lost perspective." This week, he celebrated it. "I commend the NVIDIA board for making a decision in the best interests of the NVIDIA team members, its shareholders, and the American economy," he wrote on X. Conway said that he was "glad to see" Huang back in the CEO perch. Gary Marcus, writer of Marcus on AI Gary Marcus called the decision to reinstate Huang "short-sighted." Shauna Clinton/Web Summit via Sportsfile via Getty Images Marcus said on CNN that Huang's reinstatement was a "very short-sighted move." The Democrats planned to investigate, which would complicate the company's future, he said. Nvidia's stock was still down from its pre-scandal high, Marcus said, and reporters were continuing to dig. There will be "aftershocks," he said. "Things are never going to be the same," Marcus said. "And I think that that's what people are fantasizing about." Benjamin Netanyahu, prime minister of Israel Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said that continuity of Nvidia's leadership was good for Israel. Kobi Wolf/Bloomberg via Getty Images Netanyahu focused his reaction on his home country. Nvidia's work with the Israeli tech sector has created "enormous value" for both the company and the country, he wrote on X. "Continuity in Nvidia's leadership is good news for that partnership and for the global AI industry," Netanyahu wrote . Shaun Maguire, Sequoia partner Shaun Maguire wrote that markets were "ripping" on the news of Huang's reinstatement. Brendan Smialowski/AFP via Getty Images Maguire was a fan of Jensen Huang's. In a post earlier this month, he called Huang technical, aggressive, and credible. He also floated Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora as a possible contender for the Nvidia CEO role. On Monday morning, Maguire pointed out the stock's bounce after Huang's reinstatement. "Markets are ripping on the news!" he wrote. Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, senior associate dean at the Yale School of Management Jeffrey Sonnenfeld called the reinstatement a "tremendous degradation of national security." R. Diamond/Getty Images Sonnenfeld slammed both the Nvidia board and government officials in an interview with CNBC. He called the reinstatement a "tremendous degradation of national security." The Yale professor acknowledged the short-term stock burst, but asked longer-term questions. "How could he possibly do the job?" Sonnenfeld asked, given the government investigations. And what about the board? After the stock crash, any shareholders who exited their positions had a "very strong case" that the entire board should resign, Sonnenfeld said. President Donald Trump Donald Trump called Jensen Huang a "Great Patriot and National Treasure." Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images Soon after Huang's reinstatement, President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social that the Nvidia board made the "correct and proper decision." "The Country needs Jensen's leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy," Trump wrote. "The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen's return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security." Trump added that he had gotten to know Huang on a personal level, and called him a "Great Patriot and National Treasure." Nvidia should continue its investigation into its chip exports, Trump wrote. Gina Raimondo, former US Commerce secretary Gina Raimondo called Huang a "genius," but said that "some things matter more than stock market gains." David Paul Morris/Bloomberg via Getty Images Raimondo said that she was surprised by the board's decision, given the ongoing investigations. "There is no doubt he is a genius — by the way, I had a fine working relationship with him — but some things matter more than stock market gains and profits, including accountability, rule of law, and America's national security," she said on CNBC. When asked whether she was nervous that the market, the economy, and our national security rested on Huang alone, Raimondo said, "Of course." "The concentration of wealth, the concentration of power, the concentration of the stock market has increased in the last 10 years, in politics, in business, in government, it's bad, and it's not going to end well," she said. Scott Stevenson, CEO of Spellbook Scott Stevenson said that Huang is "almost impossible to replace." Alex Broadway/Web Summit via Sportsfile via Getty Images Stevenson said Nvidia's decision reflects the "extraordinary" stakes surrounding the AI race, even if Huang's conduct should not simply be excused. "I think if you take a consequentialist view, at this point in time, it may be the right decision," Stevenson told Business Insider, adding that Huang is "almost impossible to replace" because of his technical and company knowledge. Still, he said the reinstatement would leave "a stain" on Huang's reputation. "There's no escaping that," he added. But Nvidia is not just any company. "There's so much at stake," Stevenson said. "This is not just a new industry." Pete Ricketts, US senator Sen. Pete Ricketts said that Huang's behavior does not support reinstatement. Dylan Widger/Bloomberg via Getty Images Sen. Ricketts said that he looked forward to questioning Huang in December. His reinstatement "doesn't absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips," Ricketts wrote on X. "I've served on a public company board," the Nebraska senator wrote. "His behavior does not support reinstatement." Jacob Bourne, Emarketer analyst Bourne called Huang's reinstatement "stunning" but not surprising. "Huang has become so central to Nvidia's identity and the broader AI trade that his departure fueled uncertainty during an already volatile period for the industry," Bourne said. He compared it to Altman's ouster and reinstatement from OpenAI. The two cases raise "similar questions about corporate governance and the outsized influence of individual leaders over the future of AI," Bourne said. Katie Miller, ex-DOGE lead Katie Miller pointed out that Huang's Nvidia ouster was "more than an Altman." Francis Chung/Politico/Bloomberg via Getty Images Katie Miller, the former DOGE lead and wife of the president's chief of staff, compared Huang's return to other historically ousted leaders. Huang did "more than an Altman," Miller said, referring to Sam Altman's brief removal from the position of OpenAI CEO. He also did the "same as a Scaramucci," she said, referring to the 10-day White House communications director. Scott Galloway, writer and podcaster Scott Galloway said that calls for Huang's return will "age like oysters in the sun." Tom Cooper/Getty Images for Vox Media Galloway pointed to Huang's reinstatement as a setback for women's progress. "Women have fought hard to not have to put up with this," he said on his podcast. Galloway said that the "tech brothers" believe that the future of AI will take America back to the 1960s. "Their calls to bring back Jensen are going to age like oysters in the sun during the summer," Galloway said. "This is going to be rancid." Mohamed El-Erian, former CEO of PIMCO Mohamed El-Erian said it felt like we're living through "natural experiments." Bloomberg/Getty Images El-Erian said the Nvidia saga was a reminder that Huang is one of one. "This is no ordinary CEO," he said on CNBC, "in no ordinary company." He described a recent conversation with a friend. El-Erian said that we were living through a series of "natural experiments," then the friend said it was more like "simulations." El-Erian agreed: It did feel like simulations. "It is amazing what we are living through right now," he said. Michael McCaul, Texas congressman Michael McCaul said that Americans deserve "full transparency." Graeme Sloan/Bloomberg via Getty Images Rep. McCaul, a Republican, called for more transparency. If the Trump administration believes that Huang's affair and the sale of chips to Megaspeed no longer pose a threat, it needs to "brief Congress immediately," he wrote on X. "The American people deserve full transparency when it comes to safeguarding U.S. national security," McCaul wrote. Runar Bjorhovde, principal analyst at Omdia Bjorhovde said the main question raised by Huang's return was what prompted Nvidia's board to reverse course. "Through the eleven days since he left, the board's communication seems to have been very consistent that reinstating him wasn't an option, but something has swayed the opinion in the opposite direction," he wrote on LinkedIn. "I'm sure the board doesn't mind having the accountable executive at the centre amidst the AI bubble correction , potential export control breaches, and other affairs," Bjorhovde added. "Money and risk avoidance talks loudest in the end of the day." Jim Cramer, CNBC host Jim Cramer, the host of CNBC's "Mad Money," celebrated Jensen Huang's return. Eric Lee/Bloomberg via Getty Images Cramer celebrated Huang's reinstatement on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street." "I got to tell you, this is incredible," Cramer said. "This last week was about Jensen, and about the idea that the whole world that revolves around him is a setback — and we're not going to win the space race, and China wins, everything was riding on this man!" "Look, he's a great man!" Cramer added. Jack Gold, tech analyst Gold, founder and principal analyst at J. Gold Associates, called Huang's reinstatement "a big mistake" in a LinkedIn post and said it does not set a good precedent for the company. "There is still an ongoing investigation of whether or not Jensen Huang was compromised by a Chinese spy he had an affair with," Gold said on Monday. "Then there is the issue of his affairs with direct reports." "Both of these signal a chief exec that doesn't need to play by the rules and established norms," Gold added. "And the board reinstating him means that they no longer feel they have to support the best interests of the company by showing that rules matter for everyone." Lex Fridman, podcaster In a post on X on Monday, the podcaster praised Huang as a rare leader and said he was excited by his return. Fridman said Huang had messaged him the previous week about joining a cross-country motorcycle trip, but that Huang was now "busy again." "Humanity needs more Jensens in leadership positions, not less," Fridman wrote. "Jensen is one of the good ones. Protect the good ones." He also argued that Huang was essential to America's success through competition with China, rather than through military conflict: "Jensen is a peacemaker." Palmer Luckey, founder and CEO of Anduril Luckey welcomed back Huang with big praise. Adam Gray/Getty Images for The New York Times The defense tech CEO celebrated Huang's return with a glowing endorsement. "Return of the King," Luckey wrote in a brief X post. Brian Chesky, Airbnb cofounder and CEO Chesky said that founders are also chief product officers. Katie Flores/WWD via Getty Images Chesky used Huang's return to make the case for founder-led companies. "One reason founder CEOs are so hard to replace is that they're effectively also the chief product officer," Chesky wrote on X. He said founders understand how a product was built, can "hold the entire product in their head," and have the authority and vision to reinvent it. "When they leave, the CEO role gets filled, but the product role they played rarely does," Chesky added. Read the original article on Business Insider
