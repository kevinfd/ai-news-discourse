# Tidal to Ban Fully AI-Generated Music, Remove Millions of Existing Tracks

PCMag · Jon Martindale · 2026-11-24

High-definition music streaming service Tidal is taking further steps to curb AI music on its platform by removing all fully AI-generated content, starting on Dec. 9. It will also block any further uploads of AI-generated songs and tracks.
"Over the past 90 days, 43% of all tracks distributed to the platform have been detected as wholly AI-generated," Tidal says, with the greatest influx happening on Nov. 6, when 60% of songs submitted to the streamer were AI-generated.
"Today, approximately 27 million tracks in our catalog are tagged as such, representing 6.3% of all available music on Tidal," it says. "Yet Tidal subscribers are not listening to these tracks: 96% of them have received zero streams in the last three months."
Tidal has been gradually migrating away from accepting AI on its platform. First, it tagged them and demonetized them; then it made it possible for subscribers to disable playback of AI-generated music and stopped showing them in users' personalized recommendations.
However, "Something more drastic needs to be done," tweeted Robert Anderson, Tidal Music Lead. "On December 9, we’ll begin removing all wholly AI-generated tracks from our catalog, and you’ll see them completely gone by the beginning of the year. This will result in the deletion of 29 million songs, and the estimated rejection of 80-85,000 songs per day."
The wording here suggests that some AI use in creating music will still be allowed, but that tracks made without human intervention (beyond writing the initial prompt) will no longer be allowed. As PCMag reported recently, it can be a slippery slope.
Other streaming services are also battling AI music, but have yet to ban it outright. Deezer has developed a toggle that lets users filter out all AI-generated music, detected by in-house tools that spot artifacts left in audio files by AI generation.
