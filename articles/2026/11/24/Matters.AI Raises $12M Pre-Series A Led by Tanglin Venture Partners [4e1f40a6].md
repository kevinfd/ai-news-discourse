# Matters.AI Raises $12M Pre-Series A Led by Tanglin Venture Partners

Matters.AI has raised $12 million in a pre-Series A round led by Tanglin Venture Partners, co-founder and CEO Keshava Murthy announced on November 24, 2026, bringing the data-security startup’s total funding to $18.25 million.
IIFL Capital Services Limited, Piper Serica, Foster Ventures and Tetration Ventures joined the round, while early backers Kalaari Capital and Endiya Partners returned. Murthy also named Better Capital, Carya and Lockstep among the company’s day-zero investors, alongside its angel backers.
Why Tanglin Backed Matters.AI
Matters.AI grew out of a CISO’s question during an early discussion: “who was misusing his sensitive data, what they were doing with it, and why.” Murthy’s announcement described that as the question the executive would pay to answer and the reason the team started the company.
In a separate post, Tanglin’s Sankalp Gupta described security teams facing alert overload and false positives while genuine threats still require human investigation, with autonomous AI agents accessing sensitive enterprise data expected to add further complexity. Tanglin’s investment rationale centers on Matters.AI’s combination of sensitive-data understanding, human and AI behavior, and action context to investigate and remediate risks autonomously.
The company’s leadership page identifies Harsh Sahu as co-founder and CTO and Dhiraj Khare as chief business officer. Matters.AI lists offices in San Francisco and Bangalore and describes its product as an AI-native data-security platform covering cloud, SaaS, endpoint and on-premises systems.
How Matters.AI Tracks Enterprise Data
Matters.AI’s platform documentation says it combines agentless APIs with lightweight sensors for data discovery and classification across cloud, SaaS and endpoint environments. The platform builds lineage maps showing where data comes from, where it goes and who interacts with it, providing context for risk management and incident response.
For prevention, the company says it monitors data moving through email, cloud uploads, removable media and peer-to-peer channels. Responses can include blocking a transfer, isolating a device or alerting the relevant team when exfiltration is detected.
Its real-time AI interaction controls apply that approach to prompts and responses. Matters.AI says the system inspects live natural-language traffic and evaluates user identity, data classification and model type to allow safe actions while blocking risky requests. It also tracks how files move from cloud environments through AI tools to local endpoint devices.
Remediation With Human Approval
The remediation workflow is designed to identify over-privileged users, exposed cloud storage and unprotected sensitive data. Matters.AI says its agents investigate the context, prioritize fixes by business impact and actual exposure, identify the relevant owner and request approval through email, Slack or integrated workflows.
Once approval arrives, the platform can remove excessive permissions, restrict external sharing, rotate credentials or enforce security policies. It then rescans the environment to verify that the risk has been removed and tracks the state to prevent regression.
That makes human approval the key boundary in Matters.AI’s automation model. The company is aiming autonomous AI at the investigation and remediation work that Tanglin says still burdens security teams, while keeping the authority to make changes with a human owner rather than the software itself.
