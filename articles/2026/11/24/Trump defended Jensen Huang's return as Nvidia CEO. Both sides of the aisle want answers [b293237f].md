# Trump defended Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO. Both sides of the aisle want answers.

Business Insider · Lloyd Lee, Lloyd Lee (lloydlee@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-24

Democratic Rep. Hakeem Jeffries and GOP Sen. Tom Cotton are some of the Congress members calling for investigations in light of the Nvidia scandal. Getty Images President Donald Trump said he convinced Nvidia board members to reinstate Jensen Huang. Nvidia said senior Trump officials reassured the board around Huang's reinstatement. Lawmakers from both sides of the aisle still want to hear from Huang and Nvidia's general counsel. GOP and Democratic congressmembers want to move full steam ahead with interviews and a public testimony from Jensen Huang , despite the Trump administration's reassurance that the reinstated CEO is not a national security risk. "As Chairman of the @HouseForeignGOP, export controls on our most advanced AI chips has been a top priority. The reports of Jensen Huang's alleged affair with a Chinese intelligence asset and the smuggling of our most advanced technology to China is extremely concerning," GOP Rep. Brian Mast said on X. "The American people deserve answers," he wrote. Sen. Tom Cotton, R-Arkansas, chair of the Senate Intelligence Committee, said on X that the "controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed," the Singaporean-based company that is under investigation by the Justice Department for allegations that it smuggled Nvidia chips to China, is not "resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back," adding that Congress has much to investigate. Huang was scheduled to appear before members of the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence, House Foreign Affairs Committee, and House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party on December 1 for a closed-door, transcribed interview. Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, was scheduled to appear the next day. The Select Committee on the CCP said in a statement that the interviews remain scheduled. Huang is also scheduled to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3. Some Congressmembers questioned Huang's hasty return to the company before his scheduled appearances — and Trump's role in it . GOP Sen. John Curtis of Utah said in an X post that he had questions, such as "who in our government weighed in?" "What did they tell the board? And on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?" Curtis wrote. "Mr. Huang was right to step down when the affair came to light. Just 11 days after Nvidia announced his departure, the board brought him back while its own independent investigation was still underway. Last week, the board said it didn't intend to reinstate him. I'd like to know what changed." House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries called the board's decision "indefensible" and said Congress would not be deterred from conducting an investigation into Nvidia and the Commerce Department , which is responsible for enforcing export controls around advanced AI chips. Nvidia said in its announcement of Huang's return that it formed a committee of independent directors solely focused on "trade compliance and national security risks" and is searching for a chief compliance officer. The company also said the internal investigation into the foreign intelligence matter will continue. President Donald Trump said in a Truth Social post following Huang's return that the government has "no reason to believe that Jensen's return to Nvidia poses any risk to national security." Spokespeople for Nvidia and the White House did not respond to a request for comment. Read the original article on Business Insider
