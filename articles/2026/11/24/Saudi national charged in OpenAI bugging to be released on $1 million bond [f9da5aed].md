# Saudi national charged in OpenAI bugging to be released on $1 million bond

CNBC · Dan Mangan · 2026-11-24

Abdulrahman Alfozan, the Saudi national accused of bugging an OpenAI executive conference room, will be allowed to be released from jail on a $1 million bond, a San Francisco federal court judge ruled Tuesday.
Alfozan, 35, is expected to be released on Wednesday and be fitted with an ankle monitor, according to his attorney, David Rizk.
"We are simply grateful that the Court made the right decision to release Mr. Alfozan and now we look forward to defending the case," Rizk said in a text message after a hearing before U.S. District Court Magistrate Judge Alex Tse.
Tse's ruled in favor of a release motion by Rizk filed last week that said prosecutors had admitted they had "found no new evidence to support the theory that Mr. Alfozan was stealing trade secrets, or to support the speculation that he was acting as an unregistered foreign agent for Saudi Arabia."
The San Francisco U.S. Attorney's Office opposed the former OpenAI engineer's release, saying in its response that he remains a flight risk due to his "considerable resources and ties to Saudi Arabia."
Alfozan's release request "blithely dismisses the seriousness" of the charge he faces, and "appears to presume, without support, that the government's investigation is complete," prosecutors wrote.
Alfozan, who was arrested Oct. 9, is accused of concealing a recording device in the artificial intelligence company's conference room to secretly record discussions. The device was found in a USB port in a television in the room on Sept. 30, prosecutors have said.
Prosecutors said they have found hundreds of audio files on Alfozan's personal computer, "including
dozens of recordings of meetings or conversations that occurred at OpenAI throughout September 2026, many of which involve discussions of what may be trade secrets."
Jury selection for Alfozan's trial is scheduled to begin Jan. 20.
Alfozan is a venture partner in a Saudi Arabian venture capital firm that lists Prince Khalid Bin Bader Al Saud, a member of that nation's ruling royal family, as a fellow partner.
OpenAI did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Alfozan's planned release.
