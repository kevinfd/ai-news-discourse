# Apple tightens Mac security as AI agents seek your files

Fox News · Kurt Knutsson, CyberGuy Report · 2026-11-24

Ever download an app on your Mac and click through the permission requests without giving them much thought? You're certainly not alone. But what if one of those approvals gave an AI assistant access to your private messages, financial documents or even files you thought were protected?
That's the concern behind Apple's latest Mac security controls. As AI agents become more capable of performing tasks on your computer, they're also requesting access to more personal information. And once you grant certain permissions, an app could potentially see far more than you intended.
Now, Apple is introducing new protections in macOS 27.3 that give you greater control over what these apps can access. The changes arrived in macOS 27.3 beta 2 on Nov. 16, with the broader software release still to come. Here's what Apple is changing and why you may want to check your Mac's privacy settings now.

MALICIOUS BROWSER EXTENSIONS CAN HIJACK AI ASSISTANTS
Why AI agents are raising new Mac security concerns
AI assistants have become increasingly useful, especially when you want help getting through a long list of tasks. An AI agent can go further by interacting with apps and taking actions on your behalf. For example, you might ask an AI agent to organize your Downloads folder or find a receipt buried in your email. To complete those tasks, the software may request permission to access information stored on your Mac. That's where things can get uncomfortable. Some apps request a powerful macOS permission called Full Disk Access, which can give them access to nearly everything stored on your computer, including information from other apps.
Apple warns that an app with this permission can read or modify sensitive files. It may also be able to delete or export information without asking you again. And the potential exposure goes beyond documents. Another powerful permission, called System & App Protection Override, can allow an app to control your Mac and monitor activity, including keystrokes, when you grant unrestricted access. That's a lot of trust to place in software, especially when an AI agent can take actions without asking you to approve every step.
What Apple is changing in macOS 27.3
Apple's upcoming update introduces several changes designed to make powerful permissions easier to understand. It also gives Mac owners more opportunities to limit access before an app starts working.
Clearer warnings when apps request powerful permissions
Apple is separating its existing Device Control and Data Access permission system into two settings: System & App Protection Override and Accessibility. The change helps clarify when an app is requesting permission to bypass built-in protections. Apple is also introducing more detailed warnings about the information an app could access if you approve its request. Instead of clicking through a vague permission prompt, you'll have more information about what you're allowing.
More control over which files an AI agent can access
One of the biggest changes involves Full Disk Access. Starting with macOS 27.3, some apps requesting this permission will let you choose specific protected folders instead of granting access to everything. Other apps may continue using the existing approval process, depending on their purpose.
For example, an AI tool helping you organize work documents may only need access to one folder. You could grant that limited permission while keeping other protected information off-limits. Apple is also introducing a more consistent process for granting these permissions. You'll select the requesting app from a list rather than navigating different approval methods.
AI agents can be required to ask before taking control
Another important addition gives you more control over how AI agents interact with other apps. When configuring System & App Protection Override, you can choose which apps an AI tool may control. For each approved app, you can select Ask First or At Any Time. Choosing Ask First means the AI agent must request permission before controlling that app. That extra confirmation may slow down some automated tasks, but it also gives you an opportunity to catch unwanted actions.
Apple is adding a transparency indicator that appears when an app controls another app through this permission system. That could make it easier to recognize when software is acting on your behalf.
MAC MALWARE CAN HIDE COMMANDS IN YOUR ICLOUD CALENDAR
Why Apple is tightening AI agent security now
Apple's announcement follows growing scrutiny over how much information AI-powered software can access. In September, a journalist claimed that Meta's Muse AI app had read his private messages without his permission. Meta disputed that account, saying the Messages integration requires explicit permission. Separately, a security vulnerability in OpenAI's ChatGPT Mac app could have allowed attackers to access sensitive information. OpenAI patched the flaw before it was publicly reported.
These incidents highlight the security concerns surrounding increasingly capable AI software. Even legitimate applications can create privacy risks when they receive broad access to personal information. Apple's response focuses on making those permissions more visible and giving people greater control over what they approve. Importantly, the changes won't prevent you from installing AI agents or using apps that genuinely need broader permissions.
One important catch with Apple's new Mac security controls
There's something worth knowing before assuming the update will automatically protect everything on your Mac. Apple says permissions you've already granted will remain in effect after the update. The new approval experience primarily applies when apps request permissions going forward.
That means an AI app you installed months ago could still have access to information you previously approved. You'll need to review those permissions yourself if you want to change them. Apple is also allowing developers of certain backup and accessibility applications to request special permissions that preserve their existing functionality. This should help prevent legitimate tools from losing features they need to operate.
How to check which apps have access to your Mac
You can review your Mac's privacy permissions now, even before macOS 27.3 becomes widely available.
Steps may vary slightly depending on your version of macOS.
- Click the Apple menu in the upper-left corner of your screen.
- Select System Settings.
- Click Privacy & Security in the sidebar.
- Scroll down and select Full Disk Access.
- Review the apps listed and look for anything you no longer recognize or need.
- Return to Privacy & Security and select Files & Folders to review access to protected locations.
- Check Accessibility and Automation to see which apps have permission to control parts of your Mac or interact with other applications.
If you find an app with permissions it no longer needs, you can revoke its access. Depending on your macOS version, you may see a toggle or an option to remove the app from the permission list. For macOS 27.3: Select an app and click the Remove (−) button to revoke access. To replace broad access with a more limited permission, remove the app and add it again. When prompted, choose the specific protected folders or apps it needs.
Keep in mind that some legitimate applications, including backup software and security tools, require extensive access to function properly. Removing their permissions could prevent certain features from working.

Tips to protect your privacy when using AI agents on a Mac
You don't have to stop using AI assistants to protect your information. A few precautions can help you take advantage of these tools while reducing unnecessary exposure.
1. Give AI agents only the access they need
When an app requests access to your files, consider whether it needs permission to browse your entire Mac. If the new macOS controls offer a Choose Folder or Choose Apps option, use it to limit the information the software can reach. You can adjust those permissions later if the app needs additional access.
2. Make AI agents ask before controlling other apps
If you're using macOS 27.3, look for the Ask First setting when granting an AI agent permission to control another application. This gives you another opportunity to review what the software wants to do. It's especially useful when an AI tool interacts with apps containing sensitive personal information.
3. Download AI software from trusted sources
Be careful when installing new AI agents, particularly from unfamiliar websites. We've previously reported how AI-related scams target Mac owners through convincing fake download pages. Stick with official developer websites or the Mac App Store whenever possible. Keep your installed apps updated to receive security fixes.
4. Use strong antivirus software
Your Mac includes built-in malware protection, but reputable antivirus software can add another layer of security. It can help detect malicious downloads and other threats, including malware disguised as legitimate AI tools. However, antivirus software can't reliably prevent an app from accessing information you've already given it permission to use. That's why reviewing your Mac's privacy settings remains important. 5. Review the privacy policy before sharing sensitive information
Even when an app has limited access to your files, you should understand how it handles information you choose to share. Check whether the developer stores your data or sends it to cloud servers. Apple's permission controls can restrict access to protected information on your Mac, but they don't replace an app's privacy policy.
6. Revisit permissions as your needs change
An AI tool may have needed access to a particular folder when you first installed it. That doesn't mean it needs the same permission indefinitely. Periodically check Privacy & Security and remove access that no longer serves a purpose. This is particularly important for AI apps you rarely use.
7. Back up important files before letting AI agents make changes
If you're allowing an AI agent to organize or modify files, make sure you have a recent backup. An automated task could accidentally delete something important or change a document you wanted to keep. Apple's built-in Time Machine can automatically back up your Mac and help you recover files if something goes wrong. Kurt's key takeaways
I'm glad Apple is taking a closer look at how much access AI agents can have on our computers. These tools are becoming more capable, and I can see why people would want an assistant that handles repetitive work. But giving an app permission to see everything on your Mac seems like a steep price to pay for convenience. I particularly like the option to make an AI agent ask before controlling another app. That's a level of oversight I would want, especially when personal information is involved. My biggest concern is that previously granted permissions will remain active. Apple can introduce better warnings, but those warnings won't change the decisions you've already made. I'd encourage Mac owners to spend a few minutes reviewing their settings rather than waiting for the next update.
