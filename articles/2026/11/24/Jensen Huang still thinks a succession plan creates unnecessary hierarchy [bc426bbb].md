# Jensen Huang still thinks a succession plan creates unnecessary hierarchy

TechCrunch · Kirsten Korosec · 2026-11-24

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang kicked off his return to the chipmaker he co-founded by holding an all-hands meeting that covered remorse, therapy, and even the company’s code of conduct, according to accounts from multiple insiders who spoke to the media.
The topic of succession planning also came up — a particularly sticky issue seen as a key risk at Nvidia. Huang reportedly told staff that Nvidia doesn’t require succession planning because of its unique business model, CNBC reported, citing an unnamed source who was present. Surprisingly, given the events of the last two weeks, Huang argued that a succession plan would create unnecessary hierarchy.
Investors and analysts saw the issue differently earlier this month, when Huang resigned as CEO following his admission that he had extramarital affairs with at least two people, including one whom the U.S. government suspects of being a Chinese spy.
Nvidia is a flat organization, unlike other large corporations that have a swath of C-suite executives and multiple layers of management. Huang sits at the top and says he has about 60 direct reports. Nvidia has just five executive officers running the show, including Huang. The other four are CFO Colette Kress, who was interim CEO for 12 days; Ajay Puri, who heads worldwide field operations; Debora Shoquist, executive vice president of operations; and Timothy Teter, executive vice president and general counsel, according to the company’s regulatory filings.
Huang has previously defended the company’s structure in numerous interviews, including one in March with podcaster Lex Fridman.
“When you’re designing a company, you should first think about what is it that you want the company to produce,” Huang said in the interview. “You know, I see a lot of companies’ organization charts, and they all look the same. Hamburger [company] organization charts, software organization charts, and car company organization charts. They all look the same. And it doesn’t make any sense to me. You know, the goal of company is to be the machinery, the mechanism, the system that produces the output. And that output is the product that we like to create.”
Huang’s comments to staff, and his previous remarks on the topic of succession, may not be enough to assuage the board or shareholders over the long term, however.
