# Anthropic Staff Openly Talk About ‘Taking Over the World’, Claims Max Tegmark

Novara Media · mikhael · 2026-11-24

Anthropic Staff Openly Talk About ‘Taking Over the World’, Claims Top MIT Physicist
Apparently they discuss overthrowing the US government after a few drinks.
by Clare Hymer
24 November 2026
Employees at US artificial intelligence company Anthropic openly talk about “overthrowing the US government” and “taking over the world”, a world-renowned physicist has claimed.
In an interview with Novara Media, Max Tegmark, a professor at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, said: “When I talk to people who work for Anthropic, after they’ve had a couple of drinks, the conversation often gets into overthrowing the US government and taking over the world.”
“They tend to not use words like ‘coup’ and ‘overthrow the US government’. They instead use the code phrase ‘alignment without control’,” he said.
‘Alignment without control’ is the idea that AI should be kept in check by being instilled with certain values – likely those shared by Anthropic, Tegmark said – rather than being directly controlled by humans.
“We get the sort of future that Alan Turing predicted, where it’s going to be Anthropic’s machine-god running Earth, and then the rest of us just have to hope that somehow it’s gonna be nice.”
Turing, widely regarded as the founder of computer science, famously warned in 1951 that once machines started thinking, we should expect them to take control.
Tegmark said the comments from Anthropic staff should alarm anyone who cares about democracy. “These companies really don’t seem to like it [democracy]. They have lobbied really hard to even prevent any meaningful government oversight of them,” he said.
He added: “Here in the US, OpenAI and Anthropic are less regulated than a sandwich shop.”
Novara Media has approached Anthropic for comment.
Tegmark’s disclosure comes as protests against AI are ramping up across the world.
As Anthropic started trading on the stock market at a staggering $2tn valuation on Thursday, protesters gathered at both the Nasdaq exchange in New York and the firm’s San Francisco headquarters demanding an AI development slowdown.
On Saturday, over 1,000 people marched against “AI extinction” in London in what campaigners said was “the biggest AI safety march in history”.
