# OpenAI engineer who allegedly wrapped recording bug in toilet paper to be freed on $1M bond

New York Post · Marc Vartabedian · 2026-11-24

OpenAI engineer who allegedly wrapped recording bug in toilet paper to be freed on $1M bond
See more of our coverage in your search results.
The OpenAI engineer accused of hiding a recording device wrapped in toilet paper behind a television in the AI giant’s San Francisco office was ordered released on $1 million bail.
A judge in federal court in San Francisco made the ruling Tuesday after determining Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi national, did not pose a flight risk.
Alfozan, who earlier this month pleaded not guilty to recording oral communications, will be required to wear an ankle monitor while awaiting trial, US Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse ruled.

The techie allegedly recorded dozens of meetings – all while he maintained tight ties with the Saudi Arabian government, prosecutors said in filings a day earlier.
They said they’ve obtained hundreds of audio files from Alfozan’s personal laptop including photographed confidential documents and at least one recording of a meeting between OpenAI and representatives of a foreign administration.
The 35-year-old AI wiz made headlines last month when his Sam Altman-led employer canned him after finding a recording device hidden in a conference room and traced it back to him. Photos captured Alfozan peering behind a television where he had stashed the device with a bug-eyed stare – spurring viral internet memes.

Alfozan’s lawyers have argued that evidence doesn’t show he acted on behalf of Saudi intelligence services. The attorneys have called Alfozan’s actions the work of an “overly curious tinkerer without any more nefarious intentions.”
The engineer was set for release from a Santa Clara jail on Wednesday morning, and was expected to stay at home in Millbrae until his trial starts in January.
Prosecutors sought to outline Alfozan’s alleged links to the Saudi government after his lawyers filed to have him released on bail on Friday for $ 1 million. He had been denied bail because authorities deemed him a flight risk.
The Federal Bureau of Investigations arrested Alfozan only three days before his one-way flight to Saudi Arabia was set to take off, according to prior court papers.

Prosecutors said that Alfozan repeatedly prompted Codex, an OpenAI productivity tool, for details on company meetings with Humain – an AI company backed by the Saudi investment fund PIF – and the Saudi Ministry of Communication and Information Technology.
Alfozan scoured OpenAI systems for details about a visit by MCIT officials to OpenAI’s offices on Sept. 9, according to the filings. He subsequently tried to get details on the meeting, including any “related context internally” such as Slack messages.
Prosecutors allege that Alfozan was in touch with MCIT representatives, including Abdullah Alswaha, the government minister responsible for the department, between August and September. The minister traveled to San Francisco in September and attended a roundtable discussion hosted by Alfozan, the FT previously reported.

Alfozan messaged a Saudi royal in October about joining Echelon, an AI start-up founded by Raed partner Prince Khalid Bin Bader al-Saud, after OpenAI’s planned initial public offering next year, prosecutors allege.
The alleged corporate espionage highlights the cutthroat global race by governments to develop advanced AI.
OpenAI has been competing with other leading AI labs including Anthropic and Elon Musk’s xAI, along with numerous Chinese companies and the US government has said developing the most elite AI tools is a national security imperative.

Alfozan is due to stand trial in January, and he faces up to five years in prison if he’s convicted of unlawfully intercepting or attempting to intercept oral communication.
“Evidence developed to date strongly suggests the defendant recorded many meetings that he had no authority to, and did not, attend and that he did so without notice to — let alone authorization from — anyone at OpenAI,” prosecutors said.
In addition to Alfozan’s recordings – which came over the span of three weeks in September and captured more than 160 hours of recordings – prosecutors alleged he took photographs of a confidential term sheet between OpenAI and another corporate entity.
They said that OpenAI found Alfozan’s recording device during a routine sweep in September.

Saudi media had touted Alfozan – OpenAI’s only Saudi employee – as a tech visionary. He was a venture partner at Riyadh-based Raed Ventures, which has invested in Middle Eastern tech startups.
Alfozan said he helped advise Saudi Arabia government agencies on technology and gushed in a recent podcast interview about meeting Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, then the deputy crown prince, during the prince’s 2016 visit to Silicon Valley.

Alfozan said he had been looking for guidance on whether to return to his homeland. In response, the prince advised young Saudis in Silicon Valley to hone their skills at premier tech companies before bringing that expertise home, Alfozan said in an Arabic podcast interview in September.
Alfozan’s lawyers did not immediately respond to a request for comment from The Post.
