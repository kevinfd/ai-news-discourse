# Asian tech stocks recover from ‘collateral damage’ of Jensen Huang’s departure

CNBC · Lim Hui Jie · 2026-11-24

Asian technology stocks jumped on Tuesday as investors piled back into the sector, after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO.
Key Nvidia partners and suppliers in Asia saw their shares skyrocket. Taiwan's TSMC, a critical partner to the U.S. chip giant, surged 9.85%, with Hon Hai Precision Industry — known also as Foxconn — up by 9.95%. Nvidia partner Mediatek also spiked 9.91%. Taipei's stock exchange has a daily price limit of 10% for stocks.
"Jensen's return is particularly welcome for Asian technology stocks, many of which suffered collateral damage from Nvidia's leadership crisis," Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo, said in a note Tuesday morning.
The recovery will likely favor established companies with visible orders, strong balance sheets and pricing power, compared to those who rely on "years of uninterrupted AI spending," Chanana added.
In South Korea, Nvidia suppliers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix jumped 6.7% each. The two companies, which together make up over 40% of the Kospi's weighting, sent the index up over 6%. South Korea's LG Electronics soared 15.86%.
The rise in the index triggered the Korea Exchange's sidecar on the index early in the day, reportedly making it the 100th sidecar the KRX has activated. Japan's Softbank Group Corp, a major investor in the chip space, climbed over 8% before paring gains to 4.66%.
Semiconductor test company Advantest, as well as counterpart Tokyo Electron, jumped 12% and 7%, respectively. Both companies are collaborators with Nvidia.
The exuberance did not follow into European markets at the open, with the region's technology sector down 0.3% in early deals, weighing on a broader 0.14% rise in the benchmark Stoxx 600 index. Europe's tech index popped more than 5% on Monday.
Nvidia's stock had closed up 31% following Huang's reinstatement. It had previously shed nearly 33% from the time Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on Nov. 8.
