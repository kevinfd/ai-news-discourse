# YouTuber MKBHD on the Cybertruck's discontinuation: 'Doesn't bode well for the Roadster'

Business Insider · Ben Shimkus, Ben Shimkus (bshimkus@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-15

MKBHD also said his hair is probably safe after placing a bet related to the Cybercab. Chance Yeh/Getty Images for HubSpot MKBHD says the Cybertruck's wind-down could be bad news for the elusive Roadster. Tesla is clearing factory space for the steering-wheel-less, gold-painted Cybercab. Due to a bet, a 2026 Cybercab consumer launch could cost MKBHD his hair — but he's not sweating it. Tesla's decision to wind down Cybertruck production has Marques Brownlee worried about a different delayed vehicle. "This doesn't bode well for the Roadster," Brownlee, the YouTube reviewer popularly known as MKBHD, said on a Friday episode of the "WVFRM Podcast." On November 6, Tesla CEO Elon Musk announced on X that the EV maker would stop building the Cybertruck by mid-2027 to focus on production of the steering wheel-less Cybercab. Earlier this year, Tesla also retired its Model S and Model X, as it shifts more of its attention toward autonomous vehicles and humanoid robots. That makes the Roadster, Tesla's twice-revealed and long-delayed supercar, a questionable fit in the automaker's lineup, MKBHD said. It's the only vehicle in Tesla's lineup that the automaker isn't trying to make fully autonomous. People look at a prototype of the 2028 Tesla Roadster Thrust, an all-electric vehicle featuring SpaceX-developed thrusters. Caroline Brehman / AFP via Getty Images MKBHD has followed the Roadster saga particularly closely. He previously held two reservations for the high-flying vehicle, but he said in September 2025 that he had canceled one after waiting nearly a decade for delivery. And, for the Cybertruck, he said he thinks Tesla missed a golden opportunity. "If they just made like a good, simple SUV type-thing, like the EV9 ," he said. "That would have sold a lot. People would have wanted that everywhere." About MKBHD's hair… The YouTuber has a big bet with Tesla stans: If Tesla, as it once predicted, sells Cybercabs to US consumers by the end of 2026, he'll shave his head. Tesla debuted its gold-painted Cybercabs in Austin in September , but the vehicles were used exclusively in Tesla's own ride-hailing service — not sold to individual drivers. Now, Tesla's plan to continue making Cybertrucks until mid-2027 is making MKBHD even more confident in his bet. "Alright, I'm probably good," he said. "I did just get a haircut."
