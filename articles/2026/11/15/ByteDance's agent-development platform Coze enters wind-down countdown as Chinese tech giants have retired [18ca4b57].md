# ByteDance's agent-development platform "Coze" enters wind-down countdown as Chinese tech giants have retired more than 25 standalone AI products this year

BigGo Finance · kernelangus420 · 2026-11-15

ByteDance's Coze Enterprise Edition enters wind-down countdown as Chinese tech giants have retired more than 25 standalone AI products this year
Coze, ByteDance's AI application-development platform, has released a "Coze Enterprise Edition Shutdown Notice," announcing that its Enterprise Standard and Enterprise Flagship editions will stop accepting new customer purchases from Nov. 30, 2026, with existing packages fully terminated by Dec. 31, 2027 at the latest; unused portions will be automatically refunded. It follows Tencent Yuanqi as the second agent-development platform from a leading Chinese tech giant to begin an exit process.
In an Oct. 8 notice, the Tencent Yuanqi team made clear that the platform will officially shut down on Nov. 9 due to business adjustments, at which point all existing agents will become unavailable, and users are being directed to migrate to Tencent Cloud's ADP platform. The back-to-back demise of two similarly positioned agent-development platforms is seen in the industry as a landmark event marking the shift of the giants' AI product lines from casting a wide net to narrowing their focus.
Coze Enterprise Edition launched in the second half of 2025 and began winding down after just over a year of operation; Tencent Yuanqi launched in May 2024 and is shutting down after more than two years. Even shorter-lived was Tencent QClaw — a product for remotely controlling a computer from a phone — which launched only in March 2026, drew more than 100,000 people to its first-release reservation queue, and announced in September of the same year that it would cease operations in December, just nine months from debut to exit.
According to an incomplete tally by Tech Planet, 38 AI products have been shut down or merged across major Chinese tech companies including Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent and Baidu, as well as startups such as Moonshot AI and MiniMax — 25 of them in the second half of this year alone. The consolidation clustered between July and August: on July 20, Tencent moved QClaw-related operations and some of its team into the WorkBuddy division; on July 30, ByteDance merged the Feishu and Doubao product teams; on Aug. 3, Alibaba consolidated three products — QoderWork, MuleRun and Wukong — into Qwen Office; on Aug. 6, Baidu folded dodo into Baidu Dazi; on Aug. 24, ByteDance completed the full merger of the TRAE and Coze teams into Doubao, and the next day Doubao Work was released externally.
Tencent and ByteDance have made the most intensive adjustments. Tencent's Translator Jun, live for nearly a decade, was taken offline in March 2025, with its capabilities migrated to Yuanbao; the digital-human creation tool Zhiying completed its overhaul and wiped user data in June 2025; and Yuanqi is confirmed to shut down on Nov. 9. On ByteDance's side, Volcano Writing closed on Dec. 31, 2024, redirecting users to Doubao without migrating historical content; Xiaowukong, renamed from Wukong Search, stopped updating in October 2024 and was pulled from app stores the following year; the MarsCode coding assistant was renamed the TRAE plugin; the AI camera product Xinghui had its entire team folded into Doubao; and on July 15 this year, Doubao took its self-built agent feature offline, channeling users to Maoxiang.
After this round of consolidation, ByteDance's AI map has become relatively clear: the consumer side relies on the Doubao app, the enterprise office scenario is handled by Feishu and Doubao Work, and underlying computing power and enterprise delivery are undertaken by Volcano Engine. At Alibaba, after the design product Luban, launched in 2015, ceased service in June 2025, the company stripped Tingwu of its standalone app status and folded it into Qwen, and in mid-July this year took Qwen's anthropomorphic and user-built agent features offline. Baidu's Tizi AI, which focused on ad-free intelligent search, was shut down and merged into Ernie on March 4 this year — just half a year from launch to demise; Wenxin Yige and Wenxin Kuaima were integrated into Ernie Bot and Baidu Dazi, respectively.
Note: Timing is based on platform announcements and public reports.
The wave of standalone AI tools exiting is driven by three converging pressures. The first is the substitution effect of model capabilities. The functions of single-point tools like Translator Jun, Luban and Tingwu are being directly covered by general-purpose large-model assistants; workflows that once required manual configuration on low-code platforms can now be understood by models with agent capabilities, which can break down steps on their own and call search, coding and file-processing tools. Some users said on social media that workflow products were not eliminated by AI but by "models that can plan for themselves."
The second is tightening compliance boundaries. In April 2026, five Chinese government departments issued the Interim Measures for the Administration of Anthropomorphic AI Interaction Services, effective July 15, explicitly placing "emotional dependence" within the regulatory red lines. In mid-July, Doubao, Qwen and Yuanbao simultaneously took their self-built agent features offline, affecting more than 8 million characters. The agent plazas that companies had pushed hard for the previous two years closed their entrances within a short period — essentially because compliance costs and business returns could not be matched, with the AI-companion segment hit hardest.
The third is the difficulty of closing the business-model loop. Computing costs for such products are directly tied to user time spent — the more they are used, the more money is lost. Overseas, Character.AI has 12 million active users but still bears losses of nearly $10 million a month. Low willingness to pay and continuously rising computing expenses have become a common problem for standalone AI tools.
The limits of the market space also cannot be ignored. According to IDC's "2025 China Agent Development Platform Market Share Report," Coze took a 19.3% share of China's public-cloud agent-development platform market in 2025, ranking first; but the overall size of that sub-market in the same period was only 250 million yuan (approximately $37.5 million). A No. 1 share coexisting with a tiny track is the deeper reason these platforms struggle to stand on their own. At the same time, supply keeps expanding: at the end of 2024, 302 generative AI services had completed filing in China; by Aug. 31 this year, cumulative filings had reached 1,112, with another 731 applications or features registered — the scarcity of single-point capabilities is fading fast.
Startups are in a tighter spot. When a tech giant shuts a product, its technology, users and team can usually be reassigned within the group; when a startup cuts a product line, it often means the end of a commercial attempt. Moonshot AI's overseas trials of the character-chat product Ohai and the audio-video generation tool Noisee in the first half of 2024 stopped iterating in September that year, with all resources flowing back to Kimi; StepFun's role-play agent Maopaoya launched in April 2024 and surpassed 6 million downloads across platforms, but as the company shifted focus to foundation models, the team was folded into the main line and the product ceased operations on Nov. 5, 2025; 01.AI announced on Aug. 3 this year that it would shut down its public-facing large-model platform, stopped external API services on Sept. 3, and contracted fully to B2B business for enterprise customers. MiniMax upgraded its Hailuo AI brand uniformly to MiniMax, shifting focus to the video business.
Notably, in the same week that Coze Enterprise Edition began its exit, Tencent on the afternoon of Nov. 4 released the official version of its Hunyuan Hy4 model and bound it deeply to WorkBuddy, saying Hy4 can connect chat records, email, documents, schedules and meeting information through the WeCom suite. According to QuestMobile data, AI office monthly active users reached 142 million in September 2026, up 50.6% year on year, with WorkBuddy ranking first in that sub-track at 32.353 million MAU — more than three times the runner-up. This path diverges from the "general-purpose agent-development platform" position of Coze and Yuanqi: the former embeds model capabilities directly into existing high-frequency office scenarios, while the latter must acquire customers and monetize independently.
For investors, the signal from this round of retirements is fairly clear: the yardstick for judging AI products is shifting from showcasing model capabilities to occupying high-frequency scenarios, securing a stable paying base and delivering real business results. Merely carrying AI capabilities is no longer a plus; under the multiple constraints of market size, regulatory rules and computing costs, the shakeout among standalone AI tools is expected to continue.
