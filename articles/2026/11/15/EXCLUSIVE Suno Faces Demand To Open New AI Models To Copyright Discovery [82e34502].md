# EXCLUSIVE: Suno Faces Demand To Open New AI Models To Copyright Discovery

AllHipHop · Nolan Strong · 2026-11-15

Suno‘s bid to pause a major copyright lawsuit has run into demands for answers about its newest music-making technology. Universal and Sony labels want discovery into newer models to continue while ownership questions involving more than 60,000 recordings wait.
The labels outlined that compromise in a November 13 filing in Massachusetts federal court. They want records about model training, licensing negotiations, business expansion and finances rather than a freeze covering the entire case.
“Delay would not merely postpone building the record, it would degrade it,” the labels’ attorneys wrote. They argue that engineers could leave and technical knowledge could become harder to reconstruct as Suno keeps developing new products.
Their concern includes v6, which they say has not received full scrutiny in the earlier lawsuit. They also offer reciprocal information about licensing markets and the effect of AI-generated music on their recordings.
Suno takes a different view of what should happen before the second case advances. Its October 30 motion argues that decisions in the original lawsuit could resolve claims or narrow the evidence needed here.
That first case began in June 2024 with allegations involving 560 recordings. When the labels sought to add more than 60,000 works, the judge declined to expand it and directed them toward a parallel lawsuit.
The second complaint followed in September, with Suno answering it in October and asserting fair use among its defenses. Suno says it is preserving relevant material and argues that proceeding now would impose unnecessary expense on both sides. The company also contends that a favorable ruling could eliminate the need to examine ownership of those recordings.
The company’s discovery disputes also reach investors outside the Massachusetts case. NVentures has separately sought to block testimony demanded by the labels after producing more than 28,000 pages, arguing that Suno should answer questions about its own operations.
The labels’ latest proposal would carry evidence from the first case into the second without repeating the same work. If the judge orders a broader pause, they alternatively seek a preservation order covering training data and engineering records.
Motions seeking judgment before trial in the original lawsuit are due June 22, 2027.
