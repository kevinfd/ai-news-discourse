# Amendment to the Act on the Protection of Personal Information Enacted: What Companies Using Generative AI Should Confirm Before Enforcement

note · 業務ほどき課｜Unravel Dept · 2026-11-15

SYSTEM NOTICE
Amendment to the Act on the Protection of Personal Information Enacted: What Companies Using Generative AI Should Confirm Before Enforcement
The amendment to the Act on the Protection of Personal Information was enacted on July 10, 2026, and promulgated on July 17. Enforcement is scheduled for "in principle, within a period not exceeding two years from the date of promulgation." Detailed rules will be determined by cabinet orders and regulations hereafter.
In conclusion, what you should do before enforcement is to organize the personal information your company holds and its destination. If you are using generative AI for work, inputting data into AI is one of those destinations. However, the amendment materials do not mention inputting data into generative AI. Therefore, I will first confirm the warnings issued by the committee regarding generative AI, and then look at the points that will change with the amendment.
Inputting into generative AI is based on the 2023 warning
The only mention of "AI" in the amendment is a special provision that allows for the provision of personal data to third parties without the individual's consent, or the acquisition of publicly available sensitive personal information, when used solely for creating statistics. The materials state that AI development, which can be organized as statistical creation, is also included in this. This refers to data on the AI-creation side; there is no mention in the amendment materials regarding situations where employees input data into generative AI.
Regarding input, the same committee issued "Warnings Regarding the Use of Generative AI Services" in June 2023. There are two points of caution for business operators.
- When inputting instructions (prompts) that contain personal information, sufficiently confirm whether it is within the scope of the purpose of use for that personal information
- If personal data is input without the individual's consent and is handled for purposes other than generating a response, it may be a violation of the law. Therefore, sufficiently confirm that the service provider does not use that data for machine learning, etc.
For general users as well, the risks of input personal information being used for learning and then output, or the risk of inaccurate personal information being mixed into the output, are cited, and users are urged to check terms of service and privacy policies.
This warning is still posted on the committee's website as of November 13, 2026. If you are reviewing your contractors and procedures for leaks in line with the amendment, checking generative AI at the same time will save you effort.
- Check the terms of the services you are using. Is there a setting or contract to ensure input data is not used for learning?
- List the tasks where you are inputting personal information. Is that usage within the scope of the purpose of use determined by the company?
- Include AI in your internal communication flow. Who in the company should be notified if information that should not be input is accidentally input? (Whether this constitutes a report to the committee depends on the situation.)

Change 1 in the amendment: Agreements with contractors become more important than ever
I think many companies outsource payroll calculation and customer management. In the amendment, the obligation for contractors "not to handle personal data beyond the scope necessary for the entrusted business" has been clearly written.
On the other hand, if the contractor does not decide how to handle the data themselves, a mechanism is included to exempt the contractor's obligations in principle if the handling method is agreed upon in the outsourcing contract. Even so, the obligation not to handle beyond the necessary scope and the obligation for safety management remain. The items to be stipulated in the contract will be determined by regulations hereafter.
Change 2 in the Amendment: Procedures for Data Breaches Will Change
Notification to the individual when a breach occurs will be relaxed in cases where not notifying them poses little risk to protecting their rights. The materials cite the leakage of internal service user IDs as an example.
Reporting to the Commission will also allow for some preliminary reports to be omitted, provided that third-party verification is obtained. For cases involving only one individual, such as misdirected emails, the plan is to allow for periodic consolidated reporting.
However, notification to the individual and reporting to the Commission are separate matters. Even if notification requirements are relaxed, it does not mean that reporting will no longer be required. The conditions will be determined by regulations.
Change 3 in the Amendment: Administrative Surcharges Target Major Violations
A system for administrative surcharges will also be established. This applies to serious violations that meet certain conditions, such as when the number of individuals affected exceeds 1,000. It is not a mechanism that applies uniformly to all violations. Calculation methods and other details will be determined by cabinet orders and regulations.
Other Changes
New rules will also be established for information on individuals under 16 and facial feature data used for facial recognition (not just simple facial photographs). Companies handling such data should check the details in the materials.
Before Enforcement
Many parts of the amendment have not yet been finalized in regulations. By listing whose information you hold, for what purpose, and where it is being sent—including to contractors and generative AI services—you will know exactly what needs to be reviewed once the regulations are released.
Handling personal information is not just a task for the general affairs or legal departments. Customer information is held by sales, employee information by human resources, and generative AI is used across all departments. By keeping an eye on the progress of the amendment and announcements from the Commission, and by preparing in advance for risks across the entire company—including areas outside your own responsibility—you can avoid panic just before enforcement. That is the shortest path to success.
Please check with experts or the materials from the Personal Information Protection Commission to see what applies to your specific company.
