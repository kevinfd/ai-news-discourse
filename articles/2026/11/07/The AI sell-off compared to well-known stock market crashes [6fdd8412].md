The AI sell-off compared to well-known stock market crashes
The recent AI sell-off brought a lot of fearmongering headlines. Put next to past stock market crashes, it's only a mild correction for the S&P 500, 6% below its record. Tech fell harder, with the Nasdaq down 11% and chip stocks down 36%, as much as they fell during Covid (35%). We'll be tracking the sell-off here with daily data updates.

About the data
- The S&P 500 is the price index, without dividends, from daily closes on Yahoo Finance. Before March 1957 the series is its predecessor, the S&P 90.
- A fall runs from a record close to the next record, so the 2011 fall sits inside the 2007 to 2013 one. Its depth is the lowest close before the next record.
- "AI sell-off" is our name for the slide since the 6 October 2026 record.
- The S&P 500 is not the same index it was in 1929 or 1973: technology is now about a third of it, far more than then, so a tech-led sell-off moves it more than it would have.
- "Still below 2000 high" means the Nasdaq and chip stocks had not got back to their 2000 records by 2008, so 2008 is part of their dot-com fall, not a fall of its own.
