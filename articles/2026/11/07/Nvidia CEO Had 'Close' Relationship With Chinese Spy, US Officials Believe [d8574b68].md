# Nvidia CEO Had 'Close' Relationship With Chinese Spy, US Officials Believe

PCMag · Will McCurdy · 2026-11-07

Nvidia, currently the world’s most valuable company, produces many of the chips that power AI data centers, providing the technical groundwork that enables AI tools from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. And that has made its CEO a top target for spies.
The Chinese government reportedly recruited a female executive, Alice Huang, to spy on Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and gather intelligence on the company’s most advanced chips, which are currently banned from sale in China. She reportedly had a “close” relationship with Nvidia's CEO, according to US intelligence officials cited by The New York Times.
Alice Huang (no relation to the CEO) allegedly reported directly to the main Chinese intelligence agency, according to sources who spoke to the Times. Alice is a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center operator and Nvidia customer. She reportedly also had relationships with executives at manufacturing firm Gigabyte, which assembles some of Nvidia’s supercomputers, and could speak English well.
Jensen Huang is expected to attend a closed-door briefing with members of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the chairman of the Select Committee on China, and the House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence to address national security concerns regarding his relationship with the woman.
Nvidia told the Times that Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were "not unusual" and consistent with his meetings with other customers. He added that customers do not pressure Nvidia to nudge the US government to change its policies on chip sales to China. Liu Chang, a spokesman for the Chinese embassy, called the reporting “unsubstantiated."
According to the FBI, China is continuing to find creative ways to access US state secrets, such as duping job seekers on LinkedIn into writing intelligence reports on US/Chinese relations.
