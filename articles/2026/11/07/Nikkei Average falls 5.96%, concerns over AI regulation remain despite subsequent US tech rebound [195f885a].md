# Nikkei Average falls 5.96%, concerns over AI regulation remain despite subsequent US tech rebound

note · きょうの経済ニュース, Daily Economy News · 2026-11-07

Nikkei Average falls 5.96%, concerns over AI regulation remain despite subsequent US tech rebound
While selling of AI-related stocks spread in the Tokyo market, tech stocks subsequently rebounded in the US market.
Rather than grouping markets with different trading hours together, we will organize what concerns drove the stock prices.
The resilience of employment is a factor supporting the economy, but it also relates to monetary policy that prioritizes price control.
Combining concerns over crude oil supply and buying of Japanese government bonds, we will decipher market movements and their ripple effects on daily life.
The information covered was confirmed by the morning of November 7, Japan Standard Time.
Today's Key Points
- On the November 6 Tokyo market, the Nikkei Average closed at 57,434.77 yen, down 5.96% from the previous day.
Selling spread, centered on AI-related stocks.
- On November 6 in the US, the Nasdaq rose 1.1%, the S&P 500 rose 0.3%, and the Dow fell 0.5%.
The rebound in AI-related stocks and the recovery of the market as a whole should be viewed separately.
- US non-farm payrolls for October increased by 165,000 from the previous month.
The unemployment rate was 4.1%, indicating the resilience of employment.
- Brent crude oil futures on November 6 in the US settled at $98.32 per barrel.
Expectations for diplomatic progress and supply concerns intersected.
- The closing yield on Japan's newly issued 10-year government bond on November 6 was 2.635%.
This was a decline of 0.120 percentage points from the previous business day, as government bonds were bought amid the stock market decline.
- Moving forward, we will organize specific measures for AI regulation, corporate capital investment plans, and the relationship between prices and monetary policy.
There is a difference between changes in market prices and the ripple effects on households.
1. Tokyo stock decline spreads from AI-related to a wide range of stocks
The Nikkei Average ended trading on November 6 at 57,434.77 yen, down 5.96% from the previous day.
It is characterized not only by the selling of AI-related stocks but also by the fact that the decline extended to a wide range of stocks.
Reuters reports that the backdrop is concern that AI regulation will strengthen after the US midterm elections.
However, one should distinguish between caution regarding regulation and the fact that companies have actually changed their investment plans.
When considering the impact on Japanese semiconductor-related companies, one must compare specific policy measures with announcements of orders and capital investment.
Closing prices and percentage changes: Kabutan: https://s.minkabu.jp/news/4652838 
2. US tech stocks rebound, showing a difference in temperature from the overall market
On November 6 in the US, after the Tokyo market closed, the Nasdaq rose 1.1%.
While the S&P 500 also rose 0.3%, the Dow fell 0.5%.
The Associated Press reported that AI-related stocks recovered some of their previous losses.
As long as the major indices are moving in different directions, the rebound in tech stocks cannot be treated as having the same meaning as the resolution of anxiety in the market as a whole.
There is a time lag between the decline in Tokyo stocks and the rebound in US stocks, and how Japanese stocks react subsequently will not be determined by the US market on this day alone.
US Market: AP News: https://apnews.com/article/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq-806f71d95105bc7efbc760b194dd8cad 
3. Resilience in employment also relates to monetary policy
According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, non-farm payrolls increased by 165,000 in October from the previous month, and the unemployment rate was 4.1%.
While job growth is a factor that supports the economy, it does not automatically lead to monetary easing.
Reuters reported that expectations for interest rate hikes were supported following the employment data.
When interpreting the impact on the Japanese market, it is necessary to distinguish between the employment results and the subsequent reactions in interest rates and exchange rates.
Whether economic strength and stable prices can coexist is a critical intersection for policy decisions.
Employment Situation: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics: https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm 
4. Crude oil between expectations for diplomacy and supply concerns
Brent crude oil futures for November 6, U.S. time, settled at $98.32 per barrel.
Bloomberg reported that expectations for diplomatic negotiations with Iran were mixed with uncertainties on the supply side, such as attacks on vessels.
Reports that negotiations are progressing are not the same as the reality that transportation has stabilized.
For Japanese households, the ripple effect on fuel costs is a concern, but the price movement of dollar-denominated crude oil is not immediately reflected in domestic prices.
Including the time lag for exchange rates and price pass-through, we must view the stages from import to retail separately.
Crude Oil Market: Bloomberg/Rigzone: https://www.rigzone.com/news/wire/oil_posts_biggest_weekly_drop_since_2022-06-nov-2026-185035-article/ 
5. Japanese long-term interest rates fall behind the stock market decline
In parallel with the decline in the stock market, buying entered the Japanese government bond market.
According to Kyodo News, the closing yield on the newly issued 10-year government bond on November 6 was 2.635%, down 0.120 percentage points from the previous business day.
It is reported that investors strengthened their risk-averse stance following concerns over AI-related regulations.
Since bond yields fall when bonds are bought and prices rise, it is not a contradiction for stock prices and interest rates to fall simultaneously.
However, one cannot conclude that mortgage or deposit interest rates will change immediately based solely on these changes in market interest rates.
Bond Market: Kyodo News: https://news.livedoor.com/article/detail/32749984/ 
6. Future points of focus are specific regulatory measures and prices
From these movements, it is clear that caution over AI regulation, resilience in employment, and uncertainty in crude oil supply are each affecting the market through different channels.
In the AI sector, the focus is on specific policy measures and corporate investment plans, while in monetary policy, the combination of employment and prices is the focus.
For crude oil, we look not only at headlines about negotiations but also at the realities of transportation and supply.
These are not conclusions for predicting market direction, but perspectives for interpreting future announcements.
Regarding the impact on daily life, we also distinguish between market prices and actual payment amounts.
