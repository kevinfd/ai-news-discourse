# Tokyo Stock Exchange: Nikkei Average continues to fall; concerns over AI regulation in US midterm elections lead to sell-off in semiconductor stocks

note · 日々見 · 2026-11-07

Tokyo Stock Exchange: Nikkei Average continues to fall; concerns over AI regulation in US midterm elections lead to sell-off in semiconductor stocks
In the Tokyo stock market, the Nikkei Stock Average continued to fall, with the morning closing price on November 4th at 60,835.36 yen, down 2,246.50 yen from the previous business day. It fell below 61,000 yen for the first time in about three months, spreading a sense of caution among investors.
Concerns over US AI regulation hit the market
The backdrop for this decline lies in the situation surrounding the US midterm elections. Following reports that the Democratic Party, which is considered to be in favor of strengthening regulations on artificial intelligence (AI), is gaining momentum, the possibility of tighter regulations on AI development and data center construction in the US has become a concern.
In response, sell orders spread in the Japanese market, centered on semiconductor-related stocks. The growing uncertainty over whether investment in AI-related companies will continue to expand has become a factor pushing down stock prices.
What is the impact on the Japanese economy?
Japanese semiconductor-related companies are deeply linked to US AI investment and data center demand. Therefore, changes in US policy or revisions to investment plans could affect the performance of Japanese companies and the stock market.
On the other hand, demand for AI technology itself has not disappeared, and this decline cannot be judged as the end of long-term growth.
This stock price decline shows that while expectations for the AI market are rising, changes in policy and regulation can have a major impact on financial markets. Going forward, US policy trends and the investment plans of AI-related companies are likely to be key points that will determine the direction of Japanese stocks.
