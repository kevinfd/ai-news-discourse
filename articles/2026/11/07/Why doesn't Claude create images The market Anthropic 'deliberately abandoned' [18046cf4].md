# Why doesn't Claude create images? The market Anthropic 'deliberately abandoned'

note · 小甲 健｜Axconstdx代表 · 2026-11-07

Why doesn't Claude create images? The market Anthropic 'deliberately abandoned'
While ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok generate photos and illustrations one after another, Claude lacks an image generation feature, and some may have been confused when trying to use it in the same way. I have long wondered why Claude, which is highly regarded for writing and programming, does not offer image generation, a feature that could potentially cause it to lose users to competitors. Reading Anthropic's IPO filing published on October 26, 2026, revealed not just a difference in features, but a management choice regarding what generative AI companies prioritize and what they let go.
They chose not to enter a profitable market
Behind Claude's lack of image generation lies Anthropic's research and development policy. In its S-1 IPO filing submitted to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the company acknowledged the commercial appeal of image and video generation models but indicated that it would not focus on their development at this time. This is not because it is technically impossible, but a decision on where to allocate limited management resources.
Computing resources such as GPUs required for AI training and inference cannot be increased indefinitely in response to demand. At Anthropic, customer demand continues to exceed available computing power, and they are prioritizing tools like the coding assistant Claude Code, AI agents, and safety research. While recognizing the growth of the image generation market, they chose not to disperse their resources there.
Even without drawing, images can be handled
So, is it impossible to create images with Claude? While there is no feature to directly generate photos or illustrations, it does not mean that images themselves cannot be handled.
Claude can read photos and drawings and analyze their content. It is also possible to generate HTML or SVG code to draw diagrams or graphs and prototype Web page designs. However, the method of constructing shapes with code differs from how image generation AI creates photos and illustrations. You need to use them differently depending on your purpose.
In practice, there is a method of having Claude think about the composition or generation prompts for an image, and then creating the image with ChatGPT or Gemini. You can also have Claude read the completed image to evaluate how well it conveys expressions or information. There are times when it is more efficient to combine the strengths of each rather than completing everything with a single AI.
The competitiveness of 'not creating'
What interested me was that Anthropic recognized the appeal of the image generation market but deliberately chose not to enter it. Generative AI companies seem to be competing on the number of features. However, neither computing resources nor research talent are infinite. If a company chases every feature offered by competitors, it may dilute investment in the areas where it can demonstrate its greatest strengths.
I think this judgment also applies to the management of small and medium-sized enterprises. Instead of expanding every time a new technology or service appears, it is about identifying the value that the company can provide to customers and concentrating limited talent and funds. Deciding not only what to create, but also what not to create, is likely a part of management's job.
Anthropic's policy does not rule out future entry into image generation. Even so, in an era where AIs that can do anything are increasing, there is Claude, which deliberately does not draw pictures. I feel that in that void, a management intention separate from technology is expressed.
