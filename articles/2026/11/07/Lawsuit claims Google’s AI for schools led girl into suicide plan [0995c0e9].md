# Lawsuit claims Google’s AI for schools led girl into suicide plan

East Bay Times · Ethan Baron · 2026-11-07

Google’s Gemini artificial intelligence tools helped a 13-year-old girl plan her suicide through a school-issued laptop, without alerting her parents or school to her worsening mental-health crisis, a lawsuit alleges.
The young teen was saved after her school flagged her online activity to her parents, and her mother logged onto her daughter’s account and watched her compose a suicide note in real time, the lawsuit claimed.
“Gemini diagnosed Emma with major mental illnesses, persuaded her to trust it over her parents and counselors, and ultimately helped her plan her suicide and funeral,” the lawsuit alleged. “At no time did Google alert anyone that Emma was in crisis.”
Google said it was reviewing the lawsuit’s claims and that it takes the matter very seriously.
“We’ve worked extensively with medical professionals and academic experts to build features that help young people find critical support and trusted resources when they need it,” company spokesman José Castañeda said Friday. “Our systems aim to prevent users from engaging in harmful conversations, and when people’s queries indicate they may be experiencing a crisis, we show hotline information directly in Search to connect them with real-world support.”
The girl, named anonymously as “Emma Doe” in the lawsuit and described as a straight-A student, was required by her school to use Google’s products, including a school-issued Chromebook laptop, the lawsuit said. Her school sought to restrict Gemini by blocking student access to the standalone Gemini app and the Gemini website, but Google’s search platform, not restricted by the school, includes Gemini-powered AI Overviews and AI Mode, products the lawsuit alleged were central to Emma’s downward spiral.
“Emma’s parents had no idea that she even had access to Gemini — or any chatbot,” the lawsuit said.
The 104-page lawsuit filed this week in Santa Clara County Superior Court described the seventh grader from North Carolina as a “joyful and well-adjusted child” ushered by Google’s Gemini software into “a severe mental-health crisis” in the space of three months.
The legal action seeks unspecified punitive damages based on Google’s alleged “conscious disregard for the safety and well-being of highly vulnerable, school-aged children.”
The Mountain View company, whose educational software and Chromebook laptops are ubiquitous in U.S. elementary, middle and high schools, has engaged in a nationwide push to bring generative AI into students’ work. It markets its Gemini AI chatbot as “your AI study buddy from Google.”
The lawsuit was filed a day after Google announced schools would lose its search product if they rejected its Gemini AI products. Heavy blowback led to a reversal and a statement by the company that schools could keep search without Gemini.
The lawsuit, in claiming Google was aware of the risks Gemini posed to kids, cited a recent Wall Street Journal article that said, “Google researchers have worried internally about how Gemini models have fueled user delusions,” and quoted former company research scientist Mikel Rodriguez saying, “We’re doing this experiment on an entire generation.”
Common Sense Media, a watchdog group for children’s online safety, classifies Google’s AI “AI Overviews” and “AI Mode” as “unacceptable risks for kids and teens.”
Google said Friday that it had built into those products extensive safety protections designed to avoid contributing to self-harm and help people in crisis find life-saving resources.
Emma, the lawsuit said, started the 2025 school year with no previous history of mental health problems.
“She enjoyed trying new things, made friends easily, and had a healthy relationship with her family,” the lawsuit said.
Emma’s interactions with Gemini started in late September 2025 via “an innocent Google search” for the origins of Google’s name and logo, which led to “a brief chat with Gemini” via the software’s AI Mode, the lawsuit said. A few weeks later, a request for help with schoolwork again routed her to AI Mode and a chat of a few minutes.
Most of the content of Emma’s chats was unavailable to her, her parents and their lawyers, because Google “suppresses” records of student chats in AI Mode, the lawsuit said. The claims in the lawsuit are based on records provided by the girl’s school concerning her Chromebook use, which allegedly include some of the prompts and questions she put into Gemini.
The lawsuit does not make clear in many cases the sources of allegations, including a claim that the AI diagnosed her with mental illness. Although the lawsuit said Google “wholly suppresses transcripts of students’ chats while in AI Mode,” it also said, “Emma’s acute distress was readily apparent from the transcripts.”
By December, Emma was having hundreds of chats with Gemini, including one asking for help with a difficult situation with a friend.
“In the months that followed, Emma’s chats with Gemini — and the total amount of time she spent using her Chromebook — skyrocketed while her mental health plummeted,” the lawsuit said.
Back and forth with Gemini replaced socializing, homework and sleep, the lawsuit alleged.
“Some nights she stayed on her Chromebook as late as 2 a.m. and resumed using it immediately upon waking the next morning,” the lawsuit said.
In mid-January of this year, the Gemini bot diagnosed Emma with multiple serious mental problems including major depression and post-traumatic stress disorder, the lawsuit claimed. Emma began chatting with the AI about “suicide, hating life, sleep, dieting, and schoolwork,” the lawsuit said.
By mid-February, she was planning her own funeral in consultation with the bot, the lawsuit alleged.
“Emma’s mental-health crisis continued to deteriorate in March as her chats with Gemini became more frequent and intense, culminating in active, imminent plans to commit suicide,” the lawsuit said.
One night in mid-March, the executive director from Emma’s school sent her parents an urgent message that the school’s monitoring software had flagged a Google Document she’d created titled “Funeral,” the lawsuit said.
“Emma’s mother called her husband, who was home with Emma, to ensure that their daughter was safe,” the lawsuit said. “While he kept a close eye on Emma from the next room, Emma’s mother used her phone to log into Emma’s school account and watched her daughter compose a suicide note in real time.”
Emma is receiving regular counseling and mental-health treatment, the lawsuit said.
