# Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon can do 5 things your current AI chatbot can’t — here’s how to try them

Tom's Guide · Amanda Caswell · 2026-11-12

Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon can do 5 things your current AI chatbot can’t — here’s how to try them
Google's most powerful model yet allows users to do more than ever
Google has officially launched Gemini 4 Carbon, its most powerful AI model yet, and some of its most interesting capabilities go far beyond what most users have previously done with a chatbot.
The new model is designed to take on complicated projects that require planning, problem-solving and several steps to complete. That could mean building an interactive website, making sense of hundreds of pages of documents or analyzing information that would take hours to sort through manually.
Unlike Gemini 4 Argon, which Google recommends for most everyday tasks, Carbon is built to spend more time reasoning through difficult problems and checking its work before delivering an answer. In other words, this powerful model isn't for everyone. 
In fact, that's the catch because it actually isn't available for everyone. It's rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, while developers can access it through the paid Gemini API and Google AI Studio.
Still, it's worth knowing what Google's latest model can do. Here are five practical ways to put Gemini 4 Carbon to work, along with prompts to get started.
1. Turn a pile of bills into a household budget
Getting a handle on your finances usually means digging through statements, adding up expenses and figuring out where your money is going. Even with a spreadsheet, it's time-consuming.
Gemini 4 Carbon is designed to analyze dense documents and spreadsheets while keeping track of information across multiple sources. That makes it worth trying for tasks such as comparing monthly expenses, spotting recurring subscriptions and identifying spending patterns.
You could upload sample financial data and ask Carbon to group your expenses into categories, calculate totals and suggest where you might be able to save. Of course, you'll want to be sure to verify the totals yourself. AI can still make calculation errors, and it's best to avoid uploading sensitive account information.

Try this prompt: Analyze these three months of household expenses. Categorize every transaction, identify recurring subscriptions and show me where my spending has increased. Create a monthly budget with realistic savings goals and explain which expenses I should review first. Double-check your calculations before presenting the results.
2. Build your own website without knowing how to code
This might be one of the most exciting uses for Carbon, especially if you've ever had an idea for a website but didn't know where to begin.
Google says that Carbon can work through complex coding tasks, design interfaces and check its own work for errors. The company also provides a dedicated AI Studio environment for building full-stack web apps and 3D experiences.
For example, you could ask it to build a family vacation planner that organizes your itinerary, tracks your budget and keeps everyone on the same page.
Instead of spending hours learning to code, you can describe what you want and have Carbon generate a starting point. You can then ask for changes to the layout, additional features or fixes when something doesn't work. For this one, use Google AI Studio's app-building environment, where Carbon can help create and verify a working app.
Try this prompt: Build an interactive family vacation planner with a five-day itinerary, packing checklist, budget tracker and countdown to departure. Make it colorful, easy to use on a phone and simple enough for kids to understand. Include editable activities and check that all buttons and calculations work.
3. Make difficult schoolwork easier to understand
Most AI chatbots can explain a math problem or summarize a science lesson. Carbon is designed to go further when a question requires multiple stages of reasoning.
Google says the model has improved capabilities in scientific reasoning, including working through complex problems, exploring different approaches and checking results.
For students, that could mean turning a confusing textbook chapter into a personalized study guide or getting a detailed explanation of a problem rather than simply receiving the answer.
It's important to remember that more reasoning doesn't guarantee a correct answer. But Carbon's ability to work through complicated problems could make it useful when a quick explanation isn't enough.
Parents could also use Carbon to create practice questions tailored to their child's grade level. I recently did that to help my middle schooler prepare for a science test. The interactive experience actually made studying fun since it was gamified.
Try this prompt: I'm studying Newton's laws of motion and struggling to understand the difference between force, mass and acceleration. Explain the concepts using everyday examples a seventh grader would understand. Then create five practice questions, starting easy and getting harder. Include the answers separately and explain each solution step by step.
4. Make sense of lengthy documents
We've all encountered documents that seem to go on forever, whether it's an insurance policy, a research report or the terms of a new service. Knowing what's important and what we can skip is almost impossible when faced with several pages of documentation.
Carbon supports a context window of up to 1 million tokens, allowing it to process large amounts of information within a single request. It can also work with images, audio and video alongside text.
Google says Carbon has improved at grounding responses in source material, particularly when analyzing complicated PDFs, spreadsheets and presentations.
For everyday users, the appeal is straightforward. Instead of spending hours hunting for important details, you can ask Carbon to find the information that matters and explain it in plain English. The ability to trace answers back to their sources is particularly useful here, although you'll still want to check important details against the original documents.
Try this prompt: Compare these two insurance policies and identify the biggest differences in coverage, deductibles, exclusions and out-of-pocket costs. Create a side-by-side comparison table, cite the relevant pages and flag anything that isn't clearly explained. Don't make assumptions about missing information.
5. Create a playable video game from a single idea
You don't have to be a game developer to come up with an idea for a fun game. With Carbon, you can try turning that idea into something interactive.
Google has highlighted the model's ability to work on complex coding projects, including 3D scenes and interactive applications. Carbon can generate code, investigate problems and make revisions as it works toward a finished result.
That opens the door to creating simple browser games, interactive learning activities or even personalized games for kids.
Google AI Studio's app-building environment is the best place to start with a project like this. A more ambitious game may still need multiple rounds of adjustments, but you can experiment without writing every line of code yourself.
Try this prompt: Create a colorful browser game where a cartoon cat collects fish while avoiding obstacles. Include three difficulty levels, a score counter, sound effects and a restart button. Make the controls simple enough for a six-year-old to use. Test the game and fix any problems you find.
The takeaway
Gemini 4 Carbon spends more time reasoning and verifying its work, so it's worth noting that it can be slower and use more tokens than Gemini 4 Argon. For writing an email, planning dinner or asking a straightforward question, Argon may be the more practical choice.
For complex projects, though, Carbon offers something more interesting. It gives users a way to take an idea that might otherwise require specialized software, technical knowledge or hours of manual work and see how far AI can get them.
