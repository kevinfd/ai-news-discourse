# Jensen Huang's implosion at Nvidia leaves trail of collateral damage

Axios · Nathan Bomey · 2026-11-12

Jensen Huang's self-destruction has left a trail of collateral damage that threatens to ensnare the board of Nvidia and the AI boom itself . Why it matters: Huang was a singular leader in the global economy, capable of exciting or calming markets with a single sentence and viewed as a virtually indestructible force behind Nvidia's rise. The big picture: His sudden exit after reports of multiple affairs — one with a suspected Chinese spy and the other with a direct report — has wiped out more than $600 billion in value from Nvidia's market cap. "His visionary leadership is going to be missed — it's not going to be replaced," Joel Shulman, CEO of ERShares, which has owned Nvidia shares since 2005, tells Axios. Zoom in: With the uncertainty over Huang's tenure at Nvidia now resolved, the attention will quickly pivot to a slew of unanswered questions, including: What will the investigations uncover? Just because he's gone doesn't mean this is over. If the FBI probe or the independent investigation ordered by the board show that Huang gave favorable treatment to Megaspeed — the company led by the suspected spy Alice Huang — he and Nvidia could face serious consequences. Huang is also expected to testify behind closed doors next week before a Congressional committee. Critics say the Nvidia board wasn't fulfilling its duty to provide proper oversight of Huang — particularly if it had any reason to be aware of his reported affairs before they become public. Peter Jaworski, business ethics professor at Georgetown University, said the situation raises questions about what the board knew and when they knew it. Nvidia reps haven't responded to Axios requests for comment on the board's handling of the matter. Who takes the permanent role as CEO? CFO Colette Kress was named to the interim role, but the board is conducting a global search that will consider outside and internal candidates for a permanant successor. She "has built enormous credibility internally and externally and we are big fans of her strategically," tech bull Dan Ives wrote Thursday in a research note. Prediction markets point to Nvidia hyperscaling tech exec Ian Buck as the the leading candidate, but the political ramifications of Nvidia's position in the global economy may demand someone with more experience with geopolitics. President Trump suggested former Apple CEO Tim Cook, whom Ives also floated, but Kalshi traders rate that as a 1 in 25 shot. Threat level: Could this be the catalyst that pops the AI bubble? Nvidia is the backbone of the global AI economy, with its chips, investments and financial guarantees sustaining an entire ecosystem of companies worth trillions of dollars — and Huang is at the center of that vast flywheel, Axios' Zachary Basu writes . Next week becomes a "critical" moment with Tuesday's Nvidia earnings report and Thursday's likely Anthropic IPO, Shulman says. "This Nvidia story is going to take a little air out of the balloon for the Anthropic IPO," he says. The other side: Bret Greenstein, chief AI officer at consultancy West Monroe, argued that Huang's departure won't change the demand for chips: "The company was never the source of AI demand. It has been supplying the capacity to meet that demand," which remains "enormous," he wrote today. Follow the money: Will Huang's exit fundamentally reshape the long-term competitive landscape of the chip industry? Competitors like AMD, Alphabet and Intel could benefit if chips customers are soured on working with Nvidia. "When a star leaves a Wall Street firm, the first question is always the same: did the clients sign with the banker — or with the bank? Nvidia is about to answer that question on the biggest stage in the market," writes Mark Malek, CIO at Siebert Financial. What's the floor for Nvidia's stock? Nvidia shares were down another 5.7% just before 3pm Thursday and are now down 27% over the last month, having lost about $1 trillion in value. "With this kind of leadership uncertainty, it's very difficult to predict where shares go over the next several trading sessions," writes Ken Mahoney, CEO of Mahoney Asset Management. "Sometimes huge news catalysts like this take all technicals out of the equation." The intrigue: Will someone take a risk on hiring Huang? Elon Musk has already hinted he could hire the former Nvidia chief as he moves toward a possible merger of SpaceX and Tesla . But it'll be hard for anyone to hire Huang unless and until the FBI probe in particular is resolved. The bottom line: The fallout is just beginning.
