# How the effects of Jensen Huang’s ouster are spiraling out through the economy

Fast Company · Rebecca Heilweil · 2026-11-12

Nvidia and Jensen Huang aren’t the only ones in the crosshairs.
The longtime tech executive has had a bad week. It all began when news surfaced that Huang had been having an extramarital affair with an alleged spy for the Chinese government. In response, the corporate board of Nvidia—the company Huang cofounded more than 30 years ago—began an investigation. The FBI launched a probe, too. Then news broke Wednesday that Huang had engaged in at least one other affair, this time with a company executive who reported directly to him.
By Thursday morning, Huang had resigned, and the chip company had appointed an interim CEO. But the hits kept coming. Thursday afternoon Reuters reported that Huang had engaged in an affair with a second employee.
Obviously, this is bad news for Nvidia. Stocks are down, and Democrats, now preparing to take back Congress, appear to be ramping up plans to investigate the company and any possible risks to U.S. national security. But this is also bad for a range of other entities that have a stake in Nvidia’s success.
Here are a few that stand to feel some of the blowback.
The White House
The White House, fresh off of devastating losses in the midterm elections, stands to face questions about why it held off on taking action after it learned about Huang’s affair with an alleged spy. The Trump administration already had a poor reputation for security and intelligence practices, but seemingly sleeping on this knowledge is all but certain to raise the prospect of further probes.
Trump officials, notably, have cleared deals allowing Nvidia to sell advanced computer chips to China, and even leveraged U.S. influence over the company’s chips in geopolitical negotiations.
