# Who is Jensen Huang and how the timeline of spy scandal unfolded

The Hindu · The Hindu Bureau · 2026-11-12

Nvidia and its chief executive, Jensen Huang, are facing a proposed class-action lawsuit that accuses them of misleading shareholders by failing to disclose Mr. Huang’s alleged relationship with Alice Huang, who has been linked to Chinese intelligence.
The complaint, filed in a U.S. federal court in San Jose, California, on November 9, 2026, alleges that Nvidia and Mr. Huang concealed the relationship and the national security concerns surrounding it. Shareholders contend that the alleged omission exposed the company to reputational and business risks.
Who is Jensen Huang?
Sixty-three-year-old Taiwan-born Mr. Huang has led U.S. chipmaker Nvidia for more than three decades, transforming it into what is arguably the world’s most influential technology company. He is now at the centre of a controversy stemming from his alleged relationship with Ms. Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based firm Megaspeed and an alleged Chinese spy.
Mr. Huang was born on February 17, 1963, in Taiwan, the second son of a chemical engineer and a schoolteacher. In 1968, his family moved to Thailand before relocating to the United States amid the Vietnam War, according to Graphics News. He graduated in electrical engineering from Oregon State University and later obtained a master’s degree from Stanford University, according to his Linkedin profile.
Jensen Huang timeline (according to Graphics News)
- February 17, 1963: Jensen Huang is born in Taiwan, the second son of a chemical engineer and a schoolteacher.
- 1968: His family moves to Thailand before relocating to the United States during the Vietnam War.
- 1970s: He spends part of his childhood at a boarding school in Kentucky before moving to Oregon, where he develops an interest in computing.
- 1984: Graduates with a degree in electrical engineering from Oregon State University.
- 1984-1992: Completes a master’s degree at Stanford University.
- 1993: Co-founds Nvidia with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. He becomes chief executive officer as the company enters the computer gaming market with an initial capital of $40,000.
- 1999: Nvidia goes public. The launch of the GeForce 256, promoted as the world’s first GPU, establishes the company as a major force in the technology industry.
- 2006: Under Mr. Huang’s leadership, Nvidia introduces CUDA technology, enabling graphics processors to handle complex computing tasks beyond gaming and helping lay the groundwork for the AI revolution.
- 2022: The launch of ChatGPT triggers a sharp rise in demand for advanced AI processors.
- 2024: The boom in generative AI drives Nvidia’s rapid expansion, with Mr. Huang’s net worth surpassing $100 billion.
Spy scandal timeline
- 2024-2025: Jensen Huang and Alice Huang are reportedly seen together at several industry events in Taipei.
- 2026: U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio sends a memo warning Trump administration officials about the Nvidia chief executive’s relationship with Alice Huang.
- October 29, 2026: The New York Times reports that U.S. authorities investigated Alice Huang over suspicions that she passed intelligence to Beijing and helped circumvent U.S. restrictions on advanced AI chips.
- November 5, 2026: Senior U.S. lawmakers seek a closed-door briefing from Jensen Huang over potential national security concerns.
- November 8, 2026: Mr. Huang admits he was “unfaithful” to his wife. Nvidia announces an independent investigation into the espionage allegations.
- November 9, 2026: Nvidia shares fall by more than 4%. U.S. President Donald Trump says the FBI is investigating the controversy involving Mr. Huang and Alice Huang.
The Jensen-Alice saga
Mr. Huang admitted to being unfaithful to his wife but did not disclose details about the alleged relationship with Ms. Alice Huang. The Nvidia chief executive said he was “deeply sorry” for the affair. “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did,” he said in a statement posted on Nvidia’s website.
The New York Times reported that Ms. Alice Huang is a Chinese citizen allegedly recruited by Beijing to gather intelligence on Mr. Huang.
Mr. Huang also sent a message to Nvidia employees on November 8, 2026, acknowledging the affair and apologising to both his family and company staff. While expressing regret for his actions, he did not address allegations that Alice Huang was a suspected spy.
The Times reported last month that U.S. intelligence agencies had concluded that Mr. Huang had developed a close relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese citizen and chip buyer, and alleged that she was providing information directly to Beijing.
Nvidia announced a probe into the matter on November 8, 2026. Independent directors have engaged external legal counsel to examine the circumstances surrounding the relationship. Nvidia’s board said it had “no reason to believe” that either national security or Nvidia’s proprietary information had been compromised, and that Mr. Huang would remain in charge while cooperating with the investigation.
Who is Alice Huang?
Ms. Alice Huang is a Chinese citizen who was allegedly recruited by Beijing to collect intelligence on the Nvidia chief executive, according to The New York Times, which first reported that Mr. Huang was the target of an alleged espionage operation.
Few public details are available about Ms. Huang, also known as Huang Le. She reportedly held a senior position at Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed International.
Despite sharing the same surname, Ms. Alice Huang is not related to the 63-year-old Nvidia chief executive.
An online search revealed a brief profile of Alice Huang, including her photograph, on a Chinese fund database. The profile listed previous roles at HSBC and as a reporter for Chinese media organisations CCTV and Hunan TV. However, AFP said it could not independently verify the contents of the profile or determine who created it, though it had contacted the organisations mentioned.
Singapore firm under scrutiny
Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based cloud computing company, has come under scrutiny following the allegations.
According to its website, Megaspeed is an Nvidia cloud partner in the Asia-Pacific region and a core business partner in Nvidia’s cloud computing ecosystem. The Times reported that Megaspeed purchased more than $4.8 billion worth of Nvidia chips over the past two years.
Washington has restricted exports of Nvidia’s most advanced chips to China, citing national security concerns. The measures are also seen as part of broader efforts to maintain a technological advantage in the field of artificial intelligence.
The New York Times reported that the U.S. government has been investigating Megaspeed since 2024 over concerns that Nvidia chips may have been diverted to China.
An AFP report quoting a 2025 Bloomberg News report said that Singaporean authorities were investigating Megaspeed over allegations that Nvidia chips had been smuggled into China. AFP reported that Megaspeed rejected the claims in December that year, saying Bloomberg’s reporting “contains misleading information and incorrect insinuations, including suggestions that we may have been involved in illegal chip transfers”.
The allegations involving Ms. Alice Huang, combined with concerns surrounding the movement of advanced chips, have fuelled speculation about Megaspeed’s possible role in the transfer of restricted technology to China.
Importance of Nvidia chips
Nvidia designs advanced processors originally developed to render 3D graphics in video games. Known as Graphics Processing Units (GPUs), these chips can process enormous amounts of data and are widely used to train and run artificial intelligence systems.
The launch of ChatGPT in 2022 highlighted the potential of artificial intelligence and triggered a surge in investment, with governments and technology companies racing to develop the technology further.
Washington has banned exports of Nvidia’s most advanced Blackwell and Rubin GPUs to China, citing national security concerns and the potential military applications of advanced AI.
Scandal to access AI chips?
Access to Nvidia’s GPUs has become increasingly difficult for Chinese companies since 2022 as the United States tightened controls on advanced semiconductor exports. The policy is also intended to help leading U.S. AI firms maintain their global advantage.
A major step came in October 2022, when the U.S. Commerce Department prohibited sales of Nvidia’s A100 and H100 chips to China. Nvidia subsequently developed modified versions, the A800 and H800, for the Chinese market.
Washington later imposed restrictions on the A800 and H800 as well, prompting Nvidia to develop the H20 chip to comply with revised thresholds. Each new restriction was followed by an alternative product, which in turn prompted further regulatory measures.
The Trump administration introduced licensing requirements for H20 sales in April 2025 before easing the restrictions a few months later. Nvidia and AMD subsequently agreed to provide 15% of their China-related revenue to the U.S. government, an arrangement that has drawn scrutiny within the United States.
China rejects allegations
Meanwhile, China has dismissed all allegations. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said on November 9, 2026, that “the so-called Chinese spy allegation is unsubstantiated. It is nothing but smears against China”.
State-run Global Times said, “Whenever China-U.S. relations begin to improve, there are often those firing shots from the shadows.”
China had earlier denied a New York Times report that stated Mr. Huang had developed a close relationship with Ms. Alice Huang, who operated a company accused of helping move advanced chips into China and was allegedly acting on behalf of Beijing.
FBI probe
The controversy has also drawn scrutiny from U.S. lawmakers over the distribution of advanced artificial intelligence chips and the possibility that restricted technology may have reached China through third countries.
Democratic members of the House Foreign Affairs Committee have sought answers from Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick regarding the administration’s export policies, while Republican lawmakers have requested a closed-door briefing with Mr. Huang.
U.S. President Donald Trump said on November 9, 2026, that the FBI was investigating Mr. Huang’s relationship with an alleged Chinese government agent, but added that he did not believe the Nvidia chief executive had reason for concern.
“I know the FBI is looking into it in terms of something else, and I think that he’ll be in good shape,” Mr. Trump said at a White House briefing.
“I did speak to him today just to see if he’s feeling all right, how you’re doing,” Mr. Trump said. “He said he had absolutely no idea that the person in question was a spy.”
