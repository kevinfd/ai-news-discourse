# Jensen Huang Resigns As Nvidia CEO Following Affairs With Alleged Spy, VP

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-12

Nvidia’s CEO Jensen Huang has resigned following a report that he was engaged in a second extramarital affair, this time with another Nvidia executive.
In a statement, Nvidia’s board announced that Huang resigned as "President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately." In a regulatory filing, the board said it accepted his resignation last night.
Nvidia’s board has appointed the company's CFO, Colette Kress, to lead the company as interim CEO. "The Board is initiating a search for a permanent Chief Executive Officer and will consider internal and external candidates," the announcement says. (President Trump wants it to be former Apple CEO Tim Cook.)
The news is a stunning downfall for Huang, a famed tech executive and one of the most visible faces of the AI industry, which Nvidia has been driving with its cutting-edge AI chips. For decades, Huang has also been a leading figure in PC gaming graphics, having co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and served as its sole chief executive ever since.
But on Wednesday evening, his fate as CEO looked grim. After Huang admitted on Sunday to having an affair with a woman thought to be a Chinese spy, The New York Times reported that Huang had also been in a romantic relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s VP for its healthcare business, citing five people who worked at the company.
Huang and Powell were filmed “being affectionate” outside a hotel during Nvidia’s annual GTC event in March. A bystander used a phone to record the interaction, prompting Nvidia’s security team to sequester the witness and force them to sign a nondisclosure agreement, the NYT says.
The alleged spy, Alice Huang, is an executive with Singapore-based cloud computing provider Megaspeed. The US suspects that Alice Huang is an intelligence asset for the Chinese government and that Megaspeed is a middleman funneling sensitive AI chips to China, according to The New York Times.
The scandal has sparked national security concerns, including whether Nvidia’s CEO—a major supporter of President Trump—may have leaked sensitive information to Alice Huang. Another fear is that he may have been influenced to lobby Washington to loosen export controls and permit the sale of sensitive AI chips to China.
Jensen Huang's departure is expected to create considerable uncertainty over Nvidia's future and the AI industry. He's reportedly agreed to testify before Congress before Thanksgiving.
