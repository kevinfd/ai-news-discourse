# Trump Wants Apple's Tim Cook to Be Nvidia's New CEO

PCMag · Michael Kan · 2026-11-12

With Jensen Huang out at Nvidia, who should lead as its new CEO? President Trump already has someone in mind: Former Apple chief executive Tim Cook.
On Thursday, Trump posted the recommendation on his social media platform Truth Social, writing: "NOW THAT Nvidia CEO JENSEN HUANG, who has done a fantastic job, has resigned, there is only one man to take his place —TIM COOK of Apple. A Legend replaces a Legend!”
Cook stepped down as Apple’s CEO on Sept.1, handing over the reins to John Ternus. So, Cook is technically available, though he remains on Apple’s board of directors as executive chair. The 65-year-old exec joined Apple in 1998 and was named CEO in 2011 following the death of co-founder Steve Jobs, leading the iPhone maker to new heights. He’s also had a positive relationship with Trump; Cook has made multiple trips to the White House in recent years, announcing Apple’s manufacturing investments in the US amid tariff uncertainty.
Analysts and journalists have also floated Cook as a potential successor to Huang, who abruptly resigned amid reports that he had engaged in two extramarital affairs—one with an Nvidia VP, the other with a businesswoman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Other names being circulated include former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, Apple's Chief Hardware Officer Johny Srouji, Nvidia's VP for high-performance computing, Ian Buck, and Nvidia CFO Colette Kress, who was appointed interim CEO today.
Cook hasn't responded to Trump's endorsement, and appears to still be heavily involved with Apple, promoting recent product launches on X and traveling abroad to meet with Apple developers and world leaders, including Turkey's Tayyip Erdoğan.
Huang’s resignation, meanwhile, is casting uncertainty not only on Nvidia's future but also on the AI boom, amid concerns that the industry could be facing a bubble. Hence, finding a replacement who can calm investors will likely be a major priority. Nvidia's board is currently conducting a CEO search that will consider internal and external candidates.
Another big question is what will happen to Huang, who not only resigned as CEO, but also as president and as a board member, seemingly severing ties with a company he co-founded in 1993.
Yesterday, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk tweeted that it would "be an honor” to hire Jensen, hours after The New York Times reported on Huang's affair with the Nvidia VP. If Musk hires Huang, it could be a major win for SpaceX’s chip-making ambitions. The company is building “Terafab,” a large-scale chip factory in Texas, and Huang has strong connections across the semiconductor industry.
Mr. Huang Goes to Washington?
However, Huang could be tied up answering questions about the ramifications of his relationship with the alleged Chinese spy. "Jensen Huang’s decision to resign from NVIDIA does not absolve him, other executives, or the company as a whole of responsibility for putting our national security at risk or for any potential violations of our laws. They still owe Congress answers," tweeted Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.).
According to House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast (R-Fla.), Huang has agreed to testify before Congress ahead of Thanksgiving, though a date is still being worked out, CNBC reports.
John Moolenaar, a Michigan Republican and chairman of the Select Committee on China, adds that "Nvidia must now change its corporate practices and appoint leadership that makes national security its top priority." He's calling on Congress to "act quickly to pass bipartisan export control legislation in the year-end national defense bill."
