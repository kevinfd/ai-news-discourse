# Everything you wanted to know about Nvidia but were too afraid to ask

Fast Company · Adele Peters · 2026-11-12

If you’re not in the tech world, you might only have been vaguely aware of Nvidia—and CEO Jensen Huang’s leather jackets—until he admitted to having an affair with a woman accused of being a Chinese spy. On November 12, a day after revelations of a second affair, this one with an Nvidia executive, Huang resigned. Here’s why the company’s future could affect the entire AI industry.
What Nvidia does
Nvidia is at the center of the AI boom, as the world’s largest supplier of chips used in AI data centers. By some estimates, the company controls 80% of the market. As of November 3, it was the most valuable company in the world, with a market cap of around $5 trillion. By the morning of November 12, as Nvidia’s stock continued to plummet, that figure dropped to $4 trillion. (Huang’s own net worth had fallen $7.4 billion by that point.)
Huang and cofounders Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem launched the company in 1993. They started making graphics processing units, better known as GPUs, for video games. In 2006, they rolled out a platform that enabled other uses for GPUs. In 2012, researchers used the company’s chips to train AlexNet, a breakthrough AI program that taught computers how to recognize images. By the time that ChatGPT launched, Nvidia’s tech was fundamental to AI development.
A large data center might have tens of thousands of Nvidia GPUs that run calculations to train AI models and generate responses when you type a question into ChatGPT or Claude. Training a new AI model could sometimes involve as many as 100,000 GPUs running at the same time for months. After the model is trained, the GPUs are used for “inference,” or applying the trained model to, say, rewrite your email or draw a cartoon of your cat. Inference also requires huge numbers of GPUs given that millions of people use AI at the same time. (In the U.S., around 60 million people use AI chatbots every day, according to a Pew survey this summer.)
Nvidia systems also include CPUs (central processing units) that coordinate tasks and run operating systems in data centers, along with networking equipment that lets GPUs communicate. Its software coordinates the GPUs.
Huang’s downfall
It’s not clear when Jensen Huang started his affair with Alice Huang (no relation), a woman who U.S. intelligence officials reportedly suspect of being a Chinese spy. The Chinese government has denied that she is a spy.
Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, was a senior executive at Megaspeed International, a Singapore-based company that became an Nvidia customer. Megaspeed reportedly smuggled Nvidia’s chips into China, bypassing U.S. sanctions. The U.S. first restricted the export of Nvidia’s chips to China in 2022, citing the risk that China could use them for military development and to compete in AI. Alice Huang reportedly helped move $7.5 billion worth of Nvidia chips to China.
