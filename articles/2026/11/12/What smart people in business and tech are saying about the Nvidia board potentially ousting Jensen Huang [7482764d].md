# What smart people in business and tech are saying about the Nvidia board potentially ousting Jensen Huang

Business Insider · Katherine Li,Kelsey Vlamis,Shubhangi Goel,Thibault Spirlet, Katherine Li,Kelsey Vlamis,Shubhangi Goel,Thibault Spirlet (katherineli@businessinsider.com) · 2026-11-12

Jensen Huang's potential ouster as Nvidia CEO has got people in tech talking. Tomohiro Ohsumi/Getty Images Nvidia stock fell 8% following a Wall Street Journal report that the company's board plans to oust CEO Jensen Huang. The New York Times reported on Wednesday that Huang had a second extramarital affair, this time with an Nvidia employee. This is what smart people in business and tech are saying about Huang's potential exit from Nvidia . Speculation over what Nvidia's future could look like without its high-profile chief executive ramped up on Wednesday. The chipmaker's stock fell 8% in after-hours trading Wednesday after The Wall Street Journal reported that its board was preparing to remove CEO Jensen Huang . The New York Times also reported Wednesday that Huang had an affair with an Nvidia executive who reported to him, citing five people who worked at the company. The report came days after Huang said in a memo to employees that he had an extramarital affair , and after Nvidia's board launched an investigation into whether Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. The New York Times previously reported US officials believed Huang had grown close to a tech executive, Alice Huang , who had been recruited to gather information for the Chinese government. As the chip giant's succession plans remain unknown, here is what smart people in business and tech are saying about Huang's potential exit from Nvidia . Ross Gerber, CEO of Gerber Kawasaki Wealth & Investment Management Ross Gerber has been bearish on Tesla. Emma McIntyre/Getty Images Ross Gerber said Huang's departure would be a disappointment for Nvidia shareholders but said the company could move forward without him. "Looks like Jensen is done. What a disappointment. Sad day for Nvidia shareholders and looks like another tough day for the stock. Never would have guessed he'd go down this way," Gerber wrote in one of several posts on X on Wednesday. "Jensen will be replaced and Nvidia is still Nvidia. That's business. He must go and Nvidia needs to focus on selling chips!" Gerber added. Eric Wallerstein, chief macro strategist at Clocktower Group Eric Wallerstein wrote on X on Wednesday that Huang's likely ousting could shake investor confidence in the broader AI trade , given Nvidia's role in financing the industry. "Jensen has helped foster a complex web of circular financing relationships, and ultimately backstopped the AI trade, with Nvidia's balance sheet," Wallerstein said. "Removing that Put, or even placing uncertainty on its availability, will cause investors to increase the risk premium on AI-related assets," Wallerstein added. Ed Zitron, CEO of EZ Primary Research Carlos Rodrigues/Sportsfile for Web Summit via Getty Images Ed Zitron said in a post on X that he questions what else Nvidia might be withholding from investors, pointing to the company's past regulatory troubles. "Oh so NVIDIA knew about this affair already, huh? What else is this company not telling us?" Zitron wrote on Wednesday. Zitron also cited two previous SEC cases involving Nvidia, one in 2002 over accounting practices and one in 2022 over disclosures related to cryptocurrency mining and gaming revenue. Jason Calacanis, co-host and executive producer of the All-In Podcast Jason Calacanis said that the ban was "smart" because of the risk of terrorism. Bloomberg/Getty Images Jason Calacanis said the company shouldn't be so quick to remove Huang. "A much better solution for NVIDIA's board, shareholders, customers and employees (and America) would be to (a) finish their investigation and (b) make @JensenHuang the chairman if it comes to that," Calacanis wrote on X on Wednesday. "China doesn't need NVIDIA's chips. America needs NVIDIA to win. Act accordingly," Calacanis added, alongside a screenshot of his new Nvidia share purchases. Keith Urbahn, president of Javelin Keith Urbahn, president of Javelin, a literary and creative agency that has repped prominent figures like James Comey and Tucker Carlson, said Wednesday in an X post that the situation was "a generational case study in how not to manage a crisis." He outlined several lessons to take away from the ordeal, including: "Never let a CEO's personal crisis take precedence over the interests of the company and its shareholders." "The board must speak with its own voice. It cannot outsource its institutional credibility to the CEO," he added. Byrne Hobart, venture capitalist and founder of The Diff Byrne Hobart, general partner at Anomaly Fund and founder of The Diff, a finance and tech newsletter, suggested ousting Huang could lead to new competition for Nvidia. "On one hand, boards have to do what they have to do. On the other hand, he's a guy who's worth $150bn, knows everyone in hardware, named his company after the Latin word for "spite," and he lives in a state where you can't enforce noncompetes," he wrote in a post on X. Anthony Pompliano, crypto investor and entrepreneur Slaven Vlasic/Getty Images for Anthony Pompliano Anthony Pompliano, a crypto investor and entrepreneur, wrote on X: "Jensen's yet-to-be-launched Nvidia competitor is going to do numbers in the seed round." Gene Munster, managing partner at Deepwater Asset Management Peter Foley/Bloomberg via Getty Images Gene Munster, a tech analyst and managing partner at Deepwater Asset Management, said in an X post that Nvidia will go on, with or without its famous CEO. "Remember: AI infrastructure customers will act in their best interest," he said. "That means they'll keep buying just as many $NVDA chips with or without Jensen." He also said he guesses the stock will open on Thursday at $150, or about 20% lower than the close at the end of last week, but that he expects it to "quickly rally back." Jim Cramer, host of CNBC's Mad Money Jim Cramer said Apple's new filing was "gloves off." John Nacion/Variety via Getty Images Jim Cramer, who has interviewed Huang on several occasions, questioned how investors should value Nvidia if Huang were suddenly gone. "As I said this morning, there is a bench. As Mike from Coreweave said, there is a company," Cramer said on X on Wednesday. "What is it worth when a key man is just erased?" "Let me think about how sickening this is and then i will come back to you," Cramer added. "I want to throw up." Vinod Khosla, founder of Khosla Ventures Vinod Khosla said he agreed that Huang had shown grace in his handling of the affair. Olivia Vanni/Getty Images The founder of venture capital firm Khosla Ventures backed calls to treat the matter seriously while praising Huang's record. Khosla wrote, "Agree with Ron!" on X in a response to VC Ron Conway's post. Conway said the situation should be taken seriously, but urged people to remember Huang's "determination and vision," which he said laid the foundation for much of today's technology industry. He added that Huang's statements had shown "grace." Dan Niles, founder and portfolio manager of Niles Investment Management In a post on X, Niles predicted Huang could leave Nvidia but argued the company's product pipeline would sustain it for about two years. "Given NYT article, I think Jensen is gone at $NVDA, potentially by tomorrow morning," the portfolio manager wrote, speculating that Nvidia's CFO could become interim CEO. He said the stock could bottom relative to the semiconductor index after earnings, and that Nvidia had a "deep bench of direct reports." Niles compared the situation to Apple after Steve Jobs took medical leave, saying that Nvidia's business could continue to perform for a time even without Huang. He also said the wider AI trade could face further forced selling. Balaji Srinivasan, founder of the Network School and former Coinbase CTO Balaji Srinivasan said Jensen Huang should not resign, just like Bill Clinton did not. Ore Huiying/Bloomberg via Getty Images Srinivasan said Huang should remain Nvidia's CEO. "No. Bill Clinton did not resign. Jensen Huang shouldn't either," Srinivasan wrote, referring to the former US president. "And the Nvidia board shouldn't fire him. Particularly in the heat of the moment." He urged Huang and Nvidia to "ride it out and ignore the journos," adding: "Clinton did. And then no one cared." Srinivasan also reposted a tweet from Elon Musk saying he would be honored to hire Huang. Srinivasan wrote: "If the Nvidia board is silly enough to do something rash, they will likely face immediate market consequences as all kinds of talent leaves to join Jensen at his next thing." Dan Ives, partner at Yorkville Ives & Co. Dan Ives. Astrida Valigorsky/Getty Images Tech megabull Dan Ives said early Thursday morning that he now expects Nvidia's board to remove Huang and argued Wall Street was beginning to price in a future without him. "We expect Jensen to be removed as CEO of Nvidia at this point and it's in the Board's hands," Ives wrote on X. "We see the stock valuation at $225 without Jensen ($300 with Jensen as CEO)." In a previous X post the same day, Ives said Huang's "days at Nvidia could be numbered" and described the situation as a "black eye moment" that had created a "spider web of complexity" for the company, Wall Street, Washington, and the wider tech ecosystem. He added that Nvidia's leading-edge GPU technology and its entrenched role in the AI race would continue. Sarah Guo, founder of Conviction Big Event Media/Getty Images for HumanX Conference Investor Sarah Guo said that Huang's record and the strategic stakes for the US should weigh heavily in the board's decision, describing him as "one of the most extraordinary entrepreneurs of all time." "He's bent the arc of technological progress, and the growth engine of the American economy rests on the shoulders of the company he built," Guo wrote in a Thursday X post. Guo said "greatness doesn't guarantee goodness" and called the allegations "disappointing," but rejected the idea that Huang could simply be replaced. The board should investigate the national security and judgment questions, Guo wrote, but said she expected most shareholders would want Huang to remain CEO, depending on the board's findings.
