Will Anthropic's IPO Succeed?
Amid concerns about the bursting of an AI bubble, will Anthropic's IPO succeed?
An IPO is when a company offers its shares to the public for the first time.
The stock price of AI companies reflects expectations that they will generate significant profits in the future. However, there are also concerns that expectations are running ahead of reality and that stock prices have risen too high. Anthropic, the developer of the generative AI "Claude," began investor presentations for its listing on November 10. The company's revenue from July to September was approximately $17 billion, about 12 times what it was in the same period a year ago.
The indicative price range for the share offering is $70 to $78 per share. According to the company's announcement, the official price will be determined on November 18 (US Eastern Time), and trading is scheduled to begin on the Nasdaq the following day, the 19th.
Anthropic has published a prospectus for investors. The prospectus contains the terms of the share offering, financial performance, and business risks.
In this video, based on the prospectus, we will explain the business strengths that support Anthropic's high valuation and the risks you should know before investing.
This IPO is also an opportunity for expectations regarding massive AI investments to be tested in the stock market. Anthropic's growth and stumbles will also affect companies linked to it through business.
In the second half, we will illustrate the interdependent relationships with companies like Amazon and Google, and through this IPO, we will look at the mechanisms supporting the AI boom and even their weaknesses.
Chapter 1: How Stocks and Money Move in an IPO
A stock is a small portion of a company's equity. When you buy Anthropic stock, you become a shareholder who owns a part of that company. IPO stands for Initial Public Offering. Anthropic has applied for a listing on the US stock market, the Nasdaq. Once listed, shares can be bought and sold on that market.
According to the November 10 prospectus, the majority of the shares being offered this time are newly issued by the company. The proceeds from the new shares go to the company, and the proceeds from shares sold by existing shareholders go to those shareholders.
Methods for buying Anthropic stock from Japan include applying for the pre-listing offering or buying on the market after the listing.
The indicative price range is a guide for the price at which shares will be offered before listing. Anthropic indicated a range of $70 to $78 per share. Securities firms will collect purchase requests from investors and consult with the company to determine the official price.
The offering price is the selling price of the newly issued shares. In an IPO, this offering price and the selling price of shares offered by existing shareholders are collectively called the "public offering price".
Before the listing, you can purchase shares at the offering price if you apply through a securities company that handles the offering domestically and are allocated shares through a lottery or similar process. Not everyone who applies will be able to buy them.
After the listing, you can place buy orders on the U.S. market through Japanese securities companies that handle that stock.
The opening price is the price at which the first trade is executed after the listing. The stock price will continue to change thereafter, and you will purchase at the price at the time your buy order is executed. It may be higher or lower than the offering price.
Chapter 2: Will the stock price rise or fall after the IPO?
Immediately after listing, if there are many buyers and few shares available for sale, the stock price may soar. If there are fewer buy orders than sell orders, the price is more likely to fall.
Investors determine the valuation of the entire company based on the stock price and the number of shares, and consider investing by comparing it to sales and profits.
Market capitalization is the total value of all shares issued by a company. It is calculated by multiplying the price per share by the number of shares outstanding.
The reported valuation of up to approximately $1.4 trillion includes shares that may be issued in the future. This amount is calculated based on the upper limit of the tentative price range of $78.
Anthropic's sales for the one-year period ending in September were $35.3 billion. According to the company, if the sales pace calculated from usage fees and monthly contracts at the end of October continues for one year, sales will reach $81 billion.
The valuation of approximately $1.4 trillion is about 40 times the sales of the past year and about 17 times the annualized sales pace as of the end of October.
If sales continue to grow in the future, the multiple relative to sales will decrease even at the same valuation. However, if investors expect even greater growth, the stock price may fall even with increased revenue.
If Claude's usage fee revenue increases and the outlook for profitability strengthens, more investors may be willing to buy at a higher stock price. Conversely, if usage grows but profitability remains distant due to rising costs, investors who bought in anticipation of profits may sell their shares.
Also, if investment enthusiasm for AI companies as a whole cools, the amount investors are willing to pay for the stock is likely to decrease.
At Anthropic, while sales are surging, the costs to develop and operate Claude are also ballooning.
Chapter 3: Costs to develop and operate Claude
Developing and operating Claude requires massive amounts of "computing resources." Computing resources refer to the computers used for AI development and operation, as well as their processing power. When you ask Claude a question, computers in a data center perform a massive amount of computation to generate an answer.
Computing resources are also required to develop new Claude models. Anthropic performs a massive amount of computation to train models using data.
Anthropic receives a supply of computing resources from companies like Amazon and Google and pays for them.
Anthropic's revenue from April to June 2026 was approximately $11.6 billion, increasing to about $17 billion from July to September. During the same period, costs for computing resources and related equipment also rose from approximately $5.6 billion to about $9.9 billion.
The company also bears other expenses, such as personnel costs. Operating profit is calculated by subtracting these core business expenses from revenue. From July to September, expenses exceeded revenue, resulting in an operating loss of approximately $570 million.
The company also discloses 'adjusted operating profit,' which excludes certain expenses such as stock-based compensation for employees. The total adjusted operating profit increased in line with revenue growth. However, per $100 of revenue, it decreased from $19 in the April-June period to $15 in the July-September period.
The company explains this decline in profit margin as being due to the procurement of computing resources through expensive short-term contracts. From July to September, it utilized $5 billion worth of resources under short-term contracts.
Anthropic also secures computing resources and equipment through long-term contracts in preparation for future increases in usage. Payments based on these contracts span many years. Some contracts can be canceled depending on the conditions, while others require a fixed payment regardless of usage volume.
According to the prospectus dated November 10, future payments based on contracts for computing resources and equipment amount to a multi-year total of approximately $900.9 billion.
Of this amount, approximately $70.7 billion is expected to be paid during the one-year period from October 2026 to September 2027.
Apart from this total, there are also contracts for which the payment amount has not been finalized.
If usage of Claude increases, Anthropic can use the increased revenue from usage fees to cover these payments. If client companies curb their AI spending, or if price competition reduces the revenue earned from the same volume of usage, payments for computing resources will become a heavy burden.
Chapter 4: Who benefits from this listing?
Amazon and Google, which supply computing resources to Anthropic, are also shareholders of Anthropic. Both companies have invested in Anthropic and received shares in return for providing capital.
In this IPO, Anthropic will sell new shares to raise funds, which will be used for payments for computing resources and other purposes. If the stock price rises, the value of the shares held by Amazon and Google will also increase.
In addition to the IPO offering, Anthropic plans to raise funds from major companies. Seven companies, including Google and semiconductor giant NVIDIA, have signed agreements to purchase a total of $26 billion worth of shares in conjunction with this listing. This is conditional upon the successful completion of the listing.
Amazon, Google, and others also transact with Anthropic in the sale of computing resources. If Anthropic increases its procurement in line with the expansion of Claude's usage, the revenue of these suppliers will also increase. Based on its contract with Amazon, Anthropic will pay at least $110 billion over 10 years in principle.
When companies supplying computing resources expand their facilities, semiconductors are also required. For example, the computing resources that Microsoft provides to Anthropic use NVIDIA products. If facilities increase along with the growing demand for Claude, new orders for semiconductor manufacturers can also be expected.
On the other hand, if the growth of AI spending across companies slows down, there is a risk that Anthropic's revenue from usage fees will not increase as expected. If the sluggish demand persists while payments for computing resources continue, the company may curb new procurement. If suppliers also reconsider their facility expansions, it will affect new orders for semiconductor manufacturers.
The success or failure of the IPO and the subsequent stock price
The indicative price range set by Anthropic is between $70 and $78. The final offering price and the stock price after listing will be determined from here.
Rapid revenue growth and investments from major corporations are tailwinds for Anthropic's IPO. On the other hand, recent operating losses and long-term payment obligations for computing resources are concerns for investors buying shares at a high price.
If the company can raise funds from a wide range of investors at the planned price and scale in this IPO, the fundraising at the time of listing can be considered a success. The offering price and the amount raised will reflect how investors have perceived this expectation for growth versus the concerns over cost burdens.
Even after listing, Anthropic needs to further increase the usage of Claude, cover development and operational costs with that revenue, and generate profits.
Companies like Amazon and Google are also involved in Anthropic's growth, both in terms of the value of their holdings and revenue from computing resources. If this IPO is completed at a high valuation, it will likely provide momentum for investments in other AI-related companies. Conversely, if investor appetite is weak, there is a risk that concerns will spread to the fundraising efforts of other AI firms.
