# The 12 best phones of 2026, tested by experts

Mashable · Haley Henschel · 2026-11-12

The 12 best phones of 2026, tested by experts
After extensive testing, we're ready to name the best smartphones of 2026, and we don't take these recommendations lightly.
For most of us, our phone is the most important device we own. It's a lifeline to friends and family, and our primary gateway to the internet. And unless you're still carrying around a separate camera, your phone is also your means of documenting the world around you. There's a lot riding on this pocket-sized gadget!
Mashable has been covering smartphones since 2011, and we've tested all of this year's most exciting releases so far, including the latest flagships and foldables from Apple, Samsung, and Google. Phones have definitely gotten more expensive this year, but we have recommendations for every budget across both iOS and Android operating systems.

Why are the best phones so expensive right now?
If you're in the market for a new phone right now, you should know that it'll cost you more than usual. The best phones were never cheap, but smartphone prices surged in 2026 amid a global RAM shortage driven by the AI boom (commonly known as "RAMageddon"). New AI data centers are hogging supplies of memory and SSD storage chips, driving up their prices. Consequently, consumer tech devices with memory components are also getting more expensive. (RAMageddon price hikes have also taken a toll on laptops and gaming consoles.)
So far, we've seen Apple, Google, and Samsung all raise the prices of their new smartphones at launch. This fall, Samsung implemented another $100 to $200 price hike across its Galaxy S26 lineup, while Apple increased the prices of some older iPhones by $100.

Why we picked this
Apple didn't reinvent the wheel with this one — we might get a more dramatic redesign next year — but the iPhone 17 is an awesome smartphone nonetheless. In fact, "It's easily the most compelling standard iPhone in years," per Mashable senior editor Stan Schroeder. As he pointed out in his review, it's basically an iPhone 17 Pro with no telephoto lens, vapor chamber cooling system, or RAW video support. (That's the extent of their tangible differences that we noticed in testing, but I'll add that the regular iPhone also lacks a 1TB storage option.) If you're not a specialty user in dire need of those features, it just makes sense to go with Apple's mid-range phone. For a couple of hundred dollars less than a Pro model, you still get a buttery 120Hz display, stellar performance and battery life, and very capable main cameras with the iPhone 17. Its wider range of colors and less bulky, less polarizing design seals the deal.

Read Mashable's full review of the Apple iPhone Duo.
Why we picked this
The iPhone Duo is the first and only foldable that Apple's made so far, but it's quite impressive for a first-generation device. It's basically an iPhone that doubles as an iPad when unfolded — you can look at apps side by side, enjoy e-books and games in greater immersion, or use it as a MacBook's secondary display. (We just wouldn't recommend turning it into an alarm clock as Apple suggests.) It certainly helps that the Duo's matte inner screen is "the nicest phone display I’ve ever seen," said Senior Editor Stan Schroeder, who described it as "crisp, sharp, [and] beautiful to look at; it’s downright addictive."
The Duo has a passport-style design like the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8, our favorite Android foldable, just with rounder corners for a more polished look. It runs on the same powerful A20 chip as the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, so it's zippy enough for all the multitasking your heart desires, and optimized apps work incredibly well. While the Duo doesn't last as long as those regular iPhones, its battery life is fine for a foldable.
You'll have to temper your expectations with the Duo's cameras — it lacks a telephoto lens, and the hidden FaceTime camera on its inner screen is straight-up "bad," Schroeder said. That said, this is the case with most foldables.
On a final note, some inaugural Duo owners reported that their device's inner screen cracked shortly after they started using it (a problem that's since become known as "crackgate"). The good news is that the issue doesn't seem to be as serious as social media might have you believe. In a statement, Apple told us that it's isolated to "a small number" of Duos, and that impacted buyers should contact AppleCare for a replacement.
If it helps, Schroeder has been using the Duo for nearly a month and hasn't noticed any hinge or display defects with his review unit. What's more, another Duo has withstood more than 200,000 folds (and counting) inside the "Fold-a-Phone" machine built by our sister site, CNET; they're folding it 900 times an hour until it breaks. You can catch the livestream daily on CNET's YouTube channel.

Mashable has been covering phones for over a decade and a half, since the launch of the iPhone 4S, Samsung Galaxy S II, and Motorola Droid Razr in 2011. As of 2026, our team uses the following metrics to test and evaluate the best smartphones:
We spend several days to weeks with every phone we review. We stress test them by playing games on them, opening multiple apps at once, and trying any unique software features they support. We quantify their performance using Primate Labs' Geekbench CPU benchmark.
Throughout our testing periods, we measure how much a phone's battery drains with heavy usage. We also see how fast it charges. In more recent phone reviews, we've started conducted an additional video rundown test using "Tears of Steel," an open-source Blender movie.
We assess the overall design, construction, and ergonomics of each phone as we use it. Is it comfortable to hold? Can it fit in a pocket? How sharp is its display? Is it waterproof? What colors does it come in?
