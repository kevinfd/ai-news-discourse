# AI regulations: Consultation paper in a month; safety, deepfakes among key focus areas, says Vaishnaw

The Hindu · The Hindu Bureau · 2026-10-08

The Union government will put out a “regulation-focused consultation paper which focuses on AI safety,” IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said on Thursday (October 8, 2026). Mr. Vaishnaw was referring to the issue of deepfakes in India. The “harms” caused by AI to society “are a nuisance which can cause huge problems,” the minister said, adding that “it sets a totally wrong precedent”.
Mr. Vaishnaw asked the Ministry to launch the paper within a month. He was speaking at the launch of the World Bank’s 2026 World Development Report. “We have to bring a techno-legal solution,” he said. “We have to have the stakeholders onboarded because bulk of this regulation will have to be taken by the industry itself.”
On AI, Mr. Vaishnaw said, “We have worked with the industry just the way we worked in the semiconductor and telecom sectors,” and “in the coming days we will have a series of joint workshops between MeitY and the Ministry of Education and the industry so that the universities and colleges can then adopt the new curriculum for their programs.”
AI empanelment
For government use of AI, Mr. Vaishnaw said, “we will be empanelling some of the models or the solutions,” and “the selected firms, the selected startups, can then participate in a tender from whichever government department which wants to use the AI technology.” The government has also issued an RFE for graphics processing units to do AI workloads, he said, adding, “Our demand estimate is for 10,000 GPUs and the first tender that we are coming out is for 5000.”
The World Development Report for this year is on “The Promise of Artificial Intelligence”. The report argues that “AI represents a historic opportunity for [low- and middle-income] countries, potentially enabling them to over come development challenges that have defied solu tion for decades,” and that developing countries should “adopt and adapt, then advance” on AI, and that “[c]ountries should avoid creating AI regulations that conflict with one another.”
