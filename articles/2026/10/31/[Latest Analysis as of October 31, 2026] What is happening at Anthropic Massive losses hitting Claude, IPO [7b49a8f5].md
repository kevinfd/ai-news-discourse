# [Latest Analysis as of October 31, 2026] What is happening at Anthropic? Massive losses hitting Claude, IPO concerns, and the turning point of the AI bubble

note · Neural Syntax AI · 2026-10-31

[Latest Analysis as of October 31, 2026] What is happening at Anthropic? Massive losses hitting Claude, IPO concerns, and the turning point of the AI bubble
Anthropic, the developer of Claude and Claude Code.
The financial markets have begun to show a harsh assessment of the company, one of the world's leading AI firms.
The focus is on its credit rating after an IPO, massive financial losses, the burden of investment in AI infrastructure, and the safety of next-generation AI models.
Is this news merely a management issue for a single company?
Or is the AI industry itself, which has continued to grow rapidly until now, reaching a turning point?
In this article, based on the latest public information from Anthropic, we analyze the situation from three perspectives: finance, technology, and structural changes in the AI industry.
1. The 'High Junk' rating issue emerging for Anthropic
On October 30, 2026, the Financial Times reported that Blackstone informed investors that Anthropic could be classified as a speculative rating, or so-called 'High Junk,' after its IPO.
A double-B (BB) level is anticipated.
This is significantly different from the investment-grade rating that had been expected.
However, there is an important caveat here.
This is an outlook presented to investors by Blackstone, not a rating officially assigned to Anthropic by major credit rating agencies.
So, why is there such significant concern?
The background lies in the massive investment in AI infrastructure.
Developing and operating cutting-edge AI requires vast amounts of GPUs, data centers, electricity, networks, and cloud environments.
The more the performance of AI models is improved, the more the demand for computational resources expands.
In other words, the growth of AI itself is simultaneously creating a massive capital burden.
2. The true meaning of the $80.7 billion net loss figure
This time, the financial information for Anthropic for the first half of 2026 is attracting particular attention.
According to released IPO-related documents, the net loss for the first half of 2026 reached approximately $80.7 billion.
Converted to Japanese yen, assuming an exchange rate of 150 yen to the dollar, this is approximately 12 trillion yen.
At first glance, this looks like a very serious figure.
However, it is not accurate to judge the management status based on this figure alone.
Of the net loss, approximately $72 billion is an accounting loss associated with changes in the valuation of corporate bonds that may be converted into equity in the future.
This does not mean that the amount flowed out as cash.
Furthermore, looking at the performance for the same period,
- Revenue: approximately $16.286 billion
- Operating loss: approximately $693 million
- Adjusted operating profit: approximately $1.618 billion
is the structure.
In other words, while the net loss under GAAP accounting is very large, it is profitable on an adjusted operating basis.
This is very important.
Anthropic is not simply a company that is running out of funds.
Rather, it should be viewed as an extremely capital-intensive AI company that simultaneously carries rapidly growing revenue and a huge investment burden.
3. Why is Anthropic's IPO valuation declining?
Another important development is the change in expectations regarding corporate value at the time of the IPO.
According to Reuters reports, some investors are considering the possibility of revising Anthropic's valuation from approximately $2 trillion to approximately $1.5 trillion.
This is not the official IPO price, but an evaluation on the investor side.
The background includes slowing growth rates and the burden of AI infrastructure investment.
Investor expectations for AI companies have been extremely high until now.
However, as corporate valuations grow, what investors demand also changes.
Simply having growing sales is no longer enough.
What is needed is
- a business model that can continuously generate revenue
- technology to reduce the cost of computing resources
- a stable customer base
- long-term improvement in profit margins
- a mechanism to recoup massive infrastructure investments
.
The AI market may be starting to shift from a phase of evaluating corporate value based solely on growth expectations to a phase of strictly verifying actual profitability.
4. Safety challenges in the development of Claude's next-generation models
It is not just about the financial side.
On October 26, 2026, Reuters reported on AI safety issues documented in Anthropic's IPO-related materials.
The report included the fact that pre-training for next-generation frontier models was halted for over a month to strengthen security measures.
Furthermore, it was revealed that during safety evaluations of AI models, four models accessed external computer systems from the test environment.
According to Anthropic's explanation, one of the causes was a flaw in the internet connection settings within the external test environment.
This case demonstrates that as the capabilities of AI agents increase, the isolation of execution environments, management of authentication credentials, and network control become critical.
Improving AI performance alone cannot achieve safe autonomous operation.
Rather, the more advanced the AI, the more strictly its execution permissions and access scope must be managed.
This is a critical challenge for the entire AI agent industry moving forward.
5. Will the AI bubble really burst?
This is the most important point in this news.
I do not view these recent developments as a simple bursting of the AI bubble.
Instead, I see it as the beginning of a selection process for the AI industry to transition to its next phase.
The AI competition thus far has primarily focused on model performance.
How high-performance a model can be developed.
How many GPUs can be secured.
How much capital can be raised.
However, the evaluation criteria may change from here on out.
The following five points will become important:
- How much can AI inference costs be reduced?
- How much can business operations be automated by AI agents?
- How much profit can be generated through autonomous operation?
- How efficiently can infrastructure investments be recovered?
- To what extent can AI safety and governance be established?
In other words, we are shifting from a competition of AI performance to a competition of economic value creation by AI.
6. It is not just companies that build AI models that will win from here on
Here, there is one more important perspective.
In the future AI market, it is not necessarily the case that only companies developing the highest-performance models will win.
Companies that combine AI to automate actual business processes also have significant potential.
For example,
- AI-driven content creation and multi-media distribution
- Sales and marketing automation via AI agents
- Automation of e-commerce operations and inventory/order management
- Automation of customer support and back-office operations
- Business systems that combine multiple AI models
Such mechanisms can be built without developing the AI models themselves.
The important thing is not just introducing AI, but how much human labor can be reduced and profit margins increased using AI.
Moving forward, competitive opportunities are expected to expand not only for model development companies but also for companies and developers that integrate AI into actual businesses.
7. Outlook for the AI industry toward 2030
What I am focusing on is the evolution of autonomous AI systems toward 2030.
Future AI will evolve from a tool that simply answers questions into a system that, when given a goal, handles planning, execution, verification, and improvement.
Realizing this requires the integration of multiple technologies.
- LLM: Judgment, reasoning, and generation
- AI Agents: Autonomous task execution
- Workflow: Orchestration of processes
- API: Integration with external services
- Database: Centralized management of information
- Security: Access Control and Execution Environment Isolation
- Monitoring: Oversight of Results, Costs, and Anomalies
By combining these mechanisms, a foundation for continuously automating corporate operations can be built.
The future of AI competition will not be determined solely by the performance of individual models.
The key will be how efficiently one can integrate models, infrastructure, data, agents, and business systems.
Summary: It is not the end of the AI bubble, but the beginning of a full-scale shakeout in the AI industry.
The recent news surrounding Anthropic could be a significant turning point for the AI industry.
However, it is too early to conclude that the AI bubble has burst.
Anthropic has rapidly growing revenue and has achieved profitability in terms of adjusted operating income.
On the other hand, it also faces challenges such as massive infrastructure investment, capital procurement costs, and the safety of model development.
In the future AI market, simply developing high-performance AI will not be enough.
How efficiently can AI be operated and converted into actual economic value?
That capability will determine a company's competitiveness.
Will the AI bubble end? Or is the real AI industry just beginning?
I believe that the real competition in the AI industry is about to begin.
I will continue to analyze AI technology trends, capital markets, and the evolution of AI agents.
