# Facial Recognition Could Be Coming to a Starbucks Near You

PCMag · Will McCurdy · 2026-10-31

Would you let an AI-enabled camera scan your face if it meant you got your latte faster? Facial recognition has been a major source of controversy across the globe over the past decade, with human rights campaigners highlighting privacy concerns, but the world’s biggest coffee chain may see it as a worthwhile investment.
In its latest earnings call, Starbucks implied it could be exploring the deployment of facial recognition technology. The company is “exploring experiences that recognize participating customers as they arrive, present personalized digital menus and make payment more effortless," said CEO Brian Niccol.
But don’t expect the tech to hit stores anytime soon. The CEO added that his company was still “early in this work” and would “test, learn and scale what works.”
Starbucks also said other AI-based tools are on the table; it will begin testing background AI models as part of its drive-thru offering to help its baristas “input orders more accurately and efficiently as they interact with customers” during the 2027 financial year.
Many consumers have already expressed their opposition on platforms like X and Bluesky, with one user dubbing the potential rollout of facial recognition an example of “intrusive and weird business practices.” However, Starbucks never explicitly named the tech in its earnings call.

Starbucks isn’t exactly unique in looking to roll out this type of tech. Taco Bell owner Yum Brands told investors in an April call that it is testing the use of AI to present customers at its drive-thrus with different menus.
AI has produced plenty of screw-ups in the service industry when it comes to taking customers’ orders. Last year, we saw a large number of viral videos where consumers would mess with Taco Bell’s AI ordering system, for example, ordering 18,000 cups of water.
