# Anthropic’s IPO filing is the start of the ‘put up or shut up’ era of AI

Fast Company · Chris Stokel-Walker · 2026-10-26

Tech’s worst-kept secret is finally out: Anthropic has filed its S-1 with the Securities and Exchange Commission, paving the way for an IPO. A reported investor road show could begin as soon as November 9, attempting to drum up interest befitting its targeted $1.5 trillion valuation.
In doing so, the company has opened its books, giving would-be investors their first look at its finances. And the figures are unprecedented. The company booked an $80.7 billion net loss on $16.3 billion in revenue, but operating losses are falling.
Around 250 customers pay the firm more than $10 million a year in contracts, while more than 10,000 pay at least $100,000. Its net revenue retention stands at around 900%. And every employee contributes $6.6 million to the company’s revenue, the kinds of numbers that any company would envy.
However, the S-1 filing also reveals plenty of potential problems ahead. Between 5 and 7 cents of every dollar Anthropic receives from customers comes from users the company doesn’t want on its platform and presumably intends to remove because they violate its terms of service or usage policies. Almost half of Anthropic’s revenue in the first six months of the year came through cloud marketplaces run by Amazon, Google, and Microsoft. Those firms are simultaneously its computing suppliers, business partners, and competitors.
The initial public offering will be a major test of the market’s appetite to invest in the promise and potential of artificial intelligence, ushering in the “put up or shut up” era for the technology.
“Anthropic’s IPO is shaping up to be a major test of investor appetite for AI, especially as it’s arriving at a particularly turbulent time for markets,” says Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club, a non-advisory investment service based in England.
Big numbers, big problems?
For years, investors have bought into AI’s potential to transform the economy, benefiting from ever-increasing valuations of companies across the AI ecosystem. Private investment in AI firms has increased 48-fold since 2013, surging to $290 billion last year from $131 billion in 2024. But those investors, both private and public, are banking on getting a return, and it’s far from certain they will. This weekend, Torsten Slok, chief economist at NYC-based asset manager Apollo Global Management, warned that AI has “delivered zero financial payoff for corporate America outside of tech.” (Slok declined to speak for this story.)
Slok explained that “the AI story will be decided in the S&P 493, not the Magnificent 7, and so far, there is no evidence that revenue growth and margins are improving outside the tech sector.”
