# Is the AI Boom a Bubble? Here's the Stock I'd Buy Either Way.

The Motley Fool · Will Healy · 2026-10-14

Although AI bulls may not want to hear this, an AI bubble is a valid concern. The stock indexes are near record highs, and the CAPE ratio, otherwise known as the Shiller P/E ratio, is at 41, a level that it had only reached during the dot-com bubble.
Nonetheless, such metrics do not imply that all AI-oriented tech stocks are overvalued, and exiting the sector entirely may ultimately not serve investors well. The history of the dot-com bust suggests holding many of the industry's top stocks led to massive gains once internet stocks recovered.
Such a precedent could make the case for buying and holding Google's parent company, Alphabet (GOOGL -1.74%) (GOOG -1.68%). Here's why.
Alphabet's financial concerns
Alphabet may not look like the best choice from a valuation perspective once investors dig into the numbers.
On the surface, the Google parent's P/E ratio is about 18. That comes after the stock gained around 300% since the beginning of 2023, as the generative AI race intensified over the last few years. Such a performance might even persuade investors to overlook the $195 billion to $205 billion in 2026 capital expenditures (capex) estimate.

Key Data Points
Still, the spending has raised concerns, as a company known for having massive liquidity has recently turned to the debt markets to finance it. A closer look at the income statement may reinforce those worries. In the second quarter of 2026, $98 billion of its $139 billion in income before income taxes came from a category called "other income." Dig deep into the 10-Q filing with the SEC, and most of that gain came from unrealized gains on its equity investments.
All in, Alphabet earned almost $120 billion in that quarter. Still, after accounting for income taxes and subtracting investment gains, the company earned only about $14 billion in net income from operations.
While that is not a terrible return, it does not compare to the huge margins earned by some AI companies. It could also mean operating losses if an unexpected event forced AI growth and spending to crater.
Furthermore, the company's traditional cash cow, Google Advertising, fell to just 68% of the company's revenue. That is the part of the business that likely drove most of Alphabet's $53 billion in free cash flow over the trailing 12 months, an impressive feat since that metric subtracts capex.
The Google parent's bright future
Nonetheless, investors need to consider that many of its competing hyperscalers have either turned free cash flow negative or are struggling to keep that metric positive. Hence, Alphabet is probably better positioned to keep up with this AI race than its peers, while also able to slow capex quickly in the event of a downturn.
Moreover, that race appears to be transforming Google's parent into a leading AI company. Google Cloud now accounts for 21% of the company's revenue in the latest quarter, up from 14% just one year ago.
That increase occurred as Google Cloud's revenue rose 82% during that period, suggesting the heavy spending is starting to make sense. Its Google Gemini AI platform has also held its own in an intense competition with OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude.
Additionally, CEO Sundar Pichai said he expects Alphabet's autonomous vehicle arm, Waymo, to meaningfully contribute to the company's revenue as early as 2027. That could become a massive revenue stream as drivers increasingly adopt this technology.
Also, the company's valuation appears attractive, even when excluding one-time events. The company sells at a forward earnings multiple of 29, a slight premium to the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.11%) average P/E ratio of 27. That valuation arguably makes the stock a buy while also suggesting limited downside if business conditions suddenly deteriorate.
Moving forward with Alphabet stock
Alphabet is the stock investors should own regardless of whether the AI industry is in a bubble.
Indeed, the P/E ratio is deceptively low, and the company's capex spending may appear concerning, even for those who believe in the AI story.
However, the company has undoubtedly used its capex to foster a competitive edge in AI, which should bode well for Alphabet's long-term future. Furthermore, a reasonable valuation and the ability to adjust capex should limit downside in the event of a downturn.
Thus, whatever happens in the industry, Alphabet stock should remain well-positioned to protect investor wealth while delivering long-term growth powered by AI.
