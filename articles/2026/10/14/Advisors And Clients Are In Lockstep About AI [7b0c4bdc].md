# Advisors And Clients Are In Lockstep About AI

Forbes · Dryden Pence · 2026-10-14

Eldon Dryden Pence III serves as the Chief Investment Officer of Pence Capital Management.
I recently came across two encouraging pieces of information about how artificial intelligence is being used in wealth management today.
The first finding wasn’t too surprising. A recent study found that 70% of financial advisors already use artificial intelligence. This makes sense; AI has boosted productivity across many sectors, including wealth management, since generative AI use exploded in late 2022. However, AI still can’t do everything to make advisors’ or clients’ work more efficient or meaningful. I’d also argue that it shouldn’t.
An August survey from Addition Wealth confirmed this. Almost three-quarters (72%) of people surveyed said they want a human to lead or be involved when getting financial advice, while only 5% prefer advice that comes only from AI.
Advisors and their clients agree. They want to use technology to save time on more menial tasks so they can focus on giving more personal financial advice and care. That’s great news for the future of wealth planning. So, what’s the best way for advisors to move forward?
First, Consider AI’s Limitations
Think of AI like a polygraph. It needs a human to interpret the results correctly. In financial advising, AI can’t replace the need for real conversations, especially when it comes to understanding what clients are really concerned about like losing their job, taking care of their family or ensuring that long-term healthcare expenses are managed effectively.
Put simply, AI is good at spotting issues but not at digging deeper, which is essential in financial advising. Great advisors don’t just find problems—they get to the root of them. AI doesn’t have a “bullcrap meter,” the ability to dig into clients’ concerns, or the power to help reframe bad habits—or encourage positive ones—the way people can.
I firmly believe human advisors are the critical ingredient for sharing tough news and building strong client relationships. In that vein, we should welcome AI’s help with analysis and productivity, while we focus on the personal touches that are the cornerstone of this work.
Next, Don’t Fear Robo-Advisors
More people are using robo-advisors because they’re easier to access. The global market is expected to grow from $14.1 billion in 2026 to over $41 billion by 2030.
Does that make you worry? It shouldn’t.
While robo-advisors are a helpful way to get more people started with investing, they don’t offer the personal care and customization that make financial planning valuable. Like AI, robo-advisors will work alongside human advisors, helping the market grow and become more efficient.
Most planes fly just fine on autopilot, but you still need a pilot in the cockpit for those moments when something goes wrong. A human is needed to notice when the machine isn’t working right. So while it seems like the rise of the robot is coming after your clients next, they can never compete against the emotional intelligence that you provide.
Finally, Take Charge Of Your AI Destiny
Most advisors are comfortable using AI for things like note-taking and communication, but not for important tasks like portfolio planning. AI is great for analysis, but it can’t handle the emotional and personal side of financial planning.
Here are practical ways wealth advisors can get more comfortable and improve their skills with AI tools.
First, start using a general-purpose AI tool. If you haven’t already, explore note-taking and analysis tools, like Copilot or JumpAI. These can give you helpful summaries of conversations, so you don’t miss important details or next steps, and they can make repetitive tasks more efficient, supporting the repository of the historical information you gather on clients, which lets you provide more comprehensive services.
Next, build a set of tools that fit your area of expertise. For example, analysis programs can help spot issues in tax returns and estate planning, while expanding your holistic planning offerings. If your firm does not offer these services directly, this is a great opportunity to find and refer trusted partners who will help your client shore up any needs. Let technology help clients connect the dots on what they need to do and when, pulling in outside professionals when needed. Holistiplan is a great AI tool built for financial advisors to automate tax return analysis and scenario modeling. (Full disclosure: The Pence team uses Copilot, JumpAI and Holistiplan. They have financial investment in Microsoft but are not otherwise connected to JumpAI and Holistiplan.)
Finally, stay curious about what’s possible. We’re still in the early days of using AI in the wealth management industry, and bigger opportunities are likely ahead for us and our clients. As always, make sure the technology is working for you, not against you, don’t fear the unknown and let it help shine a bigger spotlight on your firm’s greatest asset: the human touch, the advisors.
