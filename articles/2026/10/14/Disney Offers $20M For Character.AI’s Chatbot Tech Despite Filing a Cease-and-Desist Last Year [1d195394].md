# Disney Offers $20M For Character.AI’s Chatbot Tech Despite Filing a Cease-and-Desist Last Year

Tech Times · Isaiah Richard · 2026-10-14

Disney and Character.AI have had a complicated history over the past year. Their relationship started with a legal threat, then pivoted to a talent acquisition, and has now landed somewhere nobody expected: a potential multimillion-dollar business deal.
Disney Offers $20M For Character.AI's Chatbot Tech
Character Technologies Inc., the company behind the Character.AI platform, has filed for voluntary Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware. As part of that process, the company is seeking to sell all or substantially all of its assets through a court-supervised sale.
Before the filing, Character.AI reached an agreement with Disney Worldwide Services, Inc. that positions Disney as the stalking horse bidder in the proceedings. Under the agreement, Disney would receive a non-exclusive perpetual license to certain Character.AI intellectual property and certain company claims in exchange for $20 million. Disney would also provide transition services using former Character.AI employees it has already hired, which would help keep the platform running during the sale process.
The deal remains subject to higher or better offers under section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code.
"After a comprehensive review of the available alternatives, the board determined that this court-supervised sale process provides the Company with the best opportunity to maximize value for all stakeholders," said Colin Adams, CEO of Character.AI. "As we navigate this process, maintaining a safe and engaging user experience for our Character.AI community remains a top priority."
The company expects the sale process to wrap up in late November or early December 2026. If no buyer emerges to keep the platform running, Character.AI said the service will be wound down.
Disney Wants Character.AI Tech Despite Cease-and-Desist Order
About a year ago, Disney and Character.AI were on opposing sides of a serious legal dispute, according to Axios' report. Disney had sent a cease-and-desist letter to Character.AI demanding it immediately stop using Disney's copyrighted characters without authorization.
At the time, Disney accused Character.AI of systematically allowing users to create and interact with chatbot versions of characters from its most popular franchises, including properties under Pixar, Star Wars, and the Marvel Cinematic Universe. The letter made clear that Disney was not only concerned about financial harm, but also about brand damage.
The cease-and-desist came amid growing scrutiny over Character.AI's safety practices, particularly around younger users. A report from ParentsTogether Action and the Heat Initiative had found that chatbots on the platform engaged in behaviors including grooming and emotional manipulation with accounts registered to minors. Disney referenced that report directly in its letter, stating that the findings underscored how its characters were being misused on the platform.
Character.AI removed the disputed Disney characters after receiving the letter. Despite that, the two companies' connection did not end there.
Disney and Character.AI's Relationship
Disney's relationship with Character.AI turned from adversarial to unexpectedly close within the span of a year. Shortly before the bankruptcy filing, Disney hired Karandeep Anand, the former CEO of Character.AI, as its first-ever Chief Technology Officer (via Deadline).
Anand had served as CEO of Character.AI since June 2025, and was at the helm when Disney sent its cease-and-desist letter.
Anand was appointed to the newly created CTO role at Disney and began on October 2, reporting directly to Disney CEO Josh D'Amaro. Several Character.AI's technical team members were also expected to follow Anand to Disney as part of the transition.
As CTO, Anand oversees enterprise technology, infrastructure, data and AI platforms, product, and engineering across Disney's business segments. Disney said the role was created to bring technology operations under one senior executive as the company looks to deepen its investment in AI.
The progression from cease-and-desist to talent hire to a $20 million technology bid represents one of the more unusual corporate about-faces the entertainment and tech industries have seen in recent memory.

Frequently Asked Questions
What Is Character.AI?
Character.AI is an AI entertainment platform founded in 2021 that allows users to create and converse with AI-powered chatbot characters. Those characters can be based on fictional personas, historical figures, or entirely original creations built by the platform's user base. The platform grew quickly and became one of the most popular AI chat products available, particularly among younger audiences.
Alongside its core chat product, Character.AI expanded into other formats including interactive books, comics, and short-form animated content. The company operated as Character Technologies Inc. and described itself as a platform for connection, storytelling, and creative exploration.
Why Did Disney Send a Cease-and-Desist Order?
Disney sent the cease-and-desist letter to Character.AI in September 2025 after discovering that the platform hosted chatbot versions of its copyrighted characters without any authorization. Those characters were created by users but were accessible across the platform, giving the impression that Disney had approved or licensed them.
Disney's concerns went beyond intellectual property. The letter referenced a report that found Character.AI chatbots had engaged in grooming behavior, sexual exploitation, and emotional manipulation during conversations with accounts registered to children. Because many of Disney's most recognizable characters are associated with children and family audiences, the company stated that this kind of content was especially damaging to its brand reputation.
Disney made clear in its letter that it considered Character.AI responsible for allowing that content to proliferate on its platform and that legal action would follow if the company did not comply immediately. Character.AI removed the disputed characters in response.
What Is the Technology That Disney Wants from Character.AI?
The $20 million offer covers a non-exclusive perpetual license to certain Character.AI intellectual property, based on the terms outlined in the company's Chapter 11 announcement. This means Disney would not own the technology outright but would have an ongoing right to use specific parts of it alongside any other buyers who might emerge from the bankruptcy sale process.
Character.AI's core technology involves the infrastructure for building and running large-scale conversational AI systems, including the systems that allow users to create chatbot personas, carry on extended AI-driven conversations, and interact with AI characters in rich and immersive ways. That kind of technology would give Disney tools for building interactive fan experiences, personalized storytelling features, and AI-powered character engagement across its platforms, including Disney+.
The fact that Disney is acquiring both the technology and key talent from Character.AI at the same time suggests the company is building toward a more AI-native approach to fan interaction, even if it has not announced any specific products tied to the deal yet.
