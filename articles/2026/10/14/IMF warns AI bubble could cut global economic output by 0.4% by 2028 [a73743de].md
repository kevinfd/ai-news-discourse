# IMF warns AI bubble could cut global economic output by 0.4% by 2028

CNBCTV18 · Alpha Desk · 2026-10-14

IMF warns AI bubble could cut global economic output by 0.4% by 2028
- Edited byalphadesk
- Published OnOct 14, 2026 | 15:20 IST
- Last Updated OnOct 14, 2026 | 15:20 IST
The IMF warned that the AI boom's rising leverage, circular financing, and concentrated investments could lead to a market correction, potentially cutting global GDP by 0.4% and investments by 1.3% by 2028, and urged tighter regulatory controls.
The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has warned that the artificial intelligence (AI) boom is increasingly being fuelled by borrowed money, cautioning that a sharp reversal could reduce global economic output by 0.4% by 2028.
In its latest Global Financial Stability Report, the IMF said markets have remained resilient on the back of strong AI earnings, but borrowing and financial risks across the sector are rising. Large technology companies known as hyperscalers now account for 10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from just 1% in 2024.
Borrowing and risk-taking are also increasing across financial markets. Hedge funds’ equity positions and margin loans have climbed to 15% of the GDP of advanced economies. Individual stocks now make up 28% of leveraged exchange-traded funds (ETFs), up from 16% previously.

The report flagged another risk: large AI companies investing in smaller firms that then buy their products and services. This creates a cycle in which companies invest in one another while relying on future business commitments to justify those investments.
Since 2023, 14 disclosed deals have involved $300 billion in equity investments and $650 billion in debt, linked to commitments for $1.4 trillion in future revenue.
The IMF warned that if weaker companies struggle to survive, the value of these investments could fall and the promised future revenues may not materialise.
A sharp reversal in the AI boom could reduce global investment by 1.3% by 2028, with advanced Asian economies among the most exposed.
To limit these risks, the IMF urged regulators to tighten oversight of borrowing used to invest in stocks and leveraged ETFs, which amplify market gains and losses. It also recommended stronger capital requirements and limits on concentrated exposure to the AI sector where risks are significant.
The IMF said global banks would remain adequately capitalised even under a stress scenario combining an AI market correction with a sharp rise in bond yields. However, it warned that financial stability risks remain elevated.
Governments and central banks may also have less room to respond to a major shock than at most points in the past two decades, as their ability to use fiscal and monetary measures has become more constrained.
