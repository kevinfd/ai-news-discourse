# Nestlé’s CEO shares his advice for building teams that can weather a crisis

Business Insider · Ana Altchek, Ana Altchek (aaltchek@businessinsider.com) · 2026-10-14

Philipp Navratil is the CEO of Nestlé, a company that employs over 270,000 workers globally. Nespresso Nestlé CEO Philipp Navratil said resilient teams have two things in common. He said they work well together and they have one purpose. The CEO said that those teams are built on trust both from leaders and workers. As CEO of Nestlé, Philipp Navratil leads a workforce of more than 270,000 people and oversees a portfolio of more than 30 billion-dollar brands. So he's encountered his fair share of crises. "There's always something happening somewhere," Navratil told Business Insider. "Natural disasters, conflicts." Navratil said resilient teams tend to share two traits: They work well together and have a shared purpose. While leaders can't plan for every challenge, Navratil said, they can build team structures that help people pull in the same direction when challenges come up. He pointed to Nestlé employees in the Middle East and Ukraine, who have rallied around a common goal during difficult moments.

 Creating those teams While Navratil's advice may sound simple, teams don't become resilient simply by deciding to be focused and collaborative. Leaders need to create an environment where people trust one another and understand who they're working alongside, he said. "It's really about transparency, trust," Navratil said, adding that, "you want to have a leadership that you can trust, especially in a crisis situation." Additionally, leaders also need people they can count on, the CEO said. That means building strong teams of people that can make sound decisions in the company's best interest. "It's not easy," Navratil said. "It's a journey." Navratil said teams that operate that way can be "amazingly powerful," and they don't need to wait for a crisis for "that beauty to unfold." When employees make a habit of working with trust, purpose, and sound judgment every day, they're in a better position to continue moving forward, no matter what comes at them, he said. Those kinds of teams may look like they were built for crisis response, the CEO said, but that's not the case.
