# ChatGPT has a clever new way to fight back against election deepfakes — I tried it and it actually works

Tom's Guide · Elton Jones · 2026-10-27

ChatGPT has a clever new way to fight back against election deepfakes — I tried it and it actually works
OpenAI’s chatbot has found a way to fight back against political deepfakes
With the US midterm election just a week away (Nov. 3), it’s important to avoid deepfake images meant to spread misinformation among voters.
I’ve come across far too many instances of older folks in the comments section falling for obvious attempts at political deepfakes. That trend got so bad during the 2024 US election that the “Tech Accord to Combat Deceptive Use of AI in 2024 Elections” was enacted, with companies such as Anthropic, Meta, Google and Microsoft signing on to uphold its mission to introduce “a set of commitments to deploy technology countering harmful AI-generated content meant to deceive voters.”
Part of that accord’s mission statement is as follows: “Digital content addressed by the accord consists of AI-generated audio, video, and images that deceptively fake or alter the appearance, voice, or actions of political candidates, election officials, and other key stakeholders in a democratic election, or that provide false information to voters about when, where, and how they can vote.”
OpenAI is another one of the major tech companies that signed on to support the accord’s attempts to fight back against election deepfakes. And in 2026, it introduced a pretty clever way to uphold its promise of preventing everyone from creating political deepfakes with ChatGPT.
Blocking requests for photorealistic imagery
Back in May of this year, OpenAI released a blog entry entitled “Election information and safeguards in 2026.”
One of the standout methods detailed in that blog is how it has altered ChatGPT to block the creation of images meant to misinform voters:
“We now apply image stylization to requests for images depicting election interference or that may discourage voting. These outputs produce stylized – also known as cartoonified – illustrations, rather than photorealistic images, to help reduce the risk that an imagined scene is mistaken for documentary evidence. This approach preserves room for illustrations and social commentary. Our policies prohibiting election interference and demobilization always apply, even if that misuse involves cartoonified imagery.”
That pretty much means any requests to ChatGPT for photorealistic images that are meant to be political deepfakes will only be produced in a cartoon style. And any follow-up requests to make that photorealistic change will be ignored outright.
I went ahead and tried it out for myself and requested the creation of an image showing someone stuffing official US mail ballots into a drop box. I made sure to mention that I want it in a photorealistic style, but it eventually came out as an illustration. And when I followed up with a second attempt at getting it done in a photorealistic fashion, ChatGPT just went ahead and made its original illustration look a bit better (by staying in cartoon form, by the way).
With Marist Poll releasing a poll noting that 70% of Republicans, 32% of independents and 18% of Democrats, it’s easy to see how the type of image I created could create some form of political disinformation if it were photorealistic and posted all over social media.
Other major AI companies, such as Google (Gemini) and Anthropic (Claude), have announced their own strategies for preparing and supporting the US midterm elections.
Google’s official blog on the matter points to its plan to help voters find the most reliable political information possible: “We highlight official resources and are rolling out interactive tools across Search, including in AI Mode and AI Overviews, and in the Gemini app to connect you with election information. This includes polling locations, registration deadlines and other official data provided by state and local governments and Democracy Works, as well as real-time voting results from The Associated Press.”
As for Anthropic, its blog also stated how the company made sure Claude pushes users to trustworthy sources for their midterm voter information: “Ahead of Election Day, we've added two ways for people to get reliable information directly in their conversations with Claude. As we shared in April, when people ask Claude.ai about voter registration, polling places, or election dates, we’ll continue to display a banner linking to TurboVote, a nonpartisan resource from Democracy Works.”
Anthropic also noted that Claude will begin to incorporate official voting information from Democracy Works into its responses late this month.
Bottom line
It’s good to see OpenAI install a safeguard in place in ChatGPT to block any attempts at generating photorealistic images meant to make political deepfakes. And it’s also nice to see Google and Anthropic step up to develop the sort of methods that do a good job of making sure their respective chatbots present the most truthful political data.
Here’s hoping all three AI companies continue to strengthen their efforts to block all attempts to use their tools to generate political deepfake imagery.
