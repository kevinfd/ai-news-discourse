The best laptops of 2026: Out of the 90+ models we've tried, these are our 9 favorites
The best laptops are fast enough for their primary use cases, well-made, and priced fairly for their specs. Ideally, they also have enough battery power to last a full workday (but often much longer). Whether your preferred daily driver is a sleek Apple MacBook, a versatile Windows laptop, or a simple Chromebook, a reliable PC is a solid investment that feels worth it for years to come.
After testing dozens of different models, we believe these are the top laptops for 2026 buyers.

Why are laptops so expensive right now?
Laptop prices have skyrocketed in 2026 for several reasons. One is the recent boom in AI data center construction, which has caused a global shortage of RAM and SSD storage chips (often referred to as "RAMageddon"). As a result, many consumer tech devices that use these components, including the best laptops, have become vastly more expensive than they would've been otherwise. Most PC makers have been affected, including Apple, Dell, Lenovo, and Acer.
The memory crisis is expected to worsen and could last into 2028, driving up prices even further and making good values in the budget and mid-range tiers even scarcer. On top of that, the Trump administration is reportedly mulling new tariffs on semiconductors that would apply to laptops and other electronics.
Further complicating things is that Microsoft reportedly raised its Windows 11 licensing fees by higher-than-usual percentages. In other words, both hardware and software prices have gone up for PC makers this year, forcing them to pass on the extra costs to consumers. Lucky us.
The Mashable team is taking the current state of the market into consideration as we assess and select laptops to recommend to our readers. Moreover, we've gone back and re-evaluated several models that have gotten more expensive since we first tested them.
At the time of writing, only two previous top picks have gotten so pricey that we think they're no longer worth buying. That would be the Lenovo Chromebook Plus 14, which jumped from an already-high $750 to $1,000, and the Acer Chromebook Plus 516, which went from $479 to $649. We're currently testing some other Chromebook options and will update this guide with a new top pick soon.

Who it's for
In a year when laptops became extraordinarily expensive, the MacBook Neo is a breath of fresh air. And for the average person who isn't running AI models locally or messing around with photo or video editing, it's a perfectly capable everyday computer.
Apple fans on a budget: The MacBook Neo is the best choice for folks who want the traditional Apple experience — that is, a premium design and a snappy, polished feel — without traditional Apple prices.
K-12 students and other younger users: The Neo can easily handle most basic tasks and comes in fun color options.
MacBook Air or Pro owners who want a backup device: Mashable's tech editor, Timothy Beck Werth, deemed the Neo the ideal "party laptop." That's the laptop you use for "streaming, music, casual browsing, and retail therapy" once you've clocked out of work for the day, he said.

Why we picked this
Even after a $100 price bump this summer, the MacBook Neo is still an unrivaled value in the budget category. Most cheap Windows laptops and Chromebooks have plastic components, but this little guy has the same luxe, all-aluminum build quality as any other MacBook. You miss out on extra fixings like keyboard backlighting, a haptic trackpad, and Touch ID (on the base model, at least), but you still get a pretty Liquid Retina display and great speakers. It's a little thicker than a 13-inch MacBook Air, but it weighs the same.
The Neo's A18 Pro processor is technically a pared-down iPhone chip, but don't be fooled: It's extremely impressive for a budget laptop CPU. In simple single-core workloads (web browsing, word processing), the Neo is just as fast as a last-gen M4 MacBook Air. In such scenarios, it feels snappier than most Windows laptops we've tried lately, including 10 that cost $2,000 or more.
The Neo lasted 14 hours and 50 minutes in our battery life benchmark. It lags behind its M-series peers there — our battery life median for MacBooks was 19 hours before it arrived — but it's not bad for its price range. Our median for most other sub-$1,000 Windows laptops and Chromebooks is just 10.5 hours.

Read this before you buy a Windows laptop
Many great Windows laptops we've tried run on Qualcomm's Snapdragon X and X2 series chips, which use ARM-based architecture instead of standard x86 architecture like Intel and AMD chips. The Zenbook A14 and A16 are two great examples of outstanding ARM laptops.
These Qualcomm chips are great because they offer amazing performance and battery life, but they're not compatible with some specialty software and PC games. Having said that, ARM support is getting better and should dramatically improve in the near future. Right now, I would only avoid ARM laptops if you're a student or if gaming is the main thing you do on your PC. Frequently Asked Questions

That said, if you're a PC gamer or someone who dabbles in creative work (like graphic design or photo and video editing), I recommend defaulting to 1TB of storage with 32GB of RAM to accommodate your heavier workload.
Many budget laptops start with just 256GB of storage and 8GB of RAM, which is workable for casual users who mainly browse the web, write documents, and send emails. Just know that it'll feel pokey if you have too many tabs open and may not be able to run certain software. You'll also probably have to supplement it with an external hard drive eventually, unless you stash most of your files in the cloud.

Secondly, buying refurbished is just plain good for the environment. Every pre-owned laptop purchase keeps a still-usable device out of a landfill.
The key is to make sure any refurbished laptop you buy is in good-enough condition to last a few years and protected by a decent warranty. For more tips, read Mashable's guide to buying refurbished tech.

Mashable has been writing about laptops for over a decade, and I've personally been covering them since 2023. I also helped develop the rigorous hands-on testing process we currently use to review them. This methodology revolves around four criteria:
The laptops we review get put to work as our primary computers. This includes trying any unique software or use cases they support. We also subject all of our loaners to a multi-app/tab stress test and Primate Labs' Geekbench CPU test, which measures their performance in common tasks. Gaming laptops get put through additional graphical benchmarking.
To gauge a laptop's stamina, we conduct a battery rundown test that involves playing a looped 1080p version of "Tears of Steel," a short open-source Blender movie, at 50 percent brightness and 50 percent volume.
