# Intel CEO leads a $30 million bet on a startup aiming to unite hundreds of neoclouds. Read its pitch deck.

Business Insider · Geoff Weiss, Geoff Weiss (gweiss@businessinsider.com) · 2026-10-27

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan, Hyperbolic founder Jasper Zhang, and Walden Catalyst partner Shankar Chandran. Luke Dietz Infrastructure startup Hyperbolic raised a $30 million round led by Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan's VC firm. The startup connects companies with neoclouds and makes their AI chips easier to use. Hyperbolic also wants to let customers resell unused computing power. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan is backing an infrastructure startup that has raised $30 million to help companies rent compute across hundreds of different AI cloud providers. Hyperbolic, an AI chip marketplace and software startup, has raised $50 million to date. Walden Catalyst, the venture capital firm founded by Tan and former Samsung president Young Sohn, led the Series A-1 funding round. Dell Technologies Capital, General Catalyst, Vista Equity Partners, and Cambium Capital also participated. As companies scramble for computing power, one challenge is that AI cloud providers, or neoclouds , use a mix of different chips, networking, and software. Hyperbolic helps customers find compute resources from different providers and creates software that makes it easier to use them all, rather than dealing with each one separately. "It's been a really fragmented market that makes the customer experience really painful," said 31-year-old founder and CEO Jasper Zhang. Hyperbolic also wants to let customers resell compute that they're paying for but no longer need. The four-year-old startup works closely with about 30 neoclouds, including WhiteFiber and Vector Core Compute. It says it has roughly $200 million worth of compute connected to its software annually. Hyperbolic previously raised a $12 million Series A round in 2024, led by Variant and Polychain Capital, following a $7 million seed round earlier that year. The company expects to have 50 employees by the end of the year and will use its funding to expand software tools for managing AI chips across different neoclouds. Zhang said the company is aiming for $150 million in annual recurring revenue by the end of the year and is already breaking even. More than 250,000 developers, including teams at Modal, Higgsfield, and Daytona, use Hyperbolic. Before founding the company, Zhang worked as a quantitative researcher at Citadel Securities. As Wall Street explores ways to financialize AI chips , he said his startup's plans to let companies resell unused compute could help pave the way for a futures market.
