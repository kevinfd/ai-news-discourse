# Is anyone else fresked out by the high possibility of error with AI health analysis and medication prescription?

The New Sentinel · TernoftheShrew · 2026-10-27

It’s one thing to allow AI to scan a document for spelling errors so it can correct them, and another thing entirely to allow it to examine a human being and prescribe medication for them. The State of Utah has taken a distressing step across ethical and regulatory boundaries. It’s the first US State to allow AI to examine patients and prescribe medication for them without any human oversight.
Scan and Plan
This month, a startup company called Nolla Health launched the Nolla Derm App for treating acne. It requires the user to scan their face with their smartphone and submit the image. Afterwards, they’re asked a number of questions about their symptoms, life habits, and so on. Once that happens, the AI model analyzes all the data, and provides the user with a prescription and treatment plan.
The cost for this analysis and treatment is $4.99 per month.
While the average person may think this isn’t a major issue, it sets a disturbing precedent. For one thing, there may be dozens of factors contributing to the person’s breakout. Furthermore, they may not have acne at all, but more serious skin issues that the AI can’t identify.

AI Without Oversight Increases the Probability of Medication Error Injuries
Physicians study medicine for years in order to treat patients with the right medication. Similarly, pharmacists require six to eight years of education to learn all about how different pharmaceuticals interact. The combination of education, experience, human empathetic intuition, and judgment are absolute requirements for treating each individual’s needs. And still, millions of people are seriously damaged or even killed by medical mistakes every year.
This is why the thought of AI health analysis and prescriptions is so terrifying.
Programs like ChatGPT are notorious for making mistakes. Their errors run the gamut from incorrect ingredient measurements in recipes to misidentifying edible vs. toxic wild mushrooms. They certainly can’t seem to understand health-related nuances, nor all the contributing factors that may contribute to a health condition. As such, allowing AI programs to prescribe medication without human oversight is a terrifying proposition.

AI Analysis is Not a Substitute for Real Medical Care
Just because we can do something, that doesn’t mean that we should. While AI may have some helpful applications, it’s not a substitute for medical evaluation by trained professionals.
AI programs appear to have difficulty discerning differences across various human skin tones, for example. This can prevent them from diagnosing issues correctly, let alone prescribing correct medication to treat problems. It essentially reduces human beings to medical experiments, without safeguards of human judgment. This goes beyond compromising safety and puts patients’ lives at real risk.
What happens when and if the AI fails to recognize the dangers of certain drug interactions? Or misidentifies the health condition entirely?
It would be great if we actually listened to the advice of bioethicists, healthcare providers, and scholars about this issue instead of the “trust me, bro” tech oligarchs.
