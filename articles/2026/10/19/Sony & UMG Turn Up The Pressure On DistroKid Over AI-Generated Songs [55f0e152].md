# Sony & UMG Turn Up The Pressure On DistroKid Over AI-Generated Songs

HotNewHipHop · Erika Marie · 2026-10-19

Major record labels want answers on how much AI-generated music reaches streaming platforms, and they're turning to DistroKid for the numbers. Universal Music Group and Sony Music have filed a legal action seeking to compel the independent music distributor to disclose its internal data on AI uploads, particularly recordings created through Suno. The companies argue that the information could help establish whether AI-generated music is taking listeners and revenue away from copyrighted recordings.
According to Digital Music News, the labels filed their motion in Manhattan federal court on October 14 after DistroKid resisted a subpoena issued in September. The dispute ties into their ongoing copyright litigation against Suno, which they accuse of using protected recordings without authorization to develop its AI music-generation technology. DistroKid isn't a defendant in that case, but the labels believe its distribution records could show how widely Suno-generated tracks have entered the streaming market.
The companies are demanding monthly figures showing how many recordings DistroKid distributes and how many its detection software identifies as potentially AI-generated. They also want information about the streaming activity and royalties associated with those recordings. In their filing, the labels called the requested data "highly relevant information that only DistroKid possesses," arguing that the potential market impact is central to their case against Suno.

DistroKid Faces Growing Pressure Over AI-Generated Music
Further, the request comes amid a separate legal battle between Universal Music Group and DistroKid. In September, UMG sued the distributor, accusing it of facilitating copyright infringement by allowing unauthorized recordings and AI-generated content onto streaming services. The complaint cited allegedly infringing material connected to artists including Drake and Rihanna. Sony is not involved in that lawsuit.
The volume of music passing through DistroKid has become another point of contention. According to this latest filing, the distributor delivered nearly 12 million tracks to one unnamed streaming platform between March and September 2026. That exceeded the combined submissions of every other distributor to that service. The figure doesn't show how many of those songs were AI-generated, which is exactly what the labels are seeking.
DistroKid has pushed back against the relevance of the requested information to the Suno case. The company also maintains that its AI-detection technology can produce false positives. This raises questions about how accurately the data could distinguish computer-generated recordings from human-created music. In a statement to Billboard, DistroKid emphasized its position as a third party in the Suno litigation, saying it is "committed to transparency and will continue to cooperate with the legal process."

The legal pressure is already affecting some independent musicians. According to The Verge, DistroKid has removed recordings following UMG's lawsuit. Several artists alleged that DistroKid took down their original music despite them not using AI. DistroKid acknowledged removing a small number of tracks and said it was working to minimize disruptions.
Universal and Sony have proposed an October 30 deadline for DistroKid to produce the requested information, although the court must first decide whether to order compliance. The data could become a key part of the labels' effort to show commercial harm in their case against Suno, especially if it reveals how much AI-generated music is competing for streaming royalties.
