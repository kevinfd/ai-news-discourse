Investment Funding
The AI data center liquid cooling market is attracting capital from both strategic buyers and venture investors. Rising rack densities are pushing operators past what air-based systems can handle. Startup funding has followed. ZutaCore raised USD 100 million Series C in June 2026 for waterless, direct-to-chip, two-phase cooling, with Mitsubishi Electric, Carrier Ventures, and Samsung Ventures participating. Iceotope closed USD 26 million Series B in May 2026 to develop its precision liquid cooling technology. The investor mix is notable. HVAC and electrical majors are backing cooling specialists directly, often ahead of acquiring them.
Revenue Shift
Liquid cooling is moving from a niche add-on to the core thermal architecture for AI racks, and the revenue mix of the largest assets shows it. Boyd Thermal forecasts 2026 sales of USD 1.7 billion, of which USD 1.5 billion is liquid cooling, or roughly 88% of the business. CoolIT is expected to generate about USD 550 million in sales over the next 12 months. Incumbents are also repositioning their portfolios. Vertiv now offers coolant distribution units from 70 kW to 1,350 kW, direct-to-chip cold plate systems, and rear-door heat exchangers. Vertiv's backlog reached USD 15.0 billion at the end of 2025, up 109% year-over-year. That figure covers its whole portfolio, not just liquid cooling.
Mergers and Acquisitions
M&A in the AI data center liquid cooling market is on pace to exceed prior-year volume, with a rising share of deals valued above USD 1 billion as buyers prioritize integrated, liquid-ready thermal platforms. Three mega-deals defined the period: Eaton's acquisition of Boyd Thermal and Ecolab's acquisition of CoolIT Systems, announced within days of each other, alongside Trane Technologies' acquisition of LiquidStack.
