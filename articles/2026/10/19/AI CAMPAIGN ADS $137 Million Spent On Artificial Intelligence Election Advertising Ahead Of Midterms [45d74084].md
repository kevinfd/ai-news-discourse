# AI CAMPAIGN ADS: $137 Million Spent On Artificial Intelligence Election Advertising Ahead Of Midterms

The Yeshiva World · 2026-10-19

American political campaigns and outside organizations have spent approximately $137 million on artificial intelligence-generated election advertisements ahead of November’s midterm elections, with increasingly sophisticated technology being used to create fabricated scenes involving candidates and elected officials.
Research by the Wesleyan Media Project identified 220 AI-generated political advertisements through October 11. An examination of campaign advertising found that candidates from both parties and political action committees have used the technology, sometimes without informing viewers that the footage was artificially generated.
In Georgia, a Republican-aligned super PAC produced advertisements depicting Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff receiving beauty treatments. Ossoff criticized the videos as fabricated political advertising and called for their removal.
In Michigan, advertisements targeting Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed included artificially generated images placing him alongside controversial political figures. The advertisements contained disclosures identifying the use of artificial intelligence.
Political campaigns in New York, Pennsylvania, Texas and Kentucky have also employed the technology. Some advertisements depict candidates making statements they never delivered or participating in events that never occurred.
Federal law does not specifically regulate artificial intelligence-generated political advertisements. Four states prohibit certain uses of election-related deepfakes, while another 27 have enacted various disclosure requirements.
