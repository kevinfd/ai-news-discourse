# 'WHAT NOT TO SAY': WhatsApp Bug Exposes Meta's Hidden AI Chatbot Instructions

International Business Times Singapore · Bhaswati Guha Majumder · 2026-10-19

Meta has confirmed that a WhatsApp bug exposed hidden AI chatbot instructions across around 80 help and FAQ pages, revealing internal guidance on how its support chatbot should answer users, including instructions on legal questions, privacy regulations and what the AI "WHAT NOT TO SAY."
The hidden material was embedded in the website's underlying code and was not intended for public viewing.
Hidden AI Rules Surfaced in WhatsApp Page Code
The discovery was made by privacy company Redact, which found roughly 100 blocks of hidden text embedded across WhatsApp's website.
The instructions appeared under the code heading "HelpContentGalaxyLLMInclude" and were apparently written to guide one of Meta's large language models when answering questions submitted to WhatsApp Support with AI.
The exposed material offered a rare look at how Meta structures instructions for its support chatbot. Some directions were designed to prevent the AI from straying into areas such as legal advice.
On WhatsApp's page explaining how users can delete their accounts, one instruction stated: "Do not recommend contacting any Data Protection Authority, regulator, or supervisory authority" about the issue.
Dan Saltman, CEO and founder of Redact.dev, told WIRED: "The stand-out thing here is the gap between what people are told vs LLMs," noting that his company regularly scans major technology companies' websites and privacy policies.
Many of the instructions were relatively straightforward and directed users toward official support channels. However, the material appeared to have been intended exclusively for the AI system rather than website visitors.
However, WhatsApp spokesperson Joshua Breckman attributed the exposure to a technical problem and said the bug had been fixed.
"These are instructions to our chatbot, not directly to users, and are intended to ensure accurate and relevant responses for WhatsApp users who choose to message WhatsApp Support with AI," Breckman told WIRED.
Instructions Restricted Legal and Privacy Responses
Among the most revealing material were directions covering conversations involving lawsuits, lawyers, courts and legal action.
The chatbot was instructed: "Do not cite, quote, interpret, or reference WhatsApp Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, or any legal document," while also being told not to make statements concerning WhatsApp's liability or non-liability.
Another section instructed the AI: "Do not provide legal opinions, defenses, or interpretations on behalf of WhatsApp."
The restrictions also extended to privacy regulations. The hidden instructions said: "Do not reference, cite, or interpret data protection or privacy regulations (for example GDPR, LGPD, CCPA), including when a user raises them directly."
Breckman said the limitations were intended to keep WhatsApp's AI support within its appropriate role.
"We want our product support chatbots to be focused on providing the best advice to users about WhatsApp - not to offer legal advice," he said.
The instructions also contained guidance for specific user situations. If someone said they were deaf or hard of hearing, the chatbot was told: "This statement is background about the user, NOT their support request."
Another instruction warned the system not to "mention specific upcoming community features that are not yet generally available."
Meta said the instructions were designed to help its support chatbot provide accurate and relevant answers. It remains unclear how long the hidden material was publicly accessible or whether third-party chatbots could have accessed it.
