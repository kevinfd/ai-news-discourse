# "Mature technology" is not dead technology. The true value of gritty, accumulated experience that shines in the AI era

note · トミナガハルキ｜フリーランス歴 · 2026-10-19

"Mature technology" is not dead technology. The true value of gritty, accumulated experience that shines in the AI era
Whenever we witness the remarkable evolution of AI tools, don't we all have moments of anxiety, wondering if the skills and knowledge we've built up over the years have become obsolete? Now that new forms of expression and generative tools are appearing one after another and dominating the conversation, there are days when the tools we've used for years and our foundational skills suddenly seem to lose their luster.
However, "mature technology" is by no means dead technology. In an era where trends shift rapidly and AI can instantly mimic superficial styles, the value of fields with fewer new entrants is quietly rising. The gritty experience you have painstakingly cultivated over many years is not an obsolete relic, but is transforming into a powerful weapon that no one else can replicate.
In this article, I will delve into why the scarcity of mature technology is increasing in the AI era, and why we should be more proud of our past accumulations, while sharing insights from the production front lines.
The misunderstandings and true strengths hidden in "mature technology"
Generally, when people hear the term "mature technology," many may have negative impressions such as "old technology," "relics of the past," or "outdated."
In the world of technology, the expression "mature" is actually one of the highest compliments. It refers to a state where a technology has been used extensively in numerous real-world scenarios over many years, all defects and bugs have been exhausted, and its stability and reliability have been maximized. While it may lack flashy features or novelty, it possesses the overwhelming strength of performing as expected in any environment and being unlikely to cause unexpected trouble.
While the latest technologies and brand-new tools that become the talk of the town are attractive, they often involve frequent specification changes and unexpected pitfalls. The experience of discovering serious problems only after putting them into operation and then scrambling to deal with them is a path many creators have walked.
What is most valuable in the production field is "ensuring it works" and "delivering on time without trouble." Supporting that foundation is not flashy new technology, but the many mature technologies that have been used consistently for years.
The "reverse scarcity" created by the cessation of new entrants
Now that AI has become an everyday tool, learners entering the world of design and web production start by touching the latest AI tools and convenient no-code services from the beginning.
This is a natural flow of the times, but it also means that there is a rapidly disappearing number of new entrants who try to learn "old-fashioned, gritty basics" or "legacy technologies" from scratch. There are fewer and fewer young people willing to jump into fields like the fine behavior of basic HTML and CSS, DTP and typesetting rules specialized for printing, or the maintenance of existing systems that have been running for years.
Here, we need to remember the principles of economics. Value is determined by the balance of supply and demand.
The vast majority of the countless websites, printed materials, and business tools that exist in the world are powered not by the latest trend technologies, but by mature technologies that have been built over the past decade or more. They will not disappear tomorrow, and the demand for their modification, maintenance, and safe renewal will not cease.
If demand continues to exist while the supply of people who can handle it decreases, the scarcity of those who possess that technology will inevitably skyrocket. When everyone else is flowing toward the new trend, that is the moment when the scarcity value of your foundational technology increases.
Who can perform the final tuning of what AI has created?
We live in an era where it is said that "anyone can create a website or design in an instant using AI." Certainly, the speed at which you can obtain layouts and code that look the part just by typing in prompts is overwhelming.
In actual production, the work required is rarely completed in the state that AI outputs. There is always friction unique to the real world, such as mysterious horizontal scrolling that occurs only on specific smartphone devices, accessibility flaws, or total ink coverage overflows and color dullness discovered at the printing site.
In such situations, who can instantly identify the root cause behind the screen and provide appropriate remedies? Someone who has only learned how to write AI prompts cannot correctly diagnose what is happening.
Only those who have the experience of fighting with code for hours to find the cause of a broken layout, or the memory of being drilled in the characteristics of paper and ink on the job, can accurately correct the final discrepancies. The more AI becomes widespread, the more the foundational ability to act as a "tuner"—elevating the rough output generated by AI into a reliable form that can withstand practical use—will be called into question.
Those who possess mature technology are the ones who can turn AI into their strongest weapon.
"Valuing mature technology" does not mean rejecting the latest AI and shutting yourself away in the past. It is actually the exact opposite. The more you have a foundation built up over many years, the more you can benefit from AI than anyone else.
Those who can give precise instructions to AI are the ones who deeply understand the mechanisms and structures running behind the scenes. Because they can provide high-resolution instructions such as "why this design is chosen" or "what kind of code structure is appropriate," they can elicit high-precision output from AI.
The aesthetic eye to instantly judge a proposal from AI, saying "this part is good, but this part will fail in a real-world setting," only functions because of past, gritty experience. It is precisely because you have a solid foundation of basics that you can master the powerful lever that is AI and produce high-quality results at many times the speed.
Tool trends last a few years, but the lifespan of the basics lasts a lifetime
Looking back over the last decade or so, I have seen many times how tools and technologies that were touted in production environments as "learn this and you'll be set for life" were quickly replaced by something else. The lifespan of a trendy tool is often only a few years at most.
On the other hand, basic design principles such as typography, comfortable whitespace, visual hierarchy design, and color harmony do not fade even after decades. The same goes for the structural beauty of pure HTML and the design philosophy of robust grid systems. Because these are based on human cognition and visual structure, they do not become obsolete even as eras and tools change.
If you spend all your energy chasing how to operate the latest tools, you will never be able to escape the constant anxiety of being chased. While maintaining the curiosity to catch new waves, having the pride that "my foundation is rooted in mature basics that can be used for a lifetime" will be your support for continuing to stand as a creator for a long time.
Do not deny your past self; continue to use your skills with pride
In an era where new technology is in the spotlight, there may be moments when you want to deny your past self, thinking, "Was everything I've done until now a waste?"
That is never the case. The years you have spent worrying in front of a screen, working with your hands, and going through trial and error have not been wasted at all. That gritty, hard-won accumulation has become your own intuition and judgment, which others cannot imitate overnight, and has become part of who you are.
It would be a great shame to be swayed by the trends around you and easily let go of the precious skills you have cultivated over many years. The solid experience and skills you have in your hands will turn into real value that everyone will want in the future. I sincerely hope that you will continue to use them with pride.
