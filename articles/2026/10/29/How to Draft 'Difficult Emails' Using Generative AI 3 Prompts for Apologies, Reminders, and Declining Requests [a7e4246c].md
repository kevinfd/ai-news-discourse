# How to Draft 'Difficult Emails' Using Generative AI: 3 Prompts for Apologies, Reminders, and Declining Requests

note · 日々の生活お役立ち情報編集部 · 2026-10-29

How to Draft 'Difficult Emails' Using Generative AI: 3 Prompts for Apologies, Reminders, and Declining Requests
When writing emails at work, aren't the ones that require consideration for the recipient the most difficult?
For example, in situations like the following:
- You want to apologize for a mistake or delay, but you don't know how to convey it.
- You want to send a reminder for a reply or action, but you don't want to seem like you are rushing the other person.
- You want to decline a request, but you don't want to damage your relationship with the other person.
For these types of emails, simply stringing together polite words is not enough. You need text that clearly conveys your intent while remaining considerate of the recipient.
That is where generative AI comes in handy.
By reading this article, you will be able to do the following:
- Get copy-and-paste prompts for three scenarios: apologies, reminders, and declining requests.
- Create drafts in a short amount of time that do not blame the other person or sound cold.
- Know what to check before sending.
You can use these by pasting the prompts (instructions for the generative AI) into the chat box of generative AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Basic usage is introduced in our previous article.
1. Apology Email: Apologize concisely while conveying sincerity
In an apology email, it is important not only to convey your feelings of apology but also to clarify what you are apologizing for and how you will handle the situation moving forward.
On the other hand, if it becomes unnecessarily long or includes expressions that sound like excuses, it can actually make your intent harder to convey.

Key point: In an apology email, do not just end with an apology; if possible, convey future actions and specific schedules as well.
2. Reminder email: Requesting necessary actions while being considerate of the recipient
Depending on how it is written, a reminder email can put strong pressure on the recipient. For example, if you only tell someone who hasn't replied to "please reply quickly," it may give a strong impression.
The important thing is to clarify what you want to confirm and by when you want the response, while not forgetting to be considerate of the other party.

3. Declining Emails: Balancing the Reason for Refusal with Consideration for the Recipient
Emails declining requests or invitations are also often difficult to write. Even if there is no problem with the refusal itself, certain expressions can come across as cold or cause misunderstandings.
When declining, the basic approach is to express gratitude for the request and then clearly state that you cannot fulfill it. If you can offer an alternative, it is also effective to include it.

4. Is it okay to send emails created by generative AI as they are?
So far, I have introduced prompts that can be used for apology, follow-up, and refusal emails. However, avoid sending text created by generative AI without checking it first.
Before inputting
- Check if you have unnecessarily entered personal or confidential information.
Before sending
- Check if there are any errors in the recipient's name or company name.
- Check if the dates and deadlines are correct.
- Check if it contains content that differs from your intentions.
- Check if there are any unnecessarily strong or ambiguous expressions.
- Check if the intent of the apology, follow-up, or refusal is conveyed appropriately.
In particular, it is important to accurately verify the facts, such as the location of responsibility and future actions for apology emails, deadlines for follow-up emails, and the scope of what cannot be accepted for refusal emails.
Generative AI is a tool to help you create text. Please be sure to perform the final check of the content and make the final decision to send it yourself.
5. How to use the three prompts
For each type of email, here are the points you should be particularly mindful of.
- Apology email: The content of the apology and future actions
- Reminder email: The content you want to confirm and the deadline
- Refusal email: The intent to decline and consideration for the recipient
What is common and important is to specifically convey the recipient, purpose, and situation. Rather than just instructing it to 'write a polite email,' organizing and inputting the necessary information makes it easier to get a draft that suits your purpose.
Conclusion
Apology, reminder, and refusal emails are situations in work where you need to be careful about your phrasing. By leaving the drafting to generative AI, you reduce the burden of thinking about the text from scratch, and it also helps in organizing what you want to convey.
