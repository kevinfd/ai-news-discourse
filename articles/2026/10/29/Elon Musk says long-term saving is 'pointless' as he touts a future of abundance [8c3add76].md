# Elon Musk says long-term saving is 'pointless' as he touts a future of abundance

Business Insider · Theron Mohamed, Theron Mohamed (tmohamed@businessinsider.com) · 2026-10-29

Elon Musk is the CEO of Tesla and SpaceX. Apu Gomes/Getty Images Elon Musk says it's "pointless" to save for the long term, as he expects "super intelligent abundance." The Tesla and SpaceX CEO was replying to an X user who said they'd stopped stressing about spending. Musk previously said that if he's right about the future, "saving for retirement will be irrelevant." Elon Musk says it's not worth saving for the long term, as nest eggs won't matter. "Saving money for the distant future is pointless with the advent of super intelligent abundance ," Musk said in an X post on Thursday, doubling down on one of his most polarizing predictions. The Tesla and SpaceX CEO was replying to a user who said they weren't stressing over their "questionable financial decisions" because a future of abundance meant "most of it won't matter in a few years anyway." @battleangelviv Saving money for the distant future is pointless with the advent of super intelligent abundance — Elon Musk (@elonmusk) October 29, 2026 
 The world's richest person — who has a nearly $1 trillion net worth — has previously advised against saving for decades to come. "Don't worry about squirreling money away for retirement in 10 or 20 years," Musk said during a podcast episode released in January. "It won't matter." "If any of the things that we've said are true, saving for retirement will be irrelevant," he added. Musk said at the time that he expects progress in AI, energy, and robotics to supercharge productivity, resulting in plentiful resources so that "anyone can have whatever stuff they want." In that world, he continued, everyone would have access to "better medical care than anyone has today," there would be "no scarcity of goods and services," and "you can learn anything you want about anything for free." Business Insider spoke to seven personal finance and AI experts about those comments, and they all agreed people should keep saving for retirement given the risk that Musk may be wrong. Musk laid out an even more utopian vision on a recent podcast, predicting that advances in robotics and space travel would lead to a multiplanetary economy, at which point "money becomes irrelevant" and "conventional currency will not matter." Several other tech leaders have predicted AI and other technologies will transform the way we live. Microsoft cofounder Bill Gates said on a recent podcast episode that AI will lead to "true abundance" with robots able to "build houses, grow food, do surgery." But he also warned the next two decades will be "as tumultuous a period as there's been in human history," as technological advances will mean "none of the things that human labor provides to the economy will be necessary for most goods and services ." Similarly, Musk has predicted a "bumpy" transition to a brighter future. He's suggested government checks for all as the best solution to AI-fueled job destruction.
