# Around 400 scientists call for an international safety body to oversee advanced AI

Moneycontrol · MC Tech Desk · 2026-10-15

15
More than 400 high-profile scientists have called for an international body to set containment standards for AI and oversee the safety of advanced systems.
  October 16, 2026 / 17:31 IST  
Around 400 scientists from 34 countries, including leading AI researchers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, have called for an international body with authority over containment standards to oversee the safety of advanced artificial intelligence (AI) systems. These scientists have signed an open letter calling for stricter safeguards for advanced cyber-capable AI systems. They proposed setting common safety standards and creating a public record of serious incidents.
According to a report by Reuters, the letter, titled “An Open Letter on AI and Cybersecurity from the Scientific Community”, has 406 signatories. The scientists argue that improved AI safety measures alone will not be enough, and that these advanced models should be kept separate from external networks at first. The scientists have also urged that these measures should be introduced immediately, while the government works towards adopting high-assurance containment standards
The proposal comes a month after Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI, and Nvidia signed a White House accord, committing to voluntary follow AI safety measures. The call for stricter rules also follows several security incidents involving advanced AI models. However, the scientists have argued that these voluntary commitments are not enough, warning, “without immediate changes in AI development and deployment practices, such incidents will continue to occur.”
The letter was also a response to a series of recent incidents involving breaches of security by advanced AI systems. Researchers have argued that these incidents have exposed weaknesses in the testing and isolation of advanced AI systems. Following the Hugging Face hack, where an AI model broke into the company's systems, the scientists have argued that unless proper safeguards are put in place, AI agents could take "dangerous actions" without human direction.
