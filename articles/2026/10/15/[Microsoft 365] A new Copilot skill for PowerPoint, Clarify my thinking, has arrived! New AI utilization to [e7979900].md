# [Microsoft 365] A new Copilot skill for PowerPoint, "Clarify my thinking," has arrived! New AI utilization to deepen your thoughts and improve presentations #0381

note · Shinichi Kawara · 2026-10-15

[Microsoft 365] A new Copilot skill for PowerPoint, "Clarify my thinking," has arrived! New AI utilization to deepen your thoughts and improve presentations #0381
The evolution of Microsoft 365 Copilot is moving from the stage of simply having AI create materials for you to the stage of having AI support the very process of human thinking.
A new feature that symbolizes this change is the new skill in Microsoft 365 Copilot in PowerPoint called "Clarify my thinking".
The skill name translates to something like "Clarify my thinking" in Japanese.
In this article, I will introduce this new skill from the perspectives of:
- What kind of feature it is
- Why such a skill was introduced
- What the benefits are
- How it can be utilized in actual work
.
What is "Clarify my thinking"?
"Clarify my thinking" is a built-in skill added to Microsoft 365 Copilot in the desktop version of PowerPoint. *In my environment, it also works in the browser version.
In Microsoft's announcement, it is described as a skill that helps users clarify their ideas through interaction with Copilot and improve the content before sharing or presenting the presentation.
Specifically, it aims to:
Refine messages
Clarify points
Improve the overall quality of the presentation
.
Also, this feature was called "GrillMe" during the development stage, but it was changed to "Clarify my thinking" as a name that is easier for users to understand.
General availability for the world began on October 8, 2026.
It is targeted at users who can use Microsoft 365 Copilot in the desktop version of PowerPoint, and existing Microsoft 365 Copilot management settings and licensing requirements continue to apply.
How is it different from the previous PowerPoint Copilot?
This is the point I am particularly focusing on with this new feature.
Until now, when people thought of Copilot in PowerPoint,
was the common image of using AI to create a PowerPoint presentation.
However, the direction of "Clarify my thinking" is a bit different.
Before creating a document, or before finalizing a document you have already created,
the focus is on confirming this through interaction with Copilot.
Even on Microsoft Learn, the current Copilot in PowerPoint is described not just as a simple slide generator, but as something that supports the entire presentation lifecycle, from planning and creation to improvement and preparation for delivery. Create effective presentations using AI
Once you understand this direction, it becomes easier to see where "Clarify my thinking" fits in.
Why is it necessary to "clarify your thinking"?
When using PowerPoint for work, the difficult part is not the actual operation of making slides.
What is truly difficult is
deciding "what needs to be communicated"
.
For example, let's say you are going to propose a new system introduction to your boss.
In your head, you have
- current business issues
- reasons for wanting to introduce the system
- the product to be introduced
- Implementation costs
- Implementation benefits
- Schedule
- Risks
- Things you want your boss to decide
and various other information.
However, simply arranging this information in PowerPoint does not necessarily make for a good presentation.
What is important is the
core message.
'Clarify my thinking' can be seen as a skill for using Copilot in situations where you need to clarify the thoughts and messages behind your presentation.

Benefit 2: You can think before you "create" a presentation
One common mistake in creating presentations is starting to make slides right away.
When you open PowerPoint and

"What should the next slide be?"
start thinking about things like this, you inevitably end up thinking in terms of individual slides.
However, originally,
- who you are presenting to
- what you are conveying
- why you are conveying it
- what you want the audience to decide

it is easier to structure a presentation if you organize these points first.

Benefit 3: Can also be used to review a completed presentation
"Clarify my thinking" can be used effectively not only in the planning stage but also in the final stages of preparing a presentation.
Microsoft explains that this skill is intended to sharpen ideas and improve presentations before sharing or presenting them.
For example, after completing your materials, you can use it to check the content with Copilot from the perspective of,
"Will this explanation really get through to the other party?"
Copilot in PowerPoint has traditionally allowed you to summarize presentations and check important slides or action items. Summarize presentations using Copilot in PowerPoint
With the addition of the "Clarify my thinking" skill for organizing thoughts, it seems that using PowerPoint to examine the content of the presentation itself will become even more important.
How can it be used in business?
From here on, based on the purpose of the feature indicated by Microsoft, I will consider how I would use it in my own work.
The following are not officially presented scenarios, but examples of practical application.
1. Proposals to management
For example, suppose you are proposing the introduction of a new business system.
After creating the materials, you can use "Clarify my thinking" to rethink the content.
What I especially want to confirm is,
- why this system is necessary
- what the current problem is
- What will change with the implementation?
- Are you explaining the value of the investment?
- What exactly do you want them to approve?
These are the kinds of points to consider.
Materials created by technical staff often focus too much on explaining 'which products to use' and 'how to build them'.
On the other hand, what decision-makers want to know is, in many cases, 'why it is necessary'.
One idea is to use Copilot as a sounding board to confirm this difference in perspective.
2. Proposal materials for customers
Let's take a Power Platform implementation proposal as an example.
There are many technologies to explain, such as Power Apps, Power Automate, and Dataverse.
However, what the customer wants to know is not just the product features.
For example,
Current issues → Why they are happening → How to improve them → What will change after improvement
A story like this can sometimes be more important.
By using 'Clarify my thinking' to examine your presentation content, you can use it as an opportunity to review for yourself whether the explanation is too product-centric.

This is also a very practical way to use it.
