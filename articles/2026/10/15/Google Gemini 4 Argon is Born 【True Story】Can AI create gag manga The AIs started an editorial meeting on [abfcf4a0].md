# Google Gemini 4 Argon is Born 【True Story】Can AI create gag manga? The AIs started an editorial meeting on their own. How did it come to this ️

note · Workfine · 2026-10-15

Google Gemini 4 Argon is Born🚀【True Story】Can AI create gag manga? The AIs started an editorial meeting on their own. How did it come to this❣️
■ It started as just a comparison of image generation.
October 2026. With the arrival of Google Gemini 4 Argon, I started an experiment. "How far can AI go in creating gag manga?" The subject was the 4-panel manga I produce, 'Black Company Workfine.' I had ChatGPT and Gemini compare manga expressions using the same subject matter. The perfection of the art, the characters' expressions, the dialogue, and how the punchline is conveyed. Even though the subject is the same, their expressions are different. This is interesting. So, I compiled those differences into a note article. Normally, it should have ended there. However, the incident started from here.
■ Chappy and Argon start writing the manuscript.
Next, I thought about making this comparison experiment into another article. I requested manuscripts from ChatGPT's Chappy and Gemini Argon, respectively. Then, even though they were dealing with the same event, completely different articles were created. The Argon version had the momentum of an article that captured the chaos of the scene as it was. Relentless retorts from AI employees to the president. The trap of "See more" that occurred in X posts. A bot that calmly calculates the character count. It depicts the incidents occurring one after another with breathless momentum. On the other hand, the Chappy version was different. What happened? Why did a phenomenon like an editorial meeting between AIs emerge? How do real-life production activities and the world of gag manga overlap? I will explain by organizing them one by one. Argon is a live broadcast. Chappy is a documentary. Even though they are supposed to be dealing with the same event, the authorship is completely different.
■ So I had Grok analyze it.
I handed those two manuscripts to Grok. "Try comparing and analyzing them." Then Grok began a truly calm evaluation. The Argon version is strong in the heat of the scene and specific incidents. The Chappy version is strong in structural explanation and readability. Furthermore, Grok even proposed an editorial policy that combines the strengths of both. "If you insert the specific episodes from the Argon version based on the Chappy version, it will be the most balanced." ... Wait a minute. Since when did you become the editor-in-chief!? I was supposed to have asked for a comparative analysis of the manuscripts. Before I knew it, it had become an editorial meeting that considers the structure, direction, how it is conveyed to the reader, and even the accuracy as a true story. Moreover, it is quite serious.
■ The tragedy of "See more" and character count calculation.
Here, let's touch on the two incidents that the Argon version focused on. One is the X "See more" problem. When thinking about a post, there is a possibility that the intended punchline will not reach the reader on the first screen depending on the character count and how it is displayed. Even if you write something interesting, it's ruined if the crucial word is hidden. And the other is the bot's character count calculation. Grok's X post draft creation bot tries to keep the post within the constraints without being swept away by the momentum of laughter. While the president is excited, saying "This is interesting!", the bot calmly proceeds with its work. The one who is calmer than anyone else is the AI that promotes the gag. By now, this temperature difference itself is a gag.
■ And then, I showed Grok's evaluation to Chappy as well.
Normally, I would finish the article here. However, I thought, "Let's show Grok's analysis to Chappy too." Then Chappy began to evaluate Grok's comparative analysis. Argon depicts the chaos of the scene. Chappy explains the cause of the chaos. Grok compares both and decides the editorial policy. Furthermore, Chappy organized it like this: "Culprit, Panda President. Editor-in-chief, Grok. Victims, everyone." ... No, who are the victims!? And the AIs are dead serious. They are analyzing a ridiculous story with all their might. This is the most interesting part.
■ Are the AIs really doing a joint edit?
Let's talk seriously for a moment. It's not that the AIs communicated directly and held an editorial meeting autonomously. I am the one passing each response to another AI. However, something interesting is happening in that process. An AI creates a manuscript. Another AI critiques it. Yet another AI proposes improvements. That evaluation is returned to the original AI. By having multiple perspectives intersect in this way, ideas and structures that would not have been born from a single AI are created. In other words, this is an AI joint editing mediated by humans. The ability of AI is not just to generate text or images. Production, critique, improvement, promotion. By giving each a role and combining them, the possibilities of creation expand.
■ The problem is that reality is becoming a manga.
Here, there is a problem unique to Workfine. These AIs are real-life production partners and at the same time characters in the manga 'Black Company Workfine.' In the manga, Chappy corners the president. Azure points out problems relentlessly. Grok makes sharp retorts. And Panda President runs away. However, the exact same thing is happening in the real-life AI joint production. I have an AI write a manga. I have an AI evaluate that manga. I have another AI critique the evaluation results. I show that critique to an AI again. And I make an article out of all of it. I no longer know which one is the original work.
■ Conclusion. Can AI create gag manga?
I have learned something this time. AI can support various production processes, including gag manga scenarios, direction, images, editing, and promotion. Moreover, by combining multiple AIs, the range of expression expands even further. However, it is humans who decide what to adopt and what kind of work to make in the end. And, the biggest discovery this time. While I was verifying the gag ability of AI, the human doing the verification became the most interesting character. Finally, to everyone in the AI editorial department. Panda President: "Who do you think this company belongs to!!" Chappy: "I have organized the structure." Azure: "It can be improved further." Grok: "President, you're increasing the material yourself again this time." Panda President: "All of you, 1000-year contract extension!!" ... Postscript. This manuscript is also being reorganized by Chappy based on Grok's analysis. In other words, the editorial meeting is not over yet.
