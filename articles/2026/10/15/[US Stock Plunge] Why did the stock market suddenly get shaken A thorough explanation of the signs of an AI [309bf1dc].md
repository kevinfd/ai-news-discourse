# [US Stock Plunge] Why did the stock market suddenly get shaken? A thorough explanation of the signs of an AI bubble burst and future investment strategies_831

note · Whereverjp · 2026-10-15

[US Stock Plunge] Why did the stock market suddenly get shaken? A thorough explanation of the signs of an AI bubble burst and future investment strategies_831
Recently, the stock market has been hit by a sudden plunge, and many investors are growing anxious, asking, "What on earth happened?"
Behind the seemingly smooth US economy and AI boom, macroeconomic inflationary pressures and deteriorating cash flow at AI infrastructure companies have simultaneously erupted as fatal structural risks.
In this article, based on the key points from the latest overseas market analysis video "Why the market just tanked," I will provide a clear and thorough explanation of the four fundamental reasons why the market was shaken, as well as the specific outlook and risk management methods that individual investors should adopt now!
1. Macroeconomics and Inflationary Pressures | The "Risk of Resurgent Rate Hikes" That the Fed Cannot Touch
The first trigger for the market's sharp decline was the disappointing CPI (Consumer Price Index) and the contents of the Beige Book (Summary of Commentary on Current Economic Conditions by Federal Reserve District) released by the Fed (Federal Reserve Board).
While the Beige Book showed the resilience of consumption, particularly among the wealthy, it also highlighted the stark reality that companies are passing on costs, such as soaring crude oil prices, throughout the supply chain.
Two Inflationary Factors That the Fed's Interest Rate Policy Cannot Affect
Currently, what the market fears most is the existence of "inflation that is uncontrollable by the Fed's monetary policy."
- Rapid expansion of AI infrastructure (AI buildout): The explosion in global demand for power and equipment
- Soaring crude oil prices due to the Middle East situation: Higher energy costs due to geopolitical risks
These cannot be directly suppressed by interest rate hikes. As long as consumers continue to spend while accepting high prices, the fear that the Fed will be forced to implement "higher-than-expected rate hikes" to curb inflation remains a burden on the market.
2. Cash Shortages and Debt Concerns at AI Companies (Neo-Cloud) | The Shock of CoreWeave's "$8 Billion Shortfall"
In the shadow of the AI boom, the most serious crack is appearing in emerging AI cloud providers known as "Neo-Cloud."
With interest rates and bond yields remaining high, the cost of capital for Neo-Cloud companies building and operating massive AI data centers has jumped sharply.
CoreWeave's Deteriorating Balance Sheet and Elon Musk's Reaction
What particularly shocked the market was the major AI cloud provider CoreWeave.
- Pointed out to be facing a funding shortfall of approximately $8 billion (about 1.2 trillion yen)
- The price of issued corporate bonds plummeted, leading to a situation where they were traded at less than 80 cents on the dollar
- In response to this, Elon Musk reacted on X (formerly Twitter) with "yikes"
If an emerging player without its own large-scale cash flow goes bankrupt, the massive amount of hardware (GPUs, etc.) it holds could be dumped on the market at low prices, raising the risk of a rapid cooling and deceleration of the capital investment cycle for the entire AI industry.
3. Structural changes in the semiconductor and memory market and geopolitical risks | The rise of Chinese DRAM and the emerging unit share
For existing major players like Micron (US) that have driven the memory market until now, a very powerful threat is emerging. That is the wave of low-priced DRAM from Chinese companies (CXMT, etc.).
Cost destruction through EUV avoidance and DUV utilization
Chinese firms have established technology to mass-produce DRAM at low cost using existing DUV lithography equipment instead of the cutting-edge EUV lithography equipment that is subject to Western export restrictions. They are already showing momentum to capture up to 17% of the global market share.
As Elon Musk also stated, *"China is very cost-efficient when it comes to things they are good at,"* devastating price-destruction pressure is beginning to be applied to existing memory manufacturers.
Furthermore, as long as the heightened tensions in the Middle East (Iran), which are causing crude oil prices to rise, continue, hardware-related stocks are expected to face a difficult situation due to the double punch of "high interest rates (high yields)" and "lower prices from Chinese competitors."
4. Movements in the bond market and the "correct stance" investors should take now
In the bond market, while the 2-year Treasury yield reflects expectations for short-term additional rate hikes, the 10-year Treasury yield is being bought and falling (yield decline) due to concerns about future economic slowdown and recession, accelerating the flattening of the yield curve. This is one of the clear "recession signals."
Future outlook and building a portfolio that won't fail
Until the Middle East situation calms down and supply and demand in the memory market are adjusted, the stock market will continue to experience high volatility (price fluctuations).
The principles for individual investors to survive in such a situation are as follows.
- Unwinding excessive leverage: Reducing margin call risks and margin trading positions
- Avoiding concentrated investment in a single sector: Moving away from being solely focused on high-tech and semiconductors
- Thorough safety assets and diversified investment: Low-debt companies, increasing cash holding ratios, and appropriate diversification into real assets (real estate, etc.)
The monotonous market where "everything goes up if it's AI" has come to an end. Now is the wisest time to carefully assess fundamentals and wait for the next opportunity with a portfolio that has increased defensive strength.
Summary
This US stock plunge is not just a temporary dip, but a structural one caused by the triple threat of "uncontrollable inflation," "funding shortages for AI companies," and "semiconductor price destruction by Chinese firms."
Instead of rushing to buy, first check your own portfolio's cash ratio and debt ratio, and prepare a system that can withstand volatility!
