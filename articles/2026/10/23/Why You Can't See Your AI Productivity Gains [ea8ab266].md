# Why You Can't See Your AI Productivity Gains

Forbes · David Edwards, Forbes Councils Member · 2026-10-23

David Edwards is co-founder and CEO of Kiplot, an AI-native strategic portfolio management platform for enterprise transformation leaders.
While researching our 2026 State of Portfolio report, different transformation leads asked us the same question multiple times: Is everyone else being told to justify why AI hasn’t made them go faster? One of them runs a portfolio of around $250 million with a team of five. She was clear that AI is helping her work faster, and just as clear that she has no spare capacity.
The distance between feeling faster and showing faster is the problem most large organizations will spend the next few years on.
The Productivity Statistics Have Lagged Before
Robert Solow observed in 1987 that the computer age was visible everywhere except in the productivity statistics. Nearly four decades later, in January 2026, a survey of almost 6,000 executives in the U.S., U.K., Germany and Australia found nine in 10 reporting no effect of AI on their own firm’s productivity. Erik Brynjolfsson’s work on the productivity J-curve helps explain why: Getting returns from a new technology means rebuilding the organization around it, which is expensive, slow and invisible on the balance sheet. The firms that rebuilt captured the returns. The ones that just bought the technology did not.
The New Speed Is Absorbed Before It Reaches The Portfolio
That transformation lead is faster at her own work. But the work arriving has grown faster than she has. But the work arriving has grown faster. AI has cut the cost of putting an idea forward. A business case that used to take someone a week now takes an afternoon, and every one of them still lands on her desk needing a proper answer. What has barely moved is the cost of absorbing change. AI has not made legacy integration, regulatory sign-off or retraining thousands of people cheap. A five-person team standing between all of that and a $250 million budget will feel busier, not freer.
If you liked spreadsheets and shadow IT, you are going to love AI.
The Value Is Hardest To See Where It Is Largest
AI makes repetitive work faster, but on genuinely new work, its bigger contribution is often a better result.
Portfolio reporting is built to measure cost, speed and realized benefits. It can see saved hours. It is much worse at seeing a decision that came out better. Transformation is, by definition, about doing something differently. That means much of AI’s value will show up in better choices rather than faster execution.
Benefits realization gets closest, but it arrives months or years later, too late to improve the next funding decision.
Which is why the transformation lead cannot answer the question she is being asked. She is being asked to show saved hours on work where the gain was never going to arrive as hours.
What To Stop Doing
Begin with the enterprise business case. It exists because building was expensive, so organizations had to decide whether an idea deserved investment before they could make enough of it real to find out. For many ideas, a 10-page business case and a rough working version take about the same effort. But only one tells you whether the idea survives contact with reality. A prototype does not make change cheaper to absorb. It ensures scarce capacity is spent only on ideas that have already proven their worth. Approving a large initiative on the strength of the document alone is the practice that will look worst in five years.
What replaces it is not another stage gate. Discovery used to produce workshops, requirements and conceptual designs. It can now produce enough of the working thing to test the important assumptions before anyone commits serious money. Build in order to decide, rather than deciding and then building.
Most organizations still release money once a year, in amounts sized for a full project. If an idea can be tested in three weeks but has to wait months for the next budget cycle, most of the advantage is lost. Instead, give teams small amounts of money to test ideas quickly, and expect many of them to stop there. Funded openly and on purpose, this kind of experiment is the opposite of shadow IT. A working prototype creates momentum of its own, so stopping something has to be easier than starting it.
So Why Can’t You See Your AI Productivity Gains?
Because they went into more possibilities and better decisions, and neither shows up neatly on a portfolio dashboard. Solow’s paradox didn’t resolve because firms waited, and this one won’t either. You surface them when you stop approving work based on a document and start testing ideas before you fund them. And when you stop asking the people who do your thinking why they are not going faster.
