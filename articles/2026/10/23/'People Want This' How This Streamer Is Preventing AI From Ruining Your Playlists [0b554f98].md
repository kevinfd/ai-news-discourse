# 'People Want This': How This Streamer Is Preventing AI From Ruining Your Playlists

PCMag · James Peckham · 2026-10-23

If you're sick of AI-generated songs popping up on your music-streaming service of choice, Deezer is pushing to become the streamer that's best at tackling the scourge of artificial tunes.
In early October, Deezer announced the rollout of a toggle that would let subscribers filter out all AI-generated music across the platform, including in search. It comes about a year after Deezer began flagging tracks as AI-generated and removing them from playlists and recommendations.
We spoke to Deezer’s head of research and development, Manuel Moussallam, who spearheaded the effort to tackle AI music as the floodgates opened for millions of new, low-effort uploads. In April, Deezer revealed that nearly 75,000 AI-generated tracks are uploaded to the service each day, which is around 2 million new tracks per month, or 44% of all uploads. By July, AI music surpassed 50% of new music uploads on Deezer for the first time.
Moussallam says Deezer wanted to inform users about AI-generated music; he now believes they understand it well enough to decide whether to turn it off.
"The toggle is how we make the decision explicit; it becomes an action," Moussallam said. "I think it would have been very hard to have done that right away, before people knew about the extent of the phenomenon and had gotten accustomed to having access to the information. The public is much more informed now than it was a year and a half ago."
What Is Deezer Doing Differently?
Deezer has found 42 million AI-generated tracks and counting, and that's turned into a new line of business as it shares its patent-pending detection tech with music partners.
"Our tool is based on the presence of AI artifacts in the signal,” Moussallam said. “If those AI artifacts are full enough, then it's a very strong indicator that the majority of the signal has been created with a neural network.”
The tool can’t tell whether someone used generative AI for other parts of the production process, such as ideation or generating musical note ideas, but it can tell whether the track itself is using purely synthetic audio.
“Was it completely generated? Was it partially? Was it a mix of human-machine interaction? We can't always know, but at least we can tell you that AI has been used extensively to generate this piece,” says Moussallam.
Another concern for artists includes false positives from detection tech, which may flag a real artist who hasn’t used AI, leading to a drop in streams. “If you ask a generative network to generate silence, it will be really bad," Moussallam says. "It will just produce artifacts, and those will be 100% of your signal. If you ask it to generate random noise, then it will generate random noise, and those artifacts will be low.
“Everything in between is extremely complex and is not something we control," he adds. "We have tuned our detector to be specific and avoid, as much as possible, false positives.”
'What's Important Is Being Transparent'
Deezer has yet to detail how its new filter will appear for users, but it says it will be available before the end of the year. It’s intended as a one-time switch. “Everything that is completely AI-generated, where there is no doubt, this is what we have concentrated on, and that's the bulk of what we're talking about," Moussallam says.
He notes there are niche scenarios where Deezer has yet to figure out a path forward. For example, if a popular human artist were to use AI-generation tools on tracks at a later date, would their entire catalog be removed from the service? If not, would users feel short-changed if some albums had songs removed for their use of AI?
Deezer is working through those scenarios, noting to PCMag that the tool's exact specification is still in development. “It’s not the same to remove tracks from search results, compared with recommendations," Moussallam says. "What's important is being transparent with our users about what’s filtered and what's not.”
The Biggest Change to AI Music This Year
Despite the flood of AI-generated tracks this year, Deezer has seen a slight slowdown in recent weeks, which Moussallam attributes to "the latest move by Suno."
Suno allows users to generate music, lyrics, and vocals from text prompts. Starting Sept. 3, 2026, Suno restricted the number of tracks you can download for free to seven tracks per account. A $10-per-month Pro subscription gets you 20 downloads, and a $30 Premier account bumps that up to 60.
“It changes the amount of completely automated content you can create with Suno," Moussallam says. "Now we're trying to see whether it will be replaced by other models and services, or if it'll plateau at that level. We don’t know yet, but the dynamic has changed in the past few weeks. It’s an interesting moment.”
Suno and rival platform Udio have also faced numerous lawsuits from music labels; a year ago, Universal Music Group (UMG) settled its case against Udio, which included new license agreements for recorded music and publishing. UMG's case against Suno is ongoing.
AI Floods and Fraud: 'Two Sides of the Same Coin'
While monitoring AI music uploads is important for Deezer’s customers, it also poses a business risk. “From the very beginning, it was also clear to us that AI floods and fraud were two sides of the same coin," Moussallam says.
Fraud in this context involves uploading tracks to a streaming service and then using bot-like technologies to stream the music and earn royalties. If you create enough tracks, it can be harder for services like Deezer to spot bot-like activity and crack down on fraudulent behavior.
Deezer reports that AI-generated music accounts for 1-3% of its total listenership, and many of those tracks are created specifically for fraudulent streams. It is believed that 85% of streams of fully AI-generated tracks in 2025 were connected with fraud.
The influx of AI music made "everything more complicated," Moussallam notes, especially in the early days, when Deezer systems had to sift through more tracks to determine whether they were suitable for curation.
“It doubles the size of the catalog, making everything four times harder," Moussallam says. "The same goes for filtering, storage, network costs, and everything else. It creates a whole lot of issues at various levels of our R&D and operating pipelines.”
Will a Major Streamer Ever Remove All AI Music?
The new toggle may bring more AI-skeptical listeners to Deezer, as rivals like Spotify, Apple Music, and others are slower to roll out restrictions.
This year, Spotify added a new verification badge to show listeners when an artist is human and pledged to label artists' profiles whose public identity, name, and imagery “represent photorealistic AI-generated identities." But it's also embraced AI-generated personal podcasts, and its AI-infused 2024 Wrapped was widely panned.
Apple Music is expected to start labeling AI-generated songs later this year.
Could one of these services ever ban AI music entirely? Moussallam wouldn’t speak for Deezer on the whole, but he says it's difficult to predict what the future holds for AI-generated music.
“In my opinion, it's hard to know where the industry is headed. Right now, we need these filters because there are still a lot of unresolved issues. Ethical issues and copyright issues, so this knowledge is important because there is basically no good operating point for this technology.
“It creates a lot of uncertainty and a lot of frustration for our users, so they need to have this level of control, and they want to have this information," he adds.
