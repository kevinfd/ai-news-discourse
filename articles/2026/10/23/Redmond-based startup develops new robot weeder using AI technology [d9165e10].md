# Redmond-based startup develops new robot weeder using AI technology

KUOW · Ruby de Luna · 2026-10-23

A Redmond-based startup has developed a solar-powered weeder using AI technology to help distinguish weed from crop.
The weeder, known as the Element Gen 2, looks like a drafting table on wheels. The top surface is a solar panel that powers the machine. Underneath it are four metal arms that pluck the weeds and fling them behind it as it rolls through a farm plot.
Richard Wurden, cofounder of Aigen, the company behind the weeder, said the conventional method for weeding low-cost crops often involves spraying herbicides.
“We wanted to figure out how do we do that without diesel, and to do that without spraying chemicals,” Wurden said.
That’s significant because one of the challenges in weed control is that weeds are adaptable.
Rui Liu, assistant professor of weed science at Washington State University, said repeated use of herbicides can lead to resistant strains of weeds, making the chemicals ineffective.
“As a result, growers need diverse management strategies to maintain long- term weed control,” Liu said.
For Aigen founders, the pursuit of non-toxic weed control was personal. Wurden has Type 1 diabetes. Co-founder Kenny Lee has non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Both diseases have been linked to pesticide use.
Aigen isn’t the only start-up to develop technology for farming.
Seattle-based Carbon Robotics uses AI detection and laser weeding technology. But many companies still rely on diesel to power their equipment.
“That’s why we took the most efficient possible approach where you don’t need to refill something,” Wurden said. “When somebody goes and refills it and brings a truck out there, is it really autonomous? Solar is actually autonomous.”
With rising fuel prices, Wurden said they’ve received more than 30 orders for their solar-powered models.
