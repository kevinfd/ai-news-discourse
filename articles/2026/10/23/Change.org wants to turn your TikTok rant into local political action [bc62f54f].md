# Change.org wants to turn your TikTok rant into local political action

Fast Company · Adele Peters · 2026-10-23

Most Americans—more than 80%—don’t think that their elected officials care what they have to say. Change.org has been rebuilding its own platform to try to change that.
The platform, founded in 2007, is best known for campaigns that have gone viral nationally, like a petition to get justice for George Floyd in 2020 that got nearly 20 million signatures. Of course, most petitions don’t have that kind of audience. But you don’t need millions of people to make change happen, and over the last two years, Change.org has rebuilt its tools to connect people with decision-makers in their own community.
“The core problem we’re trying to solve is that people just feel unheard,” says Steve Dziedzic, who was named the organization’s new president today. “They don’t think their voice matters anymore in our democracy.”
The site now has “an explicit local focus,” says Dziedzic, who has been leading the platform’s redesign, previously as chief product officer. “That’s a big shift for the organization.” There are practical reasons for this: People care most about local issues, and local petitions are also the most likely to succeed. Even if someone wants to work on a national problem like gun control, he says, it makes the most sense to start with a local law and then expand.
When someone comes to the platform now, it helps shape the message to have the best chance of persuading a local decision-maker to take action. An AI “petition writer” tool on the site starts by asking someone for their story. It helps combine that with a logical argument for the mayor or whoever else you’re asking for change. (Dziedzic, who previously founded a couples’ therapy app that was acquired by Talkspace, says that writing a good petition has similarities with couples’ therapy; it helps to be able to dispassionately talk about what you need.) The company is investing around $100 million to rebuild its platform and add AI.
If someone wants their city to build a bike lane, for example, and they’re focusing on safety and sustainability, the tool could potentially also suggest economic arguments to help convince a mayor that taking away parking spaces won’t harm local businesses.
If someone already posted a rant on TikTok, they can paste a link to that video and the tool will use it to automatically draft a petition. TikTok or Instagram aren’t good places to reach local officials, Dziedzic says, because a mayor can’t tell how many of the people liking a post are actually constituents; Change.org now uses addresses to verify constituents, so city officials get an important signal of when they need to be paying attention. The company also uses a tool to scan social media for complaints that could turn into successful petitions, and the team reaches out to invite new users to participate.
