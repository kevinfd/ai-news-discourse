# Wait, the average Muse agent purchase is over $200? Tell me what you're buying!

Business Insider · Katie Notopoulos, Katie Notopoulos (knotopoulos@businessinsider.com) · 2026-10-23

Meta Muse, hard at work at spending your money. Smith Collection/Gado/Getty Images Meta says that over a quarter of Muse users made purchases with the AI agent. The average order was over $200 — that's a lot of clams! If this is you, please tell me what you're using it for. Something just blew my mind, and I'm asking for your help to figure it out. According to Meta's Q3 earnings call on Wednesday , more than a quarter of Muse users so far have used the AI agent make purchases — and the average order size is over $200. I'll say that again: average order size over $200. This is shocking to me, and I'm someone who has been actively and happily using Muse for a variety of things. I've been perfectly happy to give Muse access to my email, my calendar, my credit cards, and even my Apple health data. But I've been slightly reticent about using it to actually buy stuff. Not that I'm totally worried about it getting stuff wrong (although the one purchase I did try to make with it was to order a salad for lunch , and it attempted to place the order on my HSA card after I failed to recognize the last 4 digits of that card, oops). I think it's more I enjoy shopping online myself? I mean, for most things, I suppose. For "fun" stuff like clothes or personal items, I enjoy browsing and the hit of dopamine when you check out. (Others take this to the extreme, using " dopamine sites " that let you browse fake restaurants and "order" food that never arrives.) The other night I ordered — on a real site — cotton balls, razor blade refills, a plain white t-shirt, and a pair of scissors from the Target app while watching TV and even that boring purchase with a total under $30 gave me a small thrill. Why would I want to cede that joy to AI? I understand, however, that not all purchases are fun . My colleague, Peter Kafka, used Muse to help him place an order for a new washing machine — the kind of purchase that is difficult to decide on, not particularly fun or exciting to make, and fairly expensive. Things like plane tickets, washing machines, or car insurance are in that category of stuff you have to buy, cost a lot of money, are fairly complicated to decide which option to buy, but bring very little joy during checkout. I can understand why you might want to hand that off to an AI agent. If you are using Muse to place big $$$ orders, what the heck are you buying? My best guess here is that people are using Muse for airplane flights and hotels — the kind of big dollar items that are kind of annoying to book yourself, and you care mainly about just booking the right dates.
