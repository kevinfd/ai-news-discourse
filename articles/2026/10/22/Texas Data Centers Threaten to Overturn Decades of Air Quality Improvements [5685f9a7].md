# Texas Data Centers Threaten to Overturn Decades of Air Quality Improvements

PCMag · Jon Martindale · 2026-10-22

Data center construction is bad news for the environment in Texas, according to a new report from the Houston Advanced Research Center (HARC), which says the state's power and industrial sectors could see pollutants return to levels not observed since the early 2000s.
Data centers consume an enormous amount of power, and in many cases, developers source it wherever they can. Often, that has meant installing "mobile" gas turbines on site, which circumvents environmental legislation and impact reporting.
If data center demand is met by a projected mix of grid sources, fine Nitrous Oxide (NOx) particles, which are known to cause smog, could increase by over 135%, rising to levels not seen since 2004, HARC says. Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) would rise dramatically, exceeding their 2002 levels by more than 30%. Other pollutants, such as PM2.5 particles and sulfur dioxide levels, would also increase to their early-2000s levels.
This is a warning, not a definite scenario. The report highlights that many data center projects may not be built at all, and developers could opt for a mix of renewable and grid energy, with on-site capture systems for carbon, VOC, and NOX. In a more optimistic scenario, NOX emissions could be 10 times lower.
This is bad for local residents, but also erases decades of progress. The report notes that controlling vehicle tailpipe emissions, switching to cleaner fuels, phasing out coal, and reducing power plant emissions have all contributed to lowering toxic pollutant levels in our atmosphere, improving local air quality for everyone.
“Our analysis is cause for concern. There is a good chance that our children and grandchildren will breathe dirtier air than we did,” HARC CEO John Hall said in a statement. “The transportations sector, the state, and other stakeholders have been working for decades to successfully bring these numbers down, and that progress is now at risk.”
The report ultimately concludes that there is no scenario in which atmospheric pollutant levels don't rise. Data center construction will demand more power, and there's no way it can be supplied entirely by renewables. However, the impact can be dramatically lessened if developers choose to make the more environmentally conscious decision around power sources.
Data center protests have led to many being delayed, and moratoriums are in place across the country. That may be the easier solution than convincing developers to opt for slower, more costly power solutions, even if they do help keep the air clean.
