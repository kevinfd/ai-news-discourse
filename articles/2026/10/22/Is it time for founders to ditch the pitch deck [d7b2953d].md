# Is it time for founders to ditch the pitch deck?

Fast Company · Leslie Feinzaig · 2026-10-22

Last month (actually it was August—I’ve been busy), my desperate plea to founders to please stop sending AI slop decks went viral. I wrote it because my Monday-morning pitch deck review routine had gone from exciting to neutral to mildly annoying to downright soul-sucking in the course of a year. Decks used to be like a bag of Bertie Botts Every-Flavored Beans—some blah, some gross, some absolutely delightful. Today, it’s all vanilla and completely indistinguishable.
I heard from a lot of you in response.
Fellow investors adamantly agreed. Most VCs can tell when a deck is AI-generated, and are equally annoyed at the sameness. The deeper issue isn’t about the deck itself—everyone I heard from has the same allergic reaction when it’s clear that AI didn’t just design it—it did the thinking, too.
Investors weren’t alone in this—anyone who has to sift through inbound pitches for a living is in the same camp. In particular, journalists and editors in my network report that their inboxes have exploded with PR pitch slop, at an exponentially higher volume than VCs. As a VC and contributor, my inbox and I concur.
The one group I didn’t hear much from this time are founders . . . at least not in public.
I think the advice hit hard. And I get it. You hate making decks. You think they’re a time-sucking exercise in futility. You spend hundreds of hours perfecting the damn thing only for some VC to have its agent summarize it and, at best, spend 20 seconds reading it. I raise capital for my fund too—so believe you me, I hate my pitch deck too. Maybe it’s time to ditch them altogether.
Is anyone still reading decks?
One founder in particular reached out, and their comment made me pause. “What’s the point?” they said. “If VCs are using AI to review them, why shouldn’t I use AI to write them?”
