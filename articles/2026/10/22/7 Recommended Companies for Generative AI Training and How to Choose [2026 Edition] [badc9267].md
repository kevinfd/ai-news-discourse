When planning generative AI training internally, many managers stumble at the same point. They have learned how to use the screens of ChatGPT and Copilot. They have heard tips on prompting. Yet, when they return to their work the following week, they do not know which tasks to apply it to, and their hands stop—.
There is only one answer in this article to the question, "Which companies are recommended for generative AI training?" What determines the success or failure of training is not the number of courses or brand recognition, but deciding "who, what, and with what subject matter" to teach first. The type of company you should choose changes depending on whether you want to raise the level of the entire company, train people to use it in the field, or train people who can build AI systems.
In this article, we have carefully selected 7 companies that offer options for generative AI training, ensuring that the types of training methods and development approaches do not overlap. These 7 distinct companies cover: practical training with demonstrations and assignments / course catalog type / flat-rate e-learning type / reskilling package type utilizing subsidies / assessment-based type / specialized AI agent construction courses + hands-on support type / and certification exam type.
<a id="partner-importance"></a>
The Importance of Choosing a Generative AI Training Partner
Reasons Why Training Often Stops at "Just Attending"
Compared to training for other IT tools, generative AI training is a field where differences are more likely to emerge "after it is over." This is because, unlike spreadsheet software, there are no fixed rules on "this function is used for this task," and participants must think for themselves about which tasks to apply it to.
If the subject matter handled in the training consists only of fictional examples, participants often stop at the stage of applying it to their own work, even if they remember how to operate it. In addition, generative AI has inherent risks such as the handling of personal information and the output of incorrect information. If you do not cover "how far it is safe to use" during the training, people will be hesitant to use it in the field.
Things to Organize In-House Before Ordering
- Target Audience: All employees, specific departments, or promotion managers?
- Goal: Will they experience basic operations, learn to apply it to their own work, or learn to build systems using AI?
- Tools to Use: ChatGPT, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Gemini, etc., that are approved for use within the company.
- Subject Matter: Is off-the-shelf training sufficient, or do you want to use your company's own work and materials as the subject?
- Format and Time: In-person or online, half-day or multi-day, or individual learning via video?
- After Training: Do you want to consult on integration into work processes or systemization?
In particular, "subject matter" and "after training" are directly linked to choosing a company. Within the same company, there are both groups for whom off-the-shelf courses are sufficient and groups who will not start using it unless their own work is used as the subject. If you decide which group the training is aimed at before comparing, the candidates will naturally narrow down.

Irwin&co Inc. | Practical training using your own business tasks as examples and courses for beginners
Irwin&co Inc. is a company based in Maruyamacho, Shibuya, Tokyo, that provides system development, consulting, and lectures related to generative AI. Its main clients are small and medium-sized enterprises without dedicated IT staff, and it receives consultations from industries with many on-site documents and operations, mainly real estate, as well as construction, manufacturing, food, insurance, and telecommunications infrastructure. The company conducts both practical training for corporate departments and beginner courses for the general public.
Features and Strengths
The core is a format where the instructor demonstrates operations on an actual screen and participants reproduce the tasks. Rather than explaining functions with slides, we show right in front of you what happens when you provide certain inputs to generative AI and what it returns. In corporate training, we use consultations that are likely to occur in that specific department as tasks, and participants work through them from their own perspective.
We adopt this format because generative AI training often stops at "I understand how to operate it, but I don't know how to apply it to my work." By tailoring the tasks to the participants' work, the training time itself becomes practice for application to their jobs. At the same time, we answer questions about the strengths and weaknesses of generative AI and the expectations participants often have (e.g., can I create a clone of myself, does it make mistakes, etc.) during the training.
We also always convey points of caution for safe use in our courses. These are four points: do not input personal or payment information, do not take outputs at face value and verify sources, use temporary chats, and consult experts for high-risk content such as medical or investment advice.
Another difference is that Irwin&co is a development company, not a training company. Because the same team can handle both the process of deciding "which process to apply AI to" and the process of "actually building it," we can directly connect the work identified during training to consultations for business improvement or systemization.
Areas of Expertise and Track Record
For corporate clients, we conducted "AI Era Development Practice Training" for the planning department of a human resources service company. We covered the differences between the conventional approach of finalizing requirements and building everything at once versus an iterative approach starting with mocks, the concept of "releasing at 60% quality," and using demos as a common language, demonstrating the development process in a 30-minute live session. For the exercises, we set up a scenario where the participant, as a member of the planning department, receives a consultation from the sales manager, and prepared three tasks: follow-up omissions for business negotiations, creating proposal materials, and missing information during interviews.
For the general public, we have been in charge of multiple courses and lectures for residents hosted by consumer affairs centers and organizations of local governments, ranging from beginner to practical levels. For beginners, we cover what generative AI is, how to view the ChatGPT screen, creating images from photos, creating travel plans, voice input, drafting apologies, and comparing pricing plans. In the practical sessions, we advance to creating Excel functions, methods for creating and pasting tables in TSV format, macro examples, creating emails from business card images, summarizing PDFs, methods for converting HTML slides to PDFs, image translation, and custom GPTs.
As an example of moving from training to business improvement, we supported the general affairs department of a major construction company. After first conducting training sessions (Generative AI basics / PowerPoint / Excel. Focused on demonstrations), we took inventory of all 122 tasks, narrowed them down to 23, and led to a reduction of approximately 373 hours (20.3%) out of approximately 1,842 annual hours for the 23 target tasks (published on PR TIMES, company name withheld). When systemizing tasks discovered during training, we provide a rough estimate with about a 3-minute interview as AI contract development, and show a working demo before the contract. No costs are incurred until the confirmation of the demo and the presentation of the rough estimate (announcement of service release). We also support the use of subsidies for AI development.

Points for Choosing a Generative AI Training Partner
Do they use your company's work as a topic and show it through demonstrations?
What participants stumble over in generative AI training is not the operation, but "application to their own work." If the instructor shows the operation on an actual screen and uses consultations that might occur in the participant's department as topics to reproduce, the training time becomes practice for application. When receiving a proposal, check how much the exercise topics can be tailored to your company's work and how much time is spent on demonstrations.
Do they cover information handling and the "scope of permitted use" during the training?
Do not input personal or payment information, do not take outputs at face value, verify sources, and consult experts for high-risk areas like medicine or investment. If these precautions are not included in the training, staff will remain hesitant and stuck, unsure if they are allowed to use the tools. If your company has internal AI usage rules, it is also reassuring to check that the training content aligns with them.
Does the combination of number of participants, format, and cost suit your company?
Whether it is an open course for individuals, e-learning for the entire company, a package eligible for subsidies, or a corporate package with a minimum participant requirement, the cost units and prerequisites vary by format. It is important to compare training duration and learning objectives in the same table rather than just looking at the cost per person. If you plan to use subsidies, check in advance whether you can meet requirements such as training hours.
Can you consult on integrating AI into your operations after the training?
Even if you identify tasks that could be handled by AI during training, the training will end as just a training session if there is no one to help you take the next steps. Deciding at the time of ordering the training whether you can consult the same provider for business process analysis and systemization, or how to handle the handover to another support provider, makes it easier to translate training results into actual business improvements.
