# Apple Home Event Rumor Roundup: Everything We Expect to See Tomorrow

CNET · Tyler Lacoma · 2026-10-09

Coming only a few short weeks after Apple’s big fall event where the company announced its first foldable iPhone Duo, Apple is holding another event tomorrow called “Welcome Home.” Instead of focusing on iPhones and Macs, this event will be announcing Apple’s home tech, including what we expect to be all-new devices and new ways to use Siri AI.
CNET will be at the event to give you live updates and get hands-on with Apple’s plans for your home, but for now here’s what the rumors tell us. If reports are right, Apple may be releasing a new kind of smart display, an update to the HomePod line, an Apple TV and more. Let’s take a look.
A Siri smart display, possibly the “HomeView”
For more than a year, Apple has been rumored to be readying a Siri-focused smart display hub for the home. Now we have specific reports from Mark Gurman at Bloomberg that say Apple’s event will focus on the display, which is possibly called the HomeView and will ship sometime in November. This device is ruored to have an A18 chip and come in two designs, one for tables and one for wall mounting.
More from CNET
Smart displays can add a lot of functionality to smart speakers, if you don’t mind using voice assistants. Smart touchscreens are great for getting visual information, watching videos or viewing to-do lists, so a display like this is likely to have a bit of Apple TV and Safari in it, too. While the Apple TV 4K, iPad and current HomePod models can already act as HomeKit hubs, I expect Apple to offer a real UI and UX experience designed for smart displays, making it a stronger argument for having in your home alongside other devices.
However, a Siri smart display faces steep competition against established brands like Amazon’s Echo Shows and Google’s Nest Hubs, so Apple will need to carefully differentiate this “HomeView.”
Is it likely? I think it’s very likely, based on how many insider reports have mentioned this smart display, but I don’t think we have a good idea exactly how it will look and operate yet. Watch for more news on this tomorrow.
Updates to the HomePod line
Along with a smart display, rumors are flying that Apple will give its HomePod smart speaker line the first major update in years. It’s the ideal time to update the HomePod line, too, although it’s possible some of these reports have confused plans for a smart display with a new smart speaker, both of which could fall under the HomePod label.
If a new HomePod speaker is on the horizon, reports indicate that it would be a new type of HomePod Mini. That’s not surprising — the original HomePod has often been critiqued for being over $300, which is why Apple focused on the less expensive Mini model afterward. One welcome leak I’ve seen is that there will be new colors for the HomePod Mini 2, including green and pink, and a faster S-series chip.
Is it likely? Yes, I’d say this is about as likely as a Siri smart display, if Apple wants to continue developing the HomePod line. Hopefully we’ll be able to hear how it sounds in person if Apple is ready to unleash a new smart speaker.
A new Apple TV
The same reports that say a smart display and new HomePod are on the way also mention Apple is planning to release a new Apple TV model. This model is rumored to have upgraded specs, with a faster N1 processor, 8GB RAM, Wi-Fi 7 and an upgraded Siri remote, possibly with more AI features.
It’s a product line that’s sort of connected to the smart home, especially if Apple is launching a smart display. The company hasn’t released a new set-top box since 2022, so it’s about time for another. This could also give the Apple TV new hardware for extra Siri AI tricks. There’s also been some speculation that this will be compatible with the new HomePods, allowing you to set up multiple units in a surround sound-style home cinema.
Is it likely? I think it’s strange that Apple would combine smart home products with a TV announcement, as they’re not especially related. But the reports we have about a new Apple TV are very specific, so it looks like there’s a strong chance Apple will update the line, although we don’t know if we’ll be able to get hands-on with new models.
Little Apple security cams — with no recording
Recent rumors says Apple is ready to enter the home security world with its own products. Although we don’t know much, reports from Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman say these will be tiny cameras that, interestingly, won’t record any video. No recording means Apple won’t have to worry about cloud storage and may be able to avoid charging extra subscriptions for them.
The cameras are rumored to have some kind of presence-sensing or human-recognizing technology as well as AI video descriptions, suggesting Apple Home will be running analysis on the video even if it won’t record permanently. The phrase “facial recognition” was also mentioned, but this doesn’t seem nearly as likely to me. This may be related to a rumor about Apple’s Ring-like doorbell camera, which may come with Face ID for unlocking a door, but this would likely be separate from the non-recording indoor security cameras.
Is it likely? I’m betting on some kind of home security announcement, and I expect Apple would like to have its own camera line, but I don’t know if this exact product will be released. However, the extra details do lend this rumor legitimacy.
An LG collab for a new thermostat
Apple may be partnering with LG for some smart home devices, and there are even reportedly leaked product concept photos circulating on the internet. One of the biggest potential releases is an Apple-friendly thermostat. The photos show a round, Nest-like thermostat dial, and rumors say it will include far-sight presence sensing, a humidity sensor and several temperature calibration options.
Alongside the thermostat, the Apple/LG combo is expected to release a sensor that can measure the temperature at specific locations and feed that information back to the thermostat hub. That would be no surprise, because models from Nest, Ecobee and others have similar satellite sensors, although Apple’s may have adhesive strips to attach its version to the wall.
Apple partnered with LG years ago to support Apple-friendly security cameras, but those lines were rarely updated and quickly became obsolete. Maybe Apple is ready to try again with a different approach.
Is it likely? If Apple really is partnering with LG for a new wave of home devices, I think a thermostat is likely. But this is starting to be a lot of products to announce at a single October event that won’t be publicly streamed.
Further LG collabs for smart lock and maybe more
The other big rumor centering around an LG collaboration is an Apple smart lock. While many smart locks work with Apple and Apple Home Keys, the tech giant hasn’t been involved in the development of a smart lock before.
It could have automatic unlocking, tapping your phone to unlock and a variety of keypad management options. That’s not surprising, but the rumored product images show a round, twist-to-unlock, light-up outdoor keypad, and I haven’t seen anything like that before.
If Apple does release a smart lock, Ring will have beaten it to the punch with its own announcement of its first-ever lock, which will include Alexa compatibility and a unique thumbturn that can generate a charge when the battery dies.
Other rumors and purported product images suggest the LG partnership could also yield a video doorbell, floodlight camera, outdoor camera and indoor camera. These seem to be LG-branded devices, however, and I can’t imagine Apple releasing products that look like this.
Is it likely? Now this is a lot of new gadgets to announce at once. I think a smart lock is about as likely as a smart thermostat; those are both strong fields for Apple to enter. For the rest of the cameras, I’m doubtful. The indoor camera could be connected to the rumors of Apple’s no-video model. If the LG partnership line is this large, I wouldn’t be surprised if Apple is saving it for a separate announcement later on.
What role will Apple Intelligence play in all this?
Apple Intelligence will fuel many Siri AI capabilities in the Apple Home app. That’s especially true of AI video descriptions, which Apple is bringing to supported security cameras (there are very few right now, but some of Aqara’s camera hubs are compatible). I’m testing these uses of Apple Intelligence now, but if Apple releases any of its own security cameras, you can bet they’ll use AI features in the same way.
Will there be any iPad news at this event?
There are rumors that Apple will announce an iPad Mini sometime this month, but it probably doesn’t have much to do with this event. This is a strictly smart home-focused release.
