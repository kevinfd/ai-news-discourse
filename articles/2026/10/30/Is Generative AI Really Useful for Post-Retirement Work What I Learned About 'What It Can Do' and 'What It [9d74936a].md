# Is Generative AI Really Useful for Post-Retirement Work? What I Learned About 'What It Can Do' and 'What It Cannot Do'

note · Suzukan · 2026-10-30

Is Generative AI Really Useful for Post-Retirement Work? What I Learned About 'What It Can Do' and 'What It Cannot Do'
I want to keep working after retirement. However, continuing to work in the same way as before is difficult.
There are limits to physical strength. Long commutes also become a burden. There is also a need to acquire new skills.
For such seniors, generative AI can become a reliable ally.
Writing text. Researching materials. Translating English. Brainstorming work ideas. AI can support tasks that used to take time in a short period.
However, there is one question here.
If seniors become able to use generative AI, will they really be able to increase their work and earn an income?
I believe that generative AI has the potential to significantly change how seniors work.
However, being able to use AI and being able to earn compensation for work are separate issues.
I would like to consider that difference through actual work.

1. Will AI take away jobs, or will it expand them?
As generative AI becomes widespread, will work for seniors decrease?
Certainly, AI can now process tasks that were previously handled by humans, such as drafting text, translation, and organizing information, in a short amount of time.
For those who only provided these types of services, competition may become more intense.
On the other hand, AI allows some people to take on work that was previously difficult to do alone.
For example, consider someone who has accumulated expertise through many years of corporate employment and decides to start a small-scale consulting business after retirement.
In the past, they would have had to do everything themselves, such as creating sales materials, writing proposals for clients, and organizing research documents.
By using generative AI, the burden of these peripheral tasks can be reduced.
However, clients do not pay for text created by AI.
They pay for understanding the problems the client faces and providing appropriate judgment and solutions.
This is a critical turning point for seniors when utilizing AI.
2. Writing: Even if AI is fast, humans must make the judgments
I currently use generative AI for writing articles while also working on tasks related to foreign residency status.
AI is useful for brainstorming article outlines and organizing the points of complex systems.
Having a partner to discuss things with can sometimes broaden ideas more than staring at a blank page alone.
However, the content presented by AI cannot always be adopted as is.
It may mistake the timing of legal amendments. It may overlook exceptions in the system. It may also present conclusions with weak foundations in plausible-sounding text.
In fields like residency status, which directly affect a client's life and work, such errors cannot be overlooked.
What is needed is not just the ability to have AI provide answers.
It is the expertise to judge whether those answers are correct.
The situation is the same for writing articles.
AI can polish text. However, the writer must decide what to question, what to argue, and where to apply their own experience.
Precisely because we live in an era where AI writes text, the writer's own awareness of issues is being tested.
3. Translation: Can you become a translator without knowing English?
One of the fields where the progress of generative AI is particularly notable is translation.
If you input English text, it instantly returns quite natural Japanese.
So, can someone who doesn't know English work as a translator?
I think that is difficult.
Let's take contracts as an example.
In contract language, points such as under what conditions an obligation arises, to what extent exceptions are permitted, and how the scope of liability is defined become important.
Even if it is smooth as Japanese, if the meaning of the original text is slightly altered, it could affect the rights and obligations of the contracting parties.
Translators need the ability to compare the original text with the translated text and discover discrepancies in meaning.
As AI becomes more proficient, the role of the translator may shift from simple replacement work to content verification and quality control.
This could also be an opportunity for experienced seniors.
This is because they can apply the language skills and industry knowledge they have cultivated over many years to the task of evaluating AI output.
However, just being able to use AI does not mean you can win projects.
You need the ability to guarantee quality and the trust of your clients.
4. English Instruction—There are things in the classroom that AI cannot do
In English learning as well, generative AI can be a powerful supplementary teaching material.
There are many situations where it can be utilized, such as explanations of English grammar, practice problems for English composition, vocabulary checks, and conversation practice.
So, will English instructors become unnecessary?
I do not think so.
In actual instruction, it is necessary to determine where a student is stumbling in their understanding.
Is it a lack of vocabulary? A failure to understand grammar? Or is it an inability to grasp what the question is asking for?
Even with the same incorrect answer, the cause differs for each individual.
I change my explanation while observing the student's expressions and reactions, sometimes using a whiteboard to show different examples.
Even if they seem to have understood, there are times when they haven't truly grasped the material until they actually try to solve the problems.
AI can assist in creating teaching materials and preparing practice problems.
However, the work of observing the student in front of you and adjusting instruction on the spot has value unique to human instructors.
Here, too, the important thing is not to compete with AI.
It is to streamline the preparatory work that can be left to AI and to enhance the quality of instruction that only humans can provide.
5. Three conditions for seniors to turn AI into income
So, where should those who want to continue working after retirement using generative AI begin?
I believe three conditions are important.
First, clarify the value you can provide.
Simply saying 'I can use AI' is unlikely to lead to work.
Improve sales materials for small and medium-sized enterprises by leveraging years of sales experience. Organize operations for small business owners by leveraging accounting experience. Verify the quality of English documents by leveraging language skills.
In this way, it is necessary to specify whose problems you can solve and what kind of problems they are.
Second, distinguish between work that uses AI and work that does not.
It is not the case that everything should be left to AI.
Have AI assist with drafts and organizing materials. On the other hand, take responsibility for meetings with clients, important decisions, and final quality checks.
It is important to divide roles according to the nature of the work.
Third, try it out with paid work, even if it is small.
Simply learning how to use generative AI will not lead to income.
Think of a small service that leverages your expertise and try actually offering it to customers.
Only then will you understand what customers value, what they are dissatisfied with, and how much they are willing to pay.
The responsibilities required for unpaid practice and paid work are different.
The ultimate goal should not be to master AI operations, but to establish work that earns compensation.
6. Generative AI does not make experience unnecessary
When thinking about work after retirement, there is no need to avoid new technology because of your age.
Generative AI can be a powerful tool for people with physical or time constraints to continue working.
However, I cannot agree with the idea that anyone can easily earn money just by using AI.
What is valued as work is not the operation of AI itself, but the results provided to the customer.
Seniors possess knowledge, experience, human relationships, and judgment accumulated through years of work.
Of course, value is not created just by having long experience. It is also necessary to have an attitude of relearning to adapt to new environments without clinging to past success stories.
Even so, there is significant room for ways of working that were previously difficult by combining experience with generative AI.
Generative AI is not a tool that makes a senior's experience unnecessary. It can be a tool to make that experience useful to society once again.
What is important is not just knowing what AI can do.
What can I do? And who is the person who needs that ability?
Isn't that where work after retirement begins?
