# A Company That Entrusted AI Agents with Manufacturing Schedule Creation—Previously Thought to Be 'Human-Only' Work Involving 80% FAX Orders—Shares Their Story | Day 2 11/13

A Company That Entrusted AI Agents with Manufacturing Schedule Creation—Previously Thought to Be 'Human-Only' Work Involving 80% FAX Orders—Shares Their Story | Day 2 11/13
"I hear about AI, but I feel it's still too early for a company like ours."
"If that person takes a day off, work grinds to a halt. But we don't have the time or manpower to create manuals."
"I'm interested in AI, but where do I find the conviction that 'this will actually work?'"
Have you ever felt this way?
At Cybozu Days Japan 2026 on Friday, November 13, 2026, a company that took the first step from exactly this situation will be taking the stage. It is Omi Odor-Air Service Co., Ltd., a 'specialist company for odors and fragrances' based in Omihachiman City, Shiga Prefecture.
Operations run on paper, FAX, and the experience of veterans
Omi Odor-Air Service is a specialist company that provides total solutions for odor and fragrance challenges, from the manufacturing and sale of deodorants to odor measurement and analysis.
Since products are made-to-order, manufacturing schedules are created only after customer orders arrive.
In the sales administration department, about 80% of orders arrive via FAX. Some are handwritten, and the formats vary by customer. Some require checking past orders or cross-referencing with the product master. Each one was read by human eyes, re-entered into Excel production request forms, and passed on to the technical department.
The technical department creates manufacturing schedules based on those request forms. Decisions like grouping the same product numbers together or pushing back orders with flexible deadlines were only possible thanks to experience and know-how. It was work that only worked because of that specific person.
The company operated with paper and schedule boards, far removed from IT or DX. They had tried introducing tools in the past, but they didn't take hold. There wasn't even a premise of using AI.
Now, AI agents handle much of that work
Such a company is now using kintone and AI agents to handle order reading and manufacturing schedule creation. The staff's job is to check the results and make decisions.
Processing orders and creating manufacturing schedules that require complex rules and verification. How has the work that was thought to be 'human-only' changed?
What we will introduce in the session on the day
- How are AI agents working in actual operations?
- At what point did the management feel that 'this will work' and decide to invest?
- At what point did the two people on the front lines, from the sales and technical departments, feel that 'this will work' and decide to entrust the work to AI agents?
Please be sure to hear the details of the mechanism and the deciding factors in the session on the day. The more you thought 'AI agents are too early for us,' the more you will be able to relate to their story.
