# AI chip stocks bounce back as hyperscaler earnings loom, META equity raise in focus

24/7 Wall St. · Omor Ibne Ehsan · 2026-10-20

AI chip stocks bounce back as hyperscaler earnings loom, META equity raise in focus
NVIDIA collects a check whether hyperscalers borrow, issue stock, or drain their cash reserves, but Meta faces a funding squeeze that could reshape what shareholders actually own. The size of one expected equity offering may decide how this AI spending…
Chip stocks bounced on October 19, and the session showed who collects the money in this AI cycle. The Nasdaq rose 1.3% with semiconductors leading; NVIDIA (NASDAQ:NVDA | NVDA Price Prediction) gained 1.38%, and Meta Platforms (NASDAQ:META) slipped 0.55%.
Seema Mody’s segment that day focused on that split. The GPU supplier rose while the hyperscaler expected to raise outside money fell behind, although one session cannot show that equity talk drove Meta lower.
Meta’s expansion now needs more than its own cash. It guided 2026 capital expenditures to $130 billion to $145 billion, and second-quarter free cash flow fell to $784 million from $8.55 billion a year earlier.
NVIDIA reported $96.22 billion in quarterly revenue, up 105.8%. That gap between the buyer’s falling cash and the seller’s rising sales is why the coming hyperscaler reports carry more weight than a typical quarter.
Why This Pullback Could Precede Strong Hyperscaler Reports
Mody cited Evercore ISI: “Evercore ISI out with a note this morning that leans a bit more bullish saying the pullback that we’ve recently seen in these names comes ahead of what they think will be a strong hyperscaler earnings season.” The firm reads recent weakness as a setup for those reports.
NVIDIA is still down 4.08% over the past week and 2.33% over the past month, although it is up 16.67% year to date.
Meta fell 5.05% over the past week and is down 1.28% over the past year despite a 7.13% gain in 2026. The weak one-year return suggests some investors likely doubt the payoff from the spending.
Watch capital spending plans, AI revenue, and how the spending gets financed. NVIDIA expects top five hyperscaler capex to reach nearly $800 billion in 2026.
Older NVIDIA Chips Still Hold Their Value
Mody noted: “The H100 that’s Nvidia’s older chip has held up in terms of pricing ever since Anthropic’s latest model came out.” New model launches are lifting demand for older hardware too.
That signals demand for compute still exceeds supply. NVIDIA said it has supply for about 70% of demand.
It also protects collateral, because GPUs now back loans. Chips that hold their rental rates also hold their value as loan collateral.
Asia is more cautious: “Bank of America’s semiconductor team just returning from Asia, sharing with clients that investors there are a bit more anxious about the AI funding cycle.” The worry there centers on funding.
GPU-Backed Loans Are Becoming the Default
Mody said: “Consensus amongst credit folks we spoke to over the weekend is that GPU backed loans will be the go to option for these companies. Unsecured debt will be less desirable, especially at 15%.” That shifts financing risk onto chip values.
Unsecured borrowing near 15% makes long-dated data centers hard to justify. Secured loans cost less because lenders can claim the hardware.
Nebius Group (NASDAQ:NBIS) entered its first senior secured facility, about $775 million backed by GPUs and contracted cash flows, in July 2026. If used GPU prices fall, the collateral declines and lenders tighten terms.
Meta’s Expected Stock Sale Comes Down to Size
Analysts expect a Meta stock sale, and Mody said the open issue is size: “The biggest question is not whether META will issue stock, but how big META’s equity offering could be. They say the market is comfortable for 30 billion.” Meta has not announced an offering.
Alphabet (NASDAQ:GOOG, NASDAQ:GOOGL) completed an $80 billion stock issuance a couple of months ago. Dilution spreads future profits across more owners.
Meta’s apps reach over 3.6 billion daily active people. AI spending is exceeding even that level of cash generation.
Long-term debt already reached $83.66 billion, and issuing stock would likely let Meta avoid borrowing near 15%.
What Meta’s Expected Raise Means for Shareholders
The size and terms of Meta’s raise remain unknown, which leaves the valuation case uncertain. At $705.44 and a trailing P/E of 26, dilution and an open-ended spending cap limit upside for existing holders.
NVIDIA sits in a different position. At $217.09 and a trailing P/E of 44, it costs more, but it gets paid whether buyers use stock, GPU-backed loans, or cash.
Steady H100 pricing points to durable demand, and NVIDIA guided third-quarter revenue to $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%.
A smaller-than-expected raise or clear AI revenue disclosure from Meta would ease concerns about dilution and its spending payoff. Hyperscaler earnings will show whether either arrives.
