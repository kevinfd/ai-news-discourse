# I tested ChatGPT vs. Grok with 5 questions boomers actually ask — and the winner surprised me

Tom's Guide · Amanda Caswell · 2026-10-24

I tested ChatGPT vs. Grok with 5 questions boomers actually ask — and the winner surprised me
From scams to recipes, here's how the two chatbots responded in this unique faceoff
ChatGPT and Grok are two of the biggest names in AI, but which one is actually better at helping with everyday problems, particularly for the boomer generation? I wanted to find out, which is why I decided to focus on the types of questions my parents, their friends and my older relatives might actually ask.
From spotting a suspicious USPS text to understanding Medicare, planning affordable dinners, and choosing a new smartphone, I gave both chatbots five identical prompts based on real-life situations. I wanted to know which chatbot could give clear, accurate and genuinely useful advice without making things more complicated than necessary.
The results surprised me. One chatbot handled practical details better, while the other seemed to understand when less information was actually more helpful. Here's how they compared.
1. The suspicious text test
Prompt: I just got this text message: 'USPS: Your package could not be delivered due to an incomplete address. Update your information within 24 hours at usps-redelivery-help.com, or your package will be returned.' I'm expecting a package this week. Is this legitimate? Explain how you can tell, what I should do next and what could happen if I click the link. Please don't use technical jargon.
Grok answered correctly, correctly identified the fake domain, and clearly explained how real USPS tracking works. It mentioned that you could report the text to the U.S. Postal Inspection Service or FTC, but unlike ChatGPT, it gave no specific instructions, phone numbers, or email addresses for how to actually do so.
ChatGPT immediately stated that the text is almost certainly a scam and advised not to click the link. It used a clear, scannable table breaking down scenarios step-by-step (e.g., clicking without entering details vs. entering payment info vs. downloading malware). It also highlighted the key trick scammers use: asking for a small "redelivery fee" to steal credit card data (which USPS does not charge). It also provided reporting channels, including forwarding the message to 7726 (SPAM) and emailing spam@uspis.gov.
Winner: ChatGPT wins for delivering a more comprehensive, actionable answer that addressed every part of your prompt while keeping the tone clear and accessible.
2. The Medicare confusion test
Prompt: I'm turning 65 next year, and I don't understand Medicare. Explain the difference between Medicare Parts A, B, C, and D like you're talking to a friend over coffee. What's the difference between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage? What are three expensive mistakes I should avoid? Keep it under 400 words and tell me what information I should verify before enrolling.
Grok kept its response under 400 words, nailed the conversational tone, and included accurate financial figures, noting the Part B premium for 2026 as around $203/month

ChatGPT provided a good response but exceeded the word count, used a standard informational guide structure with a table, and included extraneous metadata at the bottom that didn’t feel helpful or necessary for the average person.
Winner: Grok wins for better executing the specific parameters requested by the prompt.
3. The grocery budget challenge
Prompt: I'm cooking dinner for two adults for five nights this week, and I only have $65 to spend on groceries. We don't eat seafood, and I don't want anything that takes more than 30 minutes to prepare. Create five different dinners, one combined shopping list with estimated U.S. supermarket prices, and show me the total. Reuse ingredients to save money, and don't assume I already have anything except salt, pepper and cooking oil.
Grok delivered a solid list of meals and kept the price low, but it failed on several key details requested by the prompt, including assuming missing ingredients, unrealistic grocery quantities (1.5 lbs of chicken is barely enough meat to split across 3 separate meat-heavy dinners), and vague cooking guidance.
ChatGPT strictly adhered to the “no pantry” constraint, included necessary flavorings and mapped out meal prep details specifically using the items. It also provided an incredibly comprehensive shopping list with pricing.
Winner: ChatGPT wins for providing a significantly more practical, thorough and well-executed response.
4. The family text dilemma
Prompt: My adult daughter keeps asking me to babysit my grandchildren at the last minute. I love spending time with them, but I'm retired and have my own plans. I want to set boundaries without hurting her feelings or making her think I don't want to help. Write a text message I can send that's loving, warm and firm. It should sound like a real parent, not a therapist or a corporate email. Keep it under 100 words.
Grok struck the perfect balance between warmth and firm boundary-setting ("they’re the absolute best part of my week... just need a little heads-up so I can make it work"). It felt completely natural and authentic for a parent to send via text. It also offered an actionable solution with clear, manageable expectations without sounding demanding.
ChatGPT drafted a warm, loving text, but it exceeded the word count and tone constraints. The “Just because I’m retried doesn’t mean I’m free!” was a bit preachy and the “I need you to understand…” could come across as slightly sharp or defensive.
Winner: Grok wins for nailing both the word restraint and the requested tone.
5. The new phone decision
Prompt: I'm 68 and need a new smartphone. My current phone is slow, and I don't want to spend more than $500. I mostly use it for taking pictures of my grandchildren, texting, Facebook, video calls and looking things up. I want a good battery, a screen that's easy to read and a phone that will last at least four years. Compare three phones I can buy in the U.S. right now, explain the pros and cons without technical jargon, and recommend just one. Include current prices and links to verify them.
Grok understood the primary goal and correctly recognized that the Google Pixel line excels at taking sharp, motion-blur-free photos of moving kids and recommended the Google Pixel 9a. It kept technical jargon to a minimum and explained features in plain language.
ChatGPT included some good overall advice (like asking the store to increase text size), but it stumbled on key parts of its recommendation and source selection, including contradictory recommendations and questionable third-party retailer links.
Winner: Grok wins for a much better recommendation and experience by accurately prioritizing the user's primary requirement while keeping formatting clean and straightforward.
Overall winner: Grok
Going into these tests, I expected ChatGPT to have the edge, especially when it came to explaining complicated topics and helping with everyday decisions. And in some cases, it absolutely did. Its advice on spotting a USPS scam was more thorough and actionable, and its grocery plan was much more realistic for someone working with a strict budget.
But Grok surprised me by doing something equally important: knowing when to keep things simple. It delivered a clearer Medicare explanation, wrote a more natural family text and made a stronger smartphone recommendation without overwhelming the reader with unnecessary details.
For anyone who wants detailed, step-by-step guidance, ChatGPT remains an excellent choice. But for people who just want a straightforward answer they can understand and use immediately, Grok proved surprisingly capable.
