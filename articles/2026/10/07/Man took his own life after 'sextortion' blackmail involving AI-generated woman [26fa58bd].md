# Man took his own life after 'sextortion' blackmail involving AI-generated woman

RNZ · billybuckwheat · 2026-10-07

Man took his own life after paying money to ‘sextortion’ blackmail involving AI-generated woman
This article deals with the subject of suicide and may be distressing for some people.
A man fooled by a "sextortion" scam sent images to an AI-generated woman online, which criminals used to blackmail him.
The distressed man sent the blackmailers money and took his own life later that day.
The 41-year-old's suicide has prompted a coroner to issue a warning and advice about what to do if caught in such a scam.
The man died in 2024, within hours of making contact with someone online who identified themselves as "Donna Chase".
He exchanged messages and images of a sexual nature with "Donna", and she immediately began blackmailing him.
Police do not believe Donna Chase existed, but that she was an AI-generated persona used to trick the man into sharing sexually explicit images.
They told Coroner Janet Anderson they believed the bank account the man paid money into was associated with an organised criminal network based overseas.
"I have no doubt that the actions of 'Donna' were the primary relevant factor in [his] death," Coroner Anderson said.
"She deliberately solicited images of a sexual nature ... and then used these to blackmail him. This is a practice known as 'sextortion'."
The man died "within hours" of his first communication with Donna, the coroner said.
In a published summary, Coroner Anderson said the man was under stress when he died.
A relative had died, he was struggling with a relationship break-up, was on medical leave from work and had financial issues.
The man had told a relative he was finding it hard to get out of bed.
However, he had no significant history of mental health problems and no record of self-harm.
He had been due to return to work on the day he died.
That morning he sent "farewell messages" to his family, who went to check on him and found him dead.
Donna Chase
The man used Signal, a secure messaging account, to communicate with "Donna Chase".
After the images were sent, "Donna" immediately threatened to send them to the man's contacts and demanded money, via bank account transfer and codes for pre-paid Steam gift cards.
The summary said the man handed money over, but did not specify how much.
The exchanges with Donna, which included voice calls, ended with a final message from the man at 10.09am on the day he died.
The coroner said it was reasonable to conclude the man was in a highly emotional and vulnerable state when he ended his life.
"His actions would have been impulsive and taken in direct response to the difficult and stressful situation he found himself in."
Man's suicide a second 'sextortion' case
The man's suicide was the second involving a sextortion attempt before the Coroners Court this year.
A teen who had dreams of becoming a policeman took his own life just hours after he was sexually extorted online in 2023.
The 18-year-old met a person on Facebook who called herself Laura Wilson, who wanted to exchange intimate images.
He had no way of knowing he was talking to a scammer in West Africa who then threatened to share the teen's videos with all his contacts if he didn't pay $5000.
The online safety organisation Netsafe advised Coroner Anderson that sextortion is a serious global issue and the scammers are highly organised professional criminals who can be based anywhere.
She said the Auckland man's tragic death showed "the harm inflicted by those who engage in sextortion activities goes beyond extreme emotional distress and financial loss".
"In this case, it led to the senseless loss of a father, partner and friend."
Police advice for people caught in a sextortion scam is to pay nothing, to shut down all social media and contact Netsafe for further advice.
Netsafe has also published a step-by-step guide on how to deal with sextortion on its website.
Netsafe's advice for people subject to sextortion
Don't give in to demands
The most important thing is not to give in to the blackmailer's demands or share more pictures or videos. If you do, they are more likely to continue asking for more money or content. If you have already given them money - do not give them more. Even if you have given money, the remaining steps can still help.
Get evidence
If possible, take screenshots of the threats, but don't take pictures of the private images or videos. Note down the blackmailer's account name and where this is happening online.
Let others know
It might be helpful to write a message for your friends and family, letting them know that someone is trying to scam you (you don't have to go into detail about what has happened) and ask them not to accept message requests from unknown accounts online.
Report to the platform
Report to the social media platform (e.g. Instagram, Snapchat, PornHub) where the scammer contacted you. Give them all the information you can. If your content is shared we also recommend you report to that platform.
Cut off contact
Stop talking to the blackmailer and block them on all platforms.
The scammer will focus on the people they believe will most likely pay them or send more content. The scammer will 'drop' others very quickly who block the scammer and refuse to contact them, so it is very important that all contact from them is blocked immediately.
Secure social media accounts
Ensure all your social media accounts are as private as possible or deactivate them temporarily.
Keep an eye out
Ask someone you trust to help by keeping an eye on social media activity for you. This can help keep you informed and give you a break from social media if you need it.
Get the content hashed for removal
If you are under 18, and if you have copies of the private images or videos the blackmailer is threatening you with, you can use a free tool called Take It Down.
Take It Down creates a special code or digital fingerprint from the pictures or videos, and it shares that code with online platforms that may help to find and remove them from being shared online. The tool can also be used for synthetic images (deep fakes), which deals with it in the same way as genuine content.
If you are 18 or older you can use StopNCII.
StopNCII will generate a digital fingerprint, called a hash, of the images/videos on your device. STOPNCII shares the hash with Industry Partners so they can try to help detect and remove the images/videos from being shared online. StopNCII.org also accepts synthetic images (deep fakes) and deals with them in the same way as genuine content.
You can report to the police
You can make a report to police by calling 105. If you are at immediate risk please call police on 111.
