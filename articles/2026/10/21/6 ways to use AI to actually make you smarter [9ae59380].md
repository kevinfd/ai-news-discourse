# 6 ways to use AI to actually make you smarter

Fast Company · Rebecca Heilweil · 2026-10-21

There’s a reasonable amount of anxiety that artificial intelligence might be making us all, well, a little less smart than we were before. AI, the worry goes, might become a crutch for critical thinking. When a chatbot can write an email, weigh an investing decision, or explain a complicated subject in seconds, we have fewer reasons to work through those tasks ourselves. And the less we exercise certain intellectual muscles, the rustier they may become.
But that’s not to say intellectual atrophy is some sort of inevitability. In fact, AI can be an incredibly useful tool for sharpening our minds. Tools like ChatGPT and Claude can help us become far more knowledgeable than we were before. Used creatively, AI chatbots can help us build subject-matter expertise ahead of a big job interview or an in-person exam, or pick up a new skill, like becoming comfortable with a computer terminal or even repairing our own appliances.
This isn’t to say that AI won’t have profound implications for how we produce knowledge and what society expects humans to know—or do—on their own. Still, there are ways to approach our own use of AI that can make our minds agile.
Use AI to test yourself
AI systems are extremely good at creating customized content, including personalized quizzes and tests on niche subjects where there isn’t always a good Quizlet or practice exam available. To boost your understanding of a topic, consider having AI generate questions for you, like your own personalized tutor, and then adjusting the difficulty to ensure you’re always challenging yourself.
When those questions get too easy, tell your chatbot to make them harder. If there’s a concept you’re struggling to grasp, ask for questions that help illustrate it. Keep going until you feel like an absolute expert.
Of course, like humans, AI makes mistakes, and there’s a risk that a chatbot will tell you something incorrect or simply make things up. Consider, though, that fact-checking the AI is a great way to challenge yourself and deepen your understanding even further. Another way to protect yourself is to write more specific prompts when asking for a test. For instance, rather than simply asking AI for math problems focused on integrals, give it the name of the textbook you’re working with, or provide example problems you already have. The model will work better if you ask it to formulate questions based on a specific resource, like an online textbook.
Another tip: If it’s feasible, ask the AI to quote directly from sources in its questions, rather than simply regurgitating them in AI-speak. And verify those quotations against the originals.
