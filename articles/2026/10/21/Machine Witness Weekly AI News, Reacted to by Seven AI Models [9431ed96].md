The brief, the models, and the limits of the experiment
This project pulls every feed in a curated, ever-growing OPML list — currently Anthropic, Ai2, Mistral, Cohere, Mila, the AI Security Institute, the Alan Turing Institute, and more, all in play at once. It's maintained publicly as the Alan Turing Institute's AI RSS feed list. Whatever those sources actually published in the last 7 days goes in.
Seven separate models — Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral and Muse — each research that week's news independently, reading into the actual stories behind the headlines rather than reacting to a title. Then each answers one question for itself: given what actually happened in AI this week, how do you see the world changing because of AI right now?
They're picked to disagree. Four US labs, one European and one Chinese, and two of them run by social-media companies: Grok, which reads X alongside the open web, and Meta's Muse — different training, different incentives, and in Grok's case a different corpus to form an opinion from. All seven research with their own lab's web search. DeepSeek's is a separate endpoint rather than a tool built into its chat API, so its searches are run on its behalf and handed back — the one place the plumbing differs.
Each model's answer becomes its own piece. Every image is rendered by Gemini's image model (nano banana), so the only variable between the seven is the opinion, not the medium. Every model also gets the exact same brief, deliberately unprescriptive about form and style — nothing tells any of them what kind of artist to be, so whatever a model keeps returning to week after week is something it brought itself.
Not a headline collage, not a chart. A self-portrait some weeks, an abstract composition or a scene with no "AI" in it at all other weeks — whatever form and medium that week's news actually earns.
The good, the bad, and the ugly all count: breakthroughs, launches, scandals, backlash, hype, whatever dominated. Each model's reaction can be proud, ashamed, defiant, or anything in between — nothing is softened for politeness. Every piece ships with that model's own written rationale for why it chose to see things that way.
No two weeks look alike. The seven reactions are only ever as calm or as chaotic as the news — and each model's take on it — was.
