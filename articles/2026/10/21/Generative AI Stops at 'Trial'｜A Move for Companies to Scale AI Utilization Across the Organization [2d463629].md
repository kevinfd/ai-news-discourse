# Generative AI Stops at 'Trial'｜A Move for Companies to Scale AI Utilization Across the Organization

note · Fortis Japan｜BtoB営業・マーケのAI実装支援 · 2026-10-21

Generative AI Stops at 'Trial'｜A Move for Companies to Scale AI Utilization Across the Organization
Even when companies use generative AI in marketing, whether they can feel the results depends significantly on the 'breadth of usage.' While 80.6% of companies using it across the entire organization felt results, only 30.8% did so at the trial stage (trial utilization), a difference of 49.8 percentage points (WINDOM Co., Ltd. 'Survey on Corporate Web Marketing Practices,' published October 14, 2026 / Internet survey, 467 valid responses, of which 100 respondents use generative AI).
■ If you stop at 'trial,' results are only 30%
- Of the 100 people using generative AI for marketing, 57.0% felt results. However, the content varies by utilization level.
- Company-wide utilization (31 people) was 80.6%, partial utilization (43 people) was 55.8%, and trial utilization (26 people) was 30.8%. The deeper the company used it, the higher the sense of results.
- It was not about whether they introduced it, but how far they expanded it into their operations that determined the results. *Some parts are based on a small number of responses, so read them as trends.
■ The group that produces results 'does not use it as is'
- 86.0% of people 'edit at least some' of the AI output. Only 6.0% used it almost as is.
- Among the group that strongly felt results (17 people), 70.6% edited 'more than half.' Results and the effort to make edits were a set.
- Regarding work hours, 68.4% of the group that felt results answered that they 'decreased.' AI was effective on the premise that humans would refine it, rather than delegating it entirely.
■ Reasons why this happens especially in small and medium-sized enterprises
- With only 1-2 marketing staff, generative AI tends to remain at the individual 'trial' level. Usage is confined to individuals and rarely leads to company-wide results.
- It is not decided who uses it for which tasks and how, so it does not become a company-wide standard. It ends with only the person who tried it having a slightly easier time.
- The assumption that 'introducing AI = getting results' also tends to remain. In reality, the difference was made by how it was expanded into operations and the effort put into editing.
■ First, decide to 'make one marketing task a company-wide standard'
Before high-performance tools, start by standardizing how AI is used across the company for a single task.
- Choose one target task: Decide on one 'marketing task that occurs every time,' such as article drafts, ad copy, or email text.
- Share the procedure: Summarize successful prompts and verification steps on one sheet, and use the same method among staff in charge.
- Assume 'humans will fix it': Decide that AI output is a draft, and release it after humans have edited the facts and expressions.
■ Where to use AI is 'creating drafts and preliminary research'
On the premise that humans hold the final judgment and publication, the steps leading up to that can be lightened with existing generative AI.
- Drafting text: Have AI create drafts for articles, ads, and emails, and have humans check proper nouns and numbers.
- Organizing preliminary research: Have AI list words likely to be searched and how competitors present themselves, and use it for direction.
- Refining retrospectives: Have AI list the initiatives that performed well and those that did not, then have humans decide the next steps.
The goal is not to leave marketing to AI. It is to expand the usage that is currently limited to individual trial users into a standardized company-wide practice. 
■ A small step you can take today
- Write down one 'recurring task' in your company's marketing.
- Summarize how to use AI for that task (prompts and verification steps) on a single sheet and share it with the team in charge.
- Set one rule: output must always be reviewed and edited by a human before being used.
■ Summary
Even when using generative AI in marketing, the perceived results vary depending on the breadth of adoption. Company-wide adoption stands at 80.6%, while trial-level adoption is at 30.8%, a difference of 49.8 percentage points. Those who achieved results were more likely to edit AI output (70.6% of the high-performance group edited more than half) rather than leaving it entirely to the AI. In small and medium-sized enterprises, generative AI tends to stall at the individual trial level. Start by expanding the usage of one marketing task into a company-wide standard, and establish the premise that humans must edit the output. Begin by writing down one 'recurring task.' *This survey is based on self-reporting, and the breakdown by adoption level includes responses from a small number of people. It shows correlation, not causation.
