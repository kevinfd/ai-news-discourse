# Why Your Brand’s Next Visibility Battle Is Being Decided In AI Answers, Not Search Rankings

Forbes · Ofer Familier, Forbes Councils Member · 2026-10-21

Ofer Familier, Co-Founder and CEO of Dig.
Until recently, finding recommendations online meant working through a page of search results. Now, people ask an AI and get a clear list back. What counts as visibility has changed, from clicks, impressions and rank to whether a brand shows up in an AI answer and how.
The instinct is to treat this like SEO with a new name, and brands risk losing ground because of it. An AI-generated answer doesn’t simply grab the top two Google results and hand them back. That’s the gap that explains why a brand can rank well on Google and still end up missing, misdescribed or left out of the exact conversation that shapes how people see it.
Why SEO’s Tool Kit Doesn’t Carry Over
Generative engine optimization (GEO) works entirely differently from SEO at the root. SEO rewarded climbing a ranked list through keywords and backlinks. GEO is the practice of shaping how a brand gets represented in an AI-generated answer.
The processes of SEO and GEO aren’t the same, and for GEO, ranking well based on keywords doesn’t reliably help the way it used to. Brands now need to identify where AI builds its answers from, and the sources are varied, including reference sites like Wikipedia, publications and journals, review and comparison platforms, owned websites and even social media, depending on the system. How AI systems draw on these platforms isn’t always clear.
ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude source differently. ChatGPT includes a citation in 96% of its answers but keeps the list short, averaging around five sources per response, while Gemini cites slightly less often, in 82% of responses, pulling from about eight sources when it does. Claude is the outlier, citing in only about half its responses, but going deeper each time, averaging around 13 sources. Where each system tends to look also differs, with ChatGPT using Wikipedia as its most common source, Claude using PubMed Central and Gemini favoring Reddit.
GEO cannot be optimized the way SEO could because of this sheer variance. There is no single formula to chase when the sources feeding an answer shift per system, per query and per moment.
To impact GEO authentically, brands need to go to the sources AI is using to form answers, conclusions and recommendations to create a positive brand image there that will carry through to AI engines. What’s become clear over the past few years is that social media has a sizable influence on AI answers, because that’s where much of the conversation around brands, events and news is now happening. 
What Brands Can Do
There are three things that matter here, more than any tool or dashboard.
The first is mapping the real questions and then finding what already answers them. In practice, mapping those questions means figuring out the actual recommendation, comparison and troubleshooting questions customers ask AI systems in your category, and keeping track of which publications, review sites and communities keep showing up in the answers, across whichever AI systems your audience really uses.
The second is publishing useful, specific content that closes knowledge gaps instead of generating content specifically meant to manufacture citations. Make it easy for real customers and experts to share honest experience, without scripting what they say or hiding a paid relationship behind it. If your page is already in front of the model, how it’s written changes whether it gets cited. Adding statistics, quotes, clear definitions and tight structure has been shown in studies to lift citation rates, even if nobody has yet connected any of it to revenue.
The third is watching for patterns. When the conversation about your brand shifts online, keep an eye on whether the AI answer shifts, too. Don’t expect a clean before-and-after, though. AI answers move around for all kinds of reasons, so a change lining up with your efforts is a signal worth watching, not proof that you caused it.
Ultimately, a brand’s ability to shape how AI describes it will depend less on optimizing for a particular system and more on earning a credible presence in the conversations those systems draw from. Today, that increasingly includes communities and creator platforms such as Reddit, TikTok and YouTube, where customers and experts are already sharing experiences, opinions and recommendations. Building a meaningful presence in those conversations may be unfamiliar territory, but it is becoming harder to ignore.
