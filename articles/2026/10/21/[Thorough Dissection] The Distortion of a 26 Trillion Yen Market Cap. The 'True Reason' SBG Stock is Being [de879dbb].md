[Thorough Dissection] The Distortion of a 26 Trillion Yen Market Cap. The 'True Reason' SBG Stock is Being Sold Behind the Scenes of Arm Holdings and the 10 Trillion Yen OpenAI Investment, and Future Scenarios
Hello everyone, konkonkon! I am the 'Turkish Lira-Yen Gentleman'.
While I usually face the markets amidst the rough waves of foreign exchange and individual stocks, there is currently a company in the Japanese stock market that holds a 'huge mystery that is far too distorted and most interesting' (my personal opinion).
That company is SoftBank Group (SBG: 9984), led by CEO Masayoshi Son.
As of October 2026, decisive news that is shaking the global technology market has occurred one after another, including an unprecedented court ruling surrounding its subsidiary Arm, and the completion of a massive 10 trillion yen total investment in OpenAI.
However, strangely enough, despite having such strong positive factors, SBG's stock price has plummeted from its recent highs, and its market capitalization is around 26 trillion yen, placing it in an inexplicable state of discount.
What on earth is happening beneath the surface?
I will thoroughly unravel the corporate value of Arm, the full picture of the OpenAI investment, and the 'true risk' that the market fears, based on the latest data.
1. The Market Cap Reversal Phenomenon: The Mystery of the Subsidiary (Arm) Being Worth 10 Trillion Yen More Than the Parent (SBG)
First, what we must grasp is the decisive 'value reversal phenomenon' occurring between the parent company and the subsidiary.
- SoftBank Group (Parent Company) Market Cap: Approx. 26 trillion yen
- Arm Holdings (Subsidiary) Market Cap: Approx. 35-36 trillion yen (approx. 230-240 billion dollar scale)
SBG holds approximately 86-90% of Arm's shares. In other words, even if we calculate 'only the value of the Arm shares' that SBG holds, it reaches approximately 31-32 trillion yen.
Nevertheless, the entire corporate value of SBG is evaluated at around 26 trillion yen.
This is a phenomenon known in the market as a 'conglomerate discount' (holding company discount).
The market evaluates SBG not as an 'operating company' like a manufacturer, but as an 'investment fund' that manages huge assets. Because of liquidity constraints such as 'the Arm shares held cannot be sold on the market and turned into cash immediately' and the deduction of fund management costs, it has become standard for the stock price to be traded at a 20-30% discount to the Net Asset Value (NAV).
However, even taking that into account, the current price gap is extreme. A distortion has arisen as if 'all assets held by SBG other than Arm (Vision Fund, telecommunications subsidiaries, AI-related investments, etc.) are being evaluated as negative'.
2. Conclusion of the Quagmire Lawsuit: What the 'Mere 2 Dollar' Damages Mean
The biggest risk factor shaking the foundation of Arm was the license infringement lawsuit with its major customer, the major US semiconductor company Qualcomm.
Qualcomm had been engaged in a legal battle seeking a suspension of royalty payments for up to 5 years (a reduction on the scale of billions of dollars), but the conclusion reached by a jury in Delaware, USA, in mid-October 2026 surprised the market.
- Verdict: Partially upheld Arm's breach of contract
- Damages: A mere $2 (approx. 300 yen)
The massive damages claim sought by Qualcomm was effectively rejected, meaning Arm has 'completely avoided a fatal financial blow.' By settling for nominal damages of $2, the worst-case scenario of cash flow deterioration that the market feared most has been cleared for now.
3. Breaking Away from the 'Shadow Giant': Proprietary AI Chips and 10 Trillion Yen Investment in OpenAI
Until now, Arm has been a 'shadow infrastructure company' that licenses semiconductor 'blueprints' for smartphones and other devices, earning a few percent in royalties for every unit shipped.
*A shadow infrastructure company is defined as: A company that does not appear on the main stage, but supports the foundations (infrastructure) of society, specific industries, or technologies from behind the scenes, playing a role so important that the world could not function without it.
However, currently, under the leadership of CEO Son, Arm is stepping onto the main stage itself and attempting to fundamentally transform its business model.
- Deployment of proprietary AI chips for data centers, 'Arm AGI CPU' 
  - Shifting to a policy of not just licensing blueprints, but directly developing and supplying custom chips to solve the 'enormous power consumption and memory shortages' that are the biggest challenges for AI data centers.
- Completion of a total 10 trillion yen (approx. $64.6 billion) investment in OpenAI 
  - On October 1, 2026, SBG officially completed its additional investment in OpenAI. With this, SBG's stake has reached approximately 13%, making it a top-tier external shareholder.
The blueprint CEO Son is drawing is clear.
'The world's highest-performing power-efficient semiconductors provided by Arm (hardware)' x 'The highest-performing intelligence boasted by OpenAI (software)'.
By holding both of these wheels, the vision is to monopolize and dominate the very infrastructure of the generative AI and future AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) markets.
4. Why is SBG stock being sold despite such positive news?
Arm's litigation risk has receded, and the lineup for AI infrastructure is nearing completion. Under normal circumstances, it would not be strange for the stock price to soar.
However, in reality, SBG's stock price has fallen more than 40% from its recent peak, with phases where it was hammered down to the mid-4,000 yen range.
Behind the positive news, what is the market afraid of, and why are they letting go of SBG stock?
If you only look at superficial news, you will misjudge 'why it is being sold' and whether 'this decline is a perfect buying opportunity or a dangerous trap.' The concerns that market participants are watching beneath the surface are mainly concentrated in the following three points.
- Enormous US dollar-denominated interest-bearing debt and the burden of interest payments due to high interest rates
- 'Overheating (bubble concerns)' in AI data center investment and the recovery period for massive investments
- OpenAI's stock valuation and the volatility risk inherent in unlisted assets
