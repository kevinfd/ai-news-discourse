# Tesla and SpaceX form board committees to explore $3.5T merger around AI

Vector Wire · VectorWireAI · 2026-10-21

Musk's public endorsement of a potential SpaceX-Tesla merger rattled some insiders, who worry his comments could invite shareholder scrutiny and delays, Reuters reported on October 22, citing an unnamed source2. The report described the proposed combination as a $3.5 trillion deal.
The insider concern followed a push by advisers within Musk's corporate empire to announce the merger by the first half of 2027 so the deal can close while President Trump remains in power, Bloomberg reported on October 21, citing unnamed sources4. Bloomberg described a $3.7 trillion combination and said time is running out before political and financial conditions shift.
Musk disclosed on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call on October 20 that both companies had formed special committees of independent directors to explore a potential merger1,15. The combination would rank as the largest corporate tie-up of all time, uniting companies with a combined market value approaching $3.5 trillion12,16. SpaceX carries a market capitalization of more than $2 trillion, while Tesla's is nearly $1.5 trillion19.
Musk, who serves as CEO and largest shareholder of both companies, endorsed the idea but stressed it is not a certainty. "I do think there's a lot of merit to combining the companies," he said, citing potential benefits in AI software, chips, robotics and orbital infrastructure9. He added that he is recused from the committee discussions because of his conflict of interest13. "Because I'm a conflicted party, I can't actually be in the discussions of the special committees," Musk said.
AI as the merger thesis
The AI overlap between the two companies is the deal's central rationale. SpaceX reported that its AI division generated roughly $7.6 billion in third-quarter revenue, overtaking its Starlink-led connectivity business as the company's largest revenue source17. SpaceX said its AI business grew ninefold in a year. Total quarterly revenue reached $14.58 billion, a 161% year-over-year increase, and SpaceX swung to a net profit of approximately $4.4 billion from a $541 million loss in the prior quarter18.
Musk pointed to Terrafab, a chip manufacturing joint venture between the two companies, as an example of merger synergy. He said the venture plans to produce its first test chip around May or June 2027, with mass production targeted by the third quarter of 2028. He framed the merger around what he called the "superintelligence singularity," arguing that cooperation on developing superintelligence software would yield significant gains.
In the day following the earnings call, SpaceX shares rose 8% and Tesla gained 3.5% in early afternoon trading on October 213.
Shareholder and regulatory hurdles
Musk cautioned that approval is far from guaranteed. He noted that 50% of all Tesla shares, not just those cast in a vote, would need to approve the deal, calling that a "high hurdle". "Some people out there assume that a SpaceX and Tesla merger is a certainty and I think that would be overstating it," he said. No formal merger agreement has been announced, and any transaction would face regulatory scrutiny20.
Tech analyst Gene Munster of Deepwater Asset Management said he believes there is a 95% chance a deal will take place within two years. Barclays analysts had suggested before the call that a merger could be announced ahead of Tesla's annual shareholder meeting in December. Barclays estimated that a SpaceX acquisition of Tesla could give Musk around 37% of the combined company while controlling around 56% of its voting power.
ANALYSIS SpaceX's AI division surpassing Starlink as the company's top revenue line reframes the merger discussion: the deal is less about pairing cars with rockets than about consolidating two large-scale AI and compute operations under a single corporate structure. The Terrafab joint venture already binds the companies at the silicon level, and a full merger would eliminate the governance complexity of coordinating across separate boards.
Tesla confirmed on its own Q3 earnings call on October 21 that it intends to begin selling the Cybercab robotaxi to customers before the end of 2026[2]. Tesla's unsupervised Robotaxi service logged nearly 2 million cumulative miles by the end of Q3[3].
