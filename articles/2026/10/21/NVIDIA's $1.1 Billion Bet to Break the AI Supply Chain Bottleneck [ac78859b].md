# NVIDIA's $1.1 Billion Bet to Break the AI Supply Chain Bottleneck

NVIDIA and Supply Chain Partners Expand Capacity to Accelerate AI Infrastructure Buildout
Huang calls capacity the constraint, and NVIDIA is now paying to break it: a $1.1 billion capacity reservation advance to KYEC funds new US test facilities targeting high-volume production in April 2027. Substrate capacity is set to rise across IBIDEN, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Unimicron, which puts a hard floor under how many AI systems can ship through 2028.

NVIDIA is putting serious money behind CEO Jensen Huang's argument that capacity limits AI infrastructure growth, committing a $1.1 billion advance reservation to semiconductor testing house KYEC to fund new US test facilities.
The KYEC deal is a capacity reservation advance, meaning NVIDIA is prepaying to lock in throughput before the facilities even exist. That upfront commitment signals confidence that demand will fill every unit those lines can produce.
On the substrate side, NVIDIA has lined up expanded capacity across IBIDEN, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and Unimicron. Substrates are the packaging layers connecting chips to the rest of a system, and constrained supply has been a quiet choke point in AI hardware production.
Together, the moves set a meaningful floor under how many complete AI systems can actually ship through 2028. For investors watching whether NVIDIA can translate surging orders into delivered revenue, the supply-side buildout is the mechanism that makes that conversion possible.
High-volume production at the KYEC facilities is targeted for April 2027, aiming the unlocked capacity squarely at the next product cycle.
