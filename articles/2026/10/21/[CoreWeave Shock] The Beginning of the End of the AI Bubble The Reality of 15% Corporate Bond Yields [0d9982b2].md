# [CoreWeave Shock] The Beginning of the End of the AI Bubble? The Reality of 15% Corporate Bond Yields

note · 推し株ハント · 2026-10-21

[CoreWeave Shock] The Beginning of the End of the AI Bubble? The Reality of 15% Corporate Bond Yields
You will understand what happened in the CoreWeave shock, what a corporate bond yield of about 15% means, how AI-related stocks are involved with debt, and how beginners should handle this news. I have broken down the difficult financial topics and organized them with diagrams.
Nikkei Stock Average Closing Price (2026/10/21): 64,621 yen (+546 yen from the previous day, rebound after 3 business days)
About 25% in one week,
is the range by which the stock price of the US AI cloud company CoreWeave fell in the week starting October 14 (reported by Forbes).
The trigger was not the popularity or performance of AI stocks, but a story about debt...
The Nikkei Stock Average also fell from 67,744 yen on October 14 to 64,075 yen on October 20.
I think this was a movement that was not unrelated to individual investors in Japan.
Various speculations such as Black Monday and the collapse of the AI bubble were circulating on the internet, and I was also worried.
Is it an AI bubble, or a temporary adjustment?
No one can reach a conclusion yet.
However, what has become clear from this incident is that AI stocks are influenced not only by demand but also by the flow of money (debt and interest rates).
This time, I will organize this mechanism based on published reports and figures.
▪️ What is the CoreWeave shock? What happened?
CoreWeave is an emerging cloud company that owns data centers for AI and lends computing power to companies. According to reports, the following occurred on October 14:
・Corporate bond yields rose to around 15% (the 2032 bond is reported to be around 14.8%, and the 2031 bond around 14.9%)
・Stock price fell by about 15.5% at closing (to about $66)
・Oracle fell about 9%, Nebius about 10%, Dell about 11%, and the Nasdaq Composite fell about 1.9%
After that, on October 19, Goldman Sachs raised its investment rating from neutral to buy, and the stock price rebounded temporarily.
However, even after the rebound, corporate bond yields remained in the 15% range, and opinions remain divided.
▪️ The reality of 15% corporate bond yields
First, let's organize the meaning of yield.
Corporate bonds are bonds issued by companies to borrow money.
From an investor's perspective, yield is a measure of the return when lending money to that company.
When bond prices fall, yields rise.
In other words, the reason yields soared is that these corporate bonds were sold (prices fell).
The coupon rate of the corporate bonds CoreWeave issued in June 2026 was 9.675%. In mid-October, they were traded in the market at around 15%.
What to note here is that 15% is the yield the market demands for the current corporate bonds, not the interest rate the company is actually paying at present (reported by Benzinga).
However, when borrowing new money or refinancing, there is a possibility that conditions close to this level will be required.
It is reported that this is the reason investors were cautious.
Also, S&P reports that it has assigned CoreWeave a rating of B+ (speculative level, so-called junk grade).
▪️ Organizing the flow of the shock with a diagram
AI-related investment requires a huge amount of money.
CoreWeave is reported to have about $35 billion in debt and another $16 billion in lease liabilities. TIKR reports that debt is on the scale of about $50 billion. From this, we can see the figure that has expanded through debt.
▪️ The weight of interest seen in numbers
The burden of interest is becoming heavier even when looking at the financial results figures.
・Interest expense for the April-June 2025 quarter: $267 million
・Interest expense for the April-June 2026 quarter: $640 million (about 2.4 times)
・Outlook for the July-September 2026 quarter: $860 million to $940 million
Since sales for the April-June quarter were $2.6 billion (up 112% from the same period last year), it is calculated that about 25% of sales went to interest. The net loss was $626 million.
On the other hand, demand remains strong. The contract backlog (the basis for future sales) is reported to be about $104 billion, and sales have more than doubled.
Here, I will perform a sensitivity analysis.
This is the annual interest if we assume that the $35 billion in debt all had the same yield.
・About 7.3% (a guide back-calculated from current interest performance): about $2.6 billion annually (about 25% of sales)
・8%: $2.8 billion annually (about 27%)
・10%: $3.5 billion annually (about 34%)
・12%: $4.2 billion annually (about 40%)
・15%: about $5.3 billion annually (about 50%)
Sales are calculated at about $10.4 billion, which is simply four times the $2.6 billion of the April-June quarter.
In reality, it does not happen exactly like this because of fixed interest rates, timing of refinancing, sales growth, etc.
This is just a trial calculation to get a sense of how the interest burden changes significantly when interest rates rise.
▪️ Opinions are split in two
This time, too, experts' opinions are divided.
● Cautious view
・If the interest burden is heavy and borrowing conditions become stricter, the room for cash flow will shrink
・There are also voices that see this rebound as a temporary return (a so-called dead cat bounce)
・There is a view that doubts whether AI investment will generate as much profit as expected
● Positive view
・Goldman Sachs evaluates CoreWeave as one of the superior operators in AI cloud, and sees future financing costs as only slightly higher than current borrowing costs
・Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang stated that he is optimistic about the company and pointed out that the cost of capital is falling
I think there is a large part that we cannot know which is correct until we see the next financial results (November) and future interest rate movements.
▪️ 3 points that beginners want to check
From here on is general thinking. Please make your own investment decisions.
① Weight of debt: How much of sales does interest account for?
Just comparing the interest expense in the financial statements with sales is a guide to the weight of debt. This time, about one-fourth of sales was interest.
② Yield of financing: How are corporate bond yields moving?
Sometimes the corporate bond market reacts before the stock price.
When corporate bond yields become a topic in the news, some see it as one of the signs that the degree of caution is increasing.
③ Next financial results: Look at both demand and debt
Even if the contract backlog increases, if the interest burden becomes heavy, the money remaining on hand will decrease.
The point is to look at both sales and debt.
Also, while experts' responses range from cautious to positive, what is common is the idea of paying attention to companies with low dependence on debt and narrowing down targets even within AI.
I think what we individual investors can do is to secure several months' worth of living expenses first, avoid being too biased toward one theme, and invest with an amount that is not unreasonable, while diversifying over time.
▪️ Companies likely to attract attention for their impact on Japanese stocks
This recent movement has also spilled over into related Japanese stocks. On October 20, the Nikkei Stock Average fell by 841 yen, with reports suggesting that SoftBank Group (9984) and Fujikura (5803) led the decline. On October 21, it rebounded, with Advantest (6857) and SoftBank Group pushing the Nikkei higher.
・SoftBank Group (9984): A company whose investments in AI-related fields tend to attract attention.
・Fujikura (5803): A company that tends to attract attention for its fiber optics related to data centers.
・Advantest (6857): A major manufacturer of semiconductor testing equipment.
However, CoreWeave's movements do not necessarily translate directly into financial performance. None of this is a recommendation to buy or sell specific stocks; it is presented merely as a summary of companies I am personally watching.

▪️Summary
The CoreWeave shock was an event that demonstrated that AI stocks are influenced not only by demand but also by debt and interest rates. It has been reported that corporate bond yields rose to nearly 15% and the stock price fell by about 25% in one week. On the other hand, there is a contract backlog of about $104 billion, and views are divided, with Goldman Sachs upgrading the stock.
What we as beginners can do is develop the habit of looking at three things: (1) the weight of debt, (2) the yield on financing, and (3) the next earnings report. Personally, I feel that it is important not to focus on just one theme and to continue within a reasonable range.
