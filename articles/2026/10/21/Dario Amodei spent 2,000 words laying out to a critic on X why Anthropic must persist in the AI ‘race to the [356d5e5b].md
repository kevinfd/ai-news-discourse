# Dario Amodei spent 2,000 words laying out to a critic on X why Anthropic must persist in the AI ‘race to the top’

Business Insider · Aditi Bharade, Aditi Bharade (abharade@businessinsider.com) · 2026-10-21

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei engaged in a lengthy exchange with a critic on X. Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images Dario Amodei responded to a critic on X who said Anthropic wasn't delivering on its safety commitments. He said Anthropic would be best placed to make AI safe by staying at the top of the game. The critic, Nathan Calvin from Encode AI, said Anthropic was trying to win a race to superintelligence. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei disputed a criticism that the company was putting AI safety in the back seat in its "race to the top." On Tuesday, the executive wrote a nearly 2,000-word response to a critical X post which said Anthropic was trying to win a race to superintelligence and deprioritizing AI safety . The original Monday X post was by Nathan Calvin, the chief strategy officer of Encode AI, an AI-safety advocacy nonprofit. Calvin listed nine actions by Anthropic over the past year that he said undermined its safety commitments, including signing deals for more than $500 billion in compute and increasingly relying on Claude to develop its AI models. His post was lengthy, too, at over 1,500 words. Amodei said in his response that while he was a "fan of the investigative work" Calvin had done for AI safety, he disagreed with many of Calvin's criticisms. Amodei said his position was that Anthropic can do more to keep the AI industry safe if it stays at the frontier. "Our strategy has always been what I've called the 'race to the top' — by showing how it's possible to build the technology responsibly, we can raise the bar for the entire industry," Amodei wrote. He then wrote out his own nine-item list of actions that were "very costly" to Anthropic commercially, but had "strong safety benefits." This included releasing its powerful Mythos model only to a select group of cybersecurity partners and restricting the sale of its AI services to Chinese state-controlled companies. However, Amodei acknowledged Anthropic could have handled some of the issues Calvin raised better. These included a draft bill Anthropic circulated to Congress this month — obtained exclusively by Politico — that critics said would make it harder for the federal government to block dangerous AI models. "We'll always try to do better but, again, I am extremely proud of our track record and stand by it," he wrote. Amodei and Calvin's exchange comes at a time when model safety has become one of the most pressing issues in the AI sphere. In September, AI researchers predicted an "AI apocalypse," in which the increasingly capable and rogue technology could kill all of humanity in the next decade. In response to the concerns, AI chiefs like Amodei, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, and SpaceXAI CEO Elon Musk all agreed to " pace the frontier ," referring to slowing down AI development to ensure there were enough checks and balances in place. Meanwhile, Anthropic is on the brink of its hotly anticipated IPO, expected to list at a $2 trillion valuation in November. Last week, it reported a third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion.
