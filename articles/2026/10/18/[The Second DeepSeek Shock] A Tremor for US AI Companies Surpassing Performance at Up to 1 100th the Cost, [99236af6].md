# [The Second DeepSeek Shock] A Tremor for US AI Companies: Surpassing Performance at Up to 1/100th the Cost, Potentially Shattering the AI Bubble's Foundation

note · 瀬谷空悠太郎 · 2026-10-18

[The Second DeepSeek Shock] A Tremor for US AI Companies: Surpassing Performance at Up to 1/100th the Cost, Potentially Shattering the AI Bubble's Foundation
On October 17, 2026, the Chinese AI company DeepSeek announced its new model, 'DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017'.
This announcement is not merely about an improvement in AI model performance.
It presented shocking figures: while demonstrating performance that surpasses the most advanced US AI models, the usage cost is anywhere from a few dozen to approximately 100 times lower.
If this can be replicated in actual business, it could change the revenue structures of US AI companies and even the valuation of AI-related stocks.
The 'DeepSeek Shock' that shook global stock markets in January 2025.
Perhaps its second act is beginning.
DeepSeek has surpassed the most advanced US AI
The focus this time is on a benchmark called 'DeepSWE v1.1,' which measures the success rate of AI when tasked with software development work.
According to the comparison data released by DeepSeek, the performance of the main models is as follows.
*Costs are estimates read from near the maximum settings of the graph released by DeepSeek. Success rates are also based on the same graph.
What is particularly surprising is the cost.
Approximately 1/40th compared to GPT-6 Astra.
Approximately 1/100th compared to Claude Fable 5.
If a company were to perform 10,000 development tasks of the same type, a simple calculation would look like this:
- GPT-6 Astra: Approximately $75,000
- Claude Fable 5: Approximately $220,000
- DeepSeek: Approximately $2,000
This is a massive difference.
Of course, actual costs will vary depending on the nature of the work, token consumption, retries, and the tools used.
Furthermore, the 77% figure is a test result published by DeepSeek, and it has not been proven that it outperforms US competitors in all development tasks. Independent verification will also be important.
Still, the fact that it has been shown that such a price difference is possible with nearly the same success rate is news that investors cannot ignore.
What investors should focus on is 'price destruction' rather than 'performance'
In the AI industry, performance competition has been the focus until now.
Capable of more advanced reasoning.
Capable of writing more complex programs.
Capable of processing longer texts.
Through these performance improvements, AI companies have set high prices for high-performance models.
However, what would happen if a Chinese company entered the market here with overwhelming low prices?
When companies introduce AI, they do not necessarily seek the world's highest-performance model.
They just need to be able to process the necessary tasks with sufficient accuracy, stability, and at a low cost.
For example, if you pay $7.50 for a model with a 74% success rate, while a model with a 77% success rate is available for $0.20, there is at least sufficient reason to verify the latter.
There is a possibility that the AI market will rapidly shift from a performance competition to a market that competes on the balance between performance and price.
This is by no means a small problem for US AI companies.
Can massive AI investments really be recouped?
Currently, large US technology companies are actively promoting AI-related capital investment.
They are building data centers, purchasing large quantities of GPUs, and expanding power facilities.
Behind this is the expectation that AI usage will expand further in the future and lead to significant revenue.
However, there is something I would like to consider here.
Even if the volume of AI usage increases, if the unit price of AI drops sharply, won't sales and profits fail to grow as expected?
For example, suppose an AI company lowers the price of a task it was providing for $10 per unit to $1 due to intensifying competition.
Just to maintain revenue, the number of usage instances must be increased tenfold.
Of course, if processing costs also decrease, it is possible to maintain profit margins.
However, if price cuts are implemented first to compete with low-cost models, there will be significant pressure on profitability.
The profit growth scenarios for AI businesses that investors have assumed until now may need to be re-evaluated.
What is important is not just whether the AI market will grow.
Who will capture how much profit from the grown AI market?
This will be a major point of contention moving forward.
This is not an irrelevant issue for NVIDIA either.
During the DeepSeek shock in January 2025, NVIDIA's stock price fell by approximately 17% in a single day, and its market capitalization decreased by about $590 billion.
At the time, what the market was wary of was the issue of whether developing high-performance AI might not require as massive an amount of computational resources as previously assumed.
A similar debate could reignite this time as well.
If low-cost models become widely adopted, companies will be able to automate many tasks without using expensive AI.
As a result, it might affect not only investments in AI services but also investment plans for computing infrastructure.
However, this needs to be considered carefully.
AI becoming cheaper does not necessarily mean a decrease in GPU demand.
If the unit cost of using AI drops, it will become possible to use AI for tasks that were previously not cost-effective.
In other words,
AI becomes cheaper → More companies use it → Total AI usage increases → Demand for computational resources also increases
This development is also quite plausible.
This is a way of thinking close to the 'Jevons paradox,' where technological efficiency actually leads to an increase in total demand.
On the other hand, if the same processing volume can be handled with fewer GPUs than before, the growth in capital expenditure may slow down.
Ultimately, what matters is whether the cost-saving effects from AI efficiency or the demand increase from expanded usage will be greater.
The decline in AI company profits and the decrease in semiconductor demand should not be considered as the same thing.
What will happen to US companies if China initiates an AI price war?
Another point to consider with this announcement is the presence of Chinese companies.
China has increased its production capacity and price competitiveness in many fields, such as semiconductors, electric vehicles, and solar panels.
In AI as well, there is a possibility that they will increase their presence in the global market using low prices as a weapon.
If Chinese companies adopt a strategy that prioritizes market share acquisition even at the expense of profit margins, US AI companies will be forced to make difficult decisions.
If they maintain prices, they risk losing customers.
If they lower prices, their profit margins will decline.
If they pursue higher performance, they will need even more research and development funding.
Moreover, the model weights for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 have been released under an MIT license.
This expands the options for companies to deploy it in their own environments or integrate it into their own unique services.
This could be a factor that accelerates not only price competition for API services but also the commoditization of AI itself.
However, it has not been confirmed that these low prices were achieved through Chinese government subsidies.
It is necessary to evaluate low prices resulting from technical efficiency separately from price competition driven by policy support.
To survive, US AI companies will need a 'reason to use them even if they are expensive'.
Going forward, for US AI companies to maintain their competitiveness, mere benchmark results may no longer be sufficient.
What will become important is a framework that companies can use with peace of mind.
Security, data protection, regulatory compliance, stable operation, and support systems.
Furthermore, ease of use through integration with Office products, development environments, and cloud services will also become important.
When companies choose an AI, they do not necessarily always adopt the cheapest model.
Especially in operations handling confidential information, data management methods and trust in the provider also become factors in the decision.
US companies have room to differentiate themselves in these aspects.
However, if the price difference reaches levels of 40x or 100x, they will be required to justify that price gap.
AI companies in the future will have to explain not only "how smart they are," but also "why it is necessary to pay that price."
AI-related stocks do not all move in the same direction
As an investor, one should be careful not to equate the growth of the AI market with the rise of AI-related stocks.
If price destruction like this continues, the impact will vary greatly depending on the company.
First, companies that develop and provide foundation models may see their profit margins squeezed by price cuts.
On the other hand, for companies on the side of using AI, lower costs will be a tailwind.
Examples include software development companies, business automation services, and general companies that utilize AI.
Operations that were previously unprofitable due to high AI usage fees may become viable through lower pricing.
In other words, a headwind for companies selling AI itself could potentially be a tailwind for companies that generate profit using AI.
When looking at future AI-related stocks, the following classifications will be important.
- AI model development companies: Risk of price competition and development cost burdens squeezing profits
- Semiconductor and data center-related companies: A tug-of-war between improved computational efficiency and expanding total demand
- Cloud companies: Benefits from increased AI usage versus the impact of falling service prices
- AI-utilizing companies: Productivity gains and improved profit margins due to lower usage fees
Not all companies will receive the same benefits just because they are AI-related.
In fact, the more price destruction progresses, the greater the potential gap between winners and losers may become.
What investors should check going forward
It is too early to judge that US AI companies have lost their competitiveness based solely on this announcement.
DeepSWE v1.1 is a benchmark covering 113 software development tasks and does not measure all aspects of actual corporate activity.
Furthermore, the figure of approximately $0.20 is an estimated cost per task under specific conditions, not the processing price for every type of operation.
Moving forward, what investors will want to verify includes independent re-verification, cost-reduction effects in practical applications, the spread of DeepSeek adoption by companies, API price revisions by US AI companies, and profit margins and capital expenditure plans in financial results.
Of particular importance is the extent to which unit prices are falling while the volume of AI service usage is growing.
Even if the number of usage cases is surging, if revenue and profits do not keep pace, it will disappoint the stock market.
The possibility that as AI evolves, AI companies may become less profitable
DeepSeek's new model raises important questions regarding AI investment.
The rapid evolution of AI technology and the ability of the companies that developed that technology to capture significant profits do not necessarily align.
Rather, if prices drop sharply due to technological innovation, users will reap significant benefits, while the profitability of service providers may deteriorate.
This is something that has occurred in many industries in the past.
Even if the market expands, if competition intensifies, it becomes difficult for profits to remain.
The same thing may happen with AI.
If the 2025 DeepSeek shock raised the question of whether AI development truly requires such massive investment, this announcement poses an even more profound question.
When AI becomes this cheap to use, can US AI companies continuing to make massive investments secure profits commensurate with that investment?
Even if the future of AI is bright, the future of all AI-related stocks is not necessarily bright.
What is important for investors is not to get excited about the evolution of AI technology, but to discern who will gain and who will lose from that evolution.
Whether the second DeepSeek shock will fully materialize depends on future practical evaluations and market reactions.
However, as investors, we must be keenly aware that price competition in the AI market is entering a new phase.
