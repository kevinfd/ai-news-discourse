# I feel like the tide has turned in the job and career change market. What are the jobs that AI cannot do?

note · キャリコンのつぶやき · 2026-10-18

I feel like the tide has turned in the job and career change market. What are the jobs that AI cannot do?
News about personal information leaks caused by AI is constant, but with the implementation of AI in real society now in full swing, I feel like the tide has turned in the job and career change market.
Technological development always changes the way we work. From the Industrial Revolution of the past to the information revolution led by computers and the advent of the Internet, the jobs required by society have changed from time to time.
Now, let's talk about AI. You are likely already using it in your daily lives. Or, I think you are receiving services that use AI without even realizing it.
The fact that IT-related jobs such as system development and operations are being rapidly replaced by AI can be felt in the changes in job postings, and there is no doubt that general office work, which was previously considered relatively easy to enter even for those without experience, is also being affected.
I also feel that new graduate job hunting, which has been an overwhelming seller's market due to labor shortages, will start to take on a different aspect from next fiscal year.
In the career change market, it is expected that hiring conditions will become stricter as more companies carefully assess what job seekers can actually do compared to before.
Is your job a high-value-added job that only humans can do and cannot be replaced by AI? I think the time has come to think about this carefully once again.
As is often said, tasks with clear verbalization and processes can be automated and systematized, but even jobs that were previously considered Galapagos-like and unique, which were said to be unsuitable for systematization due to many exceptions, will likely be replaced by AI. Anything in a job that could be defined as a 'technique' will likely fall into this category.
So, what will remain? I believe that what is called 'skill'—things that can only be reproduced through human senses—will remain.
This 'skill' is tricky; it is something that is passed down from master to apprentice over a long period of time and with great energy, or is kept within a family, so patience and perseverance are essential for those who take on this work. It seems to be the polar opposite of jobs focused on time and cost performance.
The fields of art and entertainment might be easy-to-understand examples.
I feel like the question, 'What about career consulting, given what you're saying?' is flying at me like an arrow (bitter laugh).
Yes, I believe that the work of career consulting and the profession of career consultant will survive. This is because I believe it is a job that helps realize the dreams and goals that cannot be fully expressed, as well as the nuances of the emotions of the person seeking advice regarding their way of life and work, including areas that cannot be verbalized.
Yes, I also have the confidence that I will not lose to AI (though I wonder if it's even worth competing)!
However, for the interpretation and judgment of already documented simple laws and social systems, I probably cannot beat the speed and the breadth of the scope that AI can grasp; I think that is already the case now, and for things like laws, I think it would be fine if the governing agencies provided official AI-based chatbots.
For consultations regarding one's entire career in a broad sense, which is not that, I am convinced that we career consultants will not be replaced by AI.
That is because I strive to study and improve my skills every day.
I would like you all to look back once again and consider whether your own work is what is called a 'skill.' And if you feel you have not yet reached that level, I would like you to aim for those heights.
I believe that is an important thing for carving out your future career.
