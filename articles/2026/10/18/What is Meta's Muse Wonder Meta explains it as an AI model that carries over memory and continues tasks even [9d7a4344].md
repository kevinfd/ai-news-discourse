note · ふみふみ · 2026-10-18

What is Meta's "Muse Wonder"? Meta explains it as an AI model that carries over memory and continues tasks even when hitting a snag. Available to developers and Muse Code subscribers [Confirmed via official source text]
"I heard Meta released a new AI model." "What can Muse Wonder do?" "Can we use it too?"
So, I read the official announcement Meta released on October 12th in the original (English) text.
Here is the conclusion first.
It was officially stated that Muse Wonder is an AI model for agents, with the selling points of "carrying over memory" and "continuing tasks even when hitting a snag."
However, as of the October 12th announcement, it is only available to developers and Muse Code subscribers. It is written that Muse Wonder will be added "soon" to Muse (the consumer AI Meta announced in September), but no date was provided.
Whether it can be used in Japan was not mentioned. (This is information from the original text as of October 18, 2026. Availability is subject to change.)
Also, I have not actually used it myself; this is an article organized by reading the official announcement. I was only able to confirm the English version, and I have not found a Japanese version.
🧠 What is Muse Wonder?
Muse Wonder is an AI model from Meta. "Muse Wonder" and "Muse Wonder Full" were announced at the same time. The differences between the two (performance, pricing, target audience) are not written in the original text.
The official source explains it as follows (author's translation).
・Meta's "most capable model yet" 
・For "agentic" tasks, from personal errands to work 
・Examples: processing insurance claims, understanding lease agreements, building functional apps
Agents are AIs that carry out work even when people are not watching. Meta also announced the AI agent "Muse" in September (from the headline of official related news).
🧩 "Changes" mentioned by the official source
I have picked out points from the original text that seem relevant to work and daily life.
① Active Memory 
・Remembers important things about you across conversations 
・Recalls and uses information relevant to the current task
② Active Focus 
・Continues toward the goal even when tasks become difficult 
・Tries different approaches, overcomes deadlocks, and recovers from errors 
・Asks questions when necessary ・Confirms before doing important things
③ Domain Expertise 
・It is written that it can handle tasks related to law, finance, and health 
・I have not confirmed the actual accuracy; please consult an expert for important decisions
④ Coding 
・It is said to handle everything from small apps to complex software
This part is interesting, isn't it? "Continuing toward the goal" sounds similar to OpenAI's "dots" or Microsoft's "Autopilot." However, it is not clear from the original text whether the fine details are the same.

This is also the part everyone is most curious about.

🔒 How was safety described?
Agents may access messages, calendars, and documents. Therefore, the official source explains it as follows:
・Do not share personal information without permission ・Refuse to assist in actually harmful acts ・Strongest measures yet against chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear (CBRN), and cyber misuse 
・On the other hand, keep necessary cyber capabilities available for defense
Furthermore, the research blog states it is the "most aligned model to date." (Alignment is a term referring to AI following human intent and safety standards; this is my explanation.) A detailed report on safety is scheduled to be released "soon."
These are the official explanations. I have not confirmed how effective they actually are.
