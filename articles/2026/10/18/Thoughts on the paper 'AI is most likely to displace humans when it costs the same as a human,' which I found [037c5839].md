# Thoughts on the paper 'AI is most likely to displace humans when it costs the same as a human,' which I found partly true but also partly not

note · 和菓子 · 2026-10-18

Thoughts on the paper 'AI is most likely to displace humans when it costs the same as a human,' which I found partly true but also partly not
From the papers I introduced this week, I will pick one about work and AI and share my thoughts.
What the paper says
This study created a hypothetical labor market inside a computer where AI applies for jobs, passes screenings, gets hired, and gets fired, just like humans, to test what happens. They ran 30 trials for each condition and took the average.
- Unemployment increased the most when the cost of using AI was the same as human wages. With the introduction of AI, the unemployment rate rose by 9.2 percentage points. There were zero layoffs; the entire increase was due to a reduction in new hiring.
- When the cost of AI was 70% of a human's, the unemployment rate rose by 4.1 percentage points. About 60% of the job losses were due to layoffs, and the rest were due to reduced hiring. However, many of those who were laid off found new jobs.
- As AI became much cheaper than humans, human jobs actually became less likely to decrease. This is because when AI is cheap, companies expand their businesses.
- What determines how many jobs are replaced is how much more a product sells when its price drops. The screening mechanism changes who gets hired, but it does not change the number of people replaced.
In other words, it is not necessarily true that 'the cheaper AI is, the more human jobs are taken.' When AI costs the same as a human, companies do not lay off people, but they do reduce the number of new hires. That is the result.
This is strictly a story about a hypothetical market inside a computer and has not been verified in real companies.
My thoughts: Partly true, but partly not
I think this is a story that is partly true, but also partly not. First, I think there are certainly jobs that will become unnecessary as AI becomes cheaper and enters various industries. These are tasks that involve handling data according to fixed procedures on a computer, such as organizing documents or bookkeeping. It is a fact that things that used to take 10 or 20 hours can now be done in an instant, and when that happens, those jobs will definitely disappear.
However, I think it is a bit scary to directly link that to 'a decrease in human employment.' In reality, one can just do other work with the time that has opened up. In other words, humans need to change. It is possible that the content of existing jobs will change, but there are definitely some people who cannot change.
If that happens, jobs that involve spending a lot of time in front of a computer within a company and have few opportunities to talk to people will certainly be taken away. This is because AI is overwhelmingly more accurate and faster at organizing documents and bookkeeping. On the other hand, the number of people who talk to others and bring in work from the outside should increase. We might enter an era where there is an overflow of people in that field instead.
Until now, keeping society running required human labor to move data. That will now be possible with just electricity costs. Running AI is overwhelmingly cheaper than hiring people. I think the efficiency of work will be defragmented all at once, and the cost of running society itself will drop significantly.
When that happens, those who have built their careers solely on that type of work will be required to change. There will be people who end up leaving their jobs or whose accumulated careers themselves disappear. I think there is still a bit of a challenge left regarding how those people should live.
If humanity gains spare time
Even so, I think there are things that will be created by the introduction of AI. Until now, society has been kept running by everyone working quite hard. What will happen if it becomes possible to keep it running without that much effort?
First, in terms of money, some people might be able to earn a living more easily. And then there is human time. When the time that was tied up in labor is liberated and humanity gains a little spare time, how will humanity change? That is what I am thinking about.
