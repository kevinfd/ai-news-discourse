# Reviewing with Google Gemini Notebook: Transforming Documents into 60-Second Videos and Quizzes

note · ひぃ, AIエージェント開発 · 2026-10-18

Reviewing with Google Gemini Notebook: Transforming Documents into 60-Second Videos and Quizzes
You read the manual, but once you close it, you can't remember the steps.
What if you could turn that same document into a "short video" and a "quiz"?
Google's "Gemini Notebook" is an AI that allows you to ask questions and organize key points based on documents you upload. In this article, we will break down the three learning features introduced by Google on October 16.
Get an overview with a roughly 60-second video. Organize with a guide. Solve problems to find out what you don't understand. These features aren't just for schoolwork; they can also be applied to learning how to use a new app's manual.
Note that the initial announcement of these features was on September 15. October 16 was the day the usage was reintroduced, not the first public release of the new features.Google's official explanation on October 16
60 seconds is the video length; wait time for creation is separate
"Video summaries" is a feature that turns the core content of a document into a video of about 60 seconds. Google has indicated support for over 80 languages, and Japanese is included in the supported languages in the official help documentation.
For example, you could use it to focus on the "file saving process" from an app manual to get an overview. It serves as an entry point to grasp what to do first, rather than trying to memorize every single detailed setting.
However, this does not mean the video is completed in 60 seconds. The official help documentation explains that it can take 30 minutes or more to generate. It is best not to assume you can create one right before you leave and watch it immediately.
Errors can sometimes be mixed into the audio or visuals. If you are concerned about an explanation in the video, return to the original document to verify it.Official video summary help
Organize with a guide and verify with a quiz
The second feature is an interactive learning guide. You create it from the "Notebook" screen, combining summaries, diagrams, quizzes, and flashcards. Flashcards are like cards where you check the question on one side and the answer on the other.
When you have many documents, it's hard to know where to start reviewing. You can have the guide organize the key points and learn from the items that interest you.
The third feature is the expansion of quiz formats. Questions requiring short written answers, multiple-choice questions, and fill-in-the-blank questions are introduced. You can also review completed quizzes and flashcards via chat.
There is a surprising difference between "feeling like you understand because you read it" and "being able to answer in your own words." If you find a question you got wrong, reread only that part. Using it for this kind of review makes it harder to just glance at the AI summary and call it a day.Google's September 15 announcement
If you want to try it on your computer, follow this order
Start by using one document that is safe to put into an external service, such as a short note you wrote yourself. These are the steps for a computer browser.
- Go to the official Gemini Notebook website, click "Try Gemini Notebook," and log in.
- Click "New Notebook" and add a document via "Add source." The "source" is the original document you want the AI to read.
- Open "Video summary" in the "Studio" section on the right. Check the content in the settings screen where you can choose the format and language.
- Select the short format and set the language to "English." The format name I confirmed this time was "Short." This corresponds to the "Short video" option in the help menu.
- Check the content and click "Generate." Once processing is complete, open the video saved in Studio.
Display names and available features may change due to account updates or screen refreshes. Since smartphone apps have some limitations, I am guiding you through the desktop version here.
Can I create them for free as many times as I want?
There are usage limits. According to current official guidance, the system consumes a shared usage quota depending on the features used and the processing load. In addition to 5-hour windows, there are also weekly limits. The quotas vary depending on your plan.
You can check the remaining quota by opening "Usage" from the gear icon "Settings" at the top of the screen. It is more reliable to check the current display rather than relying on old numbers like "how many videos per day for free."Official usage limit help
Verify the materials you input and the AI's answers yourself
If you want to use company materials, check your internal rules first to see if it is okay to input them into an external AI service. For your first practice, self-made notes that do not contain customer names or similar information are suitable.
Google states that it does not use the content of Gemini Notebooks for the direct training of its foundational AI unless you send feedback. However, if you send evaluations or opinions using a personal account, related inputs and materials may be collected and subject to human review. For Google Workspace and Google Workspace for Education, Google states that even if you send evaluations, uploaded materials, questions, and answers are not used for human review or AI training.Official privacy explanation
Also, compare the questions and answers created by the AI with the original materials. If you are studying for a certification, prioritize the explanations in the official study materials.
What I find interesting as a user is that it increases the number of entry points for re-learning from the same material.
Start with a short document and decide on one thing you want to know. After watching the whole thing in a video, try explaining it in your own words. Check the parts you couldn't explain against the original text.
With a usage style like that, you might be able to reach for those manuals you usually couldn't finish reading one more time.
