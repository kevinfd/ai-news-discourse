# AI Semiconductor Bubble Burst for NISA Beginners

AI Semiconductor Bubble Burst for NISA Beginners
PF Commander (a veteran focused on risk management) "The situation is extremely dangerous. The valuations of the AI and semiconductor sectors, led by major semiconductor firms, are completely following the trajectory of the past IT bubble. Once the collapse begins, there is a risk that the SOX index and NASDAQ 100 could fall by more than half. Beginners should take profits immediately and move their funds to 'SCHD (investment trust),' which consists of high-quality companies with high resistance to declines and a history of consecutive dividend increases. Now is the phase to strengthen your defenses!"
High-Tech Growth NISA User (a beginner all-in on semiconductors) "No way, Commander, you're being too timid! Unlike during the IT bubble, today's semiconductor companies are generating massive, tangible profits. AI isn't a passing fad; it's the infrastructure for the Fourth Industrial Revolution, right? If I run away to boring old-economy high-dividend stocks like SCHD now, I'll miss out on the explosive gains of the next 10 years. Since the new NISA's tax-free quota is for growth, going all-in on high-tech is the only choice!"
PF Commander "A company's fundamentals and its stock valuation are two different things! Look at Cisco Systems in 2000. It continued to generate profits as a leader of the internet, but it took over 20 years to catch up to its abnormal stock price at the time. Even if the AI revolution is real, the current stock price perfectly incorporates growth for several years ahead. If they miss earnings even slightly, it's Niagara Falls. With SCHD's constituent stocks, you can maintain your mental state with stable cash flow and dividends even if the entire market becomes a scorched wasteland. The most important thing in investing is 'not being forced out of the market'!"
High-Tech Growth NISA User "I'm not going to be forced out! If it goes down, it's just a big bargain sale! Since I'm buying with daily accumulation (dollar-cost averaging), a crash is actually lucky because I can buy more shares for the same amount of money. Conversely, if I use my NISA quota for a dividend-focused fund like SCHD, I'll consume my tax-free quota every time a dividend is paid, and if it's a US stock, 10% will be deducted for local taxes, so it's inefficient, right? Since I have youth and time, volatility is a source of return, not a risk!"
PF Commander "Saying 'it's lucky if it goes down' is armchair theory that you can only say now because your own assets aren't actually showing a minus 50% return! Once you experience the terror of your assets melting away by tens of thousands of yen every day, the typical beginner behavior is to cancel your accumulation settings and panic-sell at the bottom. That is precisely why I recommend a temporary evacuation to SCHD, which softens volatility and provides dividends as a 'mental stabilizer' even during a downturn. You can just return to high-tech after the worst is over."
High-Tech Growth NISA User "Trying to time the market is why people fail, that's what influencer XX said! 'Be in the market when the lightning strikes.' What if AI surges again while I'm hiding in SCHD? In the end, holding high-tech investment trusts firmly and just 'playing dead' is the best answer!"
[General Review] Both arguments have a point as investment theories.
- The Commander's Logic: No matter how excellent the theme, being 'overbought (buying at a high price)' is the greatest risk. Rotating into SCHD (consecutive dividend increases, high-financial-strength value stocks) is an extremely rational tactic to protect assets.
- The NISA User's Logic: Considering the compatibility between the new NISA's permanent tax-free system and 'long-term, cumulative' investing, continuing to hold (buy and hold) high-growth indices without selling is also a classic path.
