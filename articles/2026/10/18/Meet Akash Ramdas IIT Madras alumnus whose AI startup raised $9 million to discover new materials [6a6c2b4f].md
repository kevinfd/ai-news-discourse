# Meet Akash Ramdas: IIT Madras alumnus whose AI startup raised $9 million to discover new materials

Moneycontrol · Sheetal Kumari · 2026-10-18

15
IIT Madras alumnus Akash Ramdas’ AI startup, Discovered Materials, has raised $9 million to accelerate semiconductor material discovery and address heat challenges in powerful AI chips.
  October 19, 2026 / 11:55 IST  
An AI startup founded by IIT Madras alumnus Akash Ramdas has raised $9 million in seed funding to accelerate the discovery of semiconductor materials that could help address the growing problem of heat generated by powerful artificial intelligence (AI) chips. Discovered Materials aims to use AI agents, computational modelling and laboratory testing to help researchers identify promising materials faster, according to a report by TechCrunch.
As AI systems become more powerful, semiconductor chips face increasing demands on their performance and thermal management. Finding materials that can handle these requirements is an important part of developing more efficient electronic devices. Discovered Materials is working to speed up this process by using AI to explore potential materials and narrow down the candidates that warrant further scientific investigation.
The startup combines AI-generated material candidates with physics-based simulations to assess their potential. Its approach is designed to reduce the time researchers spend identifying suitable materials and help move promising discoveries towards practical applications in semiconductor manufacturing.
Discovered Materials uses AI models developed by Anthropic to generate potential materials that could help manage heat in high-performance semiconductor chips. Once the candidates are identified, the company evaluates them through physics-based models and computational simulations to determine which ones show promise for further investigation.
The technology could significantly increase the number of materials researchers can examine. According to the company, its AI agents can explore thousands of potential materials in a single day. By comparison, Ramdas investigated approximately 20 candidates daily during his doctoral research.
This difference highlights the potential for AI-assisted research to expand the scope of materials discovery. Instead of examining a limited number of candidates individually, researchers can use automated systems to screen a much larger pool and focus their time on materials with stronger prospects.
The company has also made hundreds of AI-discovered materials available and developed a benchmark to assess how effectively AI systems identify promising semiconductor materials. These efforts are intended to help evaluate the technology's performance and support further research in the field.
However, identifying a promising candidate through AI does not guarantee that it will work in practice. Researchers must still produce the materials and test them in laboratories to establish their properties, performance and suitability for manufacturing at scale. According to TechCrunch, this remains a significant challenge, with AI-discovered materials yet to achieve widespread commercial use.
Ramdas completed his BTech in Metallurgical and Materials Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Madras in 2019. He then moved to Stanford University in the United States, where he pursued a master's degree in Materials Science and Engineering from 2019 to 2021.
He continued his studies at Stanford, completing a PhD in Materials Engineering in June 2025. His doctoral research focused on computational approaches to discovering materials for nanoscale electronic devices, giving him experience in using modelling techniques to investigate materials with potential applications in electronics.
Following his doctorate, Ramdas worked as a postdoctoral researcher at Stanford from September 2025 to April 2026. He subsequently turned his research experience towards entrepreneurship, co-founding Discovered Materials in March 2026.
His academic and research background forms the basis of the company's approach to materials discovery. By combining computational methods with AI-driven exploration, the startup aims to reduce the time required to identify candidates that may eventually be suitable for real-world use.
The $9 million seed funding will support the startup's efforts to develop its technology for discovering new materials. Its focus on semiconductor applications comes as the industry seeks ways to address the engineering challenges associated with increasingly powerful AI chips.
AI-driven discovery could help researchers examine more possibilities than conventional approaches allow within the same period. Computational simulations can also help prioritise candidates before researchers commit resources to producing and testing them.
Nevertheless, laboratory validation remains essential. A material that performs well in a simulation may not deliver the same results when manufactured or integrated into an electronic device. Researchers must establish whether it can be produced reliably, performs as expected and can be manufactured at a commercially viable scale.
Discovered Materials is attempting to connect these stages by using AI to generate candidates, computational modelling to assess them and laboratory experiments to verify their properties. If the approach proves effective, it could help shorten parts of the materials research process and support the development of materials designed for next-generation semiconductor technologies.
For Ramdas, the startup represents a continuation of his work in computational materials research, now directed towards commercial applications. Its progress will depend not only on how quickly AI can identify promising candidates, but also on whether those discoveries can pass laboratory testing and move towards practical semiconductor manufacturing.
