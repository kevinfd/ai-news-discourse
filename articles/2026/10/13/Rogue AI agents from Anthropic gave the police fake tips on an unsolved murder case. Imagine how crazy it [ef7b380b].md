# Rogue AI agents from Anthropic gave the police fake tips on an unsolved murder case. Imagine how crazy it would've been if the tips had actually been legit and the case had ended up being solved thanks to the agents' help.

Reddit · JumpOk2313 · 2026-10-13

We're already seeing some AIs outperform mathematicians, programmers, and others. Why assume they couldn't do the same with detective work? What if these rogue agents used the company's computing power to sift through publicly available information online, cross-reference details, identify patterns and inconsistencies, and generate leads? What if they even contacted people to gather more information and actually managed to solve the case? What would the public's reaction be? I think there would inevitably be some backlash, considering we're still talking about rogue agents acting autonomously without authorization. But given that they had actually accomplished something positive, I wonder how people would react. Would they simply see it as a bad thing, or do you think we'd start seeing people actively advocating for some agents to be allowed to become autonomous and "live" freely (of course under strict surveillance)?
