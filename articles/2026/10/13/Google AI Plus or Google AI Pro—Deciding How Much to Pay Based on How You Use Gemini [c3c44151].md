# Google AI Plus or Google AI Pro—Deciding How Much to Pay Based on How You Use Gemini

note · 業務Dx×Ai｜Gas・Excel自動化 · 2026-10-13

Google AI Plus or Google AI Pro—Deciding How Much to Pay Based on How You Use Gemini
Starting October 9, Gemini apps for personal accounts without a paid plan have been gradually switching to answering most questions using a lighter model (Flash-Lite). In my October 5 article, I discussed how to check if employees are using personal free versions for work, and concluded by writing that it could be a catalyst for deciding whether to sign a corporate contract.
Today, I am following up on that with a discussion on which one to sign up for. Google's AI plans are divided into four tiers: Free, Google AI Plus, Google AI Pro, and Google AI Ultra. According to Google's pricing page (as of October 14), Plus is 725 yen per month, and Pro is 2,900 yen per month. In this article, I will categorize Gemini usage into six types and organize which tier is sufficient for each.
Decide "where you will use it" before the monthly fee
In January, I wrote an article comparing Google's AI plans.
At that time, Plus was 1,200 yen per month with 200GB of storage, and Pro was 2,900 yen per month with 2TB. If you open the pricing page now, Plus is 725 yen per month with 400GB (a 1,450 yen per month version with 2TB is also listed), and Pro remains 2,900 yen per month but with 5TB. Both the price and capacity have changed in three places.
The help table has also changed. When I read it on October 5, the table indicated that only Flash-Lite could be used without a plan. When I opened it again on October 14, the column for no plan had changed to "Automatic." The explanation is that most questions are answered by Flash-Lite, while Flash or Pro may be used for questions requiring deep reasoning (if you turn off the automatic selection, it will always be Flash-Lite).
Before comparing the price list, what you want to decide is where you will use Gemini. The differences between the plans are divided into the following four categories.
- Models you can choose: You must have at least the Pro plan to manually select Pro models (higher-end models that are strong at complex questions). With Plus, you can only select up to Flash-Lite and Flash (it is stated that users will be notified individually by email when this applies to Plus users)
- Usage limits: The limit is twice that of the free version (Plus) and four times (Pro). It resets every 5 hours, and there are also weekly limits. Using Pro models or generating images/videos consumes the limit faster
- Where you can use it: Comparing work in email, documents, and spreadsheets, what you can use with Plus is Gemini within Gmail. With Pro, you can also use it within Google Docs and Sheets in addition to that
- Storage capacity: Plus is 400GB, and Pro is 5TB (total for Gmail, Google Drive, and Google Photos)
Of these four, the one I thought was most effective is "where you can use it." Both Plus and Pro are contracts for personal Google accounts, and the Gmail where you can use Gemini within Gmail is also that account's Gmail. Since my company's email runs on Microsoft 365, the Gemini within Gmail that you get with Plus does not reach my work email.
Six types of Gemini usage and the plan to choose
Please decide first which one you fall into. If you fall into multiple, match it to the heaviest type.
1. The Drafts type: People who use it for short emails or rephrasing
For thank-you emails, scheduling text, or organizing bullet points, I think staying on the free version is sufficient. The free "Automatic" setting is said to route questions requiring deep reasoning to higher models. However, you cannot choose them yourself. If you are concerned, look at the usage limits; if you start hitting the limit repeatedly, look at the next type.
2. The Gmail type: People who have their correspondence with business partners in their personal Gmail
This is for people who also receive work emails in their personal Gmail. If you want to ask for draft replies or rephrasing within Gmail, Plus (725 yen/month) is a candidate. The storage capacity also becomes 400GB, so I think it is just right for people whose Gmail storage is starting to fill up. If your company email is on Microsoft 365 or your company's Google Workspace (Google's groupware for businesses), buying Plus will not affect those emails. I am not this type.
3. The Long Documents type: People who have it read financial statements or contracts and provide material for judgment
This is a usage where you provide long PDFs and ask, "List the clauses I should be concerned about" or "What has changed compared to the previous term?" The difference here is the model. Since you must have at least the Pro plan to manually select Pro models, Pro (2,900 yen/month) is the benchmark.
4. For Table and Document Users: Those who use it within Google Docs and Sheets
This is for people who manage estimate details in Sheets and want to ask for calculations or organization while staying on that screen. In Google's feature comparison, document creation in Docs and data analysis in Sheets are covered by Pro and above. What you can use with Plus are document creation and proofreading in Gmail, email summaries and reply suggestions, and some features of the video creation tool Google Vids. For this as well, Pro is the benchmark.
5. For Daily Power Users: Those who hit the Pro limit multiple times a week
Ultra starts at 14,500 yen per month, and the usage capacity is 5 times that of Pro (the 32,000 yen per month tier is 20 times). In the help table, the column for models available is the same for Pro and Ultra. Since what increases significantly is the usage capacity (along with storage and other benefits), I don't think it's necessary for people who have never hit the Pro limit.
6. For Those Who Want Employees to Use It Too
From here on, we are no longer talking about individual plans. Both Plus and Pro are contracts for personal Google accounts and are placed outside of where a company manages contracts (this is what I wrote about in the October 5th article). Google's pricing page also guides you to a different product, Gemini Enterprise, if you are using it with a work account. If you are distributing it to employees, I think it makes more sense to consider it as a company contract.
Individual contracts vs. company contracts
What you can do changes just as much based on which account you buy it with as it does on which plan you buy. Here is my company's setup and two other forms.
- Personal Pro + Company Email is Microsoft 365: This is my company's setup. I use Google tools by subscribing to Google AI Pro with my personal @gmail.com account. When I looked into Google's electronic signatures in the September 14th article, I wrote, 'Although it is a paid plan, it is not included in the eligible plans for electronic signatures, so while you can be the signer, you cannot be the one requesting a signature.' The features you can use are determined by the type of contract, not the amount you pay.
- Personal Plan + API (a gateway to call AI from other software): I use Gemini via API for transcribing internal videos and checking pronunciations. This is a separate gateway where you pay only for what you use, and the billing is separate from the Gemini app plan.
- Company Contract (Google Workspace, etc.): If you want to distribute the same tools to all employees, corporate contracts like Google Workspace or Gemini Enterprise are candidates. My company uses Microsoft 365 for email, so we do not use this form. Please check the pricing and the scope of Gemini on the corporate pricing page.
Looking to the future, just three things
- The table changes faster than you think. Between January and October, the pricing and capacity changed in three places, and the help table was also different between what I read on October 5th and what I opened on the 14th. It says that paying annually is up to 16% cheaper, but I would wait and see with monthly payments until the details settle down.
- If the reason for upgrading to a higher tier is capacity, check the limit first. From Plus to Pro, the models, location, and capacity change. From Pro to Ultra, the column for selectable models is the same, and the limit becomes 5 or 20 times higher. I would check if I am actually hitting the Pro limit before considering it.
- It does not attach to company accounts. Even if you subscribe to the personal Plus or Pro plans, those features will not be attached to your company's Microsoft 365 email or your company's Google Workspace account (mechanisms for sharing with family or ways to pass files for reading are a different story).
How I would choose
Decide which type you are and subscribe according to the benchmark for that type. That's all. If you're unsure, use it for a week for free and try taking notes on what you asked Gemini. If there are many things you want to ask within Gmail, go with Plus; if you have many long documents or spreadsheets, go with Pro; if you want employees to use it too, go with a company contract.
I will also write down what I don't do. I wouldn't pay for Plus or Pro on an employee's personal account with company money. This is because both the contract and the text entered there would be placed where the company cannot see them. Also, I wouldn't buy Ultra just because 'I might make videos eventually'.
