# China's high speed rail network is probably a harbinger of the future of Ai. Massive rail network, massive debt, operating at loss, low actual usage.

Reddit · pman6 · 2026-10-13

I've been casually looking at what a clusterfuck of a situation china is in lately with their economy, and I can't help thinking that very important lessons can be learned from china's mistakes. I was curious how much of current ai buildout is actually being used, since it feels like they are just overbuilding at any cost. Google search says this... Massive Overbuilding / Ahead of Curve: According to analysis from Bain & Company, existing consumer and enterprise AI services generate a fraction of what is being invested (estimated around $1.8 trillion out of a required $6 trillion in annual/cumulative value by 2031). Industry reports note that AI infrastructure is being deployed significantly ahead of the demand curve, relying heavily on speculative future use cases in robotics, autonomous machines, and specialized enterprise applications China built a massive rail network, putting train stations everywhere. What ended up happening is the entire country's rail network operates at a massive financial loss. Many train stations don't even have the ridership to justify putting a station there, so they abandoned them. High speed train is relatively expensive as they raise prices, so citizens opt to ride the cheaper slow train. . I just can't help seeing the analogy of overbuilding and eventual under-utilization.
