# How will life find an equilibrium?

Reddit · Both-Move-8418 · 2026-10-13

Lets say generously 10 years from now, everyone has staggering capability unlimited AI agents at their disposal for £20/month. 80% of computer based jobs have been hoovered up. Unemployment is extremely high. Negligable new jobs have been created for those that are now automated. There is an immense welfare bill. Demand for any products is mostly just for essentials, not luxuries. So many companies selling non essentials fold. A new equilibrium must surely emerge. It doesn't sound like a good model for society to run on. So before we get to they dire state of affairs, will government's start introducing an AI tax? Or somehow disincentivising the use of AI in work? Will people become far more entrepreneurial, so big businesses fold but there is a mass of fast moving tiny companies in their place? (Design a car, submit it for build test at a robo factory, release it relatively cheaply, get a tiny marketshare but enough to pay your ok liestyle). Prices for products plummet to increase demand and because manufacturing of new items is fast and cheap? Or will there be so much anti AI backlash that companies start advertising that they are a human-first company, and the AI first companies get dropped? Mass protests? Everyone realises that an society/economy doesn't work if most of the jobs get outsourced to robots? At the moment, society will just be a big race to the bottom for quick short term gain, but maybe an equal and opposite shift will emerge to create a new equilibrium... Same old question that everyone posts daily I guess.
