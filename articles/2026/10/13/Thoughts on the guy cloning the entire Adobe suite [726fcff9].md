# Thoughts on the guy cloning the entire Adobe suite?

Reddit · gaurishanker10 · 2026-10-13

Hey, Recently I saw a tweet and a bunch of YT videos about the cloning of the whole Adobe suite apps such as Premiere Pro, Illustrator, Photoshop, Designer etc with Claude. now, some of those apps have been around for over 35 years. I was highly distrustful that the gigantic apps such as these can be vibe-coded in just 2 weeks. Now, I’ve started seeing a lot of Twitter posts saying these cloned apps are super buggy and not thought out at all. The developer apparently just tried to copy the features for the sake of it, more focused on one‑to‑one visual mapping of the UI than the underlying robustness. Now, I haven’t tried any of them myself, but as someone who’s been dabbling in AI for the past two years and has built a few small‑to‑medium apps (two of them over 250k LOC) , the idea of cloning seven massive, decades old beast‑like programs, including for a platform Adobe doesn’t even support (Linux), is just bonkers. It feels like pure AI hype, totally untested, and if you dig deeper, nobody serious would actually use it. (Fireship and others have tested it and reviews are not flattering.) But sure, it sounds great for headlines. I’ve been working on a simple descript-like video editor and a few other video production tools that fit my workflow. They’ve grown a lot, but it took me over six months because I was more focused on stripping out bloat than adding new features. Every part of my app—from implementation to architecture—has been thought through. I haven't read the code but the each and every part of internal design and architecture has been built after thorough too and fro discussion (sometimes after discussion of 3 to 4 hours for one simple feature). I always use 2 to 3 other AI tools for cross-audit each other's changes and implementation. They don't have too many features to boast but they are pretty robust, reliable and extensible. In my opinion, if you don’t pay attention to those details, you would never realize how bad AI is (Even latest models like Opus 5.5 and GPT Astra) at making solid architectural or design decisions that make codebase robust, reliable, extensible, and maintainable. What do you think? Am I just slow at developing apps, or is the world moving faster and these guys have some secret prompts that make AI crank out such huge apps fast? PS: Would you be comfortable using any of the free blindly vibe-coded apps rather than truly open-source apps which are duly vetted and designed like Da Vinci Resolve, GIMP, and others? It feels insulting to these apps that people are celebrating these apps when such open-source alternatives have existed for more than 20 years. I am sure no one is going to use any of these apps, not because they are vibe coded, but because there seems to have been given 0 thoughts into building them. To make my point home, the developer is now developing open-source Microsoft office.
