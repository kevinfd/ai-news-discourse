# Everything announced in Apples Welcome Home release: New smart speakers, displays, and cameras

Mashable · Chance Townsend · 2026-10-13

Everything announced in Apple's 'Welcome Home' release: New smart speakers, displays, and cameras
Apple made four big announcements on Tuesday as it vies to break into the smart home business.
The announcement, part of a slate of press releases stealth-dropped by Apple (following one brief tease from Greg "Joz" Joswiak, Apple Senior Vice President of Marketing), includes a new smart display called Apple HomeView, a second-generation HomePod mini, a fourth-generation Apple TV 4K, and the Apple Home Collection, a line of smart security cameras, thermostats, and a door lock built with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric.
Unlike previous Apple smart home products, all of the new releases lean on Siri AI, the rebuilt version of Siri that Apple is now bringing to its home devices. Strangely, Siri AI will not be available at launch on the new HomePod mini 2, but only in a future update.

The announcements were not a total surprise. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman has reported for months on what to expect from Apple's October releases, and the HomePod refresh was the most anticipated piece. It is the first new HomePod hardware in roughly three years, since HomePod (2nd generation) arrived in early 2023, and the first update to the HomePod mini since the original launched in 2020.
Notably, the M6 MacBooks that many observers expected this month were absent. Gurman has indicated they're still set for release in October, however. For now, the company is focusing on the smart home, a category where Amazon and Google have long set the pace. Gurman has argued in his newsletter that the AI boom caught Apple off guard, and that to succeed, the company needs to push into areas it had previously been content to leave to competitors.
Siri AI is the common thread across all four announcements. Apple describes it as an entirely new version of Siri built on its latest Apple Foundation Models, with broad world knowledge, personal context, and more natural back-and-forth conversation. Users without an Apple Intelligence-enabled iPhone or iPad still get the general knowledge and conversation features, but personal requests require a paired device.
Here's everything that was announced as part of the Apple "Welcome Home" launch:
Apple HomeView
The headline product is HomeView, Apple's first smart display. It pairs a 7-inch Liquid Retina screen with a domed speaker base, joined by a stainless steel hinge that lets the display tilt. Apple pitches it as a personalized, shared household device. HomeView can recognize up to six people by their faces, and each gets their own Home Screen of apps and widgets.
According to Apple, information from apps like Messages and Calendar only appears when an enrolled user is recognized and their paired iPhone or iPad is nearby and recently unlocked. Shared photo albums and music remain available to everyone, including guests.
HomeView costs $299 in silver, starlight, rose pink, and space gray. A wall-mounted model in space gray and silver is also $299 and attaches with one of three Schneider Home mounts:
- Power Mount USB-C Wall Connector: $59.95
- Power Mount AC Wired: $79.95
- Power Mount PoE Wired: $129.95, arriving in early 2027
Pre-orders opened Tuesday in the U.S., Canada, the U.K., Australia, India, and New Zealand, with Singapore following on Oct. 15. Availability begins Nov. 13. HomeView will not be sold in the EU because Siri AI isn’t currently available on iOS there. Apple says it is working with the EU on a path forward.
HomePod mini 2
The second-generation HomePod mini keeps the familiar spherical design in five colors: pink, green, blue, white, and midnight. Apple says it runs on the S10 chip and uses computational audio to adjust dynamic range and loudness in real-time. Four HomePod mini 2 speakers can now be paired with the new Apple TV 4K for a four-speaker surround setup that calibrates timing and levels to the room. The speakers must be in matching front-and-rear pairs.
HomePod mini 2 can act as a speaker for Macs, serve as a home hub, support Sound Recognition for smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and announce visitors identified by Face Recognition on compatible doorbells.
In its press release, Apple called it the company's most immersive home theater experience and framed it as one piece of a connected home alongside HomeView, HomePod mini 2, and Apple Home Collection. HomePod mini 2 costs $149 in pink, green, blue, white, and midnight.
Pre-orders opened Tuesday in the U.S. and more than 23 other countries and regions, with availability beginning Oct. 30.
Apple TV 4K (4th generation)
The new Apple TV 4K brings Siri AI to the TV with a full-screen interface for finding shows and movies. Apple's examples include asking which Apple TV shows won Emmys, asking follow-up questions about a show while watching, and, with a paired iPhone, requests that rely on personal context, such as playing a show a friend recommended in a message. Answers to mid-show questions appear in a small overlay rather than taking over the screen.
Apple says the A19 chip delivers up to 50 percent faster CPU and up to 2x faster GPU performance than the previous generation, and adds a dedicated AV1 decoder. The Siri Remote gains Find My with Precision Finding. As a home hub, the Apple TV 4K now offers Apple Intelligence-generated camera activity descriptions and summarized notifications in the Home app.
For the first time, the box has built-in far-field microphones, three of them, so users can talk to Siri without picking up the remote. Apple says echo cancellation lets it hear "Siri" even while audio is playing. Apple called it the company’s most immersive home theater experience in the release.
The Apple TV 4K costs $299, or $49.83 per month for six months. Pre-orders opened Tuesday in the U.S. and 29 other countries and regions, with availability beginning Nov. 13.
Apple Home Collection
Apple Home Collection is a line of smart devices designed with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric. Each product sets up with a tap of an iPhone and is managed entirely in the Home app, with no separate app or account. Every device supports Thread and updates its own Wi-Fi credentials if the home network changes.
LG is supplying four cameras. All record up to 2.5K video, recognize people, pets, vehicles, and packages, and store 10 days of history through iCloud+, so there’s no separate camera subscription:
- Video Doorbell Wired ($249.95): front and package-facing cameras
- Floodlight Cam ($399.95)
- Outdoor Cam Plug-In ($299.95)
- Indoor Cam Plug-In ($199.95): physically tilts down to stop watching when someone in the household comes home
The cameras will be sold in the U.S., Canada, and the U.K.
LG’s Smart Deadbolt Lock ($349.95, U.S. and Canada) uses Ultra Wideband to unlock automatically as a user with a home key on iPhone or Apple Watch approaches. It also offers tap-to-unlock, a PIN pad, and a backup key, and it works locally without an internet connection.
Rounding out the line are smart thermostats from both LG and Schneider Electric, plus standalone temperature and humidity sensors. An Adaptive Temperature feature adjusts heating and cooling based on whether anyone is home. Apple did not list prices for the thermostats or sensors.
The collection arrives in December, though some accessories won’t ship until early 2027. Apple Retail will also offer paid professional installation through ADT in the U.S.
