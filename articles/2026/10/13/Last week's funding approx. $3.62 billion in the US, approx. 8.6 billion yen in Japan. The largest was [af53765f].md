# Last week's funding: approx. $3.62 billion in the US, approx. 8.6 billion yen in Japan. The largest was TypeSafe AI at $870 million | Startup Weekly 10/5-10/9

note · マイキー｜経営×投資 · 2026-10-13

Last week's funding: approx. $3.62 billion in the US, approx. 8.6 billion yen in Japan. The largest was TypeSafe AI at $870 million | Startup Weekly 10/5-10/9
In this article, I summarize weekly startup funding in Japan and the US from the perspective of a former strategy consultant involved in investment and startup management. While weekday articles take a deep dive into two companies per day, the weekly summary starts with the big picture by round and sector, looking at what kind of businesses are attracting capital and who is providing it. This time, we cover deals from last week, Monday, October 5th to Friday, October 9th.
Table of Contents
In 3 lines
- As far as I could confirm, there were 17 equity funding deals of $20 million or more for US-headquartered companies, totaling approximately $3.62 billion. The largest was TypeSafe AI, a developer of non-text AI models ($870 million, $7.5 billion valuation), which accounted for 24% of the total on its own.
- By round, Series B was the most frequent and highest in value, with 8 deals totaling $1.38 billion. By sector, 12 companies with AI at the core of their business raised approximately $2.24 billion (62%), 3 companies in nuclear power, fusion, and electricity raised $825 million (23%), and 1 quantum computing company raised $475 million.
- In Japan, the largest major deal I could confirm was fusion company Helical Fusion (Series B 2nd close, 3.23 billion yen), followed by contact center AI company Frail (Series A, 1.88 billion yen) and indent (Series B, 1.1 billion yen), which JAFCO participated in.
US: Number of deals, amount, and by round

Debt, non-US companies, and the $2 billion investment in Axiom (which is being spun off from electronics contract manufacturer Flex, with General Catalyst and Koch Equity Development acquiring shares from Flex) were excluded. - Series A: 4 deals, $513 million (Atlas Atomics approx. $400 million, Gallatin AI $50 million, Healthleap $38 million (combined Seed and Series A), Rein Security $25 million)

Insights from last week's deals
1. Some companies are seeing valuations rise rapidly in a matter of months
Oratomic's valuation rose from $1.5 billion to approximately $5.4 billion in about three months, and TypeSafe AI raised funds at a $7.5 billion valuation just weeks after its product launch. Neither has disclosed revenue. Like Instinct from the previous week (which went from $2.5 billion to $10 billion in about a month), these are fundraisings where value is placed on technology and talent rather than revenue.
2. Data center power is becoming a target for startup investment
Voltus raised funds to expand its power programs for data centers, and two nuclear/fusion companies also added corporate investors (Siemens Energy Ventures) and energy trading firms (Vitol). In Japan, Fuji Electric, Fujikura, and Tokyo Gas have invested in Helical Fusion. Corporate capital in both Japan and the US is flowing toward the power needed to run AI computing resources.
3. Japan often uses a combination of equity and debt for fundraising
Of the 23 Japanese deals last week, 4 were debt-only (14.05 billion yen disclosed), and 4 companies—Henry, indent, The Phage, and Every WiLL—disclosed amounts that combined equity and debt. Adding only the disclosed amounts would lead to an overestimation of the amount raised through equity.
