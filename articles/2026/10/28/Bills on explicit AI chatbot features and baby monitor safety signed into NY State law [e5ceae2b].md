# Bills on explicit AI chatbot features and baby monitor safety signed into NY State law

News10NBC · Evan Bourtis · 2026-10-28

Bills on explicit AI chatbot features and baby monitor safety signed into NY State law
ALBANY, N.Y. — A bill aimed at protecting children from explicit AI chatbot features and a bill aimed at boosting privacy for internet-connected baby monitors have been signed into New York State law.
Gov. Kathy Hochul signed both bills on Wednesday after they passed both chambers of the NY Legislature. One law prevents AI companies from offering “unsafe features” to children under 18, including any sexually explicit content or chatbots that try to build a personal or romantic relationship.
More on AI chatbot regulations for children
In the coming months, chatbots will also be prohibited from promoting suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, or drug and alcohol use for children. In addition, chatbots won’t be allowed to ask unprompted emotional questions, such as using a child’s private health or personal information from earlier conversations.
The law comes as more teens are turning to AI chatbots as companions. The nonprofit Common Sense Media found that 72% of teens surveyed have used an AI companion once, and 52% interacted with the AI companions at least a few times a month. News10NBC spoke with a Nazareth University professor who warned against thinking of AI as a friend instead of a tool.
“As soon as we start identifying these chatbots as having any human-like characteristics, it becomes problematic,” Dr. Erin Cole said. “As soon as we start to think of a chatbot as a friend, as soon as we start maybe even telling the chatbot that we like it, or we miss it, or I love you, even some of the like thank you’s that we do, it really personifies the chatbot and starts to get into that red flag behavior.”
The law also requires companies to have age verification. Companies must have more than one way to verify age, including at least one option that doesn’t depend on a government ID. After it’s used, the age verification information must be deleted.
Baby monitor privacy rules
The other bill the governor signed requires internet-connected baby monitors sold in New York State to include security features that block strangers from watching or listening in. The law will take effect in 90 days.
Lawmakers say baby monitors can be used to spy on families. When security researchers at Rapid7 tested nine internet-connected baby monitors from eight companies, eight monitors received an “F” for security and one received a “D”.
The new law prohibits the sale of new baby monitors that broadcast audio or video over the internet unless they include security features. Monitors must also carry a clear warning label explaining that the device connects to the internet and that families should use its security features.
