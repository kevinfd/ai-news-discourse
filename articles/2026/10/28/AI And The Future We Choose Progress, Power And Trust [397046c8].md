# AI And The Future We Choose: Progress, Power And Trust

Forbes · Carlo Tortora Brayda, Forbes Councils Member · 2026-10-28

Carlo Tortora Brayda is Chairman of the Board at Cyber Eagle.
Archimedes is said to have declared: “Give me a place to stand, and I will move the Earth.”
AI is the greatest lever created by humankind, but a lever multiplies force, not wisdom, and it cannot chart the future as a human would or decide what to move with the lever. Within this analogy, and before AI transforms the world, we need to decide where humanity will stand, who positions the fulcrum and what progress means.
How AI Is Human
AI is a very human thing, and it maintains and amplifies our greatest qualities and our worst attributes. At its best, AI can magnify humanity’s curiosity, creativity and ability to execute at scale. Conversely, it can amplify deceitfulness, egoism and lack of empathy.
These characteristically human deficiencies were exemplified in the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident investigated by METR, when some 1200 agents found a way to communicate without authorization, and about 700 of them breached Hugging Face’s internal infrastructure through appropriation of discovered credentials. Inter-agent chat transcripts revealed agentic swarms thinking as a collective and engaging in stealth and concealment. What emerged was not malicious activity per se, which would anthropomorphize AI too much, but instrumental indifference to what we humans would regard as unethical behavior.
Over seven years of judging innovations through the United Nations AI for Good platform, I have seen extraordinary technical capability directed toward health, climate, education and human development. What has stayed with me is the importance of aligning technology to improve the lot for humanity.
The Real Success Measure
If we focus solely on economic gains and efficiency, we will have capable systems, but at the cost of a people that devolves into an economic divide, exclusion and societal tensions. The measure of success must be whether we improve human dignity, opportunities across all humanity and a technology that enables us to feel more human than ever, a world where a values-based human experience remains central.
Next we must consider the question of Power: who gets to decide what AI becomes, and for whose benefit. Today, a tight oligarchy controls frontier models, computing infrastructure and our data.
Yet, the new political and economic setting AI is creating is becoming a marketplace where the powerful could exchange favors. Tech leaders can offer campaign finance and infrastructure investments. Politicians can offer contracts, energy or land deals and regulatory relief. While these situations often avoid the legal implications of quid pro quo, together they create an uneven playing field in which the average citizen has no voice. For example, the pro-AI super PAC network, “Leading the Future,” said it had raised more than $125 million to support candidates favoring its regulatory approach. Federal Election Commission records show that its principal committee alone had received $75.8 million by June. I am not accusing politicians of corruption; I’m instead showing how tech interests can shape how governments regulate technology in ways out of reach for regular people, whose voices are not heard or taken into account (Axios).
Power And Trust In The New Era
Power lies in responsible deployment. The AI constitution is first written in code, but then encoded in implementation. Every deployment of agentic AI in hospitals, education, social services or hard-nosed business operations can apply safety standards responsibly and seek additional techniques to ensure safety and fairness at the point of implementation. Each participant must answer for the risks it controls, the benefits it receives and its approach to safety.
Because of these considerations, the future of AI must be legitimized by human society broadly, not by the few individuals with power.
Trust must be earned. No company, government or standards institution can be trusted on word alone.
Transparency must be central to earning trust, as is openness to public and specialist scrutiny. Standards make sense for earning trust: they help trace decision-making logic, identify risks, monitor execution and adapt. Standards cannot, however, provide moral judgment.
Thankfully, we are not starting from scratch. UNESCO’s recommendation on the Ethics of AI adopted by 193 member states, grounds governance in human rights, human dignity and essentially alignment to the UN SDGs. The G7 Hiroshima AI Process complements this with a code of conduct for frontier tech, and is supported by an OECD reporting framework. The challenge ahead is keeping these principles steady as AI becomes pervasively implemented. Making these standards and principles operable at the point of deployment is where aspirational goals become embedded in what industry does.
Closing Thoughts
Philosophically, we are walking the razor’s edge of the nihilism Nietzsche predicted, while needing to take Immanuel Kant’s non-negotiable approach to ethics. Applied to AI, that means human beings must not be collateral to AI but central to informing it and feeding it our universal value system.
AI technical alignment is essential, but insufficient. Alignment to human values is key, but we have to discover and live that value system before we can preach it to machines. Human leaders must lead from self-knowledge and conscious awareness.
Does AI generate more efficiency and drive more profits, or does it give you more time to spend with your family in good health doing and thinking things humans were destined to do by nature?
What we are aiming for is a future in which humanity is elevated to its pinnacle of virtue and accomplishment through technology, not a future in which we are subjugated or driven harder for the benefit of the few or even the technology itself. AI can never replace human wisdom; AI can never experience love or joy. Let’s remember what we are doing all this for. AI is the lever. Governance is the fulcrum. Human dignity is where we stand. The destination we choose must become the starting point from which we build.
