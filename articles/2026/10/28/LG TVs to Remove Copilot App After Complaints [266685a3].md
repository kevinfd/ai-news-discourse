# LG TVs to Remove Copilot App After Complaints

PCMag · Michael Kan, Eric Zeman · 2026-10-28

It looks like Microsoft is killing off the Copilot app on LG TVs, nearly a year after the AI assistant’s launch sparked customer complaints.
On Wednesday morning, affected consumers spotted a pop-up on their LG TVs that said: “Notice of Microsoft Copilot Service Termination.” Windows Central first reported the news.
“We would like to inform you that Microsoft has recently decided to end support for Copilot functionality on all TVs, including the Copilot-enabled feature available on our LG TVs,” the notification says. “As a result, the Copilot-enabled feature on your TV is no longer available and may stop functioning shortly.”
The Copilot app is slated for discontinuation later this year. “The specific date is subject to Microsoft’s timeline,” the notification adds.
Microsoft has also confirmed the app's discontinuation in a support page, which says: "Copilot is being retired from TVs and will no longer be available on Samsung and LG TVs on December 1, 2026. After retirement, customers will no longer be able to access Copilot on TVs. Customers will be able to continue talking to Copilot on web, desktop and mobile platforms."
The Copilot app’s introduction on LG TVs faced immediate backlash in December after it was automatically pinned to the TV's home screen and initially couldn't be deleted.
Some said the app was another example of the tech industry needlessly forcing AI and bloatware on consumers. In response, LG created a way for users to delete the app. We suspect the Copilot app wasn’t very popular; it was actually just a shortcut to the website for Microsoft’s assistant and opened on the TV’s web browser, rather than as a native experience.
Last year, Samsung also announced a Copilot integration for its AI-powered TVs and smart monitors, including 2025 TV models like Micro RGB, Neo QLED, OLED, The Frame Pro, and The Frame, as well as smart monitors like M7, M8, and M9. But it looks like Samsung's integration is now dead too.
As for Microsoft, the company has already been rolling out Copilot experiences on Windows 11 to clean up the OS amid user complaints about bloatware. However, in other areas, it's doubling down on AI, particularly with getting AI models to run locally on higher-end PCs.
