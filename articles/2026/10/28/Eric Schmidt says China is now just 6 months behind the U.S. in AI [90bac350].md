# Eric Schmidt says China is now just 6 months behind the U.S. in AI

Fast Company · Robert Safian · 2026-10-28

The AI race between the U.S. and China is the defining competition of our age. Eric Schmidt, former CEO of Google, adviser to the Pentagon, and current CEO of Relativity Space, gives his most unvarnished assessment yet. He reveals that the gap he once estimated at two years has narrowed to six months, and takes on the open-source question that’s keeping Silicon Valley up at night. He also shares the one thing he believes could keep this from becoming a zero-sum game—even if the current political environment makes it nearly impossible.
This is an abridged transcript of an interview from Rapid Response, hosted by former Fast Company editor-in-chief Robert Safian and recorded live at the Masters of Scale Summit 2026. I get whipsawed sometimes by the saber-rattling and jingoism that may be happening around China’s advancement in AI, and I’m glad you can help demystify this for us. The U.S. had a clear first-mover advantage in AI. There are questions about whether that’s going to persist or not. You’ve said the AI gap between the U.S. and China has narrowed to roughly six months. What’s changed most recently?
The biggest change in the last year that’s notable for everyone here is that the cost of software has gone from very expensive to almost nothing. When something falls precipitously in price, you get an awful lot more of it. So when I think about the next few years, all I think about is an enormous amount of software: what will it do, who will do it, how will it work together, how will agents work, and so forth.
China thinks the same way, and my own view is that the China-U.S. competition is the defining competition of your generation, my generation, of our age. The reason is that both are trying very hard to develop reasoning and intelligence systems that are superhuman. That doesn’t mean like us. It means they have superhuman or superintelligence capabilities that are already beyond human capabilities. When you look at the Waymos running outside, they all have better vision than human drivers, especially because they don’t drink. You sort of get the idea, right?
In the race, I was part of the group that put restrictions on China some years ago on chips, and I assumed they would work for about 10 years. A year or two ago, it looked like China was a year or two behind. Now it looks like they’re six months behind, and there are many studies that show this.
How have they done this? After all, didn’t we restrict their access to chips? We did, but they’re so smart they figured out a way to do the kind of algorithms we develop, and use the knowledge we develop, with essentially one-tenth of the energy and, in some factors, less performance. Now, there’s a huge debate in the industry as to whether that gap will get smaller or bigger. But at the moment, it’s fair to say that it’s a two-horse race, and that, to me, is the defining one.
