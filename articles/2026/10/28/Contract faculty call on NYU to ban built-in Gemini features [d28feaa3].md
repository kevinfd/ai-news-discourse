# Contract faculty call on NYU to ban built-in Gemini features

Washington Square News · Rache Hu · 2026-10-28

Contract faculty are urging administrators to disable Google Gemini’s ability to create and edit Google Docs on NYU accounts, arguing that the built-in feature could encourage students to use artificial intelligence in violation of some professors’ policies.
In a resolution passed earlier this month, the Full-Time Continuing Contract Faculty Senators Council asks administrators to remove Gemini integrations from Google Docs, Drive and Chrome. The group’s concern is that students can still open Gemini outside of Google Docs and prompt the AI to create and edit a Google Doc. Then, if students enable Google Workplace permissions on Gemini, the AI will follow the command — but those document changes will appear to have been done by the student in the revision history.
CAS professor Alexander Landfair, who chairs the subcommittee that drafted the resolution, told WSN that the council aims to give faculty more control over AI usage in their courses.
“Our goal is not to disable students from using Gemini,” Landfair said. “The idea is that students can make the decision to use AI more deliberately and more strategically, rather than in response to ambient invitations from Gemini.”
The resolution also expressed concerns over the Gemini feature’s lack of transparency in documents’ revision history, which professors may rely on to check for AI usage. The council requested that administrators Clay Shirky or Jennifer Sparrow — both of whose roles focus on AI and technology — comment on the resolution’s implementation by Dec. 1.
Landfair said that the C-FSC is relying on NYU’s information technology support staff to find a way to disable the Gemini feature.
“We appreciate the complexities and challenges of this issue,” NYU spokesperson Joseph Tirella said in a statement to WSN. “We’re asking our AI Leadership Council to review the resolution, and the related policies and questions that it raises, and will be engaging with the C-FSC and other faculty in short order.”
University Senate councils such as the contract faculty council represent groups of NYU stakeholders, including students, faculty and administrators. While they do not have the power to implement resolutions themselves, they can submit proposals and recommendations for the University Senate to review.
Landfair cited a 2025 MIT study, which found that students who used large language models consistently underperformed in essay writing, as a key motivator for the resolution.
Gallatin senior Ashlee-Marie Joseph said her professors’ AI policies vary, from encouraging students to use generative AI in coursework to outright banning it. Though Joseph said she does not use AI for classwork, she sees how Gemini’s integration into NYU Google accounts may motivate some students to use it.
“As a college student, you’re working late hours, you’re frustrated over assignments — sometimes it’s very enticing and tempting to use the AI suggestion,” Joseph said. “You don’t want to be behind by using your own brain, which sounds ridiculous. But you also don’t want your brain to atrophy by relying on AI so heavily.”
NYU has partnered with Gemini since February 2025 to provide AI services to students. The university also offers discounted Gemini Pro subscriptions to faculty and staff upon request.
GPH professor Chris Dickey, a member of the contract faculty council’s leading committee, told WSN that Gemini integrations could encourage cognitive offloading, in which AI does the thinking instead of the student themselves. He also acknowledged its benefits, such as improved communication, especially for students who speak English as a second language.
“It will probably be a noticeable absence if somebody comes out of NYU and has no ability to work with AI tools,” Dickey said. “We have to let our students understand that there are very constructive ways to incorporate AI, and there are unconstructive, damaging ways to do that. That’s our responsibility to help them navigate.”
