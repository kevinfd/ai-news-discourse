# Jolly Rancher fluorescence

AI Weirdness · Janelle Shane · 2026-10-16

Jolly Rancher fluorescence
I was wondering which Halloween candy would make the best laser, and came across reports that Jolly Ranchers are fluorescent. I got out my UV flashlight and am able to definitely confirm this is true.
The type of fluorescence varies by color, but blue raspberry has a particularly dramatic color change from deep blue to hot pink.
It's interesting to note that the green Jolly Rancher actually seems to be dimmer under UV light. My guess is that it's just absorbing UV light rather than turning it into fluorescence, and it might be more fluorescent under visible light.
The grape flavor Jolly Rancher is phosphorescent, meaning that it continues to give off fluorescent light after I turn off the UV light. In other words, it's glow-in-the-dark, though the light only lasts for a second or so. To see phosphorescence I had to switch out my big 395 nm flashlight for a shorter-wavelength 365 nanometer light.
What's causing this fluorescence? It's probably the dyes that give the candies their bright colors. The package lists some pretty standard food dyes, Blue 1, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, and Red 40. They're all fluorescent, especially in very viscous foods (hard candy nicely fits the bill). I couldn't find out for sure which one is phosphorescent, but Red 40 glows for longer in more viscous materials, so that would be my first guess.
(It's worth pointing out that just because they fluoresce doesn't mean the dyes are necessarily harmful - plenty of natural substances also fluoresce, such as olive oil, coumarin, and vitamin B2.)
The gummy Jolly Ranchers are also fluorescent (they have the same dyes) although the effect isn't as dramatic. The granular coating of the sour gummies makes it a bit easier to see. None of them seemed to be phosphorescent. I'm not sure why, but one hypothesis is that viscosity thing. Gummies are closer to flowing liquid than hard candies are.
On the other hand, blue raspberry airheads are really brightly fluorescent with a very satisfying blue-to-pink color change. It also has the same dyes as Jolly Ranchers. Maybe taffy format encourages fluorescence?
