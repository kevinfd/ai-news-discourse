# AI drawing manga, and now quantum computers are joining in!? TOPPAN and Keio University announce new technology

note · てんむす定食 · 2026-10-16

AI drawing manga, and now quantum computers are joining in!? TOPPAN and Keio University announce new technology
Just when we thought we were in an era where AI draws illustrations, it seems quantum computers are now joining the fray.
On October 16, 2026, TOPPAN Holdings and Keio University announced that they had succeeded in generating manga and animation images by combining quantum computers with generative AI.
When you think of quantum computers, you get the impression of a future technology used for complex calculations.
To think that it would become involved in the production of manga and anime.
What exactly has become possible?
Quantum computers generating character images?
This research was conducted jointly by TOPPAN Holdings and Keio University.
The demonstration experiment was carried out from February 1 to July 31, 2026. The research results were announced on October 16.
The research utilized a method called "quantum flow matching."
It sounds difficult just by the name, but simply put, it is a technology that assigns part of the calculations for image generation to a quantum computer.
In current image generation AI, "diffusion models," which generate images by gradually removing noise, are widely used.
On the other hand, flow matching is a method that learns the flow of data transforming into the target image.
The research team incorporated this mechanism into a quantum computer circuit.
They then attempted to generate character images based on conditions such as hairstyles and facial expressions.
The first images generated were 16x16 pixels
Here, a slightly surprising number appears.
The images generated by the 10-qubit quantum computer used this time were 16x16 pixel grayscale images.
In other words, they were very small black-and-white images.
This alone is quite different from the anime illustrations we see every day.
That is where standard AI technology comes in.
Based on images generated by a quantum computer, an AI supplements the background, colors, and resolution.
As a result, they succeeded in outputting a 256x256 pixel color image.
This achievement does not mean that a quantum computer alone completed a high-definition illustration.
The significance lies in demonstrating a mechanism where a quantum computer and conventional AI each handle different processes.
What changes when using a quantum computer?
What we want to focus on in this research is the computational efficiency of image generation.
Image generation AI faces challenges such as computational volume, processing time, and securing training data.
The research team verified the possibility of improving these issues by utilizing the mechanisms of a quantum computer.
In basic experiments where quantum circuits were reproduced on a computer, the number of operations was reduced to about one-sixth of conventional designs by refining the circuit design.
Also, in a comparison using handwritten digit images, it was reported that the rate at which specified numbers could be correctly generated and identified improved from 16% to 78%.
Furthermore, in a simulation using 10,000 training data points, the quantum model achieved a result of 78.3%, compared to 54.3% for the conventional AI model used for comparison.
However, there is an important point to note here.
These are mainly basic verification results based on simulations.
It has not been proven that actual quantum computer hardware is faster or higher-performing than current image generation AI.
The superiority of generation accuracy and processing time on actual hardware remains a subject for future research.
Will the production of manga and anime change?
If image generation using quantum computers is put to practical use in the future, changes may occur in the creative field as well.
For example, prototyping character designs.
There is a possibility that it will become possible to efficiently generate images of the same character with different expressions or hairstyles.
Or, assisting with manga coloring and animation production.
If part of the production process can be supported by new technology, creators might have more time to focus on storytelling and direction.
However, these are considerations regarding future possibilities.
This announcement does not mean that it is now possible to automatically produce entire manga pages or animated videos.
The research team has begun a new research phase starting in October 2026, aiming for image resolution enhancement and condition specification using natural language.
They are also reportedly looking into 3D and 4D scene generation for the future.
As AI evolves, what will happen to human creativity?
From here, there is something I would like to think about for a moment.
When image generation AI appeared, a debate arose about what would happen to jobs involving drawing.
And now, the computational technology that supports that image generation AI is also about to evolve.
So, as technology advances, will the role of human creators diminish?
I do not think that is necessarily the case.
The technology to generate images and the act of thinking about what one wants to express are not the same thing.
What kind of characters should appear?
What kind of story should be told?
What do you want the reader to feel in that scene?
These creative decisions are not determined solely by computational speed.
Of course, AI itself may sometimes make creative suggestions.
Even so, human intent should still play a major role in thinking about the direction of the work one wants to complete.
Will quantum AI become a new tool for creation?
This announcement does not mean that quantum computers will immediately change the work of manga artists and animators.
At this point, it is a research stage that has confirmed technical feasibility.
Even so, it is an interesting development that quantum computers and generative AI, which have often been discussed as separate fields, have been linked in the familiar creative field of manga and anime production.
How will this technology develop in the future?
And how will it support the work of creators?
It is something we should keep an eye on.
Finally, something to think about
If, in the future, we become able to generate large quantities of illustrations in less time than now using quantum computers and AI,
would you want to use that technology?
Or would you prefer to cherish the creative process itself?
I think it would be an interesting future if the increase in technology also leads to an increase in options for expression.
What is important is what to entrust to technology and what to express yourself.
Perhaps it is the ability to make that choice.
