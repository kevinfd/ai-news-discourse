# Meta and Apple agree on at least one thing: Open-weight AI is cool

Fast Company · Harry McCracken · 2026-10-16

Meta released a new version of its Muse Spark LLM on Thursday. As its name indicates, the big news about Muse Spark Open is that it’s an open-weight model. Rather than being offered solely as a cloud service, it’s publicly available for anyone to run on their own hardware. With 816 billion total parameters and a 1-million-token context window, it’s the U.S.’s most capable open-weight model, according to Meta.
As I wrote in a recent feature, Meta’s AI story dates all the way back to the founding of its original AI lab in 2013. One of this long journey’s high points happened in 2023-2024, when its open-weight Llama model was embraced by companies that wanted to control their own AI rather than rent it from a company such as OpenAI or Anthropic. But that momentum evaporated last year when Llama 4 was widely regarded to be a dud.
That set off a chain of events inside Meta. Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang joined the company to head a new research organization, Meta Superintelligence Labs. Llama gave way to a new family of models, starting with Muse Spark. But they were closed cloud services, not open-weight.
With Muse Spark Open’s arrival, Meta has a shot at rekindling the excitement over Llama. By running open-weight models on their own infrastructure, companies can avoid uploading confidential data—from medical records to unreleased movie footage—to third-party services. They can tweak the model for specific purposes. And since they own the hardware, they can gorge on tokens without racking up any fees.
During a media briefing yesterday, I got a demo of some of Muse Spark Open’s capabilities. It wrote a script to perform math, helped code a social feed app, and selected video clips and edited them into a longer sequence. It all worked, though I’ll reserve judgment on how well it performs these sorts of tasks compared to other models until hands-on reactions from AI developers start to come in.
But the demo I saw was notable in part because it took place at Apple, not Meta. Muse Spark Open was running on two Mac Studio computers cabled together into a cluster—essentially a tiny desktop data center.
Historically, Apple and Meta have not exactly been allies. Indeed, when Apple executives such as Tim Cook touted the company’s commitment to privacy, it was seen as an implicit attack on Meta’s business model. The ability to run local AI that rivals cloud AI has unexpectedly aligned these two very different companies’ interests.
