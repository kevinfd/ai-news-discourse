# How the national debate over AI, data centers plays into Louisiana's Senate race

NOLA.com · Meghan Friedmann · 2026-10-16

Democrat Jamie Davis and Republican U.S. Rep. Julia Letlow, the two candidates vying for Louisiana’s open U.S. Senate seat, are taking different approaches to addressing heightened public concerns about AI and the data centers that power the technology.
Davis, whose odds of winning the heavily Republican state in November are slim, has sought to draw a sharp contrast on the issue by releasing an aggressive, detailed agenda to regulate AI companies that includes a moratorium on new data centers. Letlow, whose trades in tech company stock have drawn scrutiny, has been less specific – but she says her approach would balance economic development with protecting the public and ensuring residents don’t have to pay for data centers’ power needs.
In a statement, Davis said he would support liability for AI companies that deploy dangerous technologies and fight to ensure AI isn’t used in autonomous weapons systems. He also said he would back legislation mandating safety reviews of AI models before their release and requiring those models to include “kill switches” in case they need to be shut down.
“‘Self-policing’ by these massive AI companies isn’t going to cut it,” Davis said. “Congress has to step in.”
Letlow’s campaign did not answer specific questions about whether Letlow would support requiring AI models to have kill switches, liability for AI companies or limiting the use of AI in autonomous weapons. Instead, the campaign released a general statement about Letlow’s stance on AI.
“When it comes to artificial intelligence, Julia believes we must protect Americans, safeguard our national security, and ensure the United States remains the global leader in technological innovation,” the statement said. “She supports a thoughtful approach that addresses legitimate risks without imposing unnecessary regulations that put American businesses and workers at a disadvantage.”
Letlow’s campaign also noted the congresswoman supports a bill that would require state utility regulators to consider adopting a standard under which data centers would pay the full cost of the power they need to operate.
“Julia believes Louisiana has an opportunity to lead in the development of new technologies that bring investment, create good-paying jobs, and strengthen our economy,” her campaign said. “At the same time, that growth must be done responsibly, with transparency, community input, and protections to ensure Louisiana families and small businesses aren't left footing the bill for new infrastructure.”
A growing national issue
The Louisiana race is an example of how AI and data centers are shaping mid-term elections across the country, which will determine which party controls Congress.
Nationally, Democrats have been pressing Republicans on the issue, and polls show data centers are unpopular among members of both parties. Fears of AI-driven cyberattacks and recent high-profile instances of AI models breaking out of their controls have also caused alarm.
President Donald Trump, who has a close relationship with tech titans like Mark Zuckerberg, has opposed mandatory restrictions on the technology, arguing such limitations would hinder the U.S. in the AI race against China. Some AI founders have celebrated Trump’s approach, which they expect will increase profits.
But in recent months, tech leaders have also called for an AI development slowdown amid concerns that AI could break out of human controls and wreak havoc on power grids, financial institutions and transportation systems. Researchers and experts say that possibility is becoming stronger; last month, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei warned rogue AI bots could take over the internet within a year.
Skeptics say that even when AI agents have gone rogue, they have been following goals set by humans.
In Louisiana, AI is a salient issue as Meta seeks to expand its massive data center project in Richland Parish, which is known as Hyperion. The project is controversial: critics worry household customers could end up paying for Meta’s power needs, and landowners have complained that Entergy, the power company that would fuel Hyperion, is using expropriation laws to seize land for transmission lines, among other concerns.
Proponents of the project, including Letlow and state leaders like Gov. Jeff Landry, say that Meta’s data center and other large-scale developments mark a sea change for Louisiana’s economy that will create jobs and bring more community investment to the state.
Letlow addresses Meta NDA, stock trade
In 2024, Letlow signed a non-disclosure agreement with Laidley LLC, a Meta business, as Meta was working on plans for Hyperion; according to a public financial disclosure, she purchased Meta stock in the months that followed, before news of the project became public.
As Congress grapples with allegations of insider trading against its members, Letlow has received backlash over her stock trades, which WNNO reported on in August. And in February, when she was fighting to win the Republican primary, she faced criticism from Sen. Bill Cassidy, her opponent, for failing to disclose her stock trades on time.
In a statement, Letlow’s campaign said that Letlow “does not direct, execute, approve, or have prior knowledge of any individual trades.”
“These transactions were made independently by a third-party investment firm exercising discretionary authority over a managed account, without Congresswoman Letlow’s input or involvement,” the statement said.
Previously, Letlow expressed support for legislation that would ban members of Congress from purchasing new stock but that would not prevent them from trading existing stock.
The statement from Letlow’s campaign also defended her decision to sign an NDA with Meta.
“The NDA was utilized to understand the investment’s potential to have a profound impact on Northeast Louisiana,” it said. “Such agreements are common in economic development discussions, with the ultimate goal of securing new investments and creating jobs for the people of Louisiana.”
Landry’s administration has used hundreds of NDAs to keep details of major economic development projects secret as it works to bring investment to Louisiana. Dozens of officials and state employees have signed those deals.
Proponents say the agreements are helping usher in unprecedented economic growth for the state, while critics describe them as a major blow to government transparency.
In a statement, Davis said he does not support officials signing NDAs over business deals and pledged never to sign an NDA as a senator; he also said he supports banning members of Congress from trading stocks.
“Congress shouldn't be trading individual stocks while it writes the laws that move those stocks,” Davis said. “A rule that only stops new purchases and lets members keep what they already own is duct tape, and Washington loves duct tape solutions that earn credit without changing anything.”
AI’s role in the midterms
Some political observers say AI and data centers could cause problems for Republicans during the midterms, as polls show Americans overwhelmingly support government regulations on the technology.
Trump has resisted setting such restrictions and said tech companies should self-police AI. He argues a hands-off approach is the best way to make sure the United States has superior AI technology.
“Republicans don’t want to get on what they see as the wrong side of the president,” said Brendan Steinhauser, CEO of the Alliance for Secure AI, a nonprofit that works to build bipartisan support in Congress for AI guardrails. “They’re kind of caught between a rock and a hard place, the rock being Donald Trump and the hard place being public opinion.”
Trump has endorsed Letlow, helping her oust Cassidy in the Republican primary this year.
Steinhauser, who is a Republican and formerly worked as a GOP strategist, said Democrats have generally been stronger on AI and will pass regulations if they win Congress in November. But, he said, some Democrats have been working behind the scenes to slow down legislation because AI is a campaign issue that hurts Republicans.
Meanwhile, Louisiana pollster and political advisor John Couvillon has expressed doubts that data centers will cause much trouble for Republicans in the state.
“When I do polling asking about the most important issues, data centers are way down near the bottom,” Couvillon said. “It’s kind of the popular issue du jour. I reserve judgment about the electoral potency of it.”
The Associated Press contributed to this report.
