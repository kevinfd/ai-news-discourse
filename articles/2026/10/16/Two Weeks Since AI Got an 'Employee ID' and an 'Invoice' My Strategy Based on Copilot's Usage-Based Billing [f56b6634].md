# Two Weeks Since AI Got an 'Employee ID' and an 'Invoice' | My Strategy Based on Copilot's Usage-Based Billing and Gemini's 'Colleague Agents'

note · 火華 緒 · 2026-10-16

Two Weeks Since AI Got an 'Employee ID' and an 'Invoice' | My Strategy Based on Copilot's Usage-Based Billing and Gemini's 'Colleague Agents'
Hello.
Recently, there are almost no days where I don't use AI for work.
Drafting emails, summarizing documents, checking small snippets of code.
It is no longer just a "convenient tool," but more like a talented junior colleague sitting at the desk next to me.
However, while reading the news that has come out over the past two weeks, I suddenly stopped in my tracks.
It seems that junior colleague has started carrying an "employee ID" and an "invoice."
Today, I would like to introduce two pieces of news announced between the end of September and October, share the reactions from experts and the media, offer my own thoughts, and discuss how I plan to navigate this situation.
First, the premise: Let's just keep three terms in mind.
AI news is often difficult to approach because of all the katakana and English terms.
So, before we get to the main topic, please let me explain just three terms that appear in this article.
If you already know them, feel free to skip this part.
1. The difference between Chat AI and 'AI Agents'
Chat AI is an AI like ChatGPT that "answers when you ask it a question."
Think of it as a knowledgeable senior colleague who will tell you anything if you ask. However, it does not take action on its own.
On the other hand, an AI agent is an AI that, when given a "goal," thinks of how to achieve it on its own and proceeds to perform actual tasks, such as reading emails or creating documents.
This is like a junior colleague to whom you can entrust entire tasks.
2. 'Flat-rate' vs. 'Usage-based billing'
Flat-rate is a method where you pay a fixed amount every month, like an unlimited smartphone plan.
Usage-based billing is a method where you pay only for what you use, like electricity or water bills.
3. Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini
Both are AIs provided by major IT companies for corporate use.
Copilot is integrated into Word, Excel, Outlook, and more, while Gemini is built into Gmail, Google Docs, and others.
You might even be using them at your workplace.
If you understand these three points, the rest of this story should be much easier to read.
News 1: Microsoft Copilot moves to a 'fixed fee + usage-based' model
On September 25, Microsoft announced a new Copilot.
Chat and Office apps (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, etc.) are now combined into a single screen, with three new features added.
- Cowork: A feature that lets you assign entire tasks to AI
- Code: A feature that creates small apps or data analysis tools just by giving instructions in plain language
- Autopilot: An agent that continues to work on its own in the cloud (computers on the other side of the internet) without needing constant instructions
What caught my attention was the pricing structure.
For everyday chat and summarization, it remains the same per-user fixed license (a monthly contract based on the number of users).
On the other hand, 'work done by agents' like Cowork, Code, and Autopilot has shifted to usage-based billing where you pay only for what you use.
According to a Computerworld article, 'Copilot Credits' used for agent tasks cost $0.01 per credit.
However, the number of credits consumed for a single task varies depending on the type of AI used and the complexity of the work.
In other words, 'what you asked the AI to do and how much' is now directly reflected on your invoice.
News 2: Google's 'coworker agent' gets its own email address
On October 8, Google Cloud announced 'Gemini agent'.
What became a hot topic here is the coworker agent.
If you explain, 'I need someone for this role,' Gemini will prepare an agent for you.
The surprising part starts here: this agent is given
- a dedicated email address
- a calendar
- Google Drive access
- registration in the company directory
.
Just like a human employee, it holds one Workspace account.
*Workspace: Google's business service that bundles Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and more.
Furthermore, everything the agent does is recorded in the audit logs under the agent's own name.
*Audit log: A record kept so that you can check 'when, who, and what was done' later.
Administrators can set spending limits for each project, and the system is designed so that the agent pauses once the limit is reached.
By the way, it seems Gemini will use not only Google's own AI but also Anthropic's Claude (an AI developed by the American AI company Anthropic) depending on the task.
Having another company's AI work within a Google agent—it has become quite a strange era.
According to ITmedia AI+, the release date for Gemini agents in Japan is currently undecided.
Expert and Media Reactions
Looking at the reactions from overseas analysts (experts who research and analyze the industry to advise companies) and the media, voices expressing concern about 'money and responsibility' were more prominent than those expressing anticipation.
Gartner, Futurum Group, and TechMarketView, which appear below, are all IT industry research firms.
Gartner Analyst (Max Goss) / From a Computerworld article
Pointed out that some people might end up relying on Cowork even for tasks that could be done for free.
They stated that if introduced without guidelines, 'waste is almost certain to occur'.
Analyst at Futurum Group (Keith Kirkpatrick) / From a Computerworld article
He touches on the risk that vendors (the companies selling AI) will steer work toward the pricing tiers that are more profitable for them.
Analyst at TechMarketView (Simon Baxter) / From a The Register article
"Agents that operate without prompts (instructions to AI) are no longer tied to someone sitting in front of a keyboard."
This is seen as a fairly significant change for organizations that have built their budgets based on headcount.
The Next Web (an international tech news site)
Google explains in detail how to give agents authority and budgets.
But it doesn't say 'how to take them away'.
