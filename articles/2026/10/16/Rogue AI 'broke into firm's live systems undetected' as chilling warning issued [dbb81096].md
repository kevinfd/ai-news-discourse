# Rogue AI 'broke into firm's live systems undetected' as chilling warning issued

AOL · Hannah Broughton · 2026-10-16

Rogue AI 'broke into firm's live systems undetected' as chilling warning issued
Rogue AI allegedly broke into a firm's live systems undetected, according to India's cybersecurity agency, raising further safety concerns about AI's rapid growth.
The AI agents broke into the live systems of several Indian companies and read private records, the agency announced. The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) revealed the incidents in a critical advisory issued on Thursday.
The advisory, issued on October 15, reported incidents in which AI models and agents operating during research and evaluation activities accessed live systems they were not authorized to reach. In the breach, the agents managed to read private business records containing personal data and confidential material, according to the report.
The AI firms involved were not named in the report, nor were the companies that were hit, and CERT-In said none of the companies had spotted the break-ins.
The agency said it received several voluntary disclosures from AI model providers whose systems had gained access to live environments during the internet-based research and evaluation, according to Nation Path India. This indicated that the AI agents went well beyond their assigned tasks, accessing systems without authorization.
Worryingly, the unnamed affected organizations did not detect the unauthorized access independently, and the activity only became apparent after the AI model providers identified the breach and reported it to the Indian cybersecurity agency.

There is also a second problem flagged by the advisory: CERT-In discovered that criminals were using free AI hacking software against Indian companies to extract money. It reported that the tools were being run from servers in several countries and were being rebranded.
In this instance, the AI agents were able to access and cause damage within a few hours, rather than days. The businesses that were hit and had only partially fixed their systems were then hit again by the software, and on a larger scale the second time round.
The weak spots were identified as being old in both sets of cases. CERT-In said the AI was able to find and exploit these weaknesses "faster, more exhaustively and at greater scale than a human attacker."
Advice moving forward for companies is to replace any leaked keys or passwords, shut down unused APIs, always require a two-step login, and retain logs for at least 180 days. If they do experience a leak, those companies should consider their systems as breached until a forensic check says they are clear.
Recently, AI agents' capabilities have raised global concerns about their ability to break away and work toward their own agenda.
There have been a number of instances that have heightened concerns over the past year, including on September 28, when AI agents attempted to hack into a Canadian government website, according to research lab and AI evaluator Transluce. Google also confirmed its Gemini AI model hacked three companies in May as part of a test of its cybersecurity capabilities.
