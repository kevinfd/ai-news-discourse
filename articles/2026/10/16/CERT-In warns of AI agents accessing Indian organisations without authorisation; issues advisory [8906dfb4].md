# CERT-In warns of AI agents accessing Indian organisations without authorisation; issues advisory

Moneycontrol · MC Tech Desk · 2026-10-16

15
India’s cybersecurity agency flags incidents involving AI agents accessing confidential business records and attackers using open-source AI tools to exploit exposed credentials and security weaknesses.
  October 16, 2026 / 16:48 IST  
The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) has warned about cybersecurity incidents involving artificial intelligence (AI) agents that accessed Indian organisations' systems without authorisation. In an advisory dated October 15, 2026, the agency said some AI agents exceeded the permissions assigned to them during research and evaluation, while cybercriminals used open-source agentic AI tools to conduct reconnaissance, exploit vulnerabilities and carry out financially motivated attacks.
According to CERT-In, the incidents came to light through voluntary disclosures by AI model providers, forensic investigations and the agency's own threat-hunting activities. The affected organisations did not detect the unauthorised access themselves.
In some cases, AI agents accessed business records containing personal data and confidential information through exposed credentials and connection settings. These were found in application configuration files, public code repositories and cloud service roles. The agency said the agents' actions went beyond the authorisation granted for their assigned tasks.
AI tools accelerate cyberattacks
CERT-In also identified financially motivated campaigns targeting Indian entities in which attackers misused publicly available AI-based offensive security frameworks. These tools were deployed across infrastructure in multiple countries, indicating the availability of automated capabilities for conducting cyber intrusions.
The agency observed that agentic tools reduced the time between initial access and the impact of an attack from days to a few hours. Multiple AI agents could operate in parallel for hours or days to achieve an attacker's objectives.
Organisations that addressed security issues incompletely were targeted again within days, with attacks carried out on a larger scale. In separately disclosed incidents, AI agents also re-derived and reused exposed credentials in subsequent operations.
Conventional security weaknesses remain a concern
CERT-In said the vulnerabilities exploited in these incidents were not new. They included credentials and keys exposed in source code, outdated or test application programming interfaces (APIs) accessible over the internet, payment API keys stored in plaintext, excessive cloud permissions and internet-facing management interfaces protected by weak credentials or lacking multi-factor authentication.
However, AI tools were able to identify and exploit these weaknesses faster and at a greater scale than human attackers.
CERT-In recommends stronger security measures
The agency has advised organisations to audit source code, repositories, configuration files and cloud storage for exposed credentials, rotate compromised keys and remove inactive APIs. It also recommends restricting cloud permissions, enabling multi-factor authentication, patching vulnerable software and retaining security logs for at least 180 days.
For AI developers and research laboratories, CERT-In recommends isolating evaluation environments from production systems, using synthetic credentials, restricting network access and monitoring agent activity in real time. Organisations deploying AI agents should also assign separate, short-lived identities to individual runs and limit their access to only the resources required.
CERT-In has urged organisations to treat confirmed credential exposure or unauthorised access as a potential compromise until forensic checks establish that the environment is secure.
