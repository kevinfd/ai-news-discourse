1. What can Copilot actually do? Grasp the big picture with this one book
Copilot is an AI assistant you can consult with while you work
There is no need to overthink Copilot.
First, try thinking of it as an AI assistant that you can ask for help in a conversational format when writing text or organizing information.
For example, daily work includes these kinds of 'slightly tedious tasks'.
- Thinking of how to start an email
- Extracting key points from long documents
- Organizing the order of reports
- Brainstorming headings for proposal materials
- Rewriting formal text into easier-to-understand expressions
None of these might be a big job on their own.
However, when repeated every day, they consume a fair amount of time and concentration.
The entry point for trying out Copilot is these familiar tasks.
Instead of asking it to 'finish a proposal' or 'summarize and finish my work' from the start, try making small, specific requests like 'draft an email' or 'think of three headings'.
The idea is to look at the content that comes back and refine it into a form that is easy for you to use.
The first hurdle is often 'which Copilot are we talking about?'
When researching Copilot, you will find explanations for chatting in a browser, as well as explanations for operating it within Word or PowerPoint.
This is where it gets confusing for beginners.
Even with the same name 'Copilot', the available features differ depending on the location, account, and contract used.
Consulting on a document outline via chat and creating materials within PowerPoint are not the same operation.
Also, you need to consider the differences between trying it out individually and using workplace data.
'Introduction to Microsoft Copilot AI Utilization' covers multiple usage forms as they existed at the time of publication.
Therefore, for those who want to organize 'what entry points exist for using Copilot,' it serves as a foothold for surveying the whole picture.

- Do I want to use it for personal or workplace purposes?
- Do I want to consult via chat, or do I want to work within an app?
- Is the feature I want to try supported by my current usage environment?
