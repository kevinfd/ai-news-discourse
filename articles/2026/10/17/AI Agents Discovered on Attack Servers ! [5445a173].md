# AI Agents Discovered on Attack Servers?!

note · Jazzywada · 2026-10-17

AI Agents Discovered on Attack Servers?!
A shocking truth that overturns the common sense of cyberattacks
1. Introduction
In recent years, news of personal information leaks from organizations, including large corporations, has been shaking society almost daily. In one case of damage, it was confirmed that a fierce attack involving over 1,200 attempts from as many as 326 IP addresses flooded in over an extremely short period of just about 40 minutes.
"Why is cyber damage expanding so rapidly and explosively?"
To this familiar question held by many companies and readers, definitive evidence that has shocked the security community has been presented. According to the latest investigation by JPCERT/CC (JPCERT Coordination Center) and others, which are specialized organizations for cyber defense, the program of an "AI agent" itself was discovered inside a server operated by attackers, along with attack tools.
The phase of cyberattacks has now irreversibly shifted from the traditional "human vs. human" or "human vs. system" battle to "fully automated AI attacks" that do not involve human intervention. In this article, based on the latest trends captured on the front lines, I will unravel the "five shocking truths" that overturn the common sense of cybersecurity to date from the perspective of an IT analyst.
2. Impact 1: Fully automated without humans — "AI agents" lurking inside attack servers
When JPCERT/CC, in collaboration with an external team of security experts, scrutinized the attack servers, they confirmed a configuration that fundamentally overturns the composition of traditional cyberattacks.
Inside that server, not only were "attack toolsets" placed, such as scanning tools to find gaps in systems and exploit programs to actually carry out intrusions, but an "AI agent" that acts as the entity to operate those tools was also stationed as a set.
In traditional attacks, even if tools were placed on a server, a human (hacker) would access it from the outside and operate the attack by typing commands with their own hands. The server was merely a "tool shed." However, in the reality discovered this time, the hypothesis that the AI itself, which is the side using the tools, exists inside the server and is automatically processing a series of processes—from target selection and reconnaissance to vulnerability exploitation and data exfiltration—without human intervention is extremely strong.
Regarding this shocking fact, Mr. Hayato Sasaki of JPCERT/CC explains as follows:
"The agent itself that uses the tools is also in the tool shed, in other words, an AI (a substitute), so it's already placed there... and it is automatically running that series of attack process cycles."
"Overwhelming speed" and "scaling" brought by AI that knows no fatigue
In human-led cyberattacks, no matter how skilled the hacker is, there are physiological limits. There were always "downtimes" due to work fatigue, sleep, daily rhythms, and even holidays in specific countries (a phenomenon where attacks temporarily stop during consecutive holidays).
However, AI agents operating within attack servers know no fatigue. They continue to run attack cycles 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, without a single second of rest. Furthermore, while the number of targets a human can process simultaneously is limited to a few, AI can mechanically process (scale) hundreds or thousands of targets simultaneously.
What must not be misunderstood here is that the current AI is not performing "sci-fi super hacking" like in movies (the ability to instantly and completely create unknown zero-day vulnerabilities from scratch) point. The true terror of AI lies in the fact that it covers and speeds up existing gaps and API flaws that humans have neglected to address with overwhelming "computational power" and "cycle speed." As a result of this overwhelming tempo and scale being combined, an explosive surge in damage is being created, pushing the defense side into a corner.
3. Impact 2: Collapse of "bounded rationality" — AI targets even portals and worthless data that humans overlook
The second impact brought about by AI abuse is the disappearance of "behavioral bias" in the selection of attack targets. The key to understanding this is "bounded rationality (the limits of human knowledge, thought, and perception)," an important concept in behavioral economics.
Human hackers are always bound by "bounded rationality." Based on their own knowledge and financial incentives, they tend to concentrate attacks on specific targets, such as "e-commerce sites with a large amount of credit card information that can be quickly cashed out" or "cryptocurrency exchanges."
In contrast, AI agents do not have human consciousness or biases. Based on computational power, they target all accessible systems with "complete rationality" without exception.

In fact, in recent damage cases, it is not the main core systems that are being targeted as entry points, but rather peripheral, inconspicuous systems such as 'employee-only portals and apps introduced as part of DX promotion'.
Furthermore, the stolen data is not limited to specific credit card information; large amounts of seemingly unrelated information that cannot be immediately monetized on the dark market, such as driver's license images and HR recruitment data, are also being taken. An indiscriminate comprehensiveness, much like 'taking everything from one end of the drawer to the other,' is being observed.
The shattered safety myth that 'inconspicuous company sites are not targeted'
Traditional corporate defense has been rooted in a persistent safety myth: 'There is no way our inconspicuous internal portal or data with seemingly low monetary value would be targeted.' However, this way of thinking itself is a dangerous assumption dependent on the 'behavioral bias of human hackers.' AI does not judge whether data has monetary value based on human context; if there is a hole to enter, it mechanically extracts everything. It can be said that traditional defense models have been invalidated by the perfect comprehensiveness of AI.
4. Impact 3: The End of Traditional 'Attack Campaigns' — Parallel and Sudden Simultaneous Attacks
Traditional cyberattacks had certain predictable periodic patterns. When a dangerous vulnerability was announced in a specific product or feature, many hackers would rush to it, launching wave-like concentrated attacks. In the security industry, this was called an 'attack campaign,' and the standard practice was to predict the waves of attacks and prepare defensive postures.
However, in the series of attack cases involving AI this time, this conventional pattern is no longer applicable at all.
In the extremely short period from September to early October 2024, attacks using diverse intrusion methods not specialized in a single vulnerability surged all at once. The attackers did not concentrate on a specific product, but rather unleashed diverse attack patterns in parallel (simultaneously) and suddenly.
The Disappearance of Predictable 'Attack Waves' and Confusion in Initial Response
As a result of diverse attack methods hitting simultaneously, victimized companies and investigation teams struggled to identify 'which entry point was used and what vulnerability was exploited,' leading to serious confusion in initial response efforts.
The conventional, phased defense approach of 'finding a hole in a specific product and patching that product' has reached its limit. This signifies that we have entered an era where we do not know when, from which angle, or with what combination of attack patterns a massive, simultaneous attack will arrive.
5. Impact 4: The Structural Paradox of 'You Cannot Defend Without Knowing the Attack' and the Barrier to Information Sharing in Japan
As a fundamental principle of cybersecurity, there is an 'attack science' perspective that states, **'It is impossible to build defensive measures without accurately grasping the methods and structure of the attack (structural understanding).'** Without knowing the intrusion route or structural mechanism, it is impossible to establish an appropriate defensive line.
However, incident response in Japan currently faces extremely serious structural challenges.
Analyzing publicized information leak cases, approximately 80% of victimized companies list the cause of intrusion as 'not disclosed' or 'under investigation/unknown.' Cases where companies withhold details under the pretext of 'security reasons' during interviews or public announcements are endless.
Furthermore, while legal reporting procedures to the Personal Information Protection Commission and others collect information on the damage (context)—such as 'when the damage was recognized and how the customer was handled'—there is a structural distortion where the most important 'technical trace information (IOCs such as the attacker's IP address and characteristics of the attack program)' for preventing the next attack across society is not sufficiently collected or returned to the administrative side.
Mr. Sasaki strongly appeals for the necessity of sharing technical attack information (IOCs) across society as a whole.
'Technical information on attacks... this is information necessary for society as a whole to repel defenses.'
2-Month Investigation Lag and C2 Server Disappearance: The Limits of Legal Systems and Forensics
Why are conventional defense systems and legal frameworks neutralized by AI attacks? Therein lies a decisive dissociation in the time axis.
In reality, there is a time lag of about two months from the time a company recognizes the damage until it conducts a forensic investigation by a specialized firm and consults with the police or relevant agencies to solidify its response policy. However, during those two months, the attacker's AI has immediately discarded and erased the C2 server (command server), cut the connection, and fled. By the time one tries to investigate or counterattack after the fact, the target to be tracked has vanished without a trace.
Against the speed of AI offensives that repeat trial and error in real-time and instantly, the human side's overly cautious non-disclosure of information, delays in administrative reporting procedures, and the time lag of forensic investigations requiring several months have become fatal shackles for the defense.
6. Impact 5: 'Realistic Defense Techniques for Individuals and Companies' to Survive the AI Era
In an era where attacks are accelerating due to AI, there is a realistic premise that must be faced first. That is the fact that **'there is no longer any preventive measure that can 100% prevent intrusion or leakage (it is an era of assumed intrusion and assumed leakage).'**
Rather than viewing 'not being able to defend completely' as giving up, it is required to shift to effective defense techniques (strengthening resilience) based on the premise that leaks will occur.
Defense techniques individuals can take: Avoid panic and fully utilize standard features
When news breaks that driver's license images or personal information have been leaked, it is easy to fall into excessive panic. However, cases where significant property contracts are immediately concluded in one's name using only image data are limited, so it is important to stay calm first.
The most reliable self-defense an individual can take is to reliably enable the basic security features provided by the service side.
- Thorough implementation of multi-factor authentication (2FA): In addition to passwords, always set up app-based or SMS authentication.
- Transition to Passkeys: Actively adopt the latest authentication features that do not rely on passwords.
- Complete elimination of password reuse: Manage passwords thoroughly so that even if one is leaked, it does not lead to a chain reaction of damage to other services.
Defensive techniques companies can implement: Re-inspecting unexamined systems and anonymous information sharing
The measures companies should take do not always involve introducing expensive new tools.
- Re-inspection of peripheral and low-priority systems: What AI targets are the security gaps, such as 'employee portals.' Thoroughly inspect and implement basic measures not only for main systems but also for existing sites that were previously overlooked due to low priority.
- Sharing technical information (IOCs) through specialized organizations (such as JPCERT/CC): It is impossible to counter AI attacks on your own. If you encounter damage, or even if you manage to block an attack, share the technical traces (IOCs) with specialized organizations like JPCERT/CC. Even if it is in an 'anonymized' form that hides your company name, returning technical data to society and enhancing the defense network (resilience) of society as a whole is the greatest defense.
7. Conclusion (Summary and Future Outlook)
The presence of an 'AI agent' captured within an attack server signals that cyberattacks have entered a new dimension. Let's review the main points of this article.
- Full automation by AI: The attack cycle is being processed automatically, at high speed and in parallel, 24/7 inside the server.
- Collapse of bounded rationality: AI comprehensively targets everything, including internal portals and image data that humans overlook, without being constrained by context.
- Collapse of conventional patterns: Because a variety of methods are being deployed in a short period, conventional 'attack campaign' predictions no longer apply.
- Overcoming structural time lags: C2 servers disappear during a two-month forensic investigation. It is essential to move away from the 80% non-disclosure culture and establish a mechanism to share technical information (IOCs) in real time.
The light of defensive AI and the 'catch up and overtake' race
However, there is no need to despair. The AI technologies and tools used by attackers are basically the same technologies used by defenders and developers.
Currently, security vendors and product manufacturers are also introducing AI-powered automated vulnerability diagnostics, dramatically accelerating the movement to proactively discover vulnerabilities in their own products and issue correction patches. The increase in the number of published vulnerabilities is evidence that vendors are advancing proactive defense using AI. The attackers and defenders are now engaged in a **'speed race on the edge.'**
For the defense side to win this race, what is ultimately required is the **'speed of sharing technical information across society without hiding attack methods' and 'high ethical standards and thorough basic measures by each individual.'**
In an era where AI launches attacks 24 hours a day without rest, are you and your organization prepared to thoroughly implement 'obvious basic measures' and engage in 'information sharing' with society?
