# The 10 best cheap wireless earbuds, tested by audio experts

Mashable · Bethany Allard, Samantha Mangino, Alex Bracetti · 2026-10-17

The 10 best cheap wireless earbuds, tested by audio experts
Contrary to what you might think, you don't need to break the bank to get a great pair of earbuds. We spend a lot of time fawning over the latest tech here at Mashable, but new tech is usually expensive tech, especially in 2026. And as much as we love nerding out over the newest Sony headphones drop, we know that not everyone wants to spend $450 on a pair of flagship noise-cancelling headphones. So, that's why we're here to talk about the best budget earbuds. All of the products in this guide have been rigorously tested by a member of the Mashable team, and we've included our favorite wireless earbuds from Beats, Nothing, Apple, and Bose.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

How we chose these earbuds
Let's be clear: Just because these earbuds are affordable, that doesn't mean they skimp on features.
Mashable audio experts have tested all of the most popular wireless headphones and earbuds (not to mention some wired options). And the very best noise-cancelling wireless earbuds typically cost $250 to $300 — the Apple AirPods Pro 3 retail for $249 (but often go on sale for $179), and Bose QuietComfort Ultra Earbuds (Gen 2) come in at $299.
Fortunately, you don't need to spend $300 to get a great pair of wireless earbuds; in fact, you don't even need to spend $100. The best cheap wireless earbuds under $100 include some great options from JBL, Nothing, Anker Soundcore, and Beats. And if you're willing to spend a little more, the newest Apple AirPods 5 sound great and include new AI features such as live translation.
When reviewing earbuds for this guide, we looked for features such as easy connectivity, rich and balanced sound, active noise cancellation, and a comfortable fit. And among all the earbuds we've tested, which is an extensive list, these are the best budget earbuds for 2026.
Annker Soundcore P42i Wireless Earbuds
Best Overall
The Good & The Bad
- Vibrant sound with broad audio customization
- Noteworthy adaptive noise cancellation
- Some of the longest playtimes in the category
- Improved AI integration
- Attractive colorways
- Extras upon extras
- Small touch sensors make input difficult
- Inferior spatial audio
Who it's for
Those looking for the best all-around package for their dollar. Anker’s latest mid-range buds have the firepower and functionality of top-performing luxury buds. At $64.99, they’re the true embodiment of getting plenty of features without sacrificing affordability.
The P42i pack many of the same features as Anker’s premium models and performs nearly as well in audio, battery life, and noise cancellation — all at a third of the price. If you don't want to drop $150 on AirPods or Bose wireless earbuds, these are a great all-around pick under $100.
Why we picked this
Anticipate crisp instrument separation, deep bass, and sharp vocals when plugging them into your ears. The Soundcore Signature preset is well-balanced, and there are additional presets and a customizable EQ to personalize the sound. Enabling the LDAC codec boosts fidelity to hear more detail and nuance when streaming music on Android devices. However, you’ll want to set up the HearID feature, as it intuitively creates a sound profile tailored to your hearing.
Noise cancellation is also pivotal to sound performance. It provides an uptick in bass that gives contemporary recordings more oomph. Multi-level ANC produces an extra layer of noise reduction to minimize unwanted noise. Standard mode silences low- and mid-frequency sounds with ease, especially when set to Ultra ANC. Adaptive mode scans the environment to achieve stronger noise neutralization. Wind Noise Reduction is great at reducing harsh wind effects. Transparency mode is equally excellent and has a HearGuard setting that protects your hearing by automatically adjusting the amount of loud noise entering the buds.
In addition to the aforementioned features, these buds come with a Fit Test, multi-device connectivity, wearing detection, and a Game Mode that reduces latency when gaming. There’s even AI integration (e.g., Live Translation, chatbot capabilities), which is much better than it was on past Anker releases.
Fast charging (10 mins=3.5 hours) is some of the best at this price point. A full charge provides up to 12 hours of listening time, depending on how you use the buds, and the wireless charging case provides up to 56 hours of listening time.
Flaws are very few. Spatial audio drastically hollows out the midrange, and the small-stem design with tiny capacitive sensors makes tap input difficult. Besides that, these are near-flawless buds at an unbeatable value.

EarFun Air Pro 4 Plus
Runner up
The Good & The Bad
- Upgraded sound
- Longer battery life than previous version
- Versatile audio codec support
- App offers customization similar to luxury models
- Light and comfortable
- Downgraded ANC
- Inadequate mic performance
Who it's for
Audio nerds on a budget who want extensive sound customization and hi-res streaming over Bluetooth. While they retail for $99.99, you can typically buy them on sale via Amazon for $79.99, making them a solid alternative to AirPods.
Why we like it
Our previous top pick for the best cheap earbuds under $100, the EarFun Air Pro 4, didn’t need a director’s cut. Nonetheless, we received one in the Air Pro 4 Plus.
EarFun’s new dual-driver acoustic system with balanced armature enhances bass response and treble for terrific low-mid presentation. Drums and synths sound pleasant and punchy. Mids and lows also receive proper attention. EarFun spared no expense, blessing the Plus version with top-tier Bluetooth audio codecs. AptX Adaptive/Lossless and LDAC provide dynamic scaling and higher streaming quality. Other wireless audio perks like Auracast and LE Audio are appreciated.
Battery life isn’t significantly higher; you’re only getting 1 hour more per charge and 2 hours more from the charging case. That works for us, as do other retained hallmarks, including a comfortable fit, instantaneous multi-device pairing, and serviceable EQ functions.
ANC performed well on the previous model. The same can’t be said here. While electronic humming and loud voices are silent, everything else is audible and disruptive. The multiple AI noise-cancelling modes do little to suppress external sounds. Even the two ambient listening modes perform unevenly. Default produces clear vocals, but it also makes environmental noises sound harsh. Natural softens ambient noise and drastically decreases vocal presence simultaneously.
It’s hard not to fault EarFun for mishandling noise cancellation on a next-gen release. At the same time, the company overdelivered on audio performance in ways we never expected.

Budget buy
The Good & The Bad
- Engaging sound
- Companion app support with generous feature set

Who it's for
Bargain-bin shoppers who prioritize substance over style; anyone looking for the best cheap earbuds under $50. Much of the FlyBuds C3’s charm is exhibited through wonderful sound performance that receives a nice boost from Tribit’s impressively engineered music presets.
Why we picked this
Aesthetically, there’s nothing appealing about the FlyBuds C3. Their plain, long-stem design with a trite matte finish screams off-brand mediocrity. Yet what they lack in physical appeal, they vastly make up for in sound.
Are we talking Anker or JBL quality? No, but the buds still produce solid audio, and Tribit keeps the listening experience simple with user-friendly features in the mobile app to easily tweak performance on the fly.
Proprietary 10mm NdFeB drivers pump out satisfying frequencies. XBass technology sounds more balanced than ever on a Tribit product; the extra thump felt on bass-centric tracks is impactful and doesn’t bloat up the soundstage. It’s worth toying around with the EQ and presets for optimized sonic presentation with select music genres.
Niche features like antibacterial coating and Hearing Protection mode are overlooked. They help extend the buds’ lifespan and preserve your hearing, respectively. Multipoint technology also comes in clutch to seamlessly switch between two audio sources.
We'll be honest: ANC and spatial audio don’t contribute anything positive. Deluxe features like these aren’t necessary for $35. Great sound is, and the FlyBuds C3 guarantee it at a highly attainable MSRP.

Best Budget Noise-Canceling Earbuds
The Good & The Bad
- Elite ANC for under $100
- Stocked with premium Sony features
- Dynamic, punchy audio that can be customized in the Sound Connect app
- Lengthy playtimes (per charge)
- Outstanding touch controls
- Uncomfortable after 2 hours of wear

Who it's for
Brand enthusiasts who want Sony audio quality and the best noise neutralization available for under $100. The fact that these buds come with nearly every Sony proprietary sound technology should make them cost double their sale price, which is consistently around $80. These were a popular pick in our guide to the best Sony headphones as well.
Why we picked this
If the $328 Sony WF-1000xM6 are out of your financial reach, which is reasonable considering the current economy, then pick up the WF-C710N. They offer nearly everything their flagship counterpart does, from adjustable ANC to fidelity upscaling to multipoint technology.
You’re essentially getting a stripped-down version of the WF-1000xM5’s noise cancellation, which is still superior to most options in the budget and mid-range categories. It eliminates up to 85 percent of unwanted sounds. Most high-frequency noises are audible, but they end up sounding like background effects on most tracks. Then there’s the 20-level Ambient Sound mode that vastly increases awareness in all surroundings. The mics can pick up conversations from across the street and high-frequency noises from several blocks away.
Sony’s 5mm dynamic drivers dish out booming lows, expressive mids, and crisp highs, resulting in a well-balanced mix that complements most music genres and video content. Audiophiles have a plethora of sound features at their disposal, including a customizable multi-band EQ and various well-engineered presets. DSEE enhances the quality of compressed low-quality audio files to pull more detail from recordings. The WF-C710N also come with 360 Reality Audio, but the feature is underwhelming on these buds and limited to a handful of streaming services.
The long playtimes (up to 12 hours) allow for lengthy listening sessions, though your ears will need occasional breaks due to the buds’ uncomfortable oval design. Listeners with high pain thresholds shouldn’t experience any fatigue.

JBL Endurance Peak 4
Best sports earbuds
The Good & The Bad
- Practical and comfortable earhook design
- Adrenaline-inducing sound
- Great sports-ready features
- Lengthy playtimes
- Plenty of audio personalization tools
- Large charging case

Who it's for
Fitness buffs in need of high-performance sports buds that sell for nearly a third of what Apple’s top models cost. They’re a much cheaper alternative to the AirPods Pro 3 and Beats Powerbeats Pro 2 and offer far more upside for the price.
Why we picked this
It’s been several years since we’ve tested budget sports buds this great, but the Endurance Peak 4 blew us away on multiple levels.
JBL’s silicone hooks with memory wire are less stiff and more flexible than the Powerbeats Pro 2’s hooks. Even though the battery compartment is thick, it doesn’t weigh down your ears or cause wearer fatigue. Each set of tips forms a tight seal for passive noise isolation, though you can enable any of the three ANC modes — Noise Cancelling, Ambient Aware, and TalkThru – to effectively block out or let in ambient noise. Waterproof protection (IP68) comes as part of the package too.
There are several activity presets that JBL calls Sports Modes, which let you assign ANC and EQ presets to different profiles. Relax Mode plays peaceful ambient sounds to soothe the body for a set period.
Sports buds are nothing without energizing sound to push you through your workouts. The Endurance Peak 4 unleash bold bass response that pummels your eardrums. We recommend keeping the EQ off to get more mids and highs from your music. However, the best results come from the Personi-Fi feature, which optimizes sound to your hearing. It’s superb at balancing the low end with other frequencies.
The massive charging case is unflattering, while the unreliable touch controls can be frustrating to operate during breaks. But these shortcomings won’t get in the way of your workouts when you’re fully locked in.

Who it's for
Anyone who wants the Apple AirPods experience at a big discount. This fifth-gen model welcomes multiple upgrades and comes in at a lower price point than the AirPods 4.
Why we like it
Some of you may be willing to spend a few extra dollars for a little more performance, especially iPhone users. Those who can will want to look at the AirPods 5.
They come with new acoustic architecture and updated audio algorithms. The result is clearer and livelier sound. Bass has a nice kick to it and blends well with engrossing mids and airy highs. You’re not getting full, encompassing audio due to the open-ear design, but what projects from the drivers is compelling. Spatial Audio creates an immersive soundscape for Dolby Atmos content. The three-band EQ helps adjust sound to your liking. Adaptive Audio does an excellent job of blending ANC and Transparency modes, though you can use either mode independently to block out or let in ambient noise. Both work extremely well.
Now, if you’ve been an avid AirPods user, then you’re well accustomed to their flaws. Battery life remains poor at 4 to 5 hours with ANC on. That doesn’t factor in other features like Spatial Audio and Siri, which knock off another 1.5 hours. Android support is still lacking. Wireless charging also comes at a premium.
Regardless, you already know what you’re getting from a product five generations deep like the AirPods: exceptional overall quality.

These buds bridge the gap between the two mobile platforms, while combining midrange-heavy sound with strong noise cancellation to deliver a fantastic value.
Why we picked this
Multiple price drops have now placed the Studio Buds Plus firmly in the budget category. That’s huge considering how much firepower they possess.
Beats’ fine-tuned drivers produce cleaner bass with “ultra-low distortion.” Mids and highs are given plenty of room to breathe on a wider soundstage. Spatial audio may not perform as well as it does on the AirPods or Beats’ upscaled entries (*cough* the Powerbeats Pro 2 cough), but it can make certain Dolby Atmos tracks feel more spacious.
Noise cancellation is superior to the AirPods 4 with ANC; Apple’s open design lets in more ambient noise. Low- and mid-frequency noises are handled surprisingly well. Transparency mode is equally effective at increasing situational awareness, and neither mode compromises sound quality.
Battery life has been increased to give listeners roughly two days of moderate use (3-4 hours daily) before recharging. The pebble-shaped charging case and cool colorways, including the translucent option, also give these buds a distinctive look.
The lack of head tracking and wireless charging may sway some users to spend more on the AirPods. Neither omission is a dealbreaker, especially when considering that the pros outweigh the cons by a 2-to-1 margin.

What's considered budget?
Budget is a relative term. We know the Apple AirPods 5 ($129 to $149) and Bose QuietComfort Earbuds ($149.99 on sale) aren't budget earbuds for everyone, but there are plenty of good options under the $100, $50, and even $25 mark.
Our top pick is the Anker Soundcore P42i, which have a list price of $64.99, and have the noise cancellation of a much more expensive pair of earbuds. Going the budget route might mean making compromises, but not sacrifices. For under $100, you can still get active noise cancellation and self-charging cases.
We do include a model closer to $200, which we get isn't exactly everyone's idea of budget-friendly. The Apple AirPods 5 with ANC is that model. Compared to premium options, the fifth-gen AirPods offer an awfully similar experience for significantly cheaper and allow folks who want to stay in the Apple ecosystem without having to jump to the Pros.

When testing earbuds, we consider criteria such as:
The selections on this lift were driven first by price, as we wanted the options here to be truly affordable. All of the products in this guide are under $150, with a focus on models that are mostly under $100.
We tested these earbuds with various songs, from live performances to bass-heavy electronic music, R&B, rock, and folksy guitar songs. We evaluated how they sounded out of the box, along with any EQ presets or custom controls.
Though this category can be subjective, we made sure each of our selections had at least several different ear tip sizes and was comfortable to wear for three hours or more.
Realistically, you'll make calls when using your earbuds, which is why we put each of these earbuds through a call test, making sure the person on the other end of the phone could hear us clearly and consistently.
