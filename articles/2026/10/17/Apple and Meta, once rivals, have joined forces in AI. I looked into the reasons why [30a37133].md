# Apple and Meta, once rivals, have joined forces in AI. I looked into the reasons why.

note · 上村菜穂 株式会社Pr Net・Ceo · 2026-10-17

Apple and Meta, once rivals, have joined forces in AI. I looked into the reasons why.
On October 15, 2026, I saw the news that Apple and Meta had collaborated in the field of AI.
The first thing I thought was, 
Wait, weren't these two companies on bad terms?
that is.
Apple is the company that develops the iPhone and Mac. 
Meta is the company that operates Instagram, Facebook, and Threads.
Both are familiar parts of our daily lives, but they are also known for having clashed over privacy and advertising mechanisms.
Now, these two companies have joined forces on AI. 
Moreover, the top executives of both companies are reportedly praising each other's efforts.
What on earth happened?
When I looked into it out of curiosity, I saw that it wasn't just a simple reconciliation, 
but that both Apple and Meta have their own reasons for cooperating.
Apple's past policy shift had a major impact on Meta's advertising business
Let's turn back the clock a bit and start with the story from 2021.
That year, Apple introduced a mechanism called "App Tracking Transparency (ATT)."
Simply put, it requires user permission if an app wants to track user behavior across other apps and websites.
I think many of you have seen the prompt on your iPhone asking, "Allow this app to track you?"
For Apple, this is an initiative to protect user privacy.
On the other hand, it was a major problem for Meta.
One of the strengths of Instagram and Facebook ads is 
that they can deliver ads tailored to the user's interests and preferences.
However, when it becomes difficult to track behavior across apps, 
it also affects ad targeting and performance measurement.
In fact, in its 2022 annual report, Meta 
explained that the iOS changes created constraints on ad targeting and measurement.
Of course, other factors such as the economy and changes in advertising demand also affected the 2022 advertising business. 
It was not all due to Apple's policy.
Even so, it is certain that Apple's decision impacted Meta's business.
For me, as someone involved in SNS marketing, 
this change left a lasting impression.
Even while providing services on the same smartphone, 
the privacy that Apple prioritizes and 
the advertising mechanisms that Meta has leveraged as a strength did not always align.
These two companies collaborated on AI on October 15, 2026.
What was announced this time is a technical collaboration to run Meta's new AI model, 'Muse Spark Open,' on Apple's high-performance 'Mac Studio' computer.
Muse Spark Open is 
a large-scale AI model that excels at programming and complex tasks.
The total parameter count is 816 billion.
……Even if I say that, 
the number is so large that it's hard to grasp, isn't it? (laughs)
What is important here, more than the specific numbers, is 
the fact that they made it possible to run such a large-scale AI on computers we have at hand.
Generally, to run large-scale AI, 
high-performance computers in data centers are used.
However, this time, it was demonstrated that by connecting multiple Mac Studios equipped with Apple's 'M5 Ultra,' Meta's large-scale AI model could be executed locally.
Local execution means running AI on your own computer rather than sending data to an external AI service for processing every time.
Of course, this is not to say that it can be used casually on a standard MacBook.
The configuration shown this time 
combines two or four extremely high-performance Mac Studios.
Also, it was not announced that Meta's AI would be integrated into Apple Intelligence or Siri.
This is news primarily relevant to AI developers, researchers, and companies involved in advanced AI utilization. 
So, why did the two companies decide to cooperate?
Apple has a reason to demonstrate the new value of the Mac
First, let's consider it from Apple's side.
Apple has developed the Mac as a high-performance computer for creators, business professionals, and developers.
And now, they are trying to increase the presence of the Mac 
as a computer for running AI as well.
What I want to highlight in this announcement is Apple's 'Unified Memory' architecture.
I will skip the complicated explanation, but it is a technology that allows memory used for various processes within the Mac to be shared efficiently.
By utilizing this, it becomes easier to run large-scale AI models.
By using Meta's AI model this time, Apple has concretely demonstrated that the 
Mac Studio possesses such advanced processing power.
From here on is my own analysis, but I believe the benefit for Apple is not just selling high-performance computers.
“If you are developing AI or if a company is running AI in-house, 
the Mac is a viable option.”
There must be significance in having people recognize that.
I felt that in the age of AI, they are trying to expand the very role of the personal computer.
Meta has a reason for wanting to spread its AI
On the other hand, Meta also has a reason for cooperating.
The Muse Spark Open released this time is an AI model called 
an “open-weight” model.
This is a format where developers can obtain the trained data needed to run the AI 
and use it in their own environments.
Although there are terms of use, various developers will be able to use Muse Spark Open, not just within Meta's services.
In this announcement, methods for using it via cloud APIs were also provided.
So, when Meta wants to spread its own AI, what is necessary?
Creating an excellent AI model is a given, but the environment to run it is also important.
No matter how high-performance it is, if the equipment to run it is limited or 
if implementation is difficult, the user base will not expand.
That is where Apple's Mac Studio comes in.
By combining Meta's AI model with Apple's technology, 
they have shown that high-performance AI can be run on a local computer.
Apple can demonstrate the capabilities of the Mac.
Meta can expand the environment where its AI can be used.
Even though the two companies do not share the same goal, 
by cooperating, benefits are created for each of them.
This is the part I found particularly interesting about this news.
A reason to cooperate was born, not a reconciliation
Regarding this cooperation, Axios reports that 
the two companies have been working together for several months.
It was also reported that Daniel Gross, who previously worked at Apple and is now at Meta, played a role in connecting the two companies.
Furthermore, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and Apple CEO John Ternus have also shown positive reactions to this achievement.
Looking at these interactions, it feels as though the relationship between the two companies has changed significantly.
However, this cooperation does not mean that all past conflicts have been resolved.
It does not mean that Apple has abandoned its policy of prioritizing privacy, nor that Meta has changed its approach to its advertising business.
Rather, they are cooperating where they can, while leaving their disagreements aside.
When you think about it that way, this event looks a little different.
Precisely because we know about their past conflicts, the decision for both companies to cooperate in the AI field feels even more intriguing.
The AI era might even change the relationships between companies
While researching this news, I thought about something.
Do companies in the future really need to complete everything on their own?
Of course, it is important to accumulate technology and know-how in-house.
However, in fields like AI where technological progress is rapid and 
development requires significant funding and talent, 
it is not always rational for a single company to handle everything.
Focusing on areas where a company excels and combining them with the strengths of other companies 
can sometimes create value faster.
I think Apple and Meta are one example of this.
And this shouldn't just be a story about large corporations.
For example, when a small or medium-sized enterprise starts a new service.
Trying to handle everything from planning and production to customer acquisition and system development in-house takes both time and money.
However, by collaborating with companies and experts who excel in different areas, you might be able to tackle things that would have been difficult to achieve on your own.
As someone who works in social media marketing, I sometimes find myself thinking about how much I can handle in-house.
But seeing this news made me think about it all over again.
Perhaps what is important is not becoming a company that can do everything, but understanding your own strengths and thinking about who you can combine them with to deliver even better value. perhaps that is what matters.
Of course, you cannot simply apply the collaboration between giant corporations like Apple and Meta to small and medium-sized enterprises.
Also, collaborating with competitors involves challenges regarding information management, conflicts of interest, and contractual issues.
Even so, I feel that the idea of leveraging each other's strengths rather than bringing everything you lack in-house is a useful reference regardless of company size.
Competition and cooperation can coexist
When I first saw the news that Apple and Meta were collaborating in the AI field, I was surprised.
But when I looked into it, I found that Apple had its reasons, and Meta had its own.
The two companies will likely continue to compete in various fields, and that probably won't change.
Even so, if they decide that there is more to gain by cooperating, they will join forces.
I don't think the relationship between companies is a simple binary choice of being either "rivals" or "partners."
It is not yet clear how much this event will change the long-term relationship between Apple and Meta.
However, it is interesting that a new technology like AI is becoming a catalyst for rethinking the relationships between companies.
Competing and cooperating are not necessarily contradictory.
In our own business, what are our strengths, and where and with whom should we complement each other?
This was news that prompted me to think about such things.
